# Lesson M-265 — Trigonometric substitution

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Antiderivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Trigonometric substitution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trigonometric substitution**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Antiderivatives*

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$. The toolkit: power rule, substitution, parts, partial fractions, trig integrals, trig sub, reduction. Choosing the tool is the skill; carrying out the algebra is the tax.

### This skill, specifically

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Trig sub: $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ use $x=a\sin\theta$; $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ use $x=a\tan\theta$; $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ use $x=a\sec\theta$. Draw the triangle to convert back to $x$. Restrict $\theta$ so the substitution is 1-1.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx$ via $x=\sin\theta$.

**Solution.** $\int d\theta=\theta=\arcsin x+C$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int\dfrac{1}{x^2+1}\,dx$ via $x=\tan\theta$

**Solution.** $\int\cos^2/\cos^2\,d\theta=\theta=\arctan x+C$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dropping $dx=a\cos\theta\,d\theta$, or taking $\sqrt{\cos^2\theta}=|\cos\theta|$ carelessly as $-\cos$ in Q1.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
