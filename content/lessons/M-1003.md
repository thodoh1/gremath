This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Change coordinates or bases when stuck**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

When stuck, change coordinates: complete the square; polar $x=r\cos\theta$; translate the origin to a singularity or a vertex; rotate to principal axes of a quadratic form; switch to an eigenbasis to compute $A^n$. A hard integral in $xy$ may be a trivial Jacobian in polar. A coupled linear ODE may diagonalize.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Evaluate $\iint_{x^2+y^2\le 1} e^{-(x^2+y^2)}\,dA$.

**Solution.** Polar: $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 e^{-r^2} r\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi\cdot\frac12(1-e^{-1})=\pi(1-1/e)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Compute $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix}^n$ by binomial/nilpotent expansion:

**Solution.** $I+nN=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Substituting $x=r\cos\theta$ but forgetting the Jacobian $r$, or rotating a quadratic form without a translation first when linear terms are present.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2/2}\,dx$ via polar on the square of the integral.

**Solution.** $\sqrt{2\pi}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $x^2+4xy+4y^2=(x+2y)^2$: coordinates $u=x+2y$, $v=y$ turn $x^2+4xy+4y^2=1$ into

**Solution.** $u^2=1$, a pair of parallel lines.
