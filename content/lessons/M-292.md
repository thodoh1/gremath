# Lesson M-292 — Monotone sequences

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Sequences** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1; O.1`.


This lesson isolates **Monotone sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Monotone sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Monotone: $a_{n+1}\ge a_n$ (increasing) or $\le$ (decreasing). A monotone sequence either converges (if bounded) or diverges to $\pm\infty$. To prove convergence, prove monotonicity and a bound; the limit may still be unknown.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $a_1=\sqrt{2}$, $a_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+a_n}$. Show increasing and bounded by $2$.

**Solution.** Induction: $a_n\le a_{n+1}\le 2$. Converges; limit $L=\sqrt{2+L}$ so $L=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a_n=n/(n+1)$ is

**Solution.** increasing, bounded by $1$, $\to 1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Monotone $\Rightarrow$ convergent (need a bound).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $a_n=(-1)^n$ monotone?

**Solution.** No.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $a_n=1-1/n$ is

**Solution.** increasing, $\to 1$.
