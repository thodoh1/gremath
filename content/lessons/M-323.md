# Lesson M-323 — Pointwise vs uniform convergence

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Uniform convergence basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.3`.


This lesson isolates **Pointwise vs uniform convergence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Pointwise vs uniform convergence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Uniform convergence basics*

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not. Weierstrass M-test is a comparison test for functions. This is GRE awareness: enough to not interchange limit and integral blindly.

### This skill, specifically

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Pointwise convergence: for each $x$, $f_n(x)\to f(x)$. Uniform: $\sup_x |f_n(x)-f(x)|\to 0$. Uniform $\Rightarrow$ pointwise; the converse is false. Uniform keeps continuity: uniform limit of continuous is continuous.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$. Pointwise limit?

**Solution.** $0$ on $[0,1)$, $1$ at $1$. Not uniform (limit discontinuous, or $\sup=1$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f_n=x/n$ on $\mathbb{R}$

**Solution.** Pointwise $0$, not uniform ($\sup=\infty$). On $[0,1]$, uniform.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking only one $x$ and calling it uniform.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $f_n=\sin(nx)/n$ uniform on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes, $\sup\le 1/n\to 0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Uniform limit of continuous functions is

**Solution.** continuous.
