# Lesson M-854 — Poisson distribution

Part T. T. Probability · **Common distributions** · High

Prerequisites: `T.2-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Poisson distribution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Poisson distribution**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Common distributions*

Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, negative binomial, hypergeometric, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal, gamma/beta at property level. Match the story to the name.

### This skill, specifically

Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, negative binomial, hypergeometric, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal, gamma/beta at property level.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Poisson($\lambda$): $P(X=k)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k/k!$ for $k=0,1,\ldots$. Mean $\lambda$, variance $\lambda$, MGF $\exp\bigl(\lambda(e^t-1)\bigr)$. Approximation to $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$ when $n$ is large, $p$ small, $\lambda=np$ moderate. Sum of independent Poissons is Poisson with added rates.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\lambda=3$. $P(X=2)$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

**Solution.** $e^{-3}\cdot 9/2=\frac92 e^{-3}$; mean $3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Independent $X\sim\mathrm{Pois}(2)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Pois}(5)$. Law of $X+Y$.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Pois}(7)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k$ without $k!$, or variance $\lambda^2$. Also Poisson as a model for a bounded $n$-trial count without stating the rare-event regime.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $P(X=0)$ for $\mathrm{Pois}(1.5)$.

**Solution.** $e^{-1.5}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\mathrm{Bin}(100,0.03)$ is close to which Poisson?

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Pois}(3)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
