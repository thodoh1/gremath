## 3. Precise statement

Eliminate by parity, sign, or growth: $n(n+1)$ is always even; $(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$ has period $4$; a polynomial of even degree with positive leading coefficient is bounded below; $2^n$ vs $n^{10}$ — exponential wins eventually; $\sin$ values in $[-1,1]$. If a choice is negative and the quantity is a square, drop it. If a sequence is increasing and positive, a negative choice is dead.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $n^2+n+1$ ever even for integer $n$?

**Solution.** No: $n^2+n=n(n+1)$ is even, plus $1$ is odd.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Possible values of $\det(A^\top A)$ for real $A$?

**Solution.** Always $\ge 0$ (Gram determinant / product of squared singular values).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting $\sqrt{x^2}=-x$ for $x>0$ as a simplification, or claiming $2^n<n^2$ for large $n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Which grows faster as $n\to\infty$: $n^{100}$ or $1.01^n$?

**Solution.** The exponential $1.01^n$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $f(x)=x^2+1$ on $\mathbb{R}$ can equal $0$?

**Solution.** No: always $\ge 1$.
