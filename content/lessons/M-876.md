# Lesson M-876 — Moments

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Descriptive statistics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.2`.


This lesson isolates **Moments** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Moments**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The $k$th raw moment is $E[X^k]$; the $k$th central moment is $\mu_k=E[(X-\mu)^k]$. Mean is the first raw moment; variance is the second central moment. A moment-generating function $M(t)=E[e^{tX}]$, when finite in a neighborhood of $0$, determines all moments by $M^{(k)}(0)=E[X^k]$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For $X\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, $E[X^3]$.

**Solution.** $p$, because $X^3=X$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Third central moment of a symmetric distribution about $\mu$ is

**Solution.** $0$ (odd function of $X-\mu$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing raw and central moments: $E[X^2]$ is not $\mathrm{Var}(X)$ unless $\mu=0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If $E[X]=2$ and $E[X^2]=8$, then $\mathrm{Var}(X)=$

**Solution.** $4$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does finite $E[|X|]$ imply finite $E[X^2]$?

**Solution.** No: e.g. $P(X=n)\propto 1/(n^2\log n)$ style tails, or Student's $t_2$.
