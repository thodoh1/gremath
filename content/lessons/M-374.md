# Lesson M-374 — Gradient

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Curl, divergence & flux** · High

Prerequisites: `I.1-H.2`.


This lesson isolates **Gradient** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Gradient**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Curl, divergence & flux*

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface. Orientation is a sign convention you must not treat as optional.

### This skill, specifically

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

In multivariable calculus the gradient of a scalar $f$ is the vector field $\nabla f=(f_x,f_y,f_z)$. It is orthogonal to level surfaces $f=c$, and $df=\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf{r}$. (This is the same operator as in linear algebra's 'gradient', but here it produces a vector field on $\mathbb{R}^n$.)

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $f=e^{xy}+z^2$. Compute $\nabla f$ at $(0,2,3)$.

**Solution.** $\nabla f=(y e^{xy}, x e^{xy}, 2z)$, so $(2,0,6)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\nabla(x^2+y^2+z^2)$ at $(1,2,2)$ is

**Solution.** $(2,4,4)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\nabla f$ as a scalar (the divergence of a vector, or $|\nabla f|$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Level curve of $f=x^2+y^2$ through $(3,4)$ is orthogonal to

**Solution.** $\nabla f(3,4)=(6,8)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\nabla(fg)$ expands as

**Solution.** $f\nabla g+g\nabla f$.
