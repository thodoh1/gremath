# Lesson M-666 — Integrability of continuous functions

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Differentiability & integration** · High

Prerequisites: `E.3-O.4`.


This lesson isolates **Integrability of continuous functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Integrability of continuous functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Every continuous $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$ is Riemann integrable: uniform continuity makes the oscillation on each subinterval $<\varepsilon$ when the mesh is small, hence $U-L<\varepsilon(b-a)$. Monotone functions are integrable (at most countably many jumps). Products of integrable functions need a proof; compositions may fail.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $\sqrt{x}$ integrable on $[0,1]$ even though it is not differentiable at $0$?

**Solution.** It is continuous on a compact interval, hence integrable.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is a monotone function on $[a,b]$ Riemann integrable?

**Solution.** Yes.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Requiring differentiability for integrability.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $|x|$ integrable on $[-1,1]$?

**Solution.** Yes, continuous.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Continuous on $(0,1]$ and unbounded: integrable on $[0,1]$?

**Solution.** No: Riemann theory requires a bounded function on a closed interval ($1/\sqrt{x}$ is improperly integrable).
