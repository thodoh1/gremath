# Lesson M-883 — Unbiased estimator

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.1-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Unbiased estimator** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Unbiased estimator**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Estimation*

### This skill, specifically

Sample vs population, estimator as a random variable, bias $E[\hat\theta]-\theta$, unbiasedness, consistency, likelihood and MLE, Fisher information as awareness.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$\hat\theta$ is unbiased if $E_\theta[\hat\theta]=\theta$ for all $\theta$ in the model. Classic: $s^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$ in an iid sample with finite variance (not requiring normality). $\bar X$ is unbiased for $\mu$. Unbiased estimators are not unique.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $X_1$ is unbiased for $\mu$ if $E[X_i]=\mu$.

**Solution.** $E[X_1]=\mu$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ unbiased for $\sigma^2$?

**Solution.** No: expectation $\frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $n-1$ in the denominator of a variance for a listed complete population of size $n$ when the problem defined that list as the population.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** iid Poisson($\lambda$): unbiased estimator of $\lambda$ from $n$ observations.

**Solution.** $\bar X$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Two unbiased estimators of $\mu$: $\bar X$ and $X_1$. Which has smaller variance (iid, finite $\sigma^2$)?

**Solution.** $\bar X$, variance $\sigma^2/n$ vs $\sigma^2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
