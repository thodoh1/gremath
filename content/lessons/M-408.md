# Lesson M-408 — Eigenvalue-based solutions

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Systems of ODEs** · High

Prerequisites: `K.7-J.2`.


This lesson isolates **Eigenvalue-based solutions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Eigenvalue-based solutions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems of ODEs*

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes. Phase portraits are the picture. Nonlinear systems linearize at equilibria.

### This skill, specifically

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}$ with $\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}$, then $\mathbf{x}(t)=e^{\lambda t}\mathbf{v}$ solves $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$. For $n$ independent eigenvectors, $\mathbf{x}=\sum c_k e^{\lambda_k t}\mathbf{v}_k$. Complex $\lambda=\alpha\pm\beta i$ produce $e^{\alpha t}(\cos\beta t\,\mathbf{a}-\sin\beta t\,\mathbf{b})$ pairs. Repeated eigenvalues may need $t e^{\lambda t}$ generalized eigenvectors.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$. Find the general solution of $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$.

**Solution.** $\lambda=1$, $\mathbf{v}_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}$; $\lambda=2$, $\mathbf{v}_2=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}$. So $\mathbf{x}=c_1 e^{t}\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}+c_2 e^{2t}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $A\mathbf{v}=3\mathbf{v}$, a solution is

**Solution.** $e^{3t}\mathbf{v}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using eigenvectors of $A^T$ without noticing, or writing $e^{A t}\mathbf{v}$ as $e^{\lambda}\mathbf{v}$ (missing $t$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Solution when $A=I_2$.

**Solution.** $e^{t}\mathbf{c}$ for arbitrary $\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^2$ ($\lambda=1,1$ already diagonal).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $\lambda=-2\pm 4i$, solutions decay or grow?

**Solution.** Decay ($e^{-2t}$ envelope).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
