# Lesson M-793 — Bipartite graphs

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Bipartite graphs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Bipartite graphs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### This skill, specifically

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A graph is bipartite if $V=X\sqcup Y$ with every edge between $X$ and $Y$. Equivalent: no odd cycle. $K_{m,n}$ is the complete bipartite graph, with $|E|=mn$. Trees are bipartite. $2$-colorable means bipartite.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Edges in $K_{3,5}$. Odd cycles?

**Solution.** $15$ edges; no odd cycle.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $C_6$ bipartite? $C_7$?

**Solution.** Yes (parts of size $3,3$). No.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming a graph with no triangles is bipartite ($C_5$ is a counterexample), or placing some edges inside a part.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Maximum edges in a simple bipartite graph on $8$ vertices.

**Solution.** $K_{4,4}$ has $16$ (balanced is maximal by convexity of $x(n-x)$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why is a tree on $12$ vertices bipartite?

**Solution.** No cycles at all, hence no odd cycle; $2$-color by distance from a root.

## 9. Lock it in
