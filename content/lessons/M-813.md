# Lesson M-813 — Big-O growth

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph representations & algorithms** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Big-O growth** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Big-O growth**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Big-O: $f=O(g)$ if $|f(n)|\le C g(n)$ for some $C$ and all large $n$. $f=\Omega(g)$ is the reverse; $\Theta$ is both. $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to 0$. Polynomials: only the highest degree matters. $n^a=O(n^b)$ iff $a\le b$. $(\log n)^c=o(n^\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $3n^2+100n+7=O(n^2)$? $=O(n^3)$? $=\Theta(n^2)$?

**Solution.** Yes; yes; yes.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $2^n$ vs $n!$ : which is $O$ of the other?

**Solution.** $2^n=O(n!)$, not conversely.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Canceling $O(n)+O(n)=O(n)$ is fine, but $O(n)-O(n)=O(1)$ is not (difference of two $n^2$ terms can still be $n^2$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $n\log n=O(n^{1.1})$?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Smallest $\Theta$ class among $n^2$, $n\log n$, $2^n$, $n!$, $\log(n!)$ for large $n$.

**Solution.** $\log(n!)=\Theta(n\log n)$ by Stirling, which is the smallest among those except it ties the order of $n\log n$; $n\log n$ and $\log(n!)$ are $\Theta$ of each other, smaller than $n^2$.
