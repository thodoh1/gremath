# Lesson M-570 — Normal subgroup

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Homomorphisms & quotients** · High

Prerequisites: `M.3`.


This lesson isolates **Normal subgroup** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Normal subgroup**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Homomorphisms & quotients*

A homomorphism preserves the operation. Kernel is a normal subgroup. First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\phi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\phi$. Quotients exist precisely because of normality.

### This skill, specifically

A homomorphism preserves the operation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A subgroup $N\le G$ is normal ($N\trianglelefteq G$) iff $gNg^{-1}=N$ for all $g$, iff $gN=Ng$ for all $g$, iff $N$ is a union of conjugacy classes, iff $N=\ker\phi$ for some hom $\phi$. Index $2$ $\Rightarrow$ normal. $A_n\trianglelefteq S_n$, $SL_n\trianglelefteq GL_n$, $n\mathbb{Z}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{Z}$. In abelian groups every subgroup is normal.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\langle(1\,2)\rangle$ normal in $S_3$? Is $A_3$?

**Solution.** No: conjugate $(1\,3)(1\,2)(1\,3)=(2\,3)\notin\langle(1\,2)\rangle$. $A_3$ has index $2$, hence normal.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Kernel of a homomorphism is always

**Solution.** normal.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking $gHg^{-1}\subseteq H$ for generators $g$ of $G$ but forgetting $g^{-1}$ (need all $g$, or check a generating set carefully).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Are all subgroups of $Q_8$ normal?

**Solution.** Yes (it is Hamiltonian / all subgroups normal).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Index-$2$ subgroup of $D_4$: the rotation subgroup. Normal?

**Solution.** Yes.
