## 3. Precise statement

Multiple-choice distractors are the answers you get from the standard mistakes: forgetting a sign; dropping a factor $2$; using $\ln x$ not $\ln|x|$; using $n$ not $n-1$ in sample variance; taking the central angle instead of half; using $a+b$ instead of $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$; reporting $c_{-2}$ as a residue. Compute the trap on purpose, then reject it. If two choices differ by a sign, check orientation, quadrant, or inequality direction.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_0^1 \dfrac{1}{x-2}\,dx=\bigl[\ln|x-2|\bigr]_0^1=\ln 1-\ln 2=-\ln 2$. The distractor $\ln 2$ is the sign error from reversing limits or dropping absolute values.

**Solution.** $-\ln 2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Derivative of $x^x=e^{x\ln x}$ is $x^x(\ln x+1)$, not $x\cdot x^{x-1}$. The power-rule distractor treats

**Solution.** the exponent as constant.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Picking the first choice that matches an intermediate expression (e.g. the characteristic polynomial instead of an eigenvalue).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
