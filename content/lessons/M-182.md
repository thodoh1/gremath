# Lesson M-182 — Euler formula e^(iθ)

Part D. D. Complex Numbers · **Polar/exponential complex form** · High

Prerequisites: `D.1-C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Euler formula e^(iθ)** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Complex numbers appear in algebra, polynomials, eigenvalues, and as a bridge to Part W.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Euler formula e^(iθ)**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Polar/exponential complex form*

$z=re^{i\theta}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$. Multiplication adds arguments and multiplies moduli. De Moivre computes powers. The $n$th roots of a nonzero complex number are $n$ equally spaced points on a circle. Roots of unity are the unit-circle special case.

### This skill, specifically

$z=re^{i\theta}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Euler's formula: $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ for real $\theta$ (and as an entire function of complex $\theta$). Consequences: $e^{i\pi}=-1$, $\cos\theta=(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})/2$, $\sin\theta=(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})/(2i)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove $\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ has modulus $1$.

**Solution.** $|e^{i\theta}|^2=\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^{i\pi}+1$ equals

**Solution.** $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $e^{i\theta}$ as if it were real-exponential growth.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Write $\cos(\pi/3)$ via Euler.

**Solution.** $(e^{i\pi/3}+e^{-i\pi/3})/2=1/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Simplify $e^{i\theta}e^{-i\theta}$.

**Solution.** $1$.
