# Lesson M-385 — Orientation/sign conventions

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Integral theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `I.2-I.3`.


This lesson isolates **Orientation/sign conventions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Orientation/sign conventions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Integral theorems*

Green, Stokes, divergence: the FTC in dimensions 2 and 3. Choose the theorem that turns a hard integral into an easy one. Track orientation.

### This skill, specifically

Green, Stokes, divergence: the FTC in dimensions 2 and 3.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Sign conventions: Green requires positive orientation (region on the left). Stokes requires right-hand compatibility of $\mathbf{n}$ and $\partial S$. Divergence uses the outward normal on $\partial E$. Reversing orientation multiplies the integral by $-1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Unit circle clockwise, $\mathbf{F}=(-y,x)$. What is $\oint\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$?

**Solution.** Green with negative orientation: $-2\pi$. Directly: $\mathbf{r}(t)=(\cos t,-\sin t)$ yields $-2\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Outward vs inward flux of a source field through a closed surface

**Solution.** are negatives; divergence theorem as stated is outward.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking 'upward' on $z=g(x,y)$ while traversing $\partial S$ clockwise when viewed from above (incompatible).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** For $S$ the disk $z=0$, $x^2+y^2\le 1$, $\mathbf{n}=-\mathbf{k}$, the equator is oriented

**Solution.** clockwise viewed from above.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If both $S$ and $\partial S$ are reversed, Stokes' two sides

**Solution.** both change sign, equality remains.
