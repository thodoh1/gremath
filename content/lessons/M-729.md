# Lesson M-729 — Proper subset

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Proper subset** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Proper subset**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A proper subset $A\subsetneq B$ (sometimes written $A\subset B$ by authors who use $\subseteq$ for the weak relation) means $A\subset B$ and $A\neq B$, so some element of $B$ lies outside $A$. $\mathbb{N}\subsetneq\mathbb{Z}\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}$. Every set has itself as a subset, not always proper.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\{1,2\}$ a proper subset of $\{1,2,3\}$? Of $\{1,2\}$?

**Solution.** Yes; no.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does every nonempty set have a proper subset?

**Solution.** Yes: at least $\emptyset$, unless one excludes $\emptyset$ by convention; $\emptyset$ itself has no proper subset.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\subset$ without checking the author's convention on whether equality is allowed.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\mathbb{Z}\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}$ because

**Solution.** e.g. $1/2\notin\mathbb{Z}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\emptyset\subsetneq\{0\}$?

**Solution.** Yes.
