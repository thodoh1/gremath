## 3. Precise statement

Fixed-point iteration $x_{n+1}=g(x_n)$ seeks $x=g(x)$. If $g$ is $C^1$ and $|g'(p)|<1$ at a fixed point $p$, then $p$ is attracting: iteration converges for $x_0$ nearby, linearly with rate $|g'(p)|$. If $|g'(p)|>1$, it is repelling. Banach: on a closed set, a contraction $g$ ($L<1$) has a unique fixed point and iteration converges from any start in the set.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $g(x)=\cos x$ on $\mathbb{R}$: a unique fixed point (graphically $y=x$ meets $y=\cos x$ once). $|g'(p)|=|\sin p|<1$ since $p\in(0,1)$. Iteration converges.

**Solution.** Yes, to the Dottie number.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $g(x)=2x$ at $p=0$: attracting?

**Solution.** No: $|g'(0)|=2>1$ (repelling).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Iterating $g$ with $|g'(p)|>1$ and expecting convergence, or confusing Newton’s $g$ (where $g'(r)=0$ for simple roots) with a generic fixed-point map.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
