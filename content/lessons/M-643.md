# Lesson M-643 — Series as sequence of partial sums

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Series & convergence** · High

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Series as sequence of partial sums** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Series as sequence of partial sums**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges iff the sequence of partial sums $s_n=a_1+\cdots+a_n$ converges. The sum is $\lim s_n$. A necessary condition is $a_n\to 0$; it is not sufficient ($\sum 1/n$). Geometric series $\sum r^n$ converges iff $|r|<1$, sum $1/(1-r)$ from $n=0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $\sum_{n=0}^\infty(1/3)^n$ converge, and to what?

**Solution.** Yes, geometric $|r|=1/3<1$, sum $1/(1-1/3)=3/2$. Partial sums $s_n=(1-(1/3)^{n+1})/(1-1/3)\to 3/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $a_n\not\to 0$, then $\sum a_n$

**Solution.** diverges.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing the series with the sequence of terms; claiming $\sum 1/n$ converges because $1/n\to 0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $s_n=1-1/2^n$ are partial sums of what geometric series?

**Solution.** $\sum_{k=1}^n 1/2^k$, sum $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $\sum (-1)^n$ converge?

**Solution.** No: $a_n\not\to 0$.
