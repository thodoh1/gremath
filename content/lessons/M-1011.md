This lesson isolates **Work backwards from answer choices** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Work backwards from answer choices**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

When the stem is an equation and the choices are candidate numbers (or functions), plug the choices. Factoring a cubic to find ‘the real root’ is slower than testing $x=1,2,-1$. For ‘which is an antiderivative’, differentiate the choices. For ‘which matrix is $A^{-1}$’, multiply $A$ by a choice. Working backwards is not cheating; it is matching the question type.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $1,2,3$ is a root of $x^3-6x^2+11x-6=0$? Do not expand a full factoring search first.

**Solution.** Plug: $f(1)=0$. (Indeed $(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$.) The first choice that works is enough if the question asks for a root, not all roots.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Choices for $A^{-1}$: compute $A$ times (C). If $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and (C) is $\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix}$, then $A$(C)$=$

**Solution.** $I$, so (C) is the inverse.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Testing only $x=0$ for a polynomial that was never claimed to have $0$ as a root, then guessing.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f'(x)=2x$, $f(0)=3$. Which of $x^2$, $x^2+3$, $x^2-3$ is $f$? Differentiate/plug $x=0$.

**Solution.** $x^2+3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Solve $2^x=8$ by testing choices $2,3,4$.

**Solution.** $x=3$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
