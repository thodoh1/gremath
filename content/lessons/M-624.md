# Lesson M-624 — Finite fields F\_p

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Fields & extensions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.3-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Finite fields F_p** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Finite fields F_p**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Fields & extensions*

A field has every nonzero element invertible. Extensions $K/F$ have degree $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. Algebraic vs transcendental, minimal polynomials, splitting fields, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

### This skill, specifically

A field has every nonzero element invertible.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The field $\mathbb{F}_p=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for $p$ prime is the unique field with $p$ elements (up to iso), characteristic $p$. Arithmetic is mod $p$. Freshman's dream: $(a+b)^{p}=a^p+b^p$ in characteristic $p$ (binomial coefficients $\binom{p}{k}$ divisible by $p$ for $0<k<p$). Fermat: $a^p=a$ for all $a\in\mathbb{F}_p$. The multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ is cyclic of order $p-1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $(1+2)^3$ vs $1^3+2^3$ in $\mathbb{F}_3$, and find a generator of $\mathbb{F}_7^\times$.

**Solution.** In $\mathbb{F}_3$, $p=3$: $(0)^3=0=1+8\equiv 1+2=0$. Explicitly $(1+2)^3=0^3=0$ and $1+8=9\equiv 0$. Generator of $\mathbb{F}_7^\times$: $3$ (primitive root, orders to $6$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|\mathbb{F}_p^\times|$ equals

**Solution.** $p-1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using division by $p$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$, or writing $\mathbb{F}_4=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Inverse of $3$ in $\mathbb{F}_{11}$.

**Solution.** $4$, because $12\equiv 1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is every nonzero element of $\mathbb{F}_{11}$ a square?

**Solution.** No: $(p-1)/2=5$ residues. $2$ is a nonresidue? $1^2=1$, $2^2=4$, $3^2=9$, $4^2=5$, $5^2=3$. Residues $1,3,4,5,9$. $2$ is a nonresidue.
