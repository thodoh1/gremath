# Lesson M-841 — Exponential distribution

Part T. T. Probability · **Continuous random variables** · High

Prerequisites: `T.2-F.2`.


This lesson isolates **Exponential distribution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Exponential distribution**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Continuous random variables*

PDF integrates to $1$, $P(X=x)=0$, CDF is an integral of the PDF, expectation $\int x f(x)\,dx$. Uniform, exponential, normal as first models.

### This skill, specifically

PDF integrates to $1$, $P(X=x)=0$, CDF is an integral of the PDF, expectation $\int x f(x)\,dx$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Exponential law with rate $\lambda>0$: PDF $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ for $x>0$, CDF $1-e^{-\lambda x}$, mean $1/\lambda$, variance $1/\lambda^2$. Memoryless: $P(X>s+t\mid X>s)=P(X>t)$. The minimum of independent Exp$(\lambda_i)$ is Exp$(\sum\lambda_i)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda=3)$. $P(X>2)$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

**Solution.** $e^{-6}$; mean $1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Memoryless: $P(X>5\mid X>2)$ for Exp($1$).

**Solution.** $P(X>3)=e^{-3}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using PDF $e^{-\lambda x}$ without the factor $\lambda$, so it is not a density unless $\lambda=1$. Mixing rate and mean parametrizations: some texts use mean $\theta=1/\lambda$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** CDF of $\mathrm{Exp}(2)$ at $x=1$.

**Solution.** $1-e^{-2}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Independent $X\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(4)$. Law of $\min(X,Y)$.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Exp}(5)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
