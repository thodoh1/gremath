# Lesson M-733 — Complement

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Complement** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Complement**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The complement of $A$ relative to a universe $X$ is $A^c=X\setminus A$. Then $(A^c)^c=A$, $A\cup A^c=X$, $A\cap A^c=\emptyset$. De Morgan: $(A\cup B)^c=A^c\cap B^c$. Complement depends on the ambient $X$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If $X=\mathbb{Z}$ and $A$ is the even integers, what is $A^c$? If instead $X=\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** The odd integers; $\mathbb{R}\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}$, which includes odds, non-integers, etc.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(A\cap B)^c$ equals

**Solution.** $A^c\cup B^c$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking complements in two different universes in the same argument.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Complement of $(0,1)$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** $(-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\emptyset^c=X$ and $X^c=\emptyset$?

**Solution.** Yes.
