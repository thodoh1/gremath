# Lesson M-905 — Parallel/perpendicular lines

Part V. V. Geometry · **Euclidean geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Parallel/perpendicular lines** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Parallel/perpendicular lines**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

If two lines are cut by a transversal, they are parallel iff corresponding angles are equal iff alternate interiors are equal iff consecutive interiors are supplementary. Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel in the Euclidean plane. Slope criterion in coordinates: parallel $\Leftrightarrow$ equal slopes; perpendicular $\Leftrightarrow$ $m_1 m_2=-1$ (neither vertical).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Transversal with an interior angle $110^\circ$ on one side of parallel lines. Consecutive interior?

**Solution.** $70^\circ$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Slopes $2$ and $-1/2$: the lines are

**Solution.** perpendicular.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $m_1 m_2=-1$ when one line is vertical, or mixing corresponding with consecutive interior.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Line $y=3x+1$ parallel to $y=3x-4$?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A line perpendicular to $y=2x$ has slope

**Solution.** $-1/2$.
