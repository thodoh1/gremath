# Lesson M-863 — Weak-convergence intuition

Part T. T. Probability · **Limit theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `T.5-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Weak-convergence intuition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Weak-convergence intuition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limit theorems*

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation. CLT: standardized sums $\to$ normal. Continuity correction for lattice $\to$ continuous. Weak convergence is the topology behind the CLT.

### This skill, specifically

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Convergence in distribution (weak convergence): $X_n\Rightarrow X$ if $F_n(x)\to F(x)$ at all continuity points $x$ of $F$. Equivalent (bounded continuous $g$): $\mathbb{E}[g(X_n)]\to\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$. The CLT is $Z_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)$. Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution; the converse needs a constant limit.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X_n=X+1/n$ for a continuous $X$. Does $X_n\Rightarrow X$? In probability?

**Solution.** Yes and yes: $1/n\to 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Independent copies $X_n$ of a nondegenerate $X$. Does $X_n\Rightarrow X$? In probability?

**Solution.** $X_n\Rightarrow X$ always (same law), but $X_n\not\to X$ in probability unless $X$ is a.s. constant.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking $F_n(x)\to F(x)$ at a jump of $F$ and declaring failure of weak convergence (the definition exempts jumps).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
