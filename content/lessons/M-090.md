# Lesson M-090 — Combining transformations efficiently

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Function transformations** · Critical

Prerequisites: `B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Combining transformations efficiently** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Combining transformations efficiently**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Function transformations*

Replacing $x$ by $x-h$ or $x/a$, replacing $f$ by $f+k$ or $cf$, and reflecting through axes are rigid rules that people reverse under pressure. $y=f(x-h)$ shifts the graph of $f$ to the right by $h$. Inverse functions reflect through $y=x$. Combining transformations requires a consistent order.

### This skill, specifically

Replacing $x$ by $x-h$ or $x/a$, replacing $f$ by $f+k$ or $cf$, and reflecting through axes are rigid rules that people reverse under pressure.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Combine transformations by composing the corresponding maps on the $x$ and $y$ coordinates. Factor constants: $f(2x-6)=f(2(x-3))$ is compress by $1/2$ then shift right $3$ (or shift first by $6$ then compress — order matters unless you rewrite).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Rewrite $f(2x-6)$ to see the shift.

**Solution.** $f(2(x-3))$: shift right $3$, horizontal compress $1/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $2f(x-1)+4$ applied to point $(1,5)$ on $y=f(x)$ goes to

**Solution.** $(2,14)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Shifting by $6$ after compressing by $2$ without rewriting $2x-6=2(x-3)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Simplify $-(2(x+1))^2$.

**Solution.** $-4(x+1)^2$: left $1$, vertical stretch $4$, $x$-axis flip.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Two routes for $f(ax-b)$ that agree.

**Solution.** Factor $a(x-b/a)$: shift $b/a$ then scale $a$, versus scale then shift $b$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
