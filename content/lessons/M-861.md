# Lesson M-861 — Normal approximation

Part T. T. Probability · **Limit theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `T.5-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Normal approximation** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Normal approximation**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limit theorems*

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation. CLT: standardized sums $\to$ normal. Continuity correction for lattice $\to$ continuous. Weak convergence is the topology behind the CLT.

### This skill, specifically

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Normal approximation: $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\approx N\bigl(np,np(1-p)\bigr)$ when $n$ is large and $p$ is not too near $0$ or $1$ (rule of thumb: $np$ and $n(1-p)$ at least about $5$ or $10$). Poisson($\lambda$) $\approx N(\lambda,\lambda)$ for large $\lambda$. Always convert to $Z$ with the matching mean and variance.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(100,1/2)$. Approximate $P(X\le 40)$ without continuity correction.

**Solution.** $X\approx N(50,25)$, $P\bigl(Z\le(40-50)/5\bigr)=\Phi(-2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathrm{Pois}(64)$ approx $P(X\ge 80)$.

**Solution.** $N(64,64)$, $P\bigl(Z\ge(80-64)/8\bigr)=P(Z\ge 2)=1-\Phi(2)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using variance $np$ for a binomial, or approximating $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$ by $N(n,p)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(200,0.3)$. Mean and SD for the normal approx.

**Solution.** Mean $60$, SD $\sqrt{42}=\sqrt{200\cdot 0.3\cdot 0.7}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why is $\mathrm{Bin}(12,0.04)$ a poor normal approximation?

**Solution.** $np=0.48<5$; the law is right-skewed and nearly Poisson.
