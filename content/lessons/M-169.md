# Lesson M-169 — Distance between parallel lines

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Coordinate geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Distance between parallel lines** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Distance between parallel lines**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coordinate geometry*

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra. Tangency is discriminant zero. 3D distance is the same Pythagorean idea with one more square.

### This skill, specifically

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Distance between parallel lines $ax+by+c_1=0$ and $ax+by+c_2=0$ is $\dfrac{|c_1-c_2|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Distance between $x+y=1$ and $x+y=4$.

**Solution.** $3/\sqrt{2}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $y=2x+1$ and $y=2x-5$ distance

**Solution.** Write $2x-y+1=0$, $2x-y-5=0$, $|1-(-5)|/\sqrt{5}=6/\sqrt{5}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the formula on non-parallel lines.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Distance between $x=0$ and $x=3$.

**Solution.** $3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Normalize $2x+2y-2=0$ first or not?

**Solution.** The formula already handles non-monic $a,b$; don’t double-normalize inconsistently.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
