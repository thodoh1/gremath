# Lesson M-947 — Cauchy-Riemann equations

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex functions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `D.1-O.4`.


This lesson isolates **Cauchy-Riemann equations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cauchy-Riemann equations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex functions*

$f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$, limits and continuity as in $\mathbb{R}^2$, complex differentiability is much stronger, holomorphic $=$ analytic in this course’s setting, Cauchy–Riemann, harmonic real and imaginary parts.

### This skill, specifically

$f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$, limits and continuity as in $\mathbb{R}^2$, complex differentiability is much stronger, holomorphic $=$ analytic in this course’s setting, Cauchy–Riemann, harmonic real and imaginary parts.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Write $f=u+iv$. If $f'(z)$ exists, the Cauchy–Riemann equations $u_x=v_y$ and $u_y=-v_x$ hold, and $f'=u_x+iv_x=v_y-iu_y$. Conversely, if $u,v$ are $C^1$ (or even just differentiable as real functions) and CR hold in a neighborhood, then $f$ is holomorphic. CR in polar: $u_r=\frac1r v_\theta$, $v_r=-\frac1r u_\theta$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For $f(z)=z^2$, check CR at a generic $(x,y)$.

**Solution.** $u=x^2-y^2$, $v=2xy$: $u_x=2x=v_y$, $u_y=-2y=-v_x$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f(z)=\overline{z}$: CR?

**Solution.** $u_x=1$, $v_y=-1$, fail.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking CR at a single point and concluding holomorphicity on an open set; or forgetting the regularity hypothesis (there are pathological CR solutions not holomorphic).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f(z)=e^z$: $u=e^x\cos y$, $v=e^x\sin y$. $u_x$ vs $v_y$.

**Solution.** Both $e^x\cos y$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $f'=0$ on a connected open set, then $f$ is

**Solution.** constant (CR plus connectedness).
