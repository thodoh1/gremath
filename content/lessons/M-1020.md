# Lesson M-1020 — Flag and return strategy

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Speed & multiple-choice technique** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Flag and return strategy** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Flag and return strategy**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Speed & multiple-choice technique*

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

### This skill, specifically

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Flag-and-return: on first pass, do every item that is classified and computable in $\le 2$ minutes. Flag (i) unrecognized mixed stems, (ii) long computational integrals, (iii) $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ lookalikes. Second pass: flagged items with a now-warm brain; often a representation switch appears. Third pass: remaining guesses. Do not restart a flagged item from scratch if your first-pass note already named the theorem.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** First-pass decision: ‘Find $\int_{|z|=2} e^{1/z}\,dz$’. Classification?

**Solution.** Essential singularity at $0$, residue is $1$ (coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in $\sum z^{-n}/n!$), integral $2\pi i$. If that series is not immediate, flag after $90$ seconds rather than re-deriving Taylor of $e^w$ slowly.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A first-pass note ‘Rolle? but $f(a)\neq f(b)$’ is enough to

**Solution.** skip to a different theorem (MVT with the actual difference quotient) on return, not to abandon the item.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Re-reading a flagged stem for five minutes without looking at the scratch that already computed $f'$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Item: eigenvalues of a $3\times 3$ triangular matrix. Flag or compute now?

**Solution.** Compute now: they are the diagonal entries, $15$ seconds.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** After solving three linear-algebra items, a later ‘orthogonal projection’ flag is easier because

**Solution.** the representation $P=uu^\top/u^\top u$ is warm.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
