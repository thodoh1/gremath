# Lesson M-859 — Law of large numbers

Part T. T. Probability · **Limit theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `T.5-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Law of large numbers** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Law of large numbers**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limit theorems*

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation. CLT: standardized sums $\to$ normal. Continuity correction for lattice $\to$ continuous. Weak convergence is the topology behind the CLT.

### This skill, specifically

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Weak law of large numbers: if $X_i$ are i.i.d. with finite mean $\mu$, then $\bar X_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\to\mu$ in probability: $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|>\varepsilon)\to 0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Chebyshev plus $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X_n)=\sigma^2/n\to 0$ proves it when $\sigma^2<\infty$. This is typical-value concentration, not a distributional shape.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** I.i.d. with mean $4$, variance $9$, $n=100$. Chebyshev bound on $P(|\bar X-4|\ge 1)$.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)=0.09$, so $P\le 0.09/1^2=0.09$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming $\bar X_n=\mu$ for finite $n$, or confusing the WLLN with the CLT (the WLLN does not produce a normal limit by itself).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
