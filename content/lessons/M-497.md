### Landscape of *Inner-product spaces*

Inner products give geometry: orthogonality, norms, projections, Gram–Schmidt, least squares. Symmetric real matrices are orthogonally diagonalizable (spectral theorem). SVD is the rectangular cousin.

## 3. Precise statement

A singular value decomposition (SVD) is $A=U\Sigma V^T$ for real $A$ ($m\times n$), with $U,V$ orthogonal and $\Sigma$ rectangular 'diagonal' with nonnegative singular values $\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_r>0$. The $\sigma_i$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $A^T A$ (or $AA^T$). Rank $A$ equals the number of positive singular values. SVD yields orthonormal bases of the four fundamental subspaces and the best low-rank approximations.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Singular values of $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $A^T A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&4\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $4,0$, so $\sigma=2,0$. Rank $1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

- Calling negative eigenvalues of $A$ 'singular values', or thinking every matrix's SVD requires $A$ to be square and invertible.
