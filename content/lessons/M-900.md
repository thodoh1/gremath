# Lesson M-900 — Coefficient interpretation

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Regression** · Medium

Prerequisites: `K.8-U.1`.


This lesson isolates **Coefficient interpretation** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Coefficient interpretation**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Regression*

Least-squares line, residuals, normal equations $X^T X\hat\beta=X^T y$, interpreting slope, $R^2$, correlation as standardized slope in simple linear regression.

### This skill, specifically

Least-squares line, residuals, normal equations $X^T X\hat\beta=X^T y$, interpreting slope, $R^2$, correlation as standardized slope in simple linear regression.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

In $\hat y=\hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1 x$, the slope $\hat\beta_1$ is the predicted change in $y$ per one-unit increase in $x$, holding the linear model. The intercept $\hat\beta_0$ is the predicted $y$ at $x=0$, which may be an extrapolation. Changing units of $x$ rescales $\hat\beta_1$. Correlation is dimensionless; the slope is not.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If $x$ is measured in cm instead of m, what happens to $\hat\beta_1$?

**Solution.** It is divided by $100$ (same $\Delta y$, $\Delta x$ is $100$ times larger in cm).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\hat\beta_0$ is meaningful when

**Solution.** $x=0$ is inside the observed design, or a structurally meaningful origin.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reading $\hat\beta_1=2$ as ‘$x$ causes $y$ to double’, or equating $\hat\beta_1$ with $\rho$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Points $(0,1),(1,3)$: interpret $\hat\beta_1$.

**Solution.** $y$ increases by $2$ per unit $x$ along the fitted line.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $Y$ is also rescaled by $c>0$, $\hat\beta_1$ is multiplied by

**Solution.** $c$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
