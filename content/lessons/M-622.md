# Lesson M-622 — Splitting field

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Fields & extensions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.3-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Splitting field** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Splitting field**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Fields & extensions*

A field has every nonzero element invertible. Extensions $K/F$ have degree $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. Algebraic vs transcendental, minimal polynomials, splitting fields, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

### This skill, specifically

A field has every nonzero element invertible.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A splitting field of $f\in F[x]$ over $F$ is an extension $K/F$ in which $f$ factors into linear factors and $K$ is generated by the roots of $f$. It exists, is unique up to $F$-isomorphism, and $[K:F]$ divides $n!$ and is a multiple of $\deg$ of each irr factor, for $\deg f=n$. Example: splitting field of $x^2-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$; of $x^3-2$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)$ of degree $6$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Splitting field of $x^2+1$ over $\mathbb{R}$ and of $x^3-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Degrees?

**Solution.** Over $\mathbb{R}$: $\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}(i)$, degree $2$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)$, degree $6=3\cdot 2$ (real cube root degree $3$, then cyclotomic $x^2+x+1$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Splitting field of $(x^2-2)(x^2-3)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})$, degree $4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Stopping after adjoining one root of an irr cubic that has two complex roots (the splitting field is larger than the real simple extension).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $x^2-2$ split over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$?

**Solution.** Yes: $(x-\sqrt{2})(x+\sqrt{2})$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Degree of a splitting field of a quadratic over $F$

**Solution.** $1$ if it splits already, else $2$.
