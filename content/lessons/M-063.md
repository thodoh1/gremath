# Lesson M-063 — Rational root theorem

Part A. A. Foundations & Algebraic Fluency · **Polynomials** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3-A.4`.


This lesson isolates **Rational root theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Algebraic fluency is not a listed ETS bucket of its own, but it is the cost of every calculus and algebra item. Expect it to be invisible when you have it and fatal when you do not.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Rational root theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Polynomials*

A polynomial is determined by its coefficients. Degree, leading coefficient, roots with multiplicity, factor and remainder theorems, Vieta, and the fundamental theorem of algebra are one package. Over $\mathbb{R}$, complex roots come in conjugate pairs. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the rational root theorem is a finite search, not a guarantee.

### This skill, specifically

A polynomial is determined by its coefficients.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Rational root theorem: any rational root $p/q$ in lowest terms of $a_n x^n+\cdots+a_0$ has $p\mid a_0$ and $q\mid a_n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Possible rational roots of $2x^3-x+3=0$.

**Solution.** $\pm 1,3,\pm 1/2,3/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $x^3-x-1=0$ have a rational root?

**Solution.** Candidates $\pm 1$; $p(1)=-1\neq 0$, $p(-1)=-1\neq 0$. No rational root.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating the list as guaranteed roots rather than candidates.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Test whether $3/2$ is a root of $2x^3-3x^2-3x+2$.

**Solution.** $p(3/2)=2\cdot 27/8-3\cdot 9/4-3\cdot 3/2+2=27/4-27/4-9/2+2\neq 0$. Compute carefully: actually $2(27/8)=27/4$, $3(9/4)=27/4$, so they cancel; $-9/2+2=-0.5\neq 0$. Not a root.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Possible rational roots of $x^3+2x+2$.

**Solution.** $\pm 1,\pm 2$. None work, so no rational roots.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
