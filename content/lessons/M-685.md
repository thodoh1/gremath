# Lesson M-685 — Product topology

Part P. P. Topology · **Topological spaces** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1`.


This lesson isolates **Product topology** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Product topology**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Topological spaces*

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections. Bases, subbases, subspace and product topologies, continuity as preimages of opens, homeomorphism as topological isomorphism.

### This skill, specifically

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The product topology on $X\times Y$ has basis $U\times V$ with $U$ open in $X$, $V$ open in $Y$. Projections are continuous and open. For infinite products $\prod X_i$, the product topology uses finitely many nontrivial open factors (box topology allows all). $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the $n$-fold product of $\mathbb{R}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $(0,1)\times(0,1)$ open in $\mathbb{R}^2$? Is the open unit disk a basis element?

**Solution.** Yes, a basis element. The disk is open but not a single rectangle; it is a union of rectangles (or balls).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Infinite product: is $\prod_{n=1}^\infty (0,1)$ open in $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the product topology?

**Solution.** No: infinitely many nontrivial factors. It is open in the box topology.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using box-style basic opens in an infinite product when the problem uses the product topology.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
