# Lesson M-272 — Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part II

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part II** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part II**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

FTC Part II: if $F'=f$ on $[a,b]$ with $f$ integrable (e.g. continuous), then $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$. This is how you evaluate definite integrals: find any antiderivative, then plug in.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_0^\pi \cos x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\sin\pi-\sin 0=0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int_1^e \frac{1}{x}\,dx$

**Solution.** $1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using an antiderivative that is not valid on the whole interval: $\int_{-1}^1 1/x^2$ is improper and diverges, even though $-1/x$ from $-1$ to $1$ looks like $-2$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_0^1 3x^2\,dx$.

**Solution.** $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Must $F$ be $C^1$?

**Solution.** $F$ needs to be an antiderivative; $f=F'$ integrable is the hypothesis used here.
