# Lesson M-146 — Area using sine

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Triangle trigonometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Area using sine** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Area using sine**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Triangle trigonometry*

Law of sines, law of cosines, area $\frac12 ab\sin C$, Heron, and the ambiguous SSA case are the computational triangle toolkit. SSA can yield $0,1,$ or $2$ triangles; that is a GRE favorite. Inscribed-angle facts connect this topic to circles.

### This skill, specifically

## 3. Precise statement

Area $=\dfrac12 ab\sin C$. Also $\dfrac{abc}{4R}$ and $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ (Heron).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Area of SAS triangle $a=5$, $b=8$, $C=30^\circ$.

**Solution.** $\frac12\cdot 5\cdot 8\cdot \frac12=10$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Equilateral side $2$, area

**Solution.** $\frac12\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot\sin 60=\sqrt{3}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\sin$ of a non-included angle without law of sines first.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
