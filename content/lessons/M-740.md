# Lesson M-740 — Partial orders

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Partial orders** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Partial orders**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A partial order is a reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive relation. Examples: $\le$ on $\mathbb{R}$; $\subset$ on $\mathcal{P}(X)$; divisibility $\mid$ on $\mathbb{N}$. Incomparable elements may exist: $2\nmid 3$ and $3\nmid 2$. Hasse diagrams display covering relations.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is divisibility on $\mathbb{N}$ a partial order but not a total order?

**Solution.** Reflexive, antisymmetric ($a\mid b$ and $b\mid a\Rightarrow a=b$ for positive integers), transitive. Not total: $2$ and $3$ are incomparable.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\subset$ on $\mathcal{P}(\{1,2\})$ a partial order?

**Solution.** Yes. $\{1\}$ and $\{2\}$ are incomparable.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Requiring comparability of every pair (that is a total order), or dropping antisymmetry (preorders).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Maximal vs maximum in a poset.

**Solution.** Maximum is comparable to and $\ge$ every element; maximal means nothing is strictly above it.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Least element of $(\mathcal{P}(X),\subset)$.

**Solution.** $\emptyset$.
