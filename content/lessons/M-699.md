# Lesson M-699 — Connected subsets of the real line

Part P. P. Topology · **Connectedness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **Connected subsets of the real line** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Connected subsets of the real line**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Connectedness*

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens. Path-connected implies connected. Continuous images preserve connectedness. Connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are intervals.

### This skill, specifically

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A subset $A\subset\mathbb{R}$ (usual topology) is connected iff $A$ is an interval: whenever $a,b\in A$ and $a<c<b$, one has $c\in A$. Thus the connected subsets are (possibly infinite, open, closed, half-open, degenerate) intervals. This classification fails in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $(0,1)$, $[0,1]$, $[0,\infty)$, $\{0\}$, $[0,1]\cup[1,2]$, $[0,1]\cup[2,3]$ are connected in $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** All except the last: $[0,1]\cup[1,2]=[0,2]$ is an interval. The last misses $(1,2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are precisely

**Solution.** intervals.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ an interval, or thinking a disconnected set cannot be dense.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$ connected?

**Solution.** No: it is a countable disjoint union of open intervals.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A nonempty open connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is

**Solution.** an open interval (possibly infinite).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
