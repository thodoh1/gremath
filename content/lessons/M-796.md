# Lesson M-796 — Leaves

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Leaves** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Leaves**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Special graph classes*

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton. Euler is degree-parity; Hamilton is hard.

### This skill, specifically

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A leaf is a degree-$1$ vertex (in a rooted tree, typically a vertex with no children, which for the root would mean $K_1$). Every tree on $n\ge 2$ vertices has at least two leaves. Deleting a leaf from a tree leaves a tree.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** The tree that is a star $K_{1,8}$. How many leaves?

**Solution.** $8$ (the center is degree $8$, not a leaf).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A path $P_{9}$. Leaves?

**Solution.** $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling every degree-$2$ vertex a leaf, or forgetting that in a rooted binary tree an internal node can have one child and is not a leaf.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Tree on $6$ vertices with $4$ leaves. What is the degree sequence?

**Solution.** Possible: $3,3,1,1,1,1$ (two adjacent degree-$3$ vertices) or $4,2,1,1,1,1$. Both sum to $10=2\cdot 5$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Remove all leaves from $K_{1,5}$. What remains?

**Solution.** A single vertex (the center).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
