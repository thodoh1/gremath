# Lesson M-270 — Total area

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Total area** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Total area**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Total (geometric) area between $y=f(x)$ and the $x$-axis is $\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx$. Split at zeros of $f$. Net signed area can be zero while total area is not.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Total area between $y=\sin x$ and the $x$-axis on $[0,2\pi]$.

**Solution.** $2\int_0^\pi\sin x=4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Total area of $x$ on $[-1,1]$

**Solution.** $\int_{-1}^1|x|=1$, while signed area is $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting to split at roots and integrating $f$ instead of $|f|$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Total area of $x^2-1$ on $[0,2]$.

**Solution.** Root at $1$: $\int_0^1(1-x^2)+\int_1^2(x^2-1)=2/3+4/3=2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** When is total area equal to $|\int f|$?

**Solution.** When $f$ does not change sign.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
