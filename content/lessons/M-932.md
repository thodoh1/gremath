# Lesson M-932 — Cone area/volume

Part V. V. Geometry · **3D geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.6-K.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cone area/volume** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cone area/volume**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *3D geometry*

Distance in $\mathbb{R}^3$, line-plane relations, sphere equation and volume/area, cylinder, cone, pyramid, prism. Slice or unfold.

### This skill, specifically

Distance in $\mathbb{R}^3$, line-plane relations, sphere equation and volume/area, cylinder, cone, pyramid, prism.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A right circular cone of base radius $r$, height $h$, slant $l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$: volume $\frac13\pi r^2 h$, lateral area $\pi r l$, total surface $\pi r l+\pi r^2$. A frustum of heights $h$ and radii $R,r$ has volume $\frac13\pi h(R^2+Rr+r^2)$. Similarity: a plane parallel to the base cuts a small similar cone.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Volume of a cone $r=3$, $h=4$.

**Solution.** $12\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Slant of $r=5$, $h=12$.

**Solution.** $13$. Lateral area $65\pi$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\pi r^2 h$ as if it were a cylinder, or using $h$ in place of slant for lateral area.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A cone of height $6$ is cut parallel to the base $2$ units from the vertex. Volume ratio small:original.

**Solution.** $(2/6)^3=1/27$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Frustum $R=4$, $r=2$, $h=6$: volume.

**Solution.** $56\pi$ because $\frac13\pi\cdot 6(16+8+4)=56\pi$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
