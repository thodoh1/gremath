## 3. Precise statement

The ordinary generating function (OGF) of a sequence $(a_n)$ is $A(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}a_n x^n$. Product of OGFs convolves sequences: coefficients of $A(x)B(x)$ are $\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k b_{n-k}$. Geometric series: $1/(1-x)=\sum x^n$. $(1-x)^{-k}=\sum\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}x^n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Coefficient of $x^5$ in $(1-x)^{-4}$.

**Solution.** $\binom{5+4-1}{4-1}=\binom{8}{3}=56$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** OGF for the number of unrestricted compositions of $n$ with parts in $\{1,2,3\}$ is

**Solution.** $1/(1-(x+x^2+x^3))$ for $n\ge 1$ after dropping the constant $1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reading $[x^n](1-x)^{-k}$ as $\binom{n}{k}$ instead of $\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $[x^4](1+x+x^2)^3$.

**Solution.** Stars and bars with each factor contributing $0,1,$ or $2$: solutions of $y_1+y_2+y_3=4$ with $0\le y_i\le 2$. Count: $6$ (the triples $(2,2,0)$ and permutations: $3$, and $(2,1,1)$ and permutations: $3$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** OGF for $\binom{n}{2}$ is

**Solution.** $\sum\binom{n}{2}x^n=x^2/(1-x)^3$ for $n\ge 0$.
