# Lesson M-786 — Directed graphs

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Directed graphs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Directed graphs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A directed graph (digraph) has ordered pairs as arcs. In-degree $\mathrm{deg}^-(v)$ and out-degree $\mathrm{deg}^+(v)$ satisfy $\sum\mathrm{deg}^+=\sum\mathrm{deg}^-=|A|$. An underlying undirected graph forgets orientation. Tournaments orient each pair of $K_n$ exactly once.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A digraph with arcs $a\to b$, $a\to c$, $b\to c$, $c\to a$. In- and out-degrees of $a,b,c$.

**Solution.** $a$: out $2$, in $1$; $b$: out $1$, in $1$; $c$: out $1$, in $2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** How many tournaments on $4$ labeled vertices?

**Solution.** $2^{\binom{4}{2}}=2^6=64$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\sum\deg=2|E|$ on a directed graph without converting each arc to one out and one in (the directed handshaking is $\sum\mathrm{deg}^+=|A|$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A directed cycle $\vec C_5$. Each in-degree?

**Solution.** $1$, and each out-degree $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can every out-degree be $2$ on a simple digraph with $3$ vertices?

**Solution.** No: $\sum\mathrm{deg}^+=6$ would need $6$ arcs, but at most $6$ ordered pairs exist; possible if the complete bidirected $K_3$ (two arcs per pair) — not simple oriented. In an orientation of a simple graph, max out-degree sum is $\binom{3}{2}=3$.
