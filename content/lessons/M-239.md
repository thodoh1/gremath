# Lesson M-239 — Increasing/decreasing intervals

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Applications of derivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-E.4`.


This lesson isolates **Increasing/decreasing intervals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Increasing/decreasing intervals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

$f$ is increasing on an interval $I$ if $x_1<x_2$ implies $f(x_1)\le f(x_2)$ (strictly if $<$). If $f'>0$ on $I$, $f$ is strictly increasing (MVT). If $f'\ge 0$, $f$ is increasing, but $f'$ can vanish at isolated points ($x^3$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Where is $f(x)=x^3-3x$ increasing?

**Solution.** $f'=3(x^2-1)>0$ for $|x|>1$, so on $(-\infty,-1]$ and $[1,\infty)$ in the non-strict sense of joining the critical points; strictly increasing on each of $(-\infty,-1]$ and $[1,\infty)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^{-x}$ is

**Solution.** strictly decreasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding increasing from $f'(a)>0$ at a single point.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Where is $x^2$ decreasing?

**Solution.** On $(-\infty,0]$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can $f'=0$ everywhere on a subinterval while $f$ is increasing?

**Solution.** Then $f$ is constant there, so not strictly increasing.
