# Lesson M-998 — Handle degenerate cases

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Parameter problems** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.4-E.5-K.7-G.2`.


This lesson isolates **Handle degenerate cases** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Handle degenerate cases**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Parameter problems*

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter. Degenerate cases (zero leading coefficient, repeated roots) are often the point.

### This skill, specifically

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Degenerate cases: a ‘quadratic’ $at^2+bt+c=0$ becomes linear if $a=0$, and $0=c$ or $0=b$ if more coefficients vanish. A family of lines $x+ty=1$ is not parallel when $t$ varies through a direction that repeats. Division by $t-1$ is illegal at $t=1$; that slice must be substituted back into the original equation. Rank, degree, and leading-coefficient vanishing are the usual degeneracies.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $(t-1)x=t^2-1$ for $x$ in terms of $t$.

**Solution.** If $t\neq 1$, $x=t+1$. If $t=1$, the equation is $0=0$, every $x$ works.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $ax^2+x=0$ with $a=0$: solutions

**Solution.** $x=0$ only (the linear leftover).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Cancelling $t-1$ and losing the all-$x$ solution, or reporting ‘no solution’ at $t=1$ when it was $0=0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** System $x+y=1$, $tx+ty=t$. Solution set?

**Solution.** Always the line $x+y=1$: the second row is $t$ times the first, and if $t=0$ it is $0=0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ degenerates as $b\to 0$ to

**Solution.** the segment $[-a,a]$ on the $x$-axis (the curve collapses to that segment).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
