# Lesson M-487 — Minimal polynomial awareness

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Eigenvalues & eigenvectors** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.6`.


This lesson isolates **Minimal polynomial awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Minimal polynomial awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Eigenvalues & eigenvectors*

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. Characteristic polynomial, algebraic vs geometric multiplicity, diagonalization iff enough independent eigenvectors. Trace is sum of eigenvalues; det is product. Cayley–Hamilton.

### This skill, specifically

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The minimal polynomial $m_A$ is the monic polynomial of least degree with $m_A(A)=0$. It divides every polynomial that annihilates $A$, including $p_A$. Its roots are exactly the eigenvalues, and the multiplicity of $(x-\lambda)$ in $m_A$ is the size of the largest $\lambda$-Jordan block. $A$ is diagonalizable iff $m_A$ is a product of distinct linear factors.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $m_A$ for $\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\0&4\end{pmatrix}$ and for $\mathrm{diag}(4,4)$.

**Solution.** First: $(x-4)^2$. Second: $x-4$. Both have characteristic polynomial $(x-4)^2$ up to sign, but the minimal polynomials differ.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $A$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $1$ and $4$, then $m_A$ is

**Solution.** $(x-1)(x-4)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming $m_A=p_A$ always (false whenever some Jordan block is smaller than $\alpha(\lambda)$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Degree of $m_A$ versus $n$.

**Solution.** $\deg m_A\le n$, with equality possible (a single Jordan block).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $m_A$ splits into distinct linear factors, $A$ is

**Solution.** diagonalizable.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
