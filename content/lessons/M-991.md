# Lesson M-991 — Distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Theorem/definition recognition** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Q`.


This lesson isolates **Distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

‘$P\Rightarrow Q$’ makes $P$ sufficient for $Q$ and $Q$ necessary for $P$. Differentiability is sufficient for continuity, not necessary. $\det A=0$ is necessary and sufficient for a square matrix to be singular. A GRE ‘which is sufficient?’ item is often solved by testing one-way implications on tiny matrices or functions, not by computing the given $5\times 5$ example.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is ‘$f'=0$ everywhere’ necessary for $f$ constant on a connected open set in $\mathbb{R}$? Sufficient?

**Solution.** Sufficient (MVT). Necessary too, if $f$ is assumed differentiable. If differentiability is not assumed, ‘$f'=0$’ is not even meaningful, so it is not necessary in the class of all functions.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** ‘$A$ diagonal’ is sufficient for ‘$A$ symmetric’ over $\mathbb{R}$? Necessary?

**Solution.** Sufficient, not necessary (a nontrivial symmetric rotation-scaling in $2$D need not be diagonal in the given basis).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating a convenient sufficient condition as necessary: e.g. claiming a series diverges because the terms are not $O(1/n^2)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** For convergence of $\sum a_n$ with $a_n\ge 0$, is $a_n\to 0$ sufficient? Necessary?

**Solution.** Necessary, not sufficient ($\sum 1/n$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Square $A$: $A^\top A=I$ is sufficient for invertibility? Necessary?

**Solution.** Sufficient (orthogonal), not necessary ($2I$ is invertible, not orthogonal).
