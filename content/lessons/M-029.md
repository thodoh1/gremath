# Lesson M-029 — Partial fractions

Part A. A. Foundations & Algebraic Fluency · **Algebraic manipulation** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Partial fractions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Algebraic fluency is not a listed ETS bucket of its own, but it is the cost of every calculus and algebra item. Expect it to be invisible when you have it and fatal when you do not.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Partial fractions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Algebraic manipulation*

Algebraic manipulation is legal rewriting: factoring, identities, division algorithms, partial fractions, substitution. Illegal rewriting (canceling sums, dividing by zero, dropping absolute values) is how GRE items are authored. Every identity you learn here is a compression algorithm for a later calculus or linear-algebra computation.

### This skill, specifically

Algebraic manipulation is legal rewriting: factoring, identities, division algorithms, partial fractions, substitution.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Partial fractions: a proper rational function decomposes into a sum of terms whose denominators are the irreducible factors of $q$. Linear factors give $A/(x-r)$; repeated $(x-r)^k$ give terms up to $A_k/(x-r)^k$; irreducible quadratics give $(Bx+C)/(x^2+\cdots)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Decompose $\dfrac{1}{x(x-1)}$.

**Solution.** $\dfrac{A}{x}+\dfrac{B}{x-1}$, $A=-1$, $B=1$, so $\dfrac{1}{x-1}-\dfrac{1}{x}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1}{x^2-1}\,dx$ begins with

**Solution.** $\dfrac{1/2}{x-1}-\dfrac{1/2}{x+1}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using an improper decomposition without polynomial division first.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Decompose $\dfrac{x+3}{(x+1)^2}$.

**Solution.** $\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{1}{x+1}+\dfrac{2}{(x+1)^2}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why is $\dfrac{x^2}{x-1}$ not ready for partial fractions?

**Solution.** Degree numerator $\ge$ degree denominator; divide first.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
