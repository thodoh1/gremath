# Lesson M-325 — Weierstrass M-test awareness

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Uniform convergence basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.3`.


This lesson isolates **Weierstrass M-test awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Weierstrass M-test awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Uniform convergence basics*

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not. Weierstrass M-test is a comparison test for functions. This is GRE awareness: enough to not interchange limit and integral blindly.

### This skill, specifically

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Weierstrass $M$-test: if $|u_n(x)|\le M_n$ on a set $S$ for all $n$, with $\sum M_n<\infty$ (numerical), then $\sum u_n$ converges absolutely and uniformly on $S$. Power series converge uniformly on compact subintervals of the open disk of convergence.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $\sum \sin(nx)/n^2$ converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes: $|\sin(nx)/n^2|\le 1/n^2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sum x^n$ on $[-r,r]$ for $r<1$

**Solution.** Uniform by $M_n=r^n$. Not uniform on $(-1,1)$ (term $x^n$ not uniformly small near $1$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $M_n=|u_n(x)|$ depending on $x$ as if it were a numerical bound.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\sum \frac{\cos(nx)}{n!}$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** Uniform, $M_n=1/n!$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $M$-test gives absolute uniform convergence: true?

**Solution.** Yes, absolute at each $x$ and uniform.
