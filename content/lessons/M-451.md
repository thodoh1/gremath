# Lesson M-451 — Basis

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Basis** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Basis**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A basis of $V$ is a linearly independent spanning set. Every vector then has unique coordinates relative to the basis. Any two bases have the same number of elements. The standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $\{\mathbf{e}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{e}_n\}$; of $P_2$ is $\{1,x,x^2\}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}$ is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Solution.** Independent: matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ has det $-2\neq 0$, hence also spanning.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A basis of the plane $x+y+z=0$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is

**Solution.** e.g. $\{(1,-1,0),(1,0,-1)\}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling a spanning set a basis without checking independence (or an independent set without spanning).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\{1,x,x^2,x^2+1\}$ a basis of $P_2$?

**Solution.** No: four vectors in a $3$-dimensional space, dependent.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Removing one vector from a basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ yields

**Solution.** an independent set that does not span.
