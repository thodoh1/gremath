# Lesson M-763 — Counting with repetition

Part R. R. Combinatorics · **Counting principles** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.4`.


This lesson isolates **Counting with repetition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Combinatorics is additional topics and also probability setup.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Counting with repetition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Counting principles*

Add disjoint options, multiply independent successive choices, divide by overcount. Factorials, $P(n,k)$, $C(n,k)$, multinomial, repetition, circular permutations, complement counting.

### This skill, specifically

Add disjoint options, multiply independent successive choices, divide by overcount.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

With repetition allowed and order mattering, there are $n^k$ words of length $k$ on an $n$-letter alphabet (functions $[k]\to[n]$). Without repetition: $P(n,k)$. Unordered with repetition (multisets) is stars-and-bars, not $n^k$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** How many $4$-letter strings from $\{A,B,C,D,E,F,G\}$, letters reusable?

**Solution.** $7^4=2401$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $3$-digit numbers with digits from $0$--$9$, repetition allowed, leading zeros allowed as strings: count.

**Solution.** $10^3=1000$. As integers $000$ through $999$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$ when the problem is ordered (PIN codes, sequences).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Binary strings of length $8$.

**Solution.** $2^8=256$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Passwords of length $5$ from $12$ symbols, no repeat vs repeat.

**Solution.** $P(12,5)=12\cdot 11\cdot 10\cdot 9\cdot 8=95040$ vs $12^5=248832$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
