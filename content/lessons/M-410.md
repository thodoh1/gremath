# Lesson M-410 — Equilibrium stability

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Systems of ODEs** · High

Prerequisites: `K.7-J.2`.


This lesson isolates **Equilibrium stability** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Equilibrium stability**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems of ODEs*

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes. Phase portraits are the picture. Nonlinear systems linearize at equilibria.

### This skill, specifically

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

For $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, the origin is asymptotically stable iff every eigenvalue satisfies $\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)<0$. If some $\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>0$, it is unstable. Pure imaginary eigenvalues (simple) give a Lyapunov-stable center, not attracting. Nonlinear: the linearization must have no zero/imaginary eigenvalues for the Hartman–Grobman conclusion.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Stability of $\mathbf{0}$ for $A=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-2)$ and for $A=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)$.

**Solution.** Sink (attracting node); saddle (unstable).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
