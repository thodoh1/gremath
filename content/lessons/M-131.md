# Lesson M-131 — Power-reduction formulas

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Trigonometric identities** · Critical

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Power-reduction formulas** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Power-reduction formulas**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Trigonometric identities*

Sum, double-angle, half-angle, product-to-sum, and power-reduction identities are not a list to memorize in isolation. They are the algebra of $e^{i\theta}$ written in real form. On the GRE they turn a horror integral or a limit into a one-liner. Convert until you see a derivative or a standard form.

### This skill, specifically

Sum, double-angle, half-angle, product-to-sum, and power-reduction identities are not a list to memorize in isolation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Power-reduction is the half-angle identities read as reducing powers: $\sin^2=\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$, $\cos^2=\dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{2}$, $\sin^3$ via $\sin\cdot\sin^2$, etc.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Rewrite $\sin^2 x\cos^2 x$.

**Solution.** $(\sin 2x/2)^2=(1-\cos 4x)/8$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int\sin^2 x\,dx$ begins with

**Solution.** $\int(1-\cos 2x)/2\,dx$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reducing $\sin^2$ to $\sin 2x$ without the identity (that's $\sin 2x=2\sin\cos$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\cos^4 x$ in multiple angles.

**Solution.** $((1+\cos 2x)/2)^2=(1+2\cos 2x+\cos^2 2x)/4$ then reduce again.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Average value of $\sin^2$ over a period.

**Solution.** $1/2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
