# Lesson M-979 — Lagrange interpolation

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Interpolation & approximation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `K.8-U.4`.


This lesson isolates **Lagrange interpolation** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Lagrange interpolation**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Interpolation & approximation*

### This skill, specifically

Unique polynomial of degree $<n$ through $n$ points, Lagrange form, interpolation error $\propto f^{(n)}$, finite differences, least squares as overdetermined fit.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Lagrange form: $p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n y_i \ell_i(x)$ with $\ell_i(x)=\prod_{j\neq i}\dfrac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j}$. Each $\ell_i(x_k)=\delta_{ik}$. Useful for theory and for a few nodes; adding a node means rebuilding all $\ell_i$. Barycentric weights speed evaluation. Newton divided-difference form is better for adding nodes incrementally.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Lagrange interpolant of $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$.

**Solution.** $p(x)=1\cdot\dfrac{x-1}{0-1}+0\cdot\dfrac{x-0}{1-0}=1-x$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\ell_0(x)$ for nodes $0,1,2$ is

**Solution.** $\dfrac{(x-1)(x-2)}{(0-1)(0-2)}=\dfrac{(x-1)(x-2)}{2}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\ell_i$ with a product over all $j$ including $i$ (zero denominator), or evaluating $\sum y_i$ without the cardinal polynomials.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $p(x_i)$ must equal

**Solution.** $y_i$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Three nodes, $y=(1,1,1)$: Lagrange $p$ is

**Solution.** the constant $1$.
