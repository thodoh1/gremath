# Lesson M-721 — Existence proofs

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Existence proofs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Existence proofs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Existence proofs: constructive (exhibit a witness) or nonconstructive (show that nonexistence is impossible, often via IVT, pigeonhole, or contradiction). Classical logic allows the latter. $\exists x,\, x^2=2$ in $\mathbb{R}$ follows from IVT or completeness, without writing digits of $\sqrt{2}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show there exist irrationals $a,b$ with $a^b$ rational, possibly nonconstructively.

**Solution.** Let $\alpha=\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$. If $\alpha$ is rational, done ($a=b=\sqrt{2}$). If $\alpha$ is irrational, then $\alpha^{\sqrt{2}}=2$ with both exponents/bases irrational. (Gelfond--Schneider actually makes $\alpha$ transcendental.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** IVT existence of a root of $x^3-x-1$ on $[1,2]$ is

**Solution.** nonconstructive (unless one also produces a decimal).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming existence after showing a set is nonempty in a way that uses AC without comment, when an explicit witness is asked.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Constructive witness that $\exists n,\, n^2=n$.

**Solution.** $n=0$ or $n=1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Pigeonhole: among $13$ people, two share a

**Solution.** birth month (existence without naming them).

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
