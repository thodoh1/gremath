# Lesson M-411 — Linearization of nonlinear systems

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Systems of ODEs** · High

Prerequisites: `K.7-J.2`.


This lesson isolates **Linearization of nonlinear systems** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Linearization of nonlinear systems**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems of ODEs*

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes. Phase portraits are the picture. Nonlinear systems linearize at equilibria.

### This skill, specifically

Write $\mathbf{x}'=A\mathbf{x}$, then eigenvalues of $A$ produce the modes.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

To linearize $\mathbf{x}'=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})$ at an equilibrium $\mathbf{x}_*$, compute the Jacobian $DF(\mathbf{x}_*)$. The linearized system is $\mathbf{u}'=DF(\mathbf{x}_*)\mathbf{u}$ with $\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_*$. Hyperbolic equilibria ($\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\neq 0$) have the same local phase portrait as the linearization.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\mathbf{x}'=y$, $y'=x-x^3$. Jacobian at $(0,0)$ and type.

**Solution.** $DF=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1-3x^2&0\end{pmatrix}$, at $0$: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $\pm 1$, saddle.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For $x'=x(1-x)$, linearization at $x=1$ is

**Solution.** $u'=-u$ (attracting).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Linearizing at a point that is not an equilibrium, or using the Hessian instead of $DF$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Predator–prey $x'=x(1-y)$, $y'=y(x-1)$ at $(1,1)$: $DF$?

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, center in the linearization (nonlinear needs more care).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Hartman–Grobman requires the equilibrium to be

**Solution.** hyperbolic.
