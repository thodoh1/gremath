# Lesson M-161 — Rotated conics — recognition

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Conic sections** · High

This lesson isolates **Rotated conics — recognition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Rotated conics — recognition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Conic sections*

Circle, parabola, ellipse, hyperbola: each is a quadratic equation and a focus-directrix story. Standard forms, vertices, foci, eccentricity, and asymptotes (hyperbola) should be recognizable after a translation. Rotated conics on the GRE are mostly “there is an $xy$ term.”

## 3. Precise statement

A rotated conic has an $xy$ term. The quadratic form $ax^2+bxy+cy^2$ is classified by $b^2-4ac$: $<0$ ellipse (or circle/point/empty), $=0$ parabola (or degenerate), $>0$ hyperbola (or degenerate). Diagonalize by rotating axes.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Ignoring degeneracy (the invariant only classifies the type, not existence).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
