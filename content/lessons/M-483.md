# Lesson M-483 — Similarity

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Eigenvalues & eigenvectors** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.6`.


This lesson isolates **Similarity** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Similarity**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Eigenvalues & eigenvectors*

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. Characteristic polynomial, algebraic vs geometric multiplicity, diagonalization iff enough independent eigenvectors. Trace is sum of eigenvalues; det is product. Cayley–Hamilton.

### This skill, specifically

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Similarity $B=P^{-1}AP$ is the change-of-basis relation for a linear operator. Eigenvalues, algebraic multiplicities, trace, determinant, rank, characteristic and minimal polynomials are similarity invariants. Geometric multiplicities are too. Diagonalization is similarity to a diagonal matrix.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Trace and det of any matrix similar to $\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{tr}=0$, $\det=1$. (Rotation-scaling: eigenvalues $\pm i$.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $A$ is similar to $D=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,2)$, then $\mathrm{rank}\,A$ is

**Solution.** $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming similar matrices have the same eigenvectors (they correspond via $P$, but the coordinate tuples differ).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is similarity an equivalence relation?

**Solution.** Yes: reflexive, symmetric, transitive.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $A$ similar to $I$ means $A$ is

**Solution.** $I$ itself ($P^{-1}IP=I$).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
