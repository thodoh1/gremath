# Lesson M-537 — p-adic valuation basics

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Additional elementary number theory** · Medium

Prerequisites: `L.2-L.3`.


This lesson isolates **p-adic valuation basics** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **p-adic valuation basics**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The $p$-adic valuation $v_p(n)$ for prime $p$ is the largest $e$ with $p^e\mid n$ (and $v_p(0)=\infty$). Then $v_p(ab)=v_p(a)+v_p(b)$ and $v_p(a+b)\ge\min(v_p(a),v_p(b))$, with equality if the mins differ. de Polignac / Legendre: $v_p(n!)=\sum_{k\ge 1}\lfloor n/p^k\rfloor$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $v_2(48)$ and $v_5(10!)$.

**Solution.** $48=16\cdot 3=2^4\cdot 3$, so $v_2=4$. $v_5(10!)=\lfloor 10/5\rfloor=2$ (the factors $5$ and $10$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $v_p(a+b)=v_p(a)+v_p(b)$, or stopping Legendre at $\lfloor n/p\rfloor$ when $n\ge p^2$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $v_2(64)$.

**Solution.** $6$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Trailing zeros of $25!$ in base $10$.

**Solution.** $\min(v_2,v_5)=v_5(25!)=\lfloor 25/5\rfloor+\lfloor 25/25\rfloor=5+1=6$.
