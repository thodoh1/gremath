# Lesson M-725 — Well-ordering principle

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Well-ordering principle** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Well-ordering principle**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

## 3. Precise statement

Well-ordering principle: every nonempty subset of $\mathbb{N}$ has a least element. Equivalent to induction. Infinite descent is the contrapositive: there is no infinite strictly decreasing sequence of naturals. Used in the division algorithm and unique factorization.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove the division algorithm's existence via well-ordering.

**Solution.** The set of nonnegative remainders $\{a-bq:q\in\mathbb{Z},\, a-bq\ge 0\}$ is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$, hence has a least $r$, and $r<b$ else subtract one more $b$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Infinite descent proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational recasts Euclid as

**Solution.** an infinite decreasing sequence of positive integer denominators.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying well-ordering to $\mathbb{Z}$ or $(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}$ (no least element).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $\mathbb{Z}$ satisfy well-ordering under the usual $\le$?

**Solution.** No: the negatives have no least element; $\mathbb{Z}_{<0}$ is nonempty with no minimum.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Least element of $\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>5\}$.

**Solution.** $6$ (if $\mathbb{N}$ starts at $1$).
