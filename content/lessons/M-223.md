# Lesson M-223 — Logarithmic differentiation

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Derivative definition & rules** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1-E.2`.


This lesson isolates **Logarithmic differentiation** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Logarithmic differentiation**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Derivative definition & rules*

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists. Geometrically it is slope of the tangent; physically it is instantaneous rate. Power, product, quotient, chain, implicit, logarithmic, inverse, and higher derivatives are the machine that computes $f'$ without returning to the limit every time.

### This skill, specifically

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Logarithmic differentiation: for $y=f(x)^{g(x)}$ or messy products, set $\ln|y|=g\ln|f|$ (on intervals where $y\neq 0$), differentiate, then multiply by $y$. Also useful for $x^x$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Differentiate $y=x^x$ for $x>0$.

**Solution.** $\ln y=x\ln x$, $y'/y=\ln x+1$, $y'=x^x(\ln x+1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $y=(x^2+1)^{\sin x}$, $\ln y=$

**Solution.** $\sin x\cdot\ln(x^2+1)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $(x^x)'=x\cdot x^{x-1}$ (power rule with variable base and exponent).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $y=(\sin x)^x$ on $(0,\pi)$, $y'$.

**Solution.** $(\sin x)^x\bigl(\ln\sin x+x\cot x\bigr)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Product $y=x(x+1)(x+2)$ via logs.

**Solution.** $y'=y\bigl(1/x+1/(x+1)+1/(x+2)\bigr)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
