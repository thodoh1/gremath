# Lesson M-1017 — Geometric interpretation shortcuts

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Speed & multiple-choice technique** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Geometric interpretation shortcuts** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Geometric interpretation shortcuts**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Geometric shortcuts: a linear constraint is a plane/line; a quadratic with equal eigenvalues is a sphere/circle; $\mathrm{Re}(e^{-i\theta}z)=c$ is a line; $|z-a|/|z-b|=k$ is an Apollonius circle; the shortest path that reflects is equal-angle (unfold the plane). Area of a parallelogram is $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|$. Many ‘compute the min of $x^2+y^2$ on $ax+by=c$’ items are distance-to-line.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Min of $x^2+y^2$ on $3x+4y=10$.

**Solution.** Distance from origin to the line is $10/5=2$, so min of $x^2+y^2$ is $4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Area of the parallelogram on $(1,0,0)$ and $(1,1,0)$.

**Solution.** $1$ (cross product $(0,0,1)$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Expanding Lagrange multipliers for a $2$D distance that the point-line formula already is.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** The internal division point of the segment from $1$ to $i$ in the ratio $1:2$ lies on $|z-i|=2|z-1|$. That point is

**Solution.** $(2+i)/3$. Check: $|z-1|=\sqrt{2}/3$, $|z-i|=2\sqrt{2}/3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Unfolding: shortest path from $(0,1)$ to the $x$-axis then to $(2,1)$ has length

**Solution.** the straight segment from $(0,1)$ to the reflection $(2,-1)$, length $2\sqrt{2}$.
