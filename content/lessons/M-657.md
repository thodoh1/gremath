# Lesson M-657 — Extreme Value Theorem proof idea

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Continuity** · High

Prerequisites: `E.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Extreme Value Theorem proof idea** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Extreme Value Theorem proof idea**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Continuity*

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$. Types of discontinuity (removable, jump, infinite) are classified by one-sided limits. IVT and EVT are the two big existence theorems for continuous functions on closed intervals.

### This skill, specifically

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Extreme Value Theorem: a continuous real function on a compact interval $[a,b]$ attains its maximum and minimum. Proof idea: the image is bounded (else $1/(f(x_n)-n)$ tricks, or sequential compactness) and closed (sequential continuity), hence contains its supremum. Heine--Borel: compactness of $[a,b]$ is the engine.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $f(x)=x$ attain a maximum on $(0,1)$? On $[0,1]$?

**Solution.** No on $(0,1)$: $\sup=1$ not attained. Yes on $[0,1]$: max $1$ at $x=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A continuous function on a closed bounded interval is

**Solution.** bounded and attains its bounds.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying EVT on $(0,1]$ to $1/x$, which is continuous there but the domain is not compact.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Proof sketch using sequences: take $x_n$ with $f(x_n)\to\sup f$. Then

**Solution.** BW gives $x_{n_k}\to c\in[a,b]$, and continuity gives $f(c)=\sup f$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $e^{-x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ attain a minimum?

**Solution.** No: infimum $0$ not attained; domain not compact.
