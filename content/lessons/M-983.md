# Lesson M-983 — Trapezoidal rule

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Numerical integration & ODEs** · Medium

Prerequisites: `F.2-J.1`.


This lesson isolates **Trapezoidal rule** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trapezoidal rule**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Composite trapezoidal rule on $[a,b]$ with $n$ equal panels, $h=(b-a)/n$, $x_i=a+ih$: $T=\dfrac{h}{2}\bigl(y_0+2y_1+\cdots+2y_{n-1}+y_n\bigr)$. Exact for degree $\le 1$. Euler–Maclaurin: $T=\int_a^b f-\dfrac{(b-a)h^2}{12}f''(\xi)$ for some $\xi$ (if $f\in C^2$). Endpoints are weighted $1$, interior nodes $2$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Trapezoid for $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx$ with $n=1$.

**Solution.** $h=1$, $T=\frac12(0+1)=1/2$, while the integral is $1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Weights on $y_0,y_1,y_2$ for $n=2$.

**Solution.** $h/2$, $h$, $h/2$ with $h=(b-a)/2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Weighting every node by $h$ (that is a rectangle/midpoint cousin) including a double-counted last point.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
