## 3. Precise statement

For $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$ with $a\neq 0$, $a$ divides $b$ (write $a\mid b$) iff $b=aq$ for some $q\in\mathbb{Z}$. Then $b$ is a multiple of $a$. Divisibility is reflexive and transitive; $a\mid b$ and $b\mid a$ iff $a=\pm b$. Every integer divides $0$; only $\pm 1$ divide $1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $12\mid 84$? Does $12\mid 90$?

**Solution.** $84=12\cdot 7$, so yes. $90=12\cdot 7+6$, so no.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

**Solution.** $bx+cy$ for every $x,y\in\mathbb{Z}$ (linear combinations).

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding $a\nmid b$ from $a>b>0$ while forgetting $b=0$ is a multiple of every $a$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** List the positive divisors of $18$.

**Solution.** $1,2,3,6,9,18$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $8\mid 0$? Does $0\mid 8$?

**Solution.** Yes; no ($0$ divides only $0$).
