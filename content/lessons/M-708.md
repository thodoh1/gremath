# Lesson M-708 — Converse vs contrapositive vs inverse

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Propositional logic** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Converse vs contrapositive vs inverse** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Converse vs contrapositive vs inverse**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Propositional logic*

Propositions, $\land,\lor,\neg,\to,\leftrightarrow$, truth tables, tautology, contradiction, converse vs contrapositive, De Morgan. Implication is not the arrow in your heart; $P\to Q$ is false only when $P$ is true and $Q$ false.

### This skill, specifically

Propositions, $\land,\lor,\neg,\to,\leftrightarrow$, truth tables, tautology, contradiction, converse vs contrapositive, De Morgan.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

For an implication $P\to Q$: the converse is $Q\to P$, the contrapositive is $\neg Q\to\neg P$, and the inverse is $\neg P\to\neg Q$. The contrapositive is equivalent to the original; the converse is equivalent to the inverse; neither pair matches the other in general.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For ``if $n$ is divisible by $4$, then $n$ is even'', write converse, contrapositive, and inverse.

**Solution.** Converse: even $\Rightarrow$ divisible by $4$ (false). Contrapositive: odd $\Rightarrow$ not divisible by $4$ (true). Inverse: not divisible by $4$ $\Rightarrow$ odd (false).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Which of converse/contrapositive/inverse is equivalent to $P\to Q$?

**Solution.** Only the contrapositive.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Proving the converse and claiming to have proved the implication.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Contrapositive of ``if $f$ is differentiable, then $f$ is continuous''.

**Solution.** If $f$ is not continuous, then $f$ is not differentiable.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Inverse of $P\to Q$ is equivalent to

**Solution.** the converse $Q\to P$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
