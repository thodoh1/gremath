# Lesson M-790 — Cycles

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cycles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cycles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A cycle is a closed path of length at least $3$ (simple graph): distinct vertices except start $=$ end. The cycle graph $C_n$ is $2$-regular and connected. Girth is the length of a shortest cycle. Forests are graphs with no cycles.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $K_{2,4}$ contain a $3$-cycle? A $4$-cycle?

**Solution.** No odd cycle (bipartite). Yes $C_4$: any two on the size-$4$ side with the two on the other side.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** How many edges in $C_{11}$?

**Solution.** $11$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling a closed walk a cycle when it repeats vertices in the interior (e.g. a figure-eight).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Smallest $n$ for which $C_n$ is not bipartite.

**Solution.** $n=3$ (any odd $n$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A simple graph with $6$ vertices and $7$ edges. Must it have a cycle?

**Solution.** Yes: a tree would have $5$ edges; extra edges create cycles.
