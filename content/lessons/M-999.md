## 3. Precise statement

Switch algebra $\leftrightarrow$ geometry: $|z-a|=r$ is a circle; $\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}\|=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|$ is the perpendicular bisector; $x^2+y^2=2x$ completes to a circle; a linear inequality is a half-plane. Completing the square, taking moduli, and drawing the set often beats expanding.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Describe $\{z:|z-1|=|z+i|\}$.

**Solution.** Perpendicular bisector of the points $1$ and $-i$. Segment direction $(1,1)$, midpoint $(1/2,-1/2)$, so $(x-1/2)+(y+1/2)=0$, i.e. the line $x+y=0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $x^2+y^2-4y=0$ is the circle

**Solution.** center $(0,2)$, radius $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Expanding $|z|^2=z\overline{z}$ into eight terms when a geometric picture already answers ‘which points’.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
