# Lesson M-145 — Law of cosines

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Triangle trigonometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Law of cosines** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Law of cosines**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Law of cosines: $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$. Use SAS or SSS. Obtuse $C$ makes $\cos C<0$, so $c^2>a^2+b^2$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Sides $3,4$ included angle $60^\circ$. Opposite side?

**Solution.** $c^2=9+16-2\cdot 3\cdot 4\cdot 1/2=13$, $c=\sqrt{13}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using cosine law with the wrong angle (not included / not opposite the unknown side).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Find $\cos C$ if $a=5,b=6,c=7$.

**Solution.** $(a^2+b^2-c^2)/(2ab)=(25+36-49)/60=12/60=1/5$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
