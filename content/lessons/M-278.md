This lesson isolates **Volumes by shells** and trains it until it is automatic.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Volumes by shells**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Shell method: revolving a strip parallel to the axis, $V=\int 2\pi(\mathrm{radius})(\mathrm{height})(\mathrm{thickness})$. About the $y$-axis: $V=\int_a^b 2\pi x f(x)\,dx$ for a region under $f\ge 0$. Often cheaper than washers when $x=g(y)$ is messy.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Revolve $y=x^2$ on $[0,1]$ about the $y$-axis (region under the curve).

**Solution.** Shells: $2\pi\int_0^1 x\cdot x^2\,dx=2\pi/4=\pi/2$. (Washer in $y$: $\pi\int_0^1(1-y)\,dy=\pi/2$.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
