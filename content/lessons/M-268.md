# Lesson M-268 — Definition of definite integral

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Definition of definite integral** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Definition of definite integral**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f$ is bounded on $[a,b]$ and continuous except at finitely many jumps, it is Riemann integrable. $\int_a^b f$ is the unique number that all Riemann sums approach as mesh$\to 0$. Convention: $\int_a^a=0$, $\int_a^b=-\int_b^a$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is the sign function integrable on $[-1,1]$?

**Solution.** Yes (one jump). Integral $0$ by oddness, or areas cancel.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Dirichlet function $1_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on $[0,1]$

**Solution.** Not Riemann integrable (upper sums $1$, lower $0$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking a single discontinuity forbids integration ($1_{x>0}$ is integrable).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_2^2 \sin x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\int_3^1 2\,dx$.

**Solution.** $-4$.
