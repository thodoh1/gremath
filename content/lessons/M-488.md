# Lesson M-488 — General inner products

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Inner-product spaces** · High

This lesson isolates **General inner products** and trains it until it is automatic.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **General inner products**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Inner-product spaces*

Inner products give geometry: orthogonality, norms, projections, Gram–Schmidt, least squares. Symmetric real matrices are orthogonally diagonalizable (spectral theorem). SVD is the rectangular cousin.

### This skill, specifically

## 3. Precise statement

An inner product on a real vector space is a bilinear, symmetric, positive-definite form $\langle u,v\rangle$. On a complex space it is conjugate-linear in one slot (usually the first) and $\langle v,v\rangle>0$ for $v\neq 0$. It induces a norm $\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}$. Examples: $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_a^b f g\,dx$ on $C[a,b]$, $\mathbf{u}^T A\mathbf{v}$ for SPD $A$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** With $\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle=\mathbf{u}^T\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}$, compute $\langle(1,1),(1,1)\rangle$.

**Solution.** $2+1=3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using a bilinear form that is not positive definite (e.g. $\mathbf{u}^T\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}$) and calling it an inner product.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
