# Lesson M-543 — Subgroup test

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Groups** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Subgroup test** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subgroup test**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Groups*

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses. Abelian means commutative. Subgroups, cyclic groups, orders of elements and of the group: Lagrange is coming.

### This skill, specifically

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

One-step subgroup test: a nonempty $H\subseteq G$ is a subgroup iff $a,b\in H\Rightarrow ab^{-1}\in H$. Two-step: closed under the operation and under inverses. Finite case: a nonempty finite subset closed under the operation is a subgroup (inverses come from $a^{k}=e$ in the powers). The subgroup generated by a subset is the smallest subgroup containing it.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show that $H=\{A\in GL_n(\mathbb{R}):\det A=\pm 1\}$ is a subgroup by the one-step test.

**Solution.** Nonempty ($I$). If $\det A,\det B=\pm 1$, then $\det(AB^{-1})=(\det A)(\det B)^{-1}=\pm 1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Q}^\times,\cdot)$?

**Solution.** No: $2\cdot 3^{-1}=2/3\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the finite-closure test on an infinite set (positive reals under addition fail inverses).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Positive even integers under addition: subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** No: no identity $0$, no inverses.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Finite closed nonempty subset of $S_3$: $\{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}$. Subgroup?

**Solution.** Yes, $A_3$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
