# Lesson M-1018 — Know when exact calculation is unnecessary

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Speed & multiple-choice technique** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Know when exact calculation is unnecessary** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Know when exact calculation is unnecessary**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Exact calculation is unnecessary when the question only needs a comparison, a sign, existence, or an integer nearest the value. Odd integrand on a symmetric interval $\Rightarrow$ integral $0$. Dependent rows $\Rightarrow$ det $0$ without expansion. Leading-term comparison decides which of two polynomials is larger for large $x$. Stop when the asked bit is known.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\ 4&5&6\\ 7&8&9\end{pmatrix}$ the long way? No: $R_1+R_3=2R_2$, so det $=0$.

**Solution.** $0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int_{-1}^1 \dfrac{x}{1+x^4}\,dx=$

**Solution.** $0$ (odd integrand, principal value / ordinary integral exists by comparison).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Expanding all nine cofactors, or computing both $\int_0^{100} e^{-x}\,dx$ and $1$ to twelve decimals to decide which is larger (it is $1-e^{-100}<1$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Which is larger for $x>0$: $e^x$ or $1+x+x^2/2$?

**Solution.** $e^x$, because the Taylor remainder $e^\xi x^3/6>0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $f(x)=x^3-x$ on $[-1,1]$: $\int_{-1}^1 f=$

**Solution.** $0$ (odd).
