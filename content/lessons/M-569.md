# Lesson M-569 — Automorphism

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Homomorphisms & quotients** · High

Prerequisites: `M.3`.


This lesson isolates **Automorphism** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Automorphism**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Homomorphisms & quotients*

A homomorphism preserves the operation. Kernel is a normal subgroup. First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\phi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\phi$. Quotients exist precisely because of normality.

### This skill, specifically

A homomorphism preserves the operation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An automorphism is an isomorphism $G\to G$. $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ is a group under composition. Inner automorphisms $x\mapsto gxg^{-1}$ form $\mathrm{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)$. $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (send $1\mapsto\pm 1$). $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$. $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong S_3$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Describe $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})$ and its order.

**Solution.** Automorphisms send $[1]$ to a generator $[k]$ with $\gcd(k,5)=1$, so $k=1,2,3,4$. $\mathrm{Aut}\cong U(5)\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, order $4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})|$ equals

**Solution.** $\phi(8)=4$. Explicitly $k\mapsto$ odd $k$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking every automorphism is inner (false for abelian $G$ with $\mathrm{Aut}$ nontrivial: $\mathrm{Inn}$ is trivial).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Solution.** $\{id, n\mapsto -n\}\cong C_2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Inner automorphisms of an abelian group.

**Solution.** Only the identity.
