# Lesson M-724 — Strong induction

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Strong induction** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Strong induction**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Strong induction: assume $P(j)$ for all $n_0\le j\le k$ (or all $j<n$) and prove $P(k+1)$ (or $P(n)$). Equivalent to ordinary induction. Use it when the predecessor that matters is not $n-1$ (prime factors, Fibonacci, postage).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove every integer $n\ge 2$ has a prime factor, by strong induction.

**Solution.** If $n$ is prime, done. If $n=ab$ with $1<a,b<n$, the inductive hypothesis supplies a prime factor of $a$, hence of $n$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Postage: $4$ and $5$ cents make every $n\ge N$ for $N=$

**Solution.** $12$ (check a block of $4$ consecutive, then add $4$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using strong induction but only invoking $P(k)$, which is ordinary induction in disguise and may be insufficient.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
