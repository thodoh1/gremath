# Lesson M-048 — AM-GM inequality

Part A. A. Foundations & Algebraic Fluency · **Inequality techniques** · High

Prerequisites: `A.4`.


This lesson isolates **AM-GM inequality** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Algebraic fluency is not a listed ETS bucket of its own, but it is the cost of every calculus and algebra item. Expect it to be invisible when you have it and fatal when you do not.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **AM-GM inequality**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Inequality techniques*

Named inequalities (AM-GM, Cauchy–Schwarz, triangle) and crude bounds (squares, completing the square) replace impossible closed forms. Equality cases are part of the theorem: if you cannot say when equality holds, you do not own the inequality. The GRE uses these both as computation shortcuts and as “which is largest?” items.

### This skill, specifically

Named inequalities (AM-GM, Cauchy–Schwarz, triangle) and crude bounds (squares, completing the square) replace impossible closed forms.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

AM-GM: for $x_i\ge 0$, $\frac{x_1+\cdots+x_n}{n}\ge\sqrt[n]{x_1\cdots x_n}$, equality iff all $x_i$ equal (or a zero in a way that both sides vanish when $n\ge 1$ with zeros).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Minimize $x+1/x$ for $x>0$.

**Solution.** AM-GM: $x+1/x\ge 2\sqrt{x\cdot 1/x}=2$, equality at $x=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For $a,b>0$, $\frac{a}{b}+\frac{b}{a}$ is at least

**Solution.** $2$, equality $a=b$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying AM-GM to negative numbers.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Show $x^2+y^2\ge 2xy$.

**Solution.** $(x-y)^2\ge 0$, or AM-GM on $x^2,y^2$ if nonnegative — actually $x^2+y^2-2xy=(x-y)^2$ is better and works for all reals.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Equality case of AM-GM on $3$ positive numbers.

**Solution.** All three equal.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
