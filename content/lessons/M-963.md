# Lesson M-963 — Argument principle awareness

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Residue methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.3`.


This lesson isolates **Argument principle awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Argument principle awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Residue methods*

Simple-pole residue $p/q'$ or $\lim(z-z_0)f$, residue theorem $2\pi i\sum\mathrm{Res}$, argument principle, winding numbers, real integrals via semicircles as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Simple-pole residue $p/q'$ or $\lim(z-z_0)f$, residue theorem $2\pi i\sum\mathrm{Res}$, argument principle, winding numbers, real integrals via semicircles as awareness.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Argument principle: if $f$ is meromorphic inside and holomorphic and nonzero on a simple closed positively oriented $\gamma$, then $\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \dfrac{f'}{f}\,dz=N-P$, the number of zeros minus poles inside, counted with multiplicity. Equivalently $\Delta_\gamma \arg f(z)=2\pi(N-P)$. Rouché compares $N$ for $f$ and $f+g$ when $|g|<|f|$ on $\gamma$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Zeros minus poles of $f(z)=z^2$ inside the unit circle.

**Solution.** $N=2$, $P=0$, so $(1/(2\pi i))\int f'/f=2$. Indeed $f'/f=2/z$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f(z)=e^z$ on $|z|=1$: $N-P=$

**Solution.** $0$ (no zeros, no poles).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Counting zeros without multiplicity, or applying the principle when $f$ has a zero on $\gamma$ itself.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Rouché: on $|z|=1$, compare $z^5+3z+1$ to $3z$. $|z^5+1|\le 2<3=|3z|$. Zeros inside?

**Solution.** $1$ (those of $3z$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** For $f=1/\sin z$ on $|z|=1$ (zeros of $\sin z$ on the circle: none), $N-P=$

**Solution.** $-1$ (simple pole at $0$; $f$ has no zeros).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
