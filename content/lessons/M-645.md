# Lesson M-645 — Conditional convergence

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Series & convergence** · High

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Conditional convergence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Conditional convergence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Conditional convergence: $\sum a_n$ converges but $\sum|a_n|$ diverges. The alternating harmonic series $\sum (-1)^{n+1}/n$ is the prototype. Conditionally convergent series are sensitive to rearrangements (Riemann).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Classify $\sum (-1)^{n+1}/n$ as absolute, conditional, or divergent.

**Solution.** Conditional: Leibniz gives convergence; $\sum 1/n$ diverges.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can a series of nonnegative terms be conditionally convergent?

**Solution.** No: $|a_n|=a_n$, so absolute and ordinary coincide.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling alternating harmonic absolutely convergent because it converges.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Classify $\sum (-1)^n/\sqrt{n}$.

**Solution.** Conditional ($p=1/2\le 1$ for absolute; Leibniz applies).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Classify $\sum (-1)^n/n^2$.

**Solution.** Absolute.
