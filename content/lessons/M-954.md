# Lesson M-954 — Taylor series in the complex plane

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Taylor series in the complex plane** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Taylor series in the complex plane**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f$ is holomorphic in $|z-a|<R$, then $f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \dfrac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(z-a)^n$ in that disk. The radius is the distance from $a$ to the nearest singularity (or $\infty$). Termwise differentiation and integration are valid inside the disk. Uniqueness: coefficients are determined by $f$ near $a$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Taylor series of $1/(1-z)$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n$ for $|z|<1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Radius of Taylor of $1/(z-2)$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing a Taylor series in an annulus that includes a singularity, or using the real-variable remainder as if the complex radius could exceed the distance to a pole.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $e^z$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $\sum z^n/n!$, radius $\infty$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Taylor of $\sin z$ at $0$, through order $3$.

**Solution.** $z-z^3/6+O(z^5)$ (odd function: no even powers).
