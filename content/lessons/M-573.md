# Lesson M-573 — Second/third isomorphism ideas

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Homomorphisms & quotients** · High

Prerequisites: `M.3`.


This lesson isolates **Second/third isomorphism ideas** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Second/third isomorphism ideas**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Homomorphisms & quotients*

A homomorphism preserves the operation. Kernel is a normal subgroup. First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\phi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\phi$. Quotients exist precisely because of normality.

### This skill, specifically

A homomorphism preserves the operation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Second isomorphism theorem: if $H\le G$ and $N\trianglelefteq G$, then $HN\le G$, $H\cap N\trianglelefteq H$, and $HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)$. Third: if $N\le K\le G$ with $N,K\trianglelefteq G$, then $K/N\trianglelefteq G/N$ and $(G/N)/(K/N)\cong G/K$. Correspondence theorem: subgroups of $G/N$ biject with subgroups of $G$ containing $N$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Apply the second iso theorem to $H=4\mathbb{Z}$, $N=6\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $HN=2\mathbb{Z}$ (gcd $2$), $H\cap N=12\mathbb{Z}$. Then $2\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\cong 4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, both cyclic of order $3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Third iso: $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})/(4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})\cong$

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. Here $4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}=\langle 4\rangle=\{0,4,8\}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, order $3$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/12)/(\mathbb{Z}/3)\cong\mathbb{Z}/4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying second iso when $N$ is not normal in $G$ (need $N$ normal, or at least $H$ in the normalizer).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ correspond to subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $6\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $6\mathbb{Z}\le d\mathbb{Z}\le\mathbb{Z}$ iff $d\mid 6$: $d=1,2,3,6$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $HN/N$ has order $|H|/|H\cap N|$ when finite.

**Solution.** Yes, by the second iso theorem.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
