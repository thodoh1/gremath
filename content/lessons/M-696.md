# Lesson M-696 — Path-connected spaces

Part P. P. Topology · **Connectedness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **Path-connected spaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Path-connected spaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Connectedness*

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens. Path-connected implies connected. Continuous images preserve connectedness. Connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are intervals.

### This skill, specifically

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$X$ is path-connected if any two points can be joined by a continuous path $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$. Path-connected $\Rightarrow$ connected; the converse fails (topologist's sine curve). $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}$ is path-connected for $n\ge 2$, not for $n=1$. Convex sets are path-connected.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ path-connected? $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}$?

**Solution.** No: cannot join $-1$ to $1$ without hitting $0$. Yes: go around the origin on a circular arc or polygonal path.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Path-connected implies connected: true?

**Solution.** True. The converse is false.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.
