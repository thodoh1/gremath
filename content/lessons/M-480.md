# Lesson M-480 — Algebraic multiplicity

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Eigenvalues & eigenvectors** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.6`.


This lesson isolates **Algebraic multiplicity** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Algebraic multiplicity**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Eigenvalues & eigenvectors*

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. Characteristic polynomial, algebraic vs geometric multiplicity, diagonalization iff enough independent eigenvectors. Trace is sum of eigenvalues; det is product. Cayley–Hamilton.

### This skill, specifically

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The algebraic multiplicity of $\lambda$ is its multiplicity as a root of $p_A$. The sum of algebraic multiplicities (over $\mathbb{C}$) is $n$. We have $1\le\gamma(\lambda)\le\alpha(\lambda)$ where $\gamma$ is geometric multiplicity. $\alpha(\lambda)$ is the size of the $\lambda$-block in the characteristic polynomial.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\0&3\end{pmatrix}$. Algebraic multiplicity of $\lambda=3$.

**Solution.** $p_A(\lambda)=(3-\lambda)^2$, so $\alpha(3)=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For $\mathrm{diag}(3,3,5)$, $\alpha(3)$ is

**Solution.** $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing algebraic multiplicity with the number of distinct eigenvalues, or with geometric multiplicity.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
