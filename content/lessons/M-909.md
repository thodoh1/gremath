# Lesson M-909 — Pythagorean theorem

Part V. V. Geometry · **Euclidean geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Pythagorean theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Pythagorean theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Pythagorean theorem: in a right triangle with legs $a,b$ and hypotenuse $c$, $a^2+b^2=c^2$. Converse: if $a^2+b^2=c^2$, the angle opposite $c$ is right. Acute vs obtuse: $a^2+b^2>c^2$ (all angles acute if this holds for every side playing the role of $c$ as longest); $a^2+b^2<c^2$ means obtuse opposite $c$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Legs $9,12$. Hypotenuse?

**Solution.** $15$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sides $5,12,13$: the triangle is

**Solution.** right, right angle opposite $13$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $a+b=c$, or applying Pythagoras in a non-right triangle without dropping an altitude.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $6,8,11$ acute, right, or obtuse?

**Solution.** $36+64=100<121$, obtuse opposite $11$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Altitude to the hypotenuse in a right triangle creates

**Solution.** two smaller right triangles similar to each other and to the original.
