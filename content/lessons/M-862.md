# Lesson M-862 — Continuity correction awareness

Part T. T. Probability · **Limit theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `T.5-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Continuity correction awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Continuity correction awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limit theorems*

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation. CLT: standardized sums $\to$ normal. Continuity correction for lattice $\to$ continuous. Weak convergence is the topology behind the CLT.

### This skill, specifically

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Continuity correction: when approximating a lattice (integer-valued) law by a normal $Y$, replace $P(X\le k)$ by $P(Y\le k+1/2)$ and $P(X\ge k)$ by $P(Y\ge k-1/2)$, because the integer $k$ occupies $[k-1/2,k+1/2]$. It usually improves binomial and Poisson approximations.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(100,1/2)$. Approximate $P(X\ge 55)$ with continuity correction.

**Solution.** $Y\sim N(50,25)$, $P(Y\ge 54.5)=P\bigl(Z\ge(54.5-50)/5\bigr)=P(Z\ge 0.9)=1-\Phi(0.9)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $P(X\le 40)$ with correction, same $X$.

**Solution.** $P(Y\le 40.5)=P(Z\le(40.5-50)/5)=P(Z\le -1.9)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Adding $1/2$ in the wrong direction (using $k-1/2$ for a $\le k$ probability), or applying a continuity correction to an already continuous $X$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $P(X=50)$ for $\mathrm{Bin}(100,1/2)$ via a normal $Y$.

**Solution.** $P(49.5\le Y\le 50.5)=P(|Z|\le 0.1)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Poisson($16$), $P(X\le 10)$ with correction, $Y\sim N(16,16)$. $Z$-threshold?

**Solution.** $(10.5-16)/4=-1.375$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
