## 3. Precise statement

A linear map $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is multiplication by its matrix in the standard bases: the $j$th column is $T(\mathbf{e}_j)$. Composition is matrix product; invertibility is an invertible matrix; kernel is nullspace; eigenvalues of $T$ are those of any matrix of $T$. Changing basis: $A_{\mathrm{new}}=P^{-1}AP$. Operator language ‘projection, nilpotent, self-adjoint’ translates to $P^2=P$, $N^k=0$, $A^\top=A$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $T(x,y)=(x+y,x-y)$. Matrix of $T$, and $\det T$.

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ 1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $\det=-2\neq 0$, so $T$ is invertible.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Rotation by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise in $\mathbb{R}^2$ has matrix

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix}$ (columns $T\mathbf{e}_1=\mathbf{e}_2$, $T\mathbf{e}_2=-\mathbf{e}_1$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.
