# Lesson M-894 — Chi-square distribution

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Inference** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Chi-square distribution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 3. Precise statement

If $Z_1,\ldots,Z_k$ are iid $N(0,1)$, then $\sum Z_i^2\sim\chi^2_k$. Mean $k$, variance $2k$. For iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $(n-1)s^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$. Pearson statistic $\sum (O-E)^2/E$ is approximately $\chi^2$ with df $=\#\text{cells}-1-\#\text{fitted parameters}$ under $H_0$. $\chi^2$ laws are supported on $[0,\infty)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Mean of $\chi^2_{10}$.

**Solution.** $10$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $n=5$ normal data, $s^2=8$, $\sigma^2=4$, then $(n-1)s^2/\sigma^2=$

**Solution.** $8$, which is a $\chi^2_4$ observation under the model.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.
