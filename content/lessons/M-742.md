# Lesson M-742 — Injective functions

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Functions & cardinality** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.4`.


This lesson isolates **Injective functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Injective functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Functions & cardinality*

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses. Countable vs uncountable, Cantor diagonal, $|\mathbb{N}|=|\mathbb{Z}|=|\mathbb{Q}|<|\mathbb{R}|$, axiom of choice as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$f:A\to B$ is injective if $f(x)=f(y)\Rightarrow x=y$ (equivalently, distinct inputs give distinct outputs). Real functions: horizontal line test; strictly monotone functions are injective. Injective maps are left-cancellable. $|f(A)|=|A|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $f(x)=x^2$ injective on $\mathbb{R}$? On $[0,\infty)$? Is $n\mapsto n+1$ injective on $\mathbb{N}$?

**Solution.** No; yes; yes.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $f(x)=e^x$, injective?

**Solution.** Yes, strictly increasing.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking $f(x)=f(-x)$ only at one point, or confusing injective with surjective.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Prove $f(x)=3x+1$ is injective.

**Solution.** $3x+1=3y+1\Rightarrow x=y$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Injection $A\to A$ on a finite set is automatically

**Solution.** surjective (hence bijective).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
