# Lesson M-959 — Essential singularities

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Essential singularities** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Essential singularities**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex series & singularities*

### This skill, specifically

Taylor in disks of holomorphicity, Laurent in annuli, removable/pole/essential classification, residues as $(-1)$ Laurent coefficients.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An isolated singularity is essential if the principal part has infinitely many nonzero terms. Casorati–Weierstrass: the image of any punctured neighborhood is dense in $\mathbb{C}$. Picard: the image omits at most one value infinitely often. Classic: $e^{1/z}$ at $0$. Polynomials in $1/z$ of finite degree are poles, not essential.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Classify $z=0$ for $e^{1/z}$.

**Solution.** Essential: Laurent $\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-n}/n!$ has infinitely many negative powers.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sin(1/z)$ at $0$ is

**Solution.** essential.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $e^{1/z}$ a pole because it ‘blows up’, or thinking ‘essential’ means ‘important for the GRE’ rather than the classification.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $z^2 e^{1/z}$ at $0$: pole, removable, or essential?

**Solution.** Essential (still infinitely many negative powers).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $|e^{1/z}|\to\infty$ as $z\to 0$?

**Solution.** No: along the negative real axis $e^{1/x}\to 0$.

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
