# Lesson M-352 — Iterated integration

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multiple integration** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Iterated integration** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Iterated integration**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multiple integration*

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map. $|\det D\Phi|$ is the volume distortion. Setup beats brute force.

### This skill, specifically

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Iterated integration (Fubini/Tonelli): for continuous $f$ on a rectangle, $\iint f=\int_a^b\int_c^d f(x,y)\,dy\,dx=\int_c^d\int_a^b f\,dx\,dy$. On a type I region $a\le x\le b$, $g(x)\le y\le h(x)$, the inner integral is in $y$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_0^1\int_0^x 1\,dy\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^1 x\,dx=1/2$ (triangle).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Switch $\int_0^1\int_0^x f\,dy\,dx$ to $dx\,dy$

**Solution.** $0\le y\le 1$, $y\le x\le 1$: $\int_0^1\int_y^1 f\,dx\,dy$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Wrong inner limits: using constant limits on a triangular region.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_0^1\int_0^2 xy\,dy\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^1 x\cdot 2\,dx=1$. Wait: $\int_0^2 y\,dy=2$, times $x$, $\int_0^1 2x=1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** When Fubini fails (awareness).

**Solution.** Non-absolutely integrable examples on noncompact sets.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
