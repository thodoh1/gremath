# Lesson M-641 — Completeness of the reals

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `R G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Completeness of the reals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Completeness of the reals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Completeness of the reals: every Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ converges to a limit in $\mathbb{R}$. Equivalently: $\mathbb{R}$ has the least-upper-bound property. $\mathbb{Q}$ is not complete: a Cauchy sequence of rationals may converge to $\sqrt{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$. Completeness is what makes $\varepsilon$-$N$ arguments close inside $\mathbb{R}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why do decimal approximations $1$, $1.4$, $1.41$, $1.414$, $\ldots$ to $\sqrt{2}$ show $\mathbb{Q}$ is incomplete?

**Solution.** They form a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{Q}$ with no rational limit.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A Cauchy sequence of reals converges in

**Solution.** $\mathbb{R}$ (completeness).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $\mathbb{R}$ complete because it is a field, or confusing algebraic completeness (FTA) with metric completeness.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $(0,1)$ complete in the usual metric?

**Solution.** No: $1/n$ is Cauchy and leaves the space.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Name two equivalent completeness statements for $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** LUB property; Cauchy sequences converge; nested closed intervals with lengths $\to 0$ meet in a point.
