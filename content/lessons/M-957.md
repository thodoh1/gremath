# Lesson M-957 — Removable singularities

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Removable singularities** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Removable singularities**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

An isolated singularity $a$ is removable if $\lim_{z\to a}f(z)$ exists finitely (equivalently the Laurent series has no negative powers, equivalently $f$ extends holomorphically through $a$). Riemann’s criterion: if $f$ is holomorphic and bounded near $a$, then $a$ is removable. Example: $\sin z/z$ at $0$ extends by $1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Classify $z=0$ for $f(z)=\dfrac{\sin z}{z}$.

**Solution.** Removable; $\lim_{z\to 0}\sin z/z=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(e^z-1)/z$ at $0$ is

**Solution.** removable, value $1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling a hole removable because you cancelled a factor algebraically but left a different zero in the denominator, or treating a pole as removable because a calculator shows overflow.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $z/\overline{z}$ a removable singularity at $0$?

**Solution.** No: not even isolated in the holomorphic sense — the function is not holomorphic nearby, and the limit does not exist.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $f(z)=(z^2-1)/(z-1)$ at $z=1$.

**Solution.** Removable; extends as $z+1$.
