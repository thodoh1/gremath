# Lesson M-583 — Commutative rings

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Commutative rings** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Commutative rings**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A ring is commutative if $ab=ba$ for all $a,b$. $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, $F[x]$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are commutative; $M_n(F)$ for $n\ge 2$ and the Hamilton quaternions $\mathbb{H}$ are not. Polynomial rings $R[x]$ are commutative iff $R$ is. Homomorphic images of commutative rings are commutative.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Give a commutative ring that is not an integral domain, and a noncommutative ring with identity.

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$: $2\cdot 3=0$. $M_2(\mathbb{R})$: identity $I_2$, not commutative.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\mathbb{H}$ commutative?

**Solution.** No: $ij=k=-ji$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming $R[x]$ fails to be commutative when $R=\mathbb{Z}$ (it is commutative).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Subring of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$.

**Solution.** Matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\ b&a\end{pmatrix}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Product ring $R\times S$ is commutative iff

**Solution.** both $R$ and $S$ are.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
