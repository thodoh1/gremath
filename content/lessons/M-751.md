# Lesson M-751 — Basic cardinal arithmetic

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Functions & cardinality** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.4`.


This lesson isolates **Basic cardinal arithmetic** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Basic cardinal arithmetic**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Functions & cardinality*

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses. Countable vs uncountable, Cantor diagonal, $|\mathbb{N}|=|\mathbb{Z}|=|\mathbb{Q}|<|\mathbb{R}|$, axiom of choice as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Cardinal arithmetic (infinite): $\aleph_0+n=\aleph_0=\aleph_0+\aleph_0=\aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0$. For infinite $\kappa$, $\kappa+\kappa=\kappa$ under AC. $2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak{c}=|\mathbb{R}|$, and $\mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}=|\mathbb{R}^n|$. Always $2^\kappa>\kappa$ by Cantor. $|\mathcal{P}(A)|=2^{|A|}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $|\mathbb{Z}|$, $|\mathbb{Q}|$, $|\mathbb{R}|$, $|\mathbb{R}^2|$, $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})|$.

**Solution.** $\aleph_0$, $\aleph_0$, $\mathfrak{c}$, $\mathfrak{c}$, $2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak{c}$ (or at least $2^{\aleph_0}$, identified with $\mathfrak{c}$ via binary expansions).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|\mathbb{N}\cup\{-1,-2\}|$ equals

**Solution.** $\aleph_0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\infty+\infty=2\infty$ as if $\infty$ were a cardinal, or claiming $|\mathbb{R}^2|>|\mathbb{R}|$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $|\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}|$.

**Solution.** $\mathfrak{c}\cdot\aleph_0=\mathfrak{c}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why is $|\mathbb{R}|\le 2^{\aleph_0}$?

**Solution.** Each real has a binary expansion, giving a surjection $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
