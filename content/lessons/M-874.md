# Lesson M-874 — Covariance

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Descriptive statistics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.2`.


This lesson isolates **Covariance** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Covariance**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Covariance: $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=E[(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)]=E[XY]-\mu_X\mu_Y$. It is bilinear, $\mathrm{Cov}(X,X)=\mathrm{Var}(X)$, and $\mathrm{Var}(X+Y)=\mathrm{Var}(X)+\mathrm{Var}(Y)+2\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$. Independence implies $\mathrm{Cov}=0$; the converse is false.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If $X$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}$ and $Y=X$, compute $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$.

**Solution.** $E[X]=1/2$, $E[XY]=E[X^2]=1/2$, so $\mathrm{Cov}=1/2-(1/2)^2=1/4=\mathrm{Var}(X)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathrm{Cov}(X,c)=$

**Solution.** $0$ for constant $c$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding independence from zero covariance (counterexample: $X\sim N(0,1)$, $Y=X^2$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

**Solution.** $ab\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
