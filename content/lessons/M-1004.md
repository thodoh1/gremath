## 3. Precise statement

Domain traps: logs and even roots need nonnegative (or positive) interiors; denominators $\neq 0$; $\arcsin$ input in $[-1,1]$; $\tan$ undefined at odd multiples of $\pi/2$. Combining $\ln a+\ln b=\ln(ab)$ requires $a>0$ and $b>0$, not merely $ab>0$. The algebraic simplification of a formula may have a larger domain than the original expression.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Domain of $\ln(x-1)+\ln(x+1)$ vs $\ln(x^2-1)$ as real functions.

**Solution.** The sum needs $x>1$. The single log needs $|x|>1$. They agree only on $(1,\infty)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Domain of $\sqrt{x(x-2)}$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** $x\le 0$ or $x\ge 2$ (product $\ge 0$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Simplifying first, then reading the domain off the simplified expression (cancelling $x-1$ from $(x^2-1)/(x-1)$ silently adds $x=1$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Real domain of $\arcsin(2x)$.

**Solution.** $[-1/2,1/2]$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\sqrt{x^2}=x$ on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** No: $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$.
