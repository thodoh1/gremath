# Lesson M-100 — Zeros/intercepts/asymptotes and qualitative graphing

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Common function families** · High

Prerequisites: `B.1-B.2`.


This lesson isolates **Zeros/intercepts/asymptotes and qualitative graphing** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Zeros/intercepts/asymptotes and qualitative graphing**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Common function families*

Polynomials, rationals, powers, roots, absolute values, exponentials, logs, and step functions are the GRE’s menagerie. You should know domain, intercepts, asymptotes, monotonicity, and a qualitative graph for each family, and how composition mixes them.

### This skill, specifically

Polynomials, rationals, powers, roots, absolute values, exponentials, logs, and step functions are the GRE’s menagerie.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Qualitative graphing checklist: domain, intercepts, symmetries, asymptotes, critical points, concavity, end behavior. Zeros of numerator vs denominator decide intercepts vs asymptotes.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Intercepts of $y=\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}$.

**Solution.** $x$-intercept $2$, $y$-intercept $-2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A rational function with a hole at $x=1$ and no other denominator zeros has how many vertical asymptotes?

**Solution.** Zero.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Plotting a hole as an asymptote.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** End behavior of $\dfrac{3x^2}{x^2+1}$.

**Solution.** $y\to 3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** How many real zeros can $\dfrac{x^2+1}{x}$ have?

**Solution.** None.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
