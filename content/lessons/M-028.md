# Lesson M-028 — Rational expressions and domain restrictions

Part A. A. Foundations & Algebraic Fluency · **Algebraic manipulation** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Rational expressions and domain restrictions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Algebraic fluency is not a listed ETS bucket of its own, but it is the cost of every calculus and algebra item. Expect it to be invisible when you have it and fatal when you do not.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Rational expressions and domain restrictions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Algebraic manipulation*

Algebraic manipulation is legal rewriting: factoring, identities, division algorithms, partial fractions, substitution. Illegal rewriting (canceling sums, dividing by zero, dropping absolute values) is how GRE items are authored. Every identity you learn here is a compression algorithm for a later calculus or linear-algebra computation.

## 3. Precise statement

A rational expression $p/q$ (polynomials) is defined where $q\neq 0$. Simplify by canceling common factors, never common terms. The simplified formula may have a larger apparent domain; the original domain still governs.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2-2x+1}$ and state the domain of the original.

**Solution.** $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}$ for $x\neq 1$. Original also needs $x\neq 1$ only (double root). Domain $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ at $x=2$ is

**Solution.** undefined, even though the simplified $x+2$ would be $4$. Removable discontinuity.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Canceling $x$ in $\dfrac{x+2}{x+3}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Domain of $\dfrac{1}{x^2-5x+6}$.

**Solution.** $x\neq 2,3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Simplify $\dfrac{x^3-x}{x}$.

**Solution.** $x^2-1$ for $x\neq 0$.
