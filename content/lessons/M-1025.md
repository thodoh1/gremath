# Lesson M-1025 — Solve existence/uniqueness questions

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Solve existence/uniqueness questions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 3. Precise statement

Existence vs uniqueness are different GRE buttons. IVT / EVT / intermediate-value for derivatives give existence. Lipschitz / Picard / $f'<0$ / strict convexity / invertible $Df$ give uniqueness. Linear systems: existence iff $b\in\mathrm{col}(A)$; uniqueness iff $\ker A=\{0\}$. ODE $y'=y^{1/3}$, $y(0)=0$ has multiple solutions. Always answer the question that was asked (exists / unique / both / neither).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $y'=3y^{2/3}$, $y(0)=0$. Existence and uniqueness?

**Solution.** Picard fails ($f_y=2 y^{-1/3}$ unbounded at $0$). Solutions: $y=0$ and $y=t^3$. Existence yes, uniqueness no.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $Ax=b$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ 2&2\end{pmatrix}$, $b=(1,2)^\top$: existence/uniqueness?

**Solution.** Infinitely many (exists, not unique). If $b=(1,0)^\top$, none.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Citing Picard to claim uniqueness when the Lipschitz hypothesis fails, or IVT to claim a unique root.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f(x)=x^3-x-1$ on $\mathbb{R}$: a real root exists uniquely because

**Solution.** $f'=3x^2-1$ vanishes at $\pm 1/\sqrt{3}$; the local maximum is $f(-1/\sqrt{3})=2/(3\sqrt{3})-1<0$, and $f\to\pm\infty$ at $\pm\infty$, so exactly one real root (to the right of $1/\sqrt{3}$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $x=\cos x$ on $\mathbb{R}$: existence by IVT on $[0,\pi/2]$; uniqueness because

**Solution.** $1+\sin x\ge 0$ and $g(x)=x-\cos x$ has $g'=1+\sin x\ge 0$, strictly increasing except at isolated points, so unique.
