# Lesson M-1024 — Solve counterexample questions

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Solve counterexample questions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Solve counterexample questions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Counterexample questions: ‘which of the following is false?’ Work choice-by-choice with the smallest counterexample. Keep a zoo: $x^n$ on $[0,1]$; $x^2\sin(1/x)$; harmonic series; $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\ 0&0\end{pmatrix}$; $f=\overline{z}$; $\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}$; $e^{1/z}$. A true statement with a failed proof is not a counterexample. Verify the counterexample actually violates the claim.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which is false? (A) Every convergent sequence is bounded. (B) Every bounded sequence of reals has a convergent subsequence. (C) Every bounded sequence of reals converges.

**Solution.** (C) is false: $(-1)^n$. (A) is true; (B) is Bolzano–Weierstrass.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Which is false? (A) $AB$ and $BA$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues (any compatible sizes). (B) $\chi_{AB}=\chi_{BA}$ even when $A$ is $2\times 3$ and $B$ is $3\times 2$. (C) For square $A,B$, $AB$ is singular iff $BA$ is.

**Solution.** (B) is false as stated: $\chi_{AB}$ is degree $2$ and $\chi_{BA}$ is degree $3$ (they agree up to a factor of $\lambda$). (A) and (C) are true for square matrices.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Offering $f(x)=|x|$ as a counterexample to EVT on $[0,1]$ (it attains its max).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** False claim: uniformly convergent $C^1$ limits have $C^1$ limit. Counterexample sketch

**Solution.** $f_n(x)=\sqrt{x^2+1/n}$ on $[-1,1]$ converges to $|x|$, not differentiable at $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $f_n=x^n$ on $[0,1]$ a counterexample to ‘pointwise limit of continuous is continuous’?

**Solution.** Yes, at $x=1$.
