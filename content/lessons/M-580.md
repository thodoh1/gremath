# Lesson M-580 — Direct products

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Group actions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4`.


This lesson isolates **Direct products** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Direct products**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Group actions*

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Orbit-stabilizer: $|G|=|\mathrm{Orb}|\,|\mathrm{Stab}|$. Conjugacy is a special action. Finite abelian groups decompose into cyclic groups.

### This skill, specifically

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The external direct product $G\times H$ has operation $(g,h)(g',h')=(gg',hh')$. Then $G\times\{e\}\cong G$ and $\{e\}\times H\cong H$ are normal subgroups intersecting trivially and generating the product. $G\times H$ is abelian iff both factors are. $\mathbb{Z}/m\times\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/mn$ iff $\gcd(m,n)=1$. Internal direct product: $G=NK$ with $N\cap K=\{e\}$, $N,K\trianglelefteq G$, and elements of $N$ commuting with those of $K$ in the strong sense $nk=kn$ — equivalently the product map $N\times K\to G$ is an iso.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/3\cong\mathbb{Z}/6$? Is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\cong\mathbb{Z}/4$?

**Solution.** Yes, $\gcd(2,3)=1$. No: Klein vs cyclic.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|G\times H|$ equals

**Solution.** $|G|\,|H|$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming $G\times H\cong G$ whenever $H$ is nontrivial (only the trivial factor can be dropped).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Center of $G\times H$.

**Solution.** $Z(G)\times Z(H)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $S_3\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ abelian?

**Solution.** No.
