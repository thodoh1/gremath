# Lesson M-654 — Algebra of continuous functions

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Continuity** · High

Prerequisites: `E.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Algebra of continuous functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Algebra of continuous functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Continuity*

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$. Types of discontinuity (removable, jump, infinite) are classified by one-sided limits. IVT and EVT are the two big existence theorems for continuous functions on closed intervals.

### This skill, specifically

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f,g$ are continuous at $a$, so are $f+g$, $fg$, and $f/g$ when $g(a)\neq 0$. Composition: $g$ continuous at $a$ and $f$ continuous at $g(a)$ imply $f\circ g$ continuous at $a$. Polynomials, rational functions on their domains, $\exp$, $\sin$, $\cos$ are continuous.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Where is $h(x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{x-1}$ continuous?

**Solution.** On $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\}$: quotient of continuous functions, denominator zero only at $1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $e^{\sin(x^2)}$ continuous on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes: composition of continuous maps.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Cancelling a factor and restoring a removable discontinuity without checking the original formula's domain.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Product of two discontinuous functions can be continuous: example.

**Solution.** $1_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $1_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}$ multiply to $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $|f|$ is continuous if $f$ is: true?

**Solution.** Yes: composition with $t\mapsto|t|$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
