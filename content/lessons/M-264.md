# Lesson M-264 — Trigonometric integrals

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Antiderivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Trigonometric integrals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trigonometric integrals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Antiderivatives*

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$. The toolkit: power rule, substitution, parts, partial fractions, trig integrals, trig sub, reduction. Choosing the tool is the skill; carrying out the algebra is the tax.

### This skill, specifically

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Trig integrals: odd power of $\sin$ or $\cos$ — peel one factor, $u$ the rest. Even powers — power-reduce with $\sin^2=(1-\cos 2x)/2$. For $\tan^m\sec^n$: if $n$ even, save $\sec^2$; if $m$ odd, save $\sec\tan$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int\sin^3 x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\int(1-\cos^2 x)\sin x\,dx=-\cos x+\cos^3 x/3+C$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int\cos^2 x\,dx$

**Solution.** $\int\frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}=\frac{x}{2}+\frac{\sin 2x}{4}+C$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$ when you needed a double-angle to integrate an even power.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int\tan x\sec^2 x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $u=\tan x$, $u^2/2+C$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\int\sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\int\frac14\sin^2 2x=\int\frac{1-\cos 4x}{8}\,dx$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
