# Lesson M-259 — Exponential/log antiderivatives

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Antiderivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Exponential/log antiderivatives** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Exponential/log antiderivatives**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Antiderivatives*

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$. The toolkit: power rule, substitution, parts, partial fractions, trig integrals, trig sub, reduction. Choosing the tool is the skill; carrying out the algebra is the tax.

### This skill, specifically

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$\int e^{ax}\,dx=\frac{1}{a}e^{ax}+C$ ($a\neq 0$). $\int a^x\,dx=\frac{a^x}{\ln a}+C$ for $a>0,a\neq 1$. $\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C$. $\int \frac{u'}{u}\,dx=\ln|u|+C$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int e^{2x}\,dx$ and $\int 2^x\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\frac12 e^{2x}+C$ and $2^x/\ln 2+C$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int \frac{2x}{x^2+1}\,dx$

**Solution.** $\ln(x^2+1)+C$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $\int e^{x^2}\,dx$ as elementary (it is not).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int e^{-x}\,dx$.

**Solution.** $-e^{-x}+C$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\int \frac{1}{2x+3}\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\frac12\ln|2x+3|+C$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
