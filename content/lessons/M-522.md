# Lesson M-522 — Computing phi(n) from prime factorization

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Euler/Fermat tools** · High

Prerequisites: `L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Computing phi(n) from prime factorization** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Computing phi(n) from prime factorization**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Euler/Fermat tools*

Fermat: $a^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod p$ if $p\nmid a$. Euler: $a^{\phi(n)}\equiv 1\pmod n$ if $\gcd(a,n)=1$. $\phi$ is multiplicative. Orders divide $\phi(n)$.

### This skill, specifically

Fermat: $a^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod p$ if $p\nmid a$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

From $n=\prod p_i^{e_i}$, $\phi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}(1-1/p)=\prod p_i^{e_i-1}(p_i-1)$. In particular $\phi(2^k)=2^{k-1}$ for $k\ge 1$, and $\phi(pq)=(p-1)(q-1)$ for distinct primes. Never use $\phi(n)=n-1$ unless $n$ is prime.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\phi(360)$ from $360=2^3\cdot 3^2\cdot 5$.

**Solution.** $\phi(360)=360\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac45=96$. Or $2^2(1)\cdot 3(2)\cdot 4=4\cdot 6\cdot 4=96$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\phi(100)$ for $100=2^2 5^2$

**Solution.** $100\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac45=40$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\phi(p^k)=p^k-1$, or $\phi(pq)=\phi(p)\phi(q)$ when $p=q$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\phi(p^3)$ for $p=5$.

**Solution.** $5^3-5^2=100$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\phi(18)$.

**Solution.** $18=2\cdot 3^2$, $\phi=18\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac23=6$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
