# Lesson M-120 — Definitions of six trigonometric functions

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Trigonometry foundations** · Critical

Prerequisites: `B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Definitions of six trigonometric functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Definitions of six trigonometric functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Trigonometry foundations*

Radians are the calculus unit. The unit circle defines $\sin,\cos$ as coordinates, and the other four functions as ratios. Pythagorean, even/odd, and reciprocal identities are rewriting tools. Exact values at $0,\pi/6,\pi/4,\pi/3,\pi/2$ and their cousins must be instant. Quadrant signs are ASTC (or whatever mnemonic you will not botch).

### This skill, specifically

Radians are the calculus unit.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$\sin\theta=y$, $\cos\theta=x$, $\tan\theta=y/x$ on the unit circle. $\csc=1/\sin$, $\sec=1/\cos$, $\cot=1/\tan$. Right-triangle definitions match in Q1: opposite/hypotenuse, etc.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\tan\theta$ at $\theta=3\pi/4$.

**Solution.** $-1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sec\theta$ undefined when

**Solution.** $\cos\theta=0$, i.e. $\theta=\pi/2+k\pi$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using triangle definitions in Q2 without signs.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\sin(0),\cos(0),\tan(0)$.

**Solution.** $0,1,0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Express $\cot$ in $x,y$.

**Solution.** $x/y$.
