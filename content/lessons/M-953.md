# Lesson M-953 — Path deformation intuition

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex integration** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1`.


This lesson isolates **Path deformation intuition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Path deformation intuition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex integration*

Contours, $\int_\gamma f\,dz$, Cauchy’s theorem (vanishing on closed curves in simply connected holomorphic regions), Cauchy integral formula, deformation.

### This skill, specifically

Contours, $\int_\gamma f\,dz$, Cauchy’s theorem (vanishing on closed curves in simply connected holomorphic regions), Cauchy integral formula, deformation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f$ is holomorphic in a region containing two homologous closed contours (they enclose the same singularities, or their difference is a boundary in the domain of holomorphy), then $\int_{\gamma_1}f=\int_{\gamma_2}f$. In practice: shrink or expand a circle as long as you do not cross a singularity; a keyhole or indented contour can avoid a branch cut. Deformation is why $\int_{|z|=1} dz/z=\int_{|z|=2} dz/z=2\pi i$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $\int_{|z|=1} \dfrac{e^z}{z}\,dz=\int_{|z|=3}\dfrac{e^z}{z}\,dz$?

**Solution.** The integrand is holomorphic on $0<|z|<4$ except that both circles enclose the same simple pole at $0$; equivalently deform through the annulus.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can you deform $|z|=1$ to $|z-3|=1$ for $\int dz/z$?

**Solution.** No: you would cross $0$, and the integrals are $2\pi i$ vs $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Deforming across a pole and keeping the same integral, or confusing homotopy in $\mathbb{C}$ with homotopy in $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\text{poles}\}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_{|z|=1} dz/z^2$ vs $\int_{|z|=2} dz/z^2$.

**Solution.** Both $0$ (antiderivative $-1/z$ on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, or residue $0$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A triangle and a circle enclosing exactly the same poles of a meromorphic $f$ yield

**Solution.** equal integrals (same total residue, same orientation).
