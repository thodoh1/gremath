# Lesson M-465 — Invertibility

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Linear transformations** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.4`.


This lesson isolates **Invertibility** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Invertibility**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Linear transformations*

$T(au+bv)=aT(u)+bT(v)$. Kernel and image, injectivity/surjectivity, matrices of $T$, change of basis, similarity. Invertible iff kernel is trivial (in finite dimension, iff onto).

### This skill, specifically

$T(au+bv)=aT(u)+bT(v)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A linear $T:V\to W$ is invertible if there is a linear $S:W\to V$ with $S\circ T=\mathrm{id}_V$ and $T\circ S=\mathrm{id}_W$. Equivalently $T$ is bijective. Then $S=T^{-1}$. For square matrices, invertibility of $T$ is invertibility of $A$, and $[T^{-1}]=A^{-1}$. Finite-dimensional: injective iff surjective iff invertible for endomorphisms.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $T(x,y)=(x+y,x-y)$ invertible on $\mathbb{R}^2$? If so, find $T^{-1}$.

**Solution.** $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}=-2\neq 0$. $T^{-1}(a,b)=\bigl(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{a-b}{2}\bigr)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $T$ is invertible, $\ker T$ is

**Solution.** $\{\mathbf{0}\}$ and $\mathrm{im}\,T=V$ (for an endomorphism).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Inverting a non-square matrix, or concluding invertibility from injectivity when $\dim V\neq\dim W$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Inverse of a reflection through a line in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Solution.** itself ($T^{-1}=T$, $T^2=I$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $(ST)^{-1}$ equals

**Solution.** $T^{-1}S^{-1}$ when both are invertible.
