# Lesson M-588 — Zero divisors

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Zero divisors** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Zero divisors**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

Two operations, distributive.

## 3. Precise statement

A zero divisor is a nonzero $a$ such that $ab=0$ for some $b\neq 0$ (left/right versions in noncommutative rings). A domain has none. In $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ the zero divisors are the nonzero nonunits, i.e. residues sharing a factor with $n$. In $M_2(F)$, nilpotent nonzero matrices are zero divisors. The set of zero divisors is not typically an ideal.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Zero divisors in $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$. Give a matrix zero divisor.

**Solution.** Nonzero and not coprime to $12$: $2,3,4,6,8,9,10$. Matrix: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0$, and $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}^2=0$ so it is a (nilpotent) zero divisor.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $2$ is a zero divisor in $\mathbb{Z}/6$ because

**Solution.** $2\cdot 3=0$ and $3\neq 0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $0$ a zero divisor (conventions usually exclude it).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $\mathbb{Z}$ have zero divisors?

**Solution.** No.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** In $\mathbb{Z}/15$, is $5$ a unit or a zero divisor?

**Solution.** Zero divisor: $5\cdot 3=15\equiv 0$.
