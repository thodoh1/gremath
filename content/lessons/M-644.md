# Lesson M-644 — Absolute convergence

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Series & convergence** · High

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Absolute convergence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Absolute convergence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

## 3. Precise statement

Absolute convergence: $\sum|a_n|$ converges. Then $\sum a_n$ converges, and every rearrangement has the same sum. Absolute convergence is tested by comparison, ratio, root, integral tests on the nonnegative series $\sum|a_n|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $\sum (-1)^n/n^2$ converge absolutely?

**Solution.** Yes: $\sum 1/n^2$ is a convergent $p$-series, $p=2>1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Absolute convergence implies ordinary convergence: true or false?

**Solution.** True (triangle inequality on partial sums / completeness).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding absolute convergence from ordinary convergence.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\sum \sin n/n^2$ absolutely convergent?

**Solution.** Yes: $|\sin n|/n^2\le 1/n^2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $\sum (-1)^n/n$ converge absolutely?

**Solution.** No: harmonic series diverges.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
