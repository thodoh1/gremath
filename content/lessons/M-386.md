# Lesson M-386 — Conceptual relationships among the three theorems

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Integral theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `I.2-I.3`.


This lesson isolates **Conceptual relationships among the three theorems** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Conceptual relationships among the three theorems**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

Green, Stokes, divergence: the FTC in dimensions 2 and 3.

## 3. Precise statement

Green, Stokes, and divergence are one FTC in increasing dimension: a $1$-form on a curve, a $2$-form on a surface, a $3$-form on a solid. Green is Stokes for a planar surface. Divergence is the statement that $\oiint\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ equals the integral of $\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{F}$. All require compatible orientation and enough smoothness.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** How does Green sit inside Stokes?

**Solution.** Take $S\subset\{z=0\}$ with $\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{F}=(P,Q,0)$. Then $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\mathbf{k}=Q_x-P_y$, and $\partial S$ is the plane curve of Green.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

**Solution.** $0$, matching Stokes on a closed surface (empty boundary).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating the three theorems as unrelated computational tricks with no shared FTC logic, then mixing hypotheses (e.g. needing $\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{F}$ for divergence).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

**Solution.** Divergence (Stokes would need $\mathbf{F}=\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{A}$ and would give $0$ if $S$ is closed).
