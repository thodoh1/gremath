# Lesson M-819 — Complement rule

Part T. T. Probability · **Probability foundations** · High

Prerequisites: `R.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Complement rule** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Complement rule**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Probability foundations*

Sample space, events, Kolmogorov axioms, complements, unions, conditionals $P(A|B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)$, independence, total probability, Bayes.

### This skill, specifically

Sample space, events, Kolmogorov axioms, complements, unions, conditionals $P(A|B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)$, independence, total probability, Bayes.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Complement rule: $P(A^c)=1-P(A)$. Useful for `at least one' and `never'. Also $P(A\setminus B)=P(A)-P(A\cap B)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $P(\text{at least one head in }4\text{ fair coin flips})$.

**Solution.** $1-(1/2)^4=15/16$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $P(A)=0.28$. $P(A^c)$.

**Solution.** $0.72$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Computing $1-P(\text{exactly one})$ when the problem asked $P(\text{at least one})$, which still includes `exactly two', etc.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A die is rolled. $P(\text{not a }6)$.

**Solution.** $5/6$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Independent trials, success probability $0.3$, $n=5$. $P(\text{at least one success})$.

**Solution.** $1-(0.7)^5=1-0.16807=0.83193$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
