# Lesson M-297 — Completeness connection

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Sequences** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1; O.1`.


This lesson isolates **Completeness connection** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Completeness connection**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Sequences*

Sequence convergence, monotonicity, boundedness, subsequences, Cauchy sequences, and completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ are analysis language used on calculus items. $\limsup$ is awareness-level.

### This skill, specifically

Sequence convergence, monotonicity, boundedness, subsequences, Cauchy sequences, and completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ are analysis language used on calculus items.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Completeness of $\mathbb{R}$: every Cauchy sequence of reals converges to a real. Equivalent forms: LUB property, nested interval property, BW. $\mathbb{Q}$ is not complete. Completeness is why monotone bounded sequences converge in $\mathbb{R}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Give a Cauchy sequence of rationals with no rational limit.

**Solution.** Truncations of $\sqrt{2}$: $1,1.4,1.41,\ldots$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Nested intervals $[a_n,b_n]$ with length $\to 0$

**Solution.** Intersect in exactly one real (completeness).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Saying $\mathbb{Q}$ is complete because Cauchy sequences of rationals “have limits” (the limits may be irrational).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is every convergent sequence Cauchy?

**Solution.** Yes, in any metric space.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Completeness is about

**Solution.** Cauchy sequences converging inside the space.
