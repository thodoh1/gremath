# Lesson M-322 — Series multiplication basics

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Power/Taylor series** · Critical

Prerequisites: `G.2-E.5`.


This lesson isolates **Series multiplication basics** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Series multiplication basics**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Power/Taylor series*

Power series have a radius of convergence. Inside the radius you may differentiate and integrate termwise. Taylor series of $e^x,\sin x,\cos x,1/(1-x)$ and the binomial series should be writable in your sleep.

### This skill, specifically

Power series have a radius of convergence.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Cauchy product: $(\sum a_n)(\sum b_n)=\sum c_n$ with $c_n=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k b_{n-k}$, valid when at least one series is absolutely convergent (Mertens), or both absolutely. For power series, multiply and collect powers inside the common open disk.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $(\sum x^n)(\sum x^n)=\sum (n+1)x^n$ for $|x|<1$.

**Solution.** Yes: $c_n=\sum_{k=0}^n 1=n+1=1/(1-x)^2$ series.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Square of $\sum x^n/n!$ 

**Solution.** $e^{x}\cdot e^{x}=e^{2x}=\sum (2x)^n/n!$, also Cauchy product.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Multiplying two conditionally convergent series and assuming the Cauchy product converges to the product.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Linear terms of $(1+x+x^2+\cdots)(1-x+x^2-\cdots)$.

**Solution.** $1+x^2+x^4+\cdots=1/(1-x^2)$ for $|x|<1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $c_2$ for $a=(1,1,1,\ldots)$, $b=(1,-1,0,0,\ldots)$.

**Solution.** $a_0 b_2+a_1 b_1+a_2 b_0=0+1\cdot(-1)+1\cdot 1=0$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
