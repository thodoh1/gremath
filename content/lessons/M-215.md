# Lesson M-215 — Derivative from first principles

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Derivative definition & rules** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1-E.2`.


This lesson isolates **Derivative from first principles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Derivative from first principles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Derivative definition & rules*

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists. Geometrically it is slope of the tangent; physically it is instantaneous rate. Power, product, quotient, chain, implicit, logarithmic, inverse, and higher derivatives are the machine that computes $f'$ without returning to the limit every time.

### This skill, specifically

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The derivative at $a$ is $f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists. Equivalent: $\lim_{x\to a}\dfrac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}$. Differentiability at $a$ implies continuity at $a$; the converse is false.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $f'(1)$ from the definition for $f(x)=x^2$.

**Solution.** $\lim_{h\to 0}((1+2h+h^2)-1)/h=\lim(2+h)=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|x|$ at $0$

**Solution.** Not differentiable: difference quotient $\pm 1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the definition but canceling $h$ and forgetting to take $h\to 0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Difference quotient of $f(x)=3x+2$.

**Solution.** Always $3$, so $f'=3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does differentiability imply continuity?

**Solution.** Yes.

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
