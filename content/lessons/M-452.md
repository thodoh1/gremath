# Lesson M-452 — Dimension

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Dimension** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Dimension**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The dimension $\dim V$ is the number of vectors in any basis (finite-dimensional case). $\dim\mathbb{R}^n=n$, $\dim P_n=n+1$, $\dim\{\mathbf{0}\}=0$. A subspace of an $n$-dimensional space has dimension $\le n$. If $\dim V=n$, then $n$ independent vectors automatically span, and $n$ spanning vectors are automatically independent.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\dim P_2$ and $\dim M_{2\times 3}$.

**Solution.** $3$ and $6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $W\subset\mathbb{R}^5$ and $\dim W=5$, then $W$ is

**Solution.** all of $\mathbb{R}^5$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the number of defining equations as dimension (each independent equation cuts dimension by $1$, but they may be redundant).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\dim\mathrm{span}\{(1,2,3),(2,4,6),(0,1,1)\}$.

**Solution.** $2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can a proper subspace have the same dimension as $V$?

**Solution.** No, if $\dim V$ is finite.
