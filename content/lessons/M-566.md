# Lesson M-566 — Image

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Homomorphisms & quotients** · High

Prerequisites: `M.3`.


This lesson isolates **Image** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Image**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Homomorphisms & quotients*

A homomorphism preserves the operation. Kernel is a normal subgroup. First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\phi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\phi$. Quotients exist precisely because of normality.

### This skill, specifically

A homomorphism preserves the operation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The image $\mathrm{im}\,\phi=\phi(G)$ is a subgroup of the codomain. $\phi$ is surjective iff $\mathrm{im}\,\phi$ equals the codomain. $|\mathrm{im}\,\phi|=[G:\ker\phi]$ when this index is finite. First isomorphism theorem identifies $\mathrm{im}\,\phi$ with $G/\ker\phi$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Image of $\phi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, $\phi(n)=8n$.

**Solution.** $8n\bmod 12$: $8\cdot 0=0$, $8\cdot 1=8$, $8\cdot 2=16\equiv 4$, $8\cdot 3=24\equiv 0$, so $\mathrm{im}=\{0,4,8\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Image of $\det:GL_n(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}^\times$

**Solution.** all of $\mathbb{R}^\times$ (diagonal matrices with one nonzero entry $\lambda$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling the image a subset of the domain.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Image of $\mathrm{sgn}:S_n\to\mathbb{Z}^\times=\{\pm 1\}$.

**Solution.** All of $\{\pm 1\}$ for $n\ge 2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $\phi:G\to G$ is $\phi(g)=e$, image is

**Solution.** $\{e\}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
