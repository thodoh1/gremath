# Lesson M-682 — Basis

Part P. P. Topology · **Topological spaces** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1`.


This lesson isolates **Basis** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Basis**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Topological spaces*

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections. Bases, subbases, subspace and product topologies, continuity as preimages of opens, homeomorphism as topological isomorphism.

### This skill, specifically

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A basis $\mathcal{B}$ for a topology: the basis elements cover $X$, and if $x\in B_1\cap B_2$ with $B_i\in\mathcal{B}$, some $B_3\in\mathcal{B}$ has $x\in B_3\subset B_1\cap B_2$. Open sets are arbitrary unions of basis elements. Metric balls form a basis; on $\mathbb{R}$, open intervals $(a,b)$ form a basis for the order topology.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Do the intervals $(a,b)$ with $a,b$ rational form a basis for the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes: a countable basis (second countability of $\mathbb{R}$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

**Drill 1.** Is $\{\{x\}:x\in X\}$ a basis?

**Solution.** Yes: it generates the discrete topology.
