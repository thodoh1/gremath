# Lesson M-940 — Orthogonal projection

Part V. V. Geometry · **Analytic/vector geometry** · High

## 3. Precise statement

The orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{u}$ onto a nonzero $\mathbf{v}$ is $\mathrm{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}=\dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{v}$. The rejection $\mathbf{u}-\mathrm{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$. Onto a unit vector $\mathbf{e}$, this is $(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{e})\mathbf{e}$. Projection onto a plane with unit normal $\mathbf{n}$ is $\mathbf{u}-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathbf{n}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Project $(3,4)$ onto $(1,0)$.

**Solution.** $(3,0)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Project $(1,1,1)$ onto $(0,0,1)$.

**Solution.** $(0,0,1)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting to divide by $\|\mathbf{v}\|^2$, or projecting onto a plane by using a direction vector in the plane instead of the normal.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
