# Lesson M-692 — Continuous image of compact space

Part P. P. Topology · **Separation & compactness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **Continuous image of compact space** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Continuous image of compact space**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Separation & compactness*

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens. Compact: every open cover has a finite subcover. Continuous images of compact sets are compact. In $\mathbb{R}^n$, compact $\Leftrightarrow$ closed and bounded (Heine–Borel).

### This skill, specifically

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f:X\to Y$ is continuous and $X$ is compact, then $f(X)$ is compact. Hence a continuous real function on a compact space is bounded and attains its extrema. A continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why does $f(x)=e^x$ fail to map $\mathbb{R}$ onto a compact set?

**Solution.** $\mathbb{R}$ is not compact; $f(\mathbb{R})=(0,\infty)$ is not compact. Continuity alone does not create compactness.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Continuous image of $[0,1]$ in $\mathbb{R}$ is

**Solution.** a closed bounded interval (connected compact).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying the theorem when the domain is $(0,1)$, or when $f$ is not continuous.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Projection $X\times Y\to X$ with $Y$ compact: closed map?

**Solution.** Yes (tube lemma / closed projection).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Continuous bijection $[0,2\pi)\to S^1$, $\theta\mapsto e^{i\theta}$: homeomorphism?

**Solution.** No: domain not compact, inverse fails continuity at $1$.
