# Lesson M-377 — Flux through surfaces

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Curl, divergence & flux** · High

Prerequisites: `I.1-H.2`.


This lesson isolates **Flux through surfaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Flux through surfaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Curl, divergence & flux*

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface. Orientation is a sign convention you must not treat as optional.

### This skill, specifically

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Flux of $\mathbf{F}$ through an oriented surface $S$ is $\iint_S\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=\iint_S\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS$. For a graph $z=g(x,y)$, $d\mathbf{S}=(-g_x,-g_y,1)\,dx\,dy$ (upward). For a sphere, $\mathbf{n}\,dS$ is the outward area element.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Flux of $\mathbf{F}=(x,y,z)$ through the unit sphere, outward.

**Solution.** On $|r|=1$, $\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}=1$, so flux equals surface area $4\pi$. (Equivalently $\iiint 3\,dV=4\pi$.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Upward flux of $(0,0,1)$ through $z=0$ on the unit disk is

**Solution.** area $\pi$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the orientation: using $(-g_x,-g_y,1)$ when the problem asked for downward flux.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Flux of $(0,0,z)$ through $z=1$, $x^2+y^2\le 1$, upward.

**Solution.** $1\cdot\pi=\pi$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Solution.** $0$.
