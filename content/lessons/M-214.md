# Lesson M-214 — Extreme Value Theorem

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Continuity** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Extreme Value Theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Extreme Value Theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Continuity*

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$. Types of discontinuity (removable, jump, infinite) are classified by one-sided limits. IVT and EVT are the two big existence theorems for continuous functions on closed intervals.

### This skill, specifically

$f$ is continuous at $a$ when $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

EVT: a continuous $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$ attains a maximum and a minimum. Proof uses boundedness of continuous functions on compact intervals, then a sequence approaching the sup. On open or unbounded intervals, EVT can fail.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $f(x)=x$ on $(0,1)$ attain a max?

**Solution.** No. EVT needs a closed bounded interval.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Where does $x^2$ on $[-1,2]$ attain its max?

**Solution.** $x=2$, value $4$. Min at $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Looking only at critical points and forgetting endpoints.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $e^{-x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ attain a min?

**Solution.** No min (approaches $0$); max at $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why check endpoints for absolute extrema on $[a,b]$?

**Solution.** EVT says extrema exist; they occur at critical points or endpoints.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
