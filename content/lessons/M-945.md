# Lesson M-945 — Complex differentiability

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex functions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `D.1-O.4`.


This lesson isolates **Complex differentiability** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Complex differentiability**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex functions*

$f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$, limits and continuity as in $\mathbb{R}^2$, complex differentiability is much stronger, holomorphic $=$ analytic in this course’s setting, Cauchy–Riemann, harmonic real and imaginary parts.

### This skill, specifically

$f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$, limits and continuity as in $\mathbb{R}^2$, complex differentiability is much stronger, holomorphic $=$ analytic in this course’s setting, Cauchy–Riemann, harmonic real and imaginary parts.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$f$ is complex-differentiable at $z_0$ if $\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)}{h}$ exists in $\mathbb{C}$. The limit must be path-independent. $f(z)=z^2$ is differentiable everywhere with $f'=2z$; $f(z)=\overline{z}$ is differentiable nowhere. Complex differentiability on an open set is much stronger than real differentiability of $(u,v)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Difference quotient of $\overline{z}$ at $0$ is $\overline{h}/h$. Does $\lim_{h\to 0}\overline{h}/h$ exist?

**Solution.** Does not exist (path-dependent).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f(z)=z^3$ at $z=1$: $f'(1)=$

**Solution.** $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the real derivative of $u$ along $x$ and calling it $f'$, or thinking $|z|^2$ is complex-differentiable at $0$ only — actually $|z|^2=z\overline{z}$ is differentiable only at $0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Show $f(z)=|z|^2$ is complex-differentiable at $0$.

**Solution.** $(h\overline{h})/h=\overline{h}\to 0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $|z|^2$ complex-differentiable at $1$?

**Solution.** No.
