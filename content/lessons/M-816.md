# Lesson M-816 — Sample spaces

Part T. T. Probability · **Probability foundations** · High

Prerequisites: `R.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Sample spaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Sample spaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A sample space $\Omega$ is the set of all outcomes of an experiment. Discrete GRE models take $\Omega$ finite or countable. Outcomes $\omega\in\Omega$ are the atoms; events are subsets. Specify $\Omega$ before assigning probabilities: two dice as $36$ ordered pairs, not $11$ sums.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Two fair distinguishable dice. $|\Omega|$? Describe $\Omega$.

**Solution.** $36$; $\Omega=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}^2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Flip a coin until the first head. A usable $\Omega$ is

**Solution.** $\{H,TH,TTH,TTTH,\ldots\}$, countably infinite.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking $\Omega=\{\text{sum}=2,\ldots,12\}$ with equal weights for two dice (those $11$ outcomes are not equally likely).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A $5$-card hand from $52$. $|\Omega|$ if hands are unordered.

**Solution.** $\binom{52}{5}=2598960$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** One card from a $52$-card deck. $|\Omega|$.

**Solution.** $52$.
