# Lesson M-939 — Cross product and area

Part V. V. Geometry · **Analytic/vector geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `K.1-C.5`.


This lesson isolates **Cross product and area** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cross product and area**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

In $\mathbb{R}^3$, $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}$ is perpendicular to both, with $\|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|\sin\theta$ equal to the parallelogram area, orientation by the right-hand rule. Determinant formula: $\mathbf{i}(u_2 v_3-u_3 v_2)-\mathbf{j}(u_1 v_3-u_3 v_1)+\mathbf{k}(u_1 v_2-u_2 v_1)$. Anticommutative: $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{u}=-\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}$. The scalar triple product $\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})$ is the parallelepiped volume (signed).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $(1,0,0)\times(0,1,0)$.

**Solution.** $(0,0,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Area of the parallelogram spanned by $(1,2,0)$ and $(3,4,0)$.

**Solution.** $|(1\cdot 4-2\cdot 3)|=2$ (magnitude of the $z$-component).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $\times$ as commutative, or using the cross product as a number in $\mathbb{R}^3$ problems that need a vector normal.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
