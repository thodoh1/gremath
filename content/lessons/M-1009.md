# Lesson M-1009 — Applying a theorem without checking hypotheses

This lesson isolates **Applying a theorem without checking hypotheses** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Applying a theorem without checking hypotheses**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Theorem-without-hypotheses traps: L’Hôpital requires a $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ form (and extra hypotheses on $f'/g'$); IVT needs continuity on the whole interval; termwise differentiation of series needs more than pointwise convergence; $\sum a_n b_n=\sum a_n\sum b_n$ is false in general. If you cannot name the hypothesis, do not apply the theorem.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why does L’Hôpital not apply to $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\cos x}{x}$?

**Solution.** Not $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$: $\cos 0=1\neq 0$. The limit diverges (like $1/x$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can you apply MVT to $f(x)=x^{1/3}$ on $[-1,1]$?

**Solution.** No: $f'$ does not exist at $0$. Also $\frac{f(1)-f(-1)}{2}=0$ but $f'(c)=\frac13 c^{-2/3}$ is never $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- L’Hôpital on a form that is already $1/0$, or IVT on $1/x$ over an interval containing $0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Termwise product: if $\sum a_n=1$ and $\sum b_n=1$, is $\sum a_n b_n=1$?

**Solution.** No: $a=(1,0,0,\ldots)$, $b=(0,1,0,\ldots)$, product series is $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** IVT on $f(x)=1/x$ to get a root in $[-1,1]$ because $f(-1)<0<f(1)$: valid?

**Solution.** No: $f$ is not continuous (not even defined) on the whole of $[-1,1]$.
