# Lesson M-635 — Limit laws for sequences

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Limit laws for sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Limit laws for sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Limit laws: if $a_n\to A$ and $b_n\to B$ are finite, then $a_n\pm b_n\to A\pm B$, $a_nb_n\to AB$, and $a_n/b_n\to A/B$ if $B\neq 0$. Squeeze: $a_n\le c_n\le b_n$ and $a_n,b_n\to L$ imply $c_n\to L$. Algebra fails for $\infty-\infty$ or $0\cdot\infty$ forms.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If $a_n\to 2$ and $b_n\to 3$, find $\lim(a_n b_n-4)$.

**Solution.** $6-4=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $a_n=n$, $b_n=1/n$ give $\lim a_nb_n=\lim a_n\lim b_n$?

**Solution.** No: left side $1$, right side is not a product of finite limits.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying quotient law when $b_n\to 0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Squeeze: $-1/n\le\sin n/n\le 1/n$ implies

**Solution.** $\sin n/n\to 0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $a_n\to 0$ and $b_n$ bounded, $a_nb_n$.

**Solution.** Goes to $0$.
