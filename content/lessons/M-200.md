# Lesson M-200 — Trigonometric limits

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Limits** · Critical

Prerequisites: `B.1-C.3`.


This lesson isolates **Trigonometric limits** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trigonometric limits**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limits*

A limit $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L$ says: values of $f$ can be forced within $\varepsilon$ of $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$ (but not necessarily equal). One-sided limits, infinite limits, and limits at infinity are the same idea with different metrics. Algebra of limits, squeeze, standard trig limits, and L’Hôpital (with hypotheses) are the toolkit. Indeterminate forms are not numbers; they are invitations to rewrite.

### This skill, specifically

A limit $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L$ says: values of $f$ can be forced within $\varepsilon$ of $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$ (but not necessarily equal).

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Standard trigonometric limits (radians): $\lim_{\theta\to 0}\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\theta}=1$, $\lim_{\theta\to 0}\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\theta^2}=\dfrac12$, $\lim_{\theta\to 0}\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\theta}=1$. Scale: $\lim\dfrac{\sin(kx)}{x}=k$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sin 5x}{x}$.

**Solution.** $5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{1-\cos x}{x}$

**Solution.** $0$ (numerator $\sim x^2/2$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using degrees with these limits (the derivative of $\sin$ would then be $\pi/180\cos$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\tan 3x}{2x}$.

**Solution.** $3/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin 4x}$.

**Solution.** $1/4$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
