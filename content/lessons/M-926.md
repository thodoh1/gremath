# Lesson M-926 — Area formulas for common quadrilaterals

Part V. V. Geometry · **Circle & polygon geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `V.1-C.5`.


This lesson isolates **Area formulas for common quadrilaterals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Area formulas for common quadrilaterals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

### Landscape of *Circle & polygon geometry*

Chords, tangents, secants, central vs inscribed angles (inscribed angle is half the arc), arc length, sector area, cyclic quadrilaterals, regular polygons, quadrilateral areas.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Parallelogram area: base $\times$ height $=|ad\sin\theta|=|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|$ in 2D. Rectangle $ab$; rhombus $\frac12 d_1 d_2$; square $s^2$; trapezoid $\frac12(b_1+b_2)h$; kite $\frac12 d_1 d_2$. A rhombus is a parallelogram with equal sides; its diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of each other.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Rhombus diagonals $6$ and $8$. Area?

**Solution.** $\frac12\cdot 6\cdot 8=24$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Trapezoid bases $4,10$, height $5$: area

**Solution.** $35$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\frac12 d_1 d_2$ for a parallelogram that is not a rhombus or kite (diagonals of a general parallelogram are not perpendicular).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Parallelogram sides $5,8$ with included angle $30^\circ$: area.

**Solution.** $20$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Square of diagonal $6$: area.

**Solution.** $18$ (since $s^2=d^2/2$).
