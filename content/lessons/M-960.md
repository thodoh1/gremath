## 3. Precise statement

The residue of $f$ at an isolated singularity $a$ is $\mathrm{Res}(f,a)=c_{-1}$, the coefficient of $(z-a)^{-1}$ in the Laurent series. Equivalently $\mathrm{Res}(f,a)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z-a|=\varepsilon} f(z)\,dz$ for small $\varepsilon$. Residues are $\mathbb{C}$-linear in $f$. At a removable singularity the residue is $0$, but residue $0$ does not imply removable (e.g. $1/z^2$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\mathrm{Res}(1/z,0)$.

**Solution.** $1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathrm{Res}(1/z^2,0)=$

**Solution.** $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking $c_{-2}$ or $c_1$ as the residue, or thinking residue $0$ means the integral around $a$ vanishes only if there are no other singularities — the local integral is $2\pi i c_{-1}$ regardless.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
