# Lesson M-584 — Identity element in rings

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Identity element in rings** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Identity element in rings**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A multiplicative identity $1_R$ satisfies $1_R a=a 1_R=a$. If it exists it is unique. A unital homomorphism sends $1_R$ to $1_S$. The ring $2\mathbb{Z}$ of even integers has no identity. Some authors require rings to be unital; then subrings share the same $1$. $1=0$ iff $R$ is the zero ring.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $2\mathbb{Z}$ have a multiplicative identity? Does $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** $2\mathbb{Z}$: no $e$ with $e\cdot 2=2$ and $e$ even except $e=1\notin 2\mathbb{Z}$. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ has identity $[1]$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The identity in $M_n(F)$ is

**Solution.** $I_n$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking $1$ of a subring to be a different element (e.g. $2$ in $2\mathbb{Z}$ if one pretends it is unital with identity $2$: then $2\cdot 4=8\neq 4$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Can a ring with $1$ satisfy $1=0$?

**Solution.** Only the zero ring.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Units live in a unital ring. Is $-1$ always a unit?

**Solution.** Yes: $(-1)(-1)=1$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
