# Lesson M-977 — Convergence intuition of iterative methods

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Root finding** · Medium

Prerequisites: `E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Convergence intuition of iterative methods** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Convergence intuition of iterative methods**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Root finding*

Bisection (slow, safe), Newton (fast, derivative, local), secant, fixed-point $x=g(x)$, convergence rates as ideas.

### This skill, specifically

Bisection (slow, safe), Newton (fast, derivative, local), secant, fixed-point $x=g(x)$, convergence rates as ideas.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Linear convergence: $e_{n+1}/e_n\to\lambda$ with $0<|\lambda|<1$ (bisection $\lambda=1/2$; fixed point $\lambda=g'(p)$). Superlinear: $e_{n+1}/e_n\to 0$. Order $p>1$: $e_{n+1}/e_n^p\to C\neq 0$ (Newton $p=2$ at simple roots; secant $p=\varphi$). More accurate local models beat a globally safe but slow method once a basin is reached. Divergence: Newton can send iterates to infinity; overshoot is typical for bad starts.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If errors are $10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-8}$, the observed order is

**Solution.** $2$ (each error is about the square of the previous).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Bisection’s error bound halves each step: order

**Solution.** $1$ (linear).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling Newton ‘always quadratic’ even at multiple roots, or reading a last-step ratio with roundoff-limited errors as the order.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Errors $0.1,0.01,0.001$: order?

**Solution.** $1$ (linear, rate $0.1$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why can Newton diverge from a distant $x_0$ even if a simple root exists?

**Solution.** The tangent intercept can jump far; the quadratic local analysis is only local.
