# Lesson M-185 — Complex roots

Part D. D. Complex Numbers · **Polar/exponential complex form** · High

Prerequisites: `D.1-C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Complex roots** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Complex numbers appear in algebra, polynomials, eigenvalues, and as a bridge to Part W.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Complex roots**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Polar/exponential complex form*

$z=re^{i\theta}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$. Multiplication adds arguments and multiplies moduli. De Moivre computes powers. The $n$th roots of a nonzero complex number are $n$ equally spaced points on a circle. Roots of unity are the unit-circle special case.

### This skill, specifically

$z=re^{i\theta}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The $n$th roots of $w=Re^{i\phi}$ are $z_k=R^{1/n}\exp\bigl(i(\phi+2\pi k)/n\bigr)$ for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$. They lie on the circle of radius $R^{1/n}$, equally spaced by $2\pi/n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Find all square roots of $-1$.

**Solution.** $-1=e^{i\pi}$. Roots $e^{i\pi/2}=i$ and $e^{i(\pi+2\pi)/2}=e^{i 3\pi/2}=-i$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Cube roots of $8$ are

**Solution.** $2$, $2e^{i 2\pi/3}$, $2e^{i 4\pi/3}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Listing only the positive real root when $n$ is even.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Fourth roots of $1$.

**Solution.** $1,i,-1,-i$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Product of all $n$th roots of $1$.

**Solution.** $0$ if $n>1$? Wait: they are the roots of $z^n-1=0$, product is $(-1)^n(-1)=(-1)^{n+1}$ times constant: for $z^n-1$, product of roots is $(-1)^n(-1)/1=(-1)^{n+1}$. For $n=2$: $1\cdot(-1)=-1=(-1)^{3}$. Yes.
