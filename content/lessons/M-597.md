# Lesson M-597 — Polynomial ideals basics

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Ideals & quotient rings** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Polynomial ideals basics** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Polynomial ideals basics**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Ideals & quotient rings*

Ideals are kernels of ring maps. Principal, prime, maximal. $R/I$ is a field iff $I$ is maximal (in commutative unital rings). Polynomial ideals in $F[x]$ are principal.

### This skill, specifically

Ideals are kernels of ring maps.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

In $F[x]$, every ideal is principal $(f)$, generated by any minimal-degree nonzero element (or $0$). The ideal $(f,g)=(d)$ where $d=\gcd(f,g)$. Vanishing ideals: polynomials vanishing at $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ (distinct) equal $( (x-a_1)\cdots(x-a_k) )$ in $F[x]$. Generators can be replaced by a gcd.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Simplify $(x^2-1,x^2+x)$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, and the ideal of polynomials vanishing at $0$ and $1$.

**Solution.** $\gcd(x^2-1,x^2+x)=\gcd(x^2-1,x(x+1))$. Euclidean: $(x^2+x)-(x^2-1)=x+1$, then $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$, gcd $x+1$. So $(x+1)$. Vanishing at $0,1$: $(x(x-1))$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(x,x+1)$ in $F[x]$ equals

**Solution.** $(1)=F[x]$, because $\gcd=1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $(f,g)=(fg)$ (that is the product of principal ideals only in special cases; generally $(f)\cap(g)=(lcm)$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $(2x,4)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$: principal?

**Solution.** No (similar to $(2,x)$ after scaling); contains $4$ and $2x$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
