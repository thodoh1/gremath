# Lesson M-638 — Subsequences

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Subsequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subsequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Sequences*

## 3. Precise statement

A subsequence $(a_{n_k})$ is obtained by a strictly increasing map $k\mapsto n_k\in\mathbb{N}$. If $a_n\to L$, every subsequence $\to L$. Conversely, if two subsequences have different limits, $(a_n)$ diverges. Every sequence has a monotone subsequence.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For $a_n=(-1)^n$, name two subsequences with different limits.

**Solution.** $a_{2k}\to 1$ and $a_{2k-1}\to -1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If every subsequence of $(a_n)$ converges, must $(a_n)$ converge?

**Solution.** Yes: all those limits coincide with $\lim a_n$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking a sub-subsequence and changing the claimed limit of the original sequence.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $a_n=n$ have a bounded subsequence?

**Solution.** No.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $a_{n_k}\to L$ along one subsequence, does $a_n\to L$?

**Solution.** Not necessarily.

## 9. Lock it in
