# Lesson M-354 — Changing integration order

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multiple integration** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Changing integration order** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Changing integration order**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multiple integration*

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map. $|\det D\Phi|$ is the volume distortion. Setup beats brute force.

### This skill, specifically

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Changing order: sketch the region, rewrite inequalities in the other order. The integrand may become easier (a nested integral that has no elementary inner antiderivative in one order can in the other).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Rewrite $\int_0^1\int_x^1 e^{y^2}\,dy\,dx$ (inner is non-elementary).

**Solution.** Region: $0\le x\le y\le 1$. So $\int_0^1\int_0^y e^{y^2}\,dx\,dy=\int_0^1 y e^{y^2}\,dy=\frac12(e-1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The classic $\int_0^1\int_y^1 \sin(x)/x\,dx\,dy$

**Solution.** Switch to $\int_0^1\int_0^x \sin x/x\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1\sin x\,dx=1-\cos 1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Switching limits independently as if the region were a rectangle.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Triangle under $y=x$ in first square: $dy\,dx$ vs $dx\,dy$.

**Solution.** $0\le x\le 1$, $0\le y\le x$ vs $0\le y\le 1$, $y\le x\le 1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why switch order?

**Solution.** To make the inner integral elementary or to match a density.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
