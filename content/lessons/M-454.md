# Lesson M-454 — Column space

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Column space** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Column space**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Vector spaces*

Axioms, subspaces, span, independence, basis, dimension, coordinates, the four fundamental subspaces, rank-nullity. This is the language that makes matrices mean something.

### This skill, specifically

Axioms, subspaces, span, independence, basis, dimension, coordinates, the four fundamental subspaces, rank-nullity.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The column space $\mathrm{Col}\,A$ is the span of the columns of $A$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$ if $A$ is $m\times n$. It equals $\{A\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n\}$. A basis is the pivot columns of the original $A$ (not of RREF). $\dim\mathrm{Col}\,A=\mathrm{rank}\,A$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Basis of $\mathrm{Col}\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&7\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** RREF has pivots in columns $1$ and $3$; original pivot columns $\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}3\\7\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is solvable iff $\mathbf{b}$ lies in

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Col}\,A$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking the pivot columns of the RREF as a column-space basis (those live in a different coordinate description; they span a space isomorphic but are not the original columns).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\mathrm{Col}\,I_n$ is

**Solution.** $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If two columns of $A$ are identical and there are no others, $\dim\mathrm{Col}\,A$ is

**Solution.** $1$ (if the column is nonzero).

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
