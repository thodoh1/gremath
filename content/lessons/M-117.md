# Lesson M-117 — Basic polar area formulas

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Parametric & polar basics** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Basic polar area formulas** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Basic polar area formulas**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Parametric & polar basics*

Parametric equations describe a curve by $x(t),y(t)$. Polar coordinates describe a point by radius and angle. Eliminating $t$ recovers a Cartesian relation when you need it; keeping $t$ is better for motion. Polar area uses $\frac12\int r^2\,d\theta$.

### This skill, specifically

Parametric equations describe a curve by $x(t),y(t)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Polar area: $A=\dfrac12\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} r(\theta)^2\,d\theta$ over the $\theta$-interval that traces the region once.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Area of $r=2$, $\theta$ from $0$ to $\pi/2$.

**Solution.** $\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}4\,d\theta=\pi$. A quarter disk of radius $2$ has area $\pi$, yes.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Area of one petal of $r=\sin(2\theta)$ uses $\theta$ from

**Solution.** $0$ to $\pi/2$ (where $r\ge 0$ for that petal).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the $1/2$ or integrating a petal twice.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Area inside $r=1+\cos\theta$ (cardioid).

**Solution.** $\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(1+2\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta)\,d\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why $r^2\,d\theta/2$?

**Solution.** A thin sector has area $\frac12 r^2\Delta\theta$.
