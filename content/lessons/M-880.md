# Lesson M-880 — Sample vs population

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.1-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Sample vs population** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Sample vs population**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A population is the probability law (or finite list) of interest; a sample is observed data drawn from it. Parameters ($\mu,\sigma,p,\ldots$) belong to the population; statistics ($\bar x,s,\hat p,\ldots$) are functions of the sample. Sampling variability means a statistic is a random variable before the sample is seen.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

**Problem.** In ‘heights of all current U.S. GRE examinees’, the sample mean of $40$ examinees is a

**Solution.** statistic; the mean height of all such examinees is a parameter.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\sigma$ a statistic?

**Solution.** No: it is a population parameter (unless computed from a listed finite population treated as the whole).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points
