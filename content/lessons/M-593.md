# Lesson M-593 — Prime ideals

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Ideals & quotient rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1`.


This lesson isolates **Prime ideals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Prime ideals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Ideals & quotient rings*

Ideals are kernels of ring maps. Principal, prime, maximal. $R/I$ is a field iff $I$ is maximal (in commutative unital rings). Polynomial ideals in $F[x]$ are principal.

### This skill, specifically

Ideals are kernels of ring maps.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A proper ideal $P$ is prime if $ab\in P\Rightarrow a\in P$ or $b\in P$ (commutative unital setting). Equivalent: $R/P$ is an integral domain. In $\mathbb{Z}$, prime ideals are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for $p$ prime. In $F[x]$, $(0)$ and $(f)$ for irreducible $f$. $(0)$ is prime iff $R$ is a domain.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $(4)$, $(5)$, $(0)$ are prime in $\mathbb{Z}$? Is $(x^2)$ prime in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$?

**Solution.** $(5)$ yes, $\mathbb{Z}/5$ a field. $(4)$ no: $2\cdot 2\in(4)$ but $2\notin(4)$. $(0)$ yes, $\mathbb{Z}$ a domain. $(x^2)$ no: $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2)$ has nilpotents, not a domain.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $R/P$ is a domain iff

**Solution.** $P$ is prime.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing prime ideals with prime elements when $R$ is not a domain, or thinking $(p^k)$ is prime.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $(x)$ prime in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$?

**Solution.** Yes: $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, a domain.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $(x,2)$ prime in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$?

**Solution.** Yes: quotient $\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, a field, so actually maximal.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
