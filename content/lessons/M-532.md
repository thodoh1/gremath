# Lesson M-532 — Primitive roots

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Additional elementary number theory** · Medium

Prerequisites: `L.2-L.3`.


This lesson isolates **Primitive roots** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Primitive roots**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A primitive root modulo $n$ is a generator of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$, i.e. an $a$ with $\mathrm{ord}_n(a)=\phi(n)$. Primitive roots exist iff $n=2,4,p^k,2p^k$ for odd prime $p$. There are $\phi(\phi(n))$ of them when they exist. To test: $a^{\phi(n)/q}\not\equiv 1\pmod{n}$ for every prime $q\mid\phi(n)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $3$ is a primitive root mod $7$. How many primitive roots mod $7$?

**Solution.** $\phi(7)=6=2\cdot 3$. $3^2=2\not\equiv 1$, $3^3=27\equiv 6\not\equiv 1\pmod{7}$. Order $6$. Number: $\phi(6)=2$, namely $3$ and $3^5\equiv 5\pmod{7}$ (the $k$ coprime to $6$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $8$ have a primitive root?

**Solution.** No: $(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is Klein, not cyclic.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Testing only $a^{n-1}\not\equiv 1$ (wrong exponent; need $\phi(n)$ and its prime divisors).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $2$ a primitive root mod $9$?

**Solution.** $\phi(9)=6$. $2^1=2$, $2^2=4$, $2^3=-1$, $2^6=1$, order $6$: yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Primitive roots mod $5$.

**Solution.** $2$ and $3$: $2,4,3,1$ and $3,4,2,1$.
