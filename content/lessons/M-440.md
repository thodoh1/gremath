# Lesson M-440 — Reduced row echelon form

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Systems & row reduction** · Critical

This lesson isolates **Reduced row echelon form** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 3. Precise statement

Reduced row echelon form (RREF): REF plus each pivot is $1$ and is the only nonzero entry in its column (zeros above and below). The RREF of a matrix is unique. It displays the general solution of $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ immediately: pivot variables in terms of free variables.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** RREF of $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&8\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1$ gives $(0,0,2)$; scale to $(0,0,1)$; then $R_1\leftarrow R_1-3R_2$. RREF $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** RREF of an invertible $n\times n$ matrix is

**Solution.** $I_n$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Stopping at REF and reading pivot variables as if zeros were already above the pivots.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** RREF of $\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&4\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Uniqueness of RREF means

**Solution.** any two sequences of row ops that fully reduce yield the same matrix.
