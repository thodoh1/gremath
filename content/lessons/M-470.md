# Lesson M-470 — Determinant row-operation rules

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Determinants** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2-K.4`.


This lesson isolates **Determinant row-operation rules** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Determinant row-operation rules**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Determinants*

Det is an alternating multilinear form, computable by cofactors or row ops. $\det(AB)=\det A\det B$, $\det A\neq 0$ iff invertible, geometry as signed volume, Cramer’s rule as a museum piece that still appears.

### This skill, specifically

Det is an alternating multilinear form, computable by cofactors or row ops.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Row-operation rules for determinants: swapping two rows multiplies $\det$ by $-1$; scaling a row by $k$ multiplies $\det$ by $k$; adding a multiple of one row to another leaves $\det$ unchanged. Hence $\det(EA)=(\det E)\det A$ for elementary $E$. Identical rules hold for columns.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\det\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\2&3\end{pmatrix}$ after swapping rows, compared to $\det\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** Swap: first det $=0\cdot 3-1\cdot 2=-2$. Second det $=2$. They differ by a sign.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
