# Lesson M-324 — Uniform convergence intuition

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Uniform convergence basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.3`.


This lesson isolates **Uniform convergence intuition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Uniform convergence intuition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Uniform convergence basics*

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not. Weierstrass M-test is a comparison test for functions. This is GRE awareness: enough to not interchange limit and integral blindly.

### This skill, specifically

Pointwise convergence can lose continuity; uniform convergence does not.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Intuition: uniform means a single $N(\varepsilon)$ works for all $x$ in the set. Graphs of $f_n$ sit in an $\varepsilon$-tube around $f$. A moving bump of height $1$ and width $1/n$ converges pointwise to $0$ but not uniformly.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** The bump $f_n$ of height $1$ on $[n,n+1]$. Pointwise vs uniform on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** Pointwise $0$ (each $x$ is eventually outside the bump); $\sup=1$, not uniform.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Weierstrass: if $|f_n|\le M_n$ and $\sum M_n<\infty$

**Solution.** then $\sum f_n$ converges uniformly (M-test).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking “fast pointwise” is automatically uniform.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $f_n(x)=e^{-(x-n)^2}$ converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$ to $0$?

**Solution.** No, $\sup=1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** On a compact set, $C^0$ plus pointwise plus monotone ($Dini$) can give

**Solution.** uniform convergence.
