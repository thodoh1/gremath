# Lesson M-1019 — Manage 170-minute / ~66-question pacing

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Speed & multiple-choice technique** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Manage 170-minute / ~66-question pacing** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Manage 170-minute / ~66-question pacing**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Speed & multiple-choice technique*

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

### This skill, specifically

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The GRE Mathematics Test is $170$ minutes for about $66$ questions, roughly $2.5$ minutes each. Budget: many items are $60$–$90$ seconds (definitions, $2\times 2$ determinants, standard limits); a few are $4$–$6$ minutes. If three minutes have produced no classification, mark and move. A raw score is number correct (no guessing penalty), so never leave a blank. Practice with a visible clock in $10$-question blocks of $25$ minutes.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** You are at question $8$, a contour integral that has already taken $4$ minutes, and you have $58$ items left with $155$ minutes. What is the craft move?

**Solution.** Mark it, guess if needed later, and spend the next two minutes on a rank-nullity one-liner. $155/58\approx 2.7$ min/item remains — you cannot afford another $6$ minutes here yet.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** No penalty for wrong answers means

**Solution.** never leave a blank; eliminate and guess.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Spending $12$ minutes on an early hard item because ‘it should be doable’, then rushing $20$ easy later items.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A $2\times 2$ determinant and a residue-at-infinity item: which first in a timed setting?

**Solution.** The determinant (often $30$ seconds); flag the residue if it does not open in one minute.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Target average if $10$ items will be slow ($5$ min each) in a $66$-item / $170$-min test.

**Solution.** The other $56$ must average about $2.1$ min.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
