# Lesson M-981 — Finite-difference derivatives

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Interpolation & approximation** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Finite-difference derivatives** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

## 3. Precise statement

Forward difference $f'(x)\approx \dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ has truncation $O(h)$ and roundoff $O(\varepsilon/h)$; the total error is minimized at $h\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. Central difference $\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}$ is $O(h^2)$ truncation. Second derivative: $\dfrac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}=f''(x)+O(h^2)$. Always track both truncation and roundoff.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Central difference of $f(x)=x^2$ at $x=1$ with $h=0.1$.

**Solution.** $((1.1)^2-(0.9)^2)/0.2=(1.21-0.81)/0.2=2$, exact ($f''$ constant, higher even terms vanish).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Forward difference of $e^x$ at $0$ with $h=0.1$: $(e^{0.1}-1)/0.1\approx$

**Solution.** $1.0517$, while $f'(0)=1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Taking $h=10^{-16}$ in double precision to ‘get more accuracy’ (subtractive cancellation dominates).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
