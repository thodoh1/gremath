# Lesson M-741 — Total orders

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Total orders** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Total orders**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A total (linear) order is a partial order in which every two elements are comparable: $a\le b$ or $b\le a$. $(\mathbb{R},\le)$ and $(\mathbb{N},\le)$ are total. Divisibility on $\mathbb{N}$ is not. Lexicographic order on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is total. A well-order is a total order in which every nonempty subset has a least element.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is lexicographic order on $\{1,2\}\times\{1,2\}$ total? Name the least and greatest pairs.

**Solution.** Yes. Least $(1,1)$, greatest $(2,2)$, with $(1,2)<(2,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(\mathbb{N},\mid)$ is total?

**Solution.** No.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling any partial order total, or confusing well-order with total order ($\mathbb{Z}$ is total not well-ordered).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Usual order on $\mathbb{Z}$: total? well-order?

**Solution.** Total yes; well-order no.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Dictionary order on words of a fixed length over an alphabet is

**Solution.** a total order.
