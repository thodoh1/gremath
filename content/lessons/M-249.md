# Lesson M-249 — Mean Value Theorem

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Applications of derivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-E.4`.


This lesson isolates **Mean Value Theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Mean Value Theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

MVT: if $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then some $c\in(a,b)$ has $f'(c)=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$. Mean slope is a tangent slope. Hypotheses matter: $|x|$ on $[-1,1]$ fails differentiability at $0$ and MVT fails for the two-sided slope $0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Apply MVT to $f(x)=x^2$ on $[1,3]$.

**Solution.** $f'(c)=2c=(9-1)/2=4$, $c=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Why MVT implies $f'=0\Rightarrow f$ constant on an interval

**Solution.** For $x_1<x_2$, $f(x_2)-f(x_1)=f'(c)(x_2-x_1)=0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using MVT when $f$ is not differentiable on the whole open interval.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does there exist $c\in(0,1)$ with $e^c=e-1$?

**Solution.** MVT on $e^x$ on $[0,1]$: $e^c=e-1$, yes $c=\ln(e-1)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $f(x)=x^{1/3}$ on $[-1,1]$: MVT?

**Solution.** Not in the finite-derivative sense: $f'(0)$ infinite. Average slope $0$, no finite $c$ with $f'(c)=0$.
