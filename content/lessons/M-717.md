# Lesson M-717 — Proof by contrapositive

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Proof by contrapositive** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Proof by contrapositive**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A proof by contrapositive establishes $P\to Q$ by proving $\neg Q\to\neg P$. Use it when the negated conclusion is easier to manipulate (parity, non-membership, failure of an inequality). It is not a proof of the converse.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove: if $n^2$ is even then $n$ is even, by contrapositive.

**Solution.** Assume $n$ odd, $n=2k+1$. Then $n^2=4k^2+4k+1$ odd. So $n$ odd $\Rightarrow$ $n^2$ odd.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Contrapositive of ``if $f'(a)$ exists then $f$ is continuous at $a$''.

**Solution.** If $f$ is discontinuous at $a$, then $f'(a)$ does not exist.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Negating incorrectly (``$n$ not even'' written as ``$n$ negative'') or proving the converse instead.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Why is this better than contradiction for the even-square lemma?

**Solution.** The odd case is an explicit computation; no extra false assumption to discharge.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Prove: if $A\cap B=\emptyset$ then $A\subset B^c$, by contrapositive.

**Solution.** If $A\not\subset B^c$, some $x\in A\cap B$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
