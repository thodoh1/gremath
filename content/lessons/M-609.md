# Lesson M-609 — Quotient modules

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Modules** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1`.


This lesson isolates **Quotient modules** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Quotient modules**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Modules*

Modules are vector spaces over rings. Submodules, quotients, homomorphisms, generators, free modules. When the ring is a field, you recover linear algebra.

### This skill, specifically

Modules are vector spaces over rings.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $N\subseteq M$ is a submodule, the quotient module $M/N$ is the abelian group of cosets with $r(m+N)=rm+N$. Then $M/N=0$ iff $N=M$. Examples: $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$; $R/I$ as an $R$-module; $F^n/W$ for a subspace $W$. Correspondence: submodules of $M/N$ biject with submodules of $M$ containing $N$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Identify $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ as a quotient of the $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Z}$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/8)/(\,4\cdot\mathbb{Z}/8\,)$.

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. In $\mathbb{Z}/8$, the submodule generated by $4$ is $\{0,4\}$, quotient order $4$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $M/N$ is cyclic if $M$ is cyclic.

**Solution.** Yes: the image of a generator generates.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Defining $r(m+N)=rm+N$ when $N$ is only a subgroup not absorbing $R$ (not well-defined).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Quotient of $F^2$ by a $1$-dimensional subspace, as $F$-modules.

**Solution.** One-dimensional, $\cong F$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
