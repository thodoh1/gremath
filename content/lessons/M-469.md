# Lesson M-469 — Cofactor expansion

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Determinants** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2-K.4`.


This lesson isolates **Cofactor expansion** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cofactor expansion**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Determinants*

Det is an alternating multilinear form, computable by cofactors or row ops. $\det(AB)=\det A\det B$, $\det A\neq 0$ iff invertible, geometry as signed volume, Cramer’s rule as a museum piece that still appears.

### This skill, specifically

Det is an alternating multilinear form, computable by cofactors or row ops.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Cofactor $C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}$ where $M_{ij}$ is the minor (delete row $i$, column $j$). Expansion along row $i$: $\det A=\sum_j a_{ij}C_{ij}$ (any row or column). Prefer a row/column with many zeros. For triangular matrices this recovers the diagonal product.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Expand $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&4&5\\0&0&6\end{pmatrix}$ along the first column.

**Solution.** $1\cdot C_{11}=1\cdot\det\begin{pmatrix}4&5\\0&6\end{pmatrix}=24$. Other first-column cofactors multiply $0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sign of the $(2,1)$-cofactor is

**Solution.** $(-1)^{3}=-1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the checkerboard sign $(-1)^{i+j}$, or expanding along a dense row when a sparse one exists.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\det\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\0&3&0\\4&5&7\end{pmatrix}$ along column $3$.

**Solution.** $7\cdot C_{33}=7\cdot 6=42$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A $3\times 3$ expansion produces how many $2\times 2$ determinants in general?

**Solution.** $3$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
