# Lesson M-867 — Transforming random variables

Part T. T. Probability · **Generating/transform methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.4-T.6; H.4`.


This lesson isolates **Transforming random variables** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Transforming random variables**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Generating/transform methods*

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases. Transformations: CDF method, Jacobian for densities.

### This skill, specifically

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Transforming one random variable: if $Y=g(X)$ with $g$ strictly monotone and $C^1$, the PDF is $f_Y(y)=f_X(x)\bigl/|g'(x)|\bigr|$ at $x=g^{-1}(y)$. Discrete: just push masses, $P(Y=y)=\sum_{g(x)=y}p_X(x)$. Linear $Y=aX+b$ with $a\neq 0$: $f_Y(y)=f_X((y-b)/a)/|a|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$, $Y=-2\ln X$. PDF of $Y$ on $(0,\infty)$.

**Solution.** $x=e^{-y/2}$, $dx/dy=-e^{-y/2}/2$, $f_Y(y)=1\cdot e^{-y/2}/2$, i.e. Gamma/Erlang: actually $\frac12 e^{-y/2}=\mathrm{Exp}(\text{rate }1/2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $Y=aX+b$ ($a\neq 0$). Law of $Y$.

**Solution.** $N(a\mu+b,a^2\sigma^2)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dropping the absolute value $|g'|$, or using $f_Y(y)=f_X(y) |g'(y)|$ with $g$ not inverted.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $X$ has PDF $2x$ on $(0,1)$, $Y=X^2$. For $y\in(0,1)$, $f_Y(y)$.

**Solution.** $x=\sqrt{y}$, $dy/dx=2x$, $f_Y(y)=2\sqrt{y}/(2\sqrt{y})=1$ on $(0,1)$: $Y\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $X$ discrete, $P(X=-1)=P(X=1)=1/2$, $Y=X^2$. Law of $Y$.

**Solution.** $Y=1$ a.s.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
