# Lesson M-870 — Median

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Descriptive statistics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.2`.


This lesson isolates **Median** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Median**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

Mean, median, mode, variance, sd, covariance, correlation, moments, quantiles, percentiles, skewness — as numbers computed from a sample or a distribution.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The median of an ordered sample $x_{(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{(n)}$ is $x_{((n+1)/2)}$ if $n$ is odd, and any value in $\bigl[x_{(n/2)},x_{(n/2+1)}\bigr]$ (conventionally the average of those two) if $n$ is even. It minimizes $\sum|x_i-a|$ in $a$. Unlike the mean, it is unchanged by moving an extreme observation farther out.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Median of $1,2,100$ and of $1,2,3,100$.

**Solution.** $2$; $(2+3)/2=2.5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** In $1,3,3,7$ the median is

**Solution.** $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Sorting after dropping an outlier that the problem did not authorize, or using the mean of all four when $n$ is even without using the two central order statistics.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Median of $4,1,7,2,9$.

**Solution.** $4$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does the median of $0,0,0,10$ equal the mean?

**Solution.** No: median $0$, mean $2.5$.
