# Lesson M-559 — Coset equivalence

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Cosets & Lagrange** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-M.2`.


This lesson isolates **Coset equivalence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Coset equivalence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Cosets & Lagrange*

Left/right cosets partition $G$. $|G|=|H|\,[G:H]$. Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Consequences: orders of elements divide $|G|$; groups of prime order are cyclic.

### This skill, specifically

Left/right cosets partition $G$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Coset equality: $aH=bH\Leftrightarrow a^{-1}b\in H\Leftrightarrow b^{-1}a\in H$. Equivalence relation $a\sim b$ iff $a^{-1}b\in H$ (left). The equivalence classes are the left cosets. You never ‘add representatives’ without a normality guarantee. Distinct representatives can label the same coset.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** In $\mathbb{Z}$, when is $a+6\mathbb{Z}=b+6\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** Iff $6\mid(a-b)$. So $17+6\mathbb{Z}=5+6\mathbb{Z}$ because $12$ is a multiple of $6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $aH=H$ iff

**Solution.** $a\in H$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Canceling $aH=bH$ to $a=b$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** In $S_3$, is $(1\,2\,3)H=(1\,3\,2)H$ for $H=A_3$?

**Solution.** Yes: both $3$-cycles lie in $A_3$, so both cosets equal $H$ itself.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $aH=bH$ and $H$ is finite, then

**Solution.** $a$ and $b$ differ by an element of $H$; $|aH|=|bH|=|H|$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
