# Lesson M-456 — Null space

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Null space** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Null space**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

## 3. Precise statement

The null space $\mathrm{Nul}\,A=\{\mathbf{x}:A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}\}$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Find it by row-reducing $A$ and writing the parametric form of the homogeneous solution. A basis is obtained from the free variables (one special solution per free variable). $\dim\mathrm{Nul}\,A$ is the nullity.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Basis of $\mathrm{Nul}\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&6\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $x+2y+3z=0$. Free $y=s$, $z=t$: $\mathbf{x}=s(-2,1,0)+t(-3,0,1)$. Basis $\{(-2,1,0),(-3,0,1)\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathrm{Nul}\,I_n$ is

**Solution.** $\{\mathbf{0}\}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting a basis of $\mathrm{Col}\,A$ when asked for $\mathrm{Nul}\,A$, or forgetting that null-space vectors have $n$ components.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

**Solution.** $0$.
