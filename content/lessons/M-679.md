# Lesson M-679 — Cauchy sequences

Part P. P. Topology · **Metric/topological basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `O.1-O.4`.


This lesson isolates **Cauchy sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cauchy sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Metric/topological basics*

A metric measures distance. Open balls generate open sets. Closed sets contain their limits. Interior, closure, boundary, limit points. Completeness: Cauchy sequences converge.

### This skill, specifically

A metric measures distance.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A sequence is Cauchy in a metric space if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ with $m,n\ge N\Rightarrow d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon$. Convergent $\Rightarrow$ Cauchy always. The converse is the definition of completeness. Cauchy sequences are bounded. $d(x_{n+1},x_n)\to 0$ is not sufficient.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $x_n=1/n$ Cauchy in $(0,1)$? Does it converge in $(0,1)$?

**Solution.** Yes Cauchy (same as in $\mathbb{R}$). No: $0\notin(0,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Every convergent sequence is Cauchy: true or false?

**Solution.** True, in any metric space.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking only consecutive terms $d(x_{n+1},x_n)\to 0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is a Cauchy sequence in a discrete space eventually constant?

**Solution.** Yes: take $\varepsilon=1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Cauchy sequences are bounded: proof idea.

**Solution.** Fix $\varepsilon=1$, finitely many initial terms, plus a ball of radius $1$ about $x_N$.
