# Lesson M-974 — Newton method

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Root finding** · Medium

Prerequisites: `E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Newton method** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Newton method**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

Bisection (slow, safe), Newton (fast, derivative, local), secant, fixed-point $x=g(x)$, convergence rates as ideas.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Newton: $x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$ when $f'(x_n)\neq 0$. It is the root of the tangent line at $x_n$. If $f(r)=0$, $f'(r)\neq 0$, and $f''$ is continuous, then for $x_0$ close enough one has quadratic convergence: $e_{n+1}\approx \dfrac{f''(r)}{2f'(r)} e_n^2$. Multiple roots drop the order to linear. Division by small $f'$ is unstable.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** One Newton step for $f(x)=x^2-2$ from $x_0=1$.

**Solution.** $x_1=1-(1-2)/2=1.5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Newton for $1/x-a=0$ (reciprocal) is

**Solution.** $x_{n+1}=x_n(2-a x_n)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Continuing Newton when $f'(x_n)=0$, or assuming quadratic convergence at a double root.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
