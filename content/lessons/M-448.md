# Lesson M-448 — Subspaces

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Subspaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subspaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A subspace $W\subseteq V$ is a subset that is itself a vector space under the same operations. Test: $W$ nonempty (or contains $\mathbf{0}$) and closed under addition and scalar multiplication. Equivalently, closed under linear combinations. Lines through the origin, $\mathrm{Nul}\,A$, $\mathrm{Col}\,A$, $P_n\subset P_{n+1}$ are subspaces.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\{(x,y):y=2x\}$ a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$? Is $\{(x,y):xy=0\}$?

**Solution.** Yes (a line through $\mathbf{0}$). No: $(1,0)+(0,1)=(1,1)$ has $xy\neq 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\{\mathbf{0}\}$ a subspace?

**Solution.** Yes, the trivial subspace.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Accepting an affine line not through $\mathbf{0}$ as a subspace, or the unit circle.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Solution set of $x+2y+3z=0$: subspace?

**Solution.** Yes, a plane through the origin.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $x+2y+3z=6$: subspace?

**Solution.** No: $\mathbf{0}$ is not a solution.
