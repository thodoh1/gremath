# Lesson M-698 — Continuous image of connected space

Part P. P. Topology · **Connectedness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **Continuous image of connected space** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Continuous image of connected space**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Connectedness*

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens. Path-connected implies connected. Continuous images preserve connectedness. Connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are intervals.

### This skill, specifically

A space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A continuous image of a connected space is connected. This is the topological form of the IVT: $f([a,b])$ is connected in $\mathbb{R}$, hence an interval, hence contains all intermediate values. There is no continuous surjection from a connected space onto a discrete two-point space.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is there no continuous $f:[0,1]\to\{0,1\}$ onto, with $\{0,1\}$ discrete?

**Solution.** The domain is connected, the codomain is not, and a continuous image would be connected.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Continuous image of $\mathbb{R}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ is

**Solution.** an interval (possibly unbounded, possibly a point).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming the image of a disconnected space is disconnected (constant maps).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Projection of the connected set $\{(x,y):xy=1,x>0\}$ onto the $x$-axis.

**Solution.** $(0,\infty)$, connected.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $X$ is connected and $f$ is continuous, can $f(X)$ be a single point?

**Solution.** Yes: constant functions.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
