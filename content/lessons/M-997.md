# Lesson M-997 — Solve parameterized inequalities

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Parameter problems** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.4-E.5-K.7-G.2`.


This lesson isolates **Solve parameterized inequalities** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Solve parameterized inequalities**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Parameter problems*

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter. Degenerate cases (zero leading coefficient, repeated roots) are often the point.

### This skill, specifically

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Parameterized inequalities: move all terms, factor, sign-chart in $x$ with $t$ as a labeled root, or complete the square. For quadratics $ax^2+bx+c>0$ for all $x$, need $a>0$ and $\Delta<0$ (or $\Delta=0$ for $\ge$). For $|x-t|<r$ read an interval. Never multiply an inequality by an expression whose sign depends on the parameter without splitting cases.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For which $t$ is $x^2-2tx+1\ge 0$ for all real $x$?

**Solution.** $a=1>0$ and $\Delta=4t^2-4\le 0$, so $|t|\le 1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Solve $(x-t)(x-1)<0$ for $x$, in terms of $t>1$.

**Solution.** $x$ between $1$ and $t$, i.e. $1<x<t$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Multiplying by $t-x$ without cases, or using $\Delta>0$ when you needed the quadratic always positive.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $e^x>t$ for all real $x$. For which $t$?

**Solution.** Iff $t<0$ (range of $e^x$ is $(0,\infty)$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $|x-3|<t$ has solutions iff

**Solution.** $t>0$ (then $(3-t,3+t)$).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
