# Lesson M-163 — Midpoint formula

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Coordinate geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Midpoint formula** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Midpoint formula**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coordinate geometry*

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra. Tangency is discriminant zero. 3D distance is the same Pythagorean idea with one more square.

## 3. Precise statement

Midpoint $\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$. Section formula for a ratio $m:n$ is the weighted average.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Midpoint of $(2,3)$ and $(8,-1)$.

**Solution.** $(5,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Point one-third of the way from $A$ to $B$ is

**Solution.** $\frac{2}{3}A+\frac{1}{3}B$ wait: from $A$ to $B$, one-third is $\frac{2}{3}A+\frac{1}{3}B$? No: $(2A+B)/3$ is closer to $A$. One-third from $A$ to $B$ is $\frac{2}{3}A+\frac{1}{3}B$. Yes.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Averaging coordinates but subtracting instead of adding.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If midpoint of $(x,2)$ and $(4,6)$ is $(1,4)$, find $x$.

**Solution.** $(x+4)/2=1$, $x=-2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so midpoints match.

**Solution.** Vector characterization.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
