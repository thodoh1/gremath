# Lesson M-605 — Unique factorization domains

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Polynomial rings & factorization** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1-N.2`.


This lesson isolates **Unique factorization domains** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Unique factorization domains**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Polynomial rings & factorization*

$F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and UFD. Irreducibility tests, rational root theorem, division. CRT for rings: $R/(I\cap J)\cong R/I\times R/J$ when $I+J=R$.

### This skill, specifically

$F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and UFD.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A UFD is a domain where every nonunit nonzero element factors into irreducibles uniquely up to order and units. PID $\Rightarrow$ UFD, not conversely: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a UFD, not a PID. In a UFD, irreducible $\Leftrightarrow$ prime. $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is not a UFD: $6=2\cdot 3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$ with two essentially different irreducible factorizations. Atomic domains need not be UFDs.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show the two factorizations of $6$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ are genuinely distinct, using the norm $N(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2$.

**Solution.** $N(2)=4$, $N(3)=9$, $N(1\pm\sqrt{-5})=6$. Units have norm $1$, so $\pm 1$. None of $2,3,1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ are associates (norms differ or $2$ does not divide $1+\sqrt{-5}$). All four are irreducible because their norms are not composite in the image of $N$ as $N(z)N(w)$ with both $>1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

**Drill 1.** Is $F[x,y]$ a UFD?

**Solution.** Yes ($F[x]$ UFD $\Rightarrow F[x][y]$ UFD).
