# Lesson M-338 — Tangent planes

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Differentiability** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-K.4`.


This lesson isolates **Tangent planes** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Tangent planes**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Differentiability*

Existence of partials is not differentiability. The derivative is a linear map (Jacobian). Directional derivatives are $\nabla f\cdot u$. The chain rule in several variables is matrix multiplication of Jacobians.

### This skill, specifically

Existence of partials is not differentiability.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The tangent plane to $z=f(x,y)$ at $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ is $z=z_0+f_x(x_0,y_0)(x-x_0)+f_y(x_0,y_0)(y-y_0)$. For a level surface $F(x,y,z)=c$, the tangent plane is $\nabla F\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0)=0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Tangent plane to $z=x^2+y^2$ at $(1,1,2)$.

**Solution.** $z=2+2(x-1)+2(y-1)$, i.e. $z=2x+2y-2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=9$ at $(2,2,1)$

**Solution.** $2x+2y+z=9$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\nabla f$ of the graph function as if it were a level-surface gradient in $\mathbb{R}^3$ without converting $F=z-f(x,y)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Plane for $z=xy$ at $(2,3,6)$.

**Solution.** $z=3(x-2)+2(y-3)+6=3x+2y-6$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
