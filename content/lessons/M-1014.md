## 3. Precise statement

Invariance and dimension checks: a cubic polynomial $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ has at least one real root (odd degree). A linear map $\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^n$ is injective iff surjective. Homogeneity: if every term in a proposed identity has a different degree, the identity is false unless both sides are $0$. Units/dimensions: an area formula cannot equal a length. Trace, det, rank, and degree are cheap filters on choices.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Can $x^3+x+1=0$ have no real root?

**Solution.** No: odd-degree real polynomial, $f\to\pm\infty$ at $\pm\infty$, IVT.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A proposed identity $(u\times v)\cdot w=\|u\|\|v\|\|w\|$: degrees/grades?

**Solution.** Left side is a scalar triple product $\le$ product of norms, equality only in a very special case; as an identity it is false (take orthonormal $\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}$: left $1$, right $1$, accidentally holds, but $u=v=w=\mathbf{i}$ gives $0\neq 1$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Accepting a ‘volume’ choice that is homogeneous of degree $2$ in the side length of a cube.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Linear $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\ker T$ of dimension $1$: $\dim\mathrm{im}\,T=$

**Solution.** $2$ (rank-nullity).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Choices for $\int_0^1(x^2+x)\,dx$: $5/6$ vs $5/3$. Degree/size: the integrand is $\le 2$, integral $\le 2$, but more tightly $\le 1\cdot 2=2$; $5/3>1$ while the average of $x^2+x$ on $[0,1]$ is about $0.8$, so

**Solution.** $5/6$.
