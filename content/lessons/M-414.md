# Lesson M-414 — Shifting/step-function idea

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Transforms & expansions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `J.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Shifting/step-function idea** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Shifting/step-function idea**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Transforms & expansions*

Laplace transforms turn ODEs into algebra. Fourier series expand periodic functions in sines and cosines. Separation of variables is the PDE cameo.

### This skill, specifically

Laplace transforms turn ODEs into algebra.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

First shifting: $\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f(t)\}=F(s-a)$. Second (step) shifting: if $u_c(t)$ is the Heaviside function jumping at $c>0$, then $\mathcal{L}\{u_c(t)f(t-c)\}=e^{-cs}F(s)$. Step functions encode delayed forcing.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\mathcal{L}\{e^{2t}\sin t\}$.

**Solution.** $F(s)=1/(s^2+1)$ for $\sin t$, so $1/((s-2)^2+1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathcal{L}\{u_3(t)\}$ equals

**Solution.** $e^{-3s}/s$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $F(s+a)$ instead of $F(s-a)$ for the $e^{at}$ multiplier, or forgetting to shift the time argument of $f$ in the second theorem.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\mathcal{L}\{e^{-t}t\}$.

**Solution.** $1/(s+1)^2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Inverse of $e^{-2s}/s^2$.

**Solution.** $u_2(t)\,(t-2)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
