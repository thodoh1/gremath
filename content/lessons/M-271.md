# Lesson M-271 — Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part I

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `I F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part I** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part I**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

FTC Part I: if $f$ is continuous on an interval containing $a$, then $F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ is an antiderivative: $F'(x)=f(x)$. More generally $\frac{d}{dx}\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f=f(v)v'-f(u)u'$ (Leibniz rule).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Differentiate $\int_0^{x^2}\sin t\,dt$.

**Solution.** $\sin(x^2)\cdot 2x$ by FTC I and chain rule.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\dfrac{d}{dx}\int_x^{2x} e^{-t^2}\,dt$

**Solution.** $2e^{-4x^2}-e^{-x^2}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Differentiating under the integral sign and also substituting the bound into $f$ as if it were $F$ not $F'$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $F(x)=\int_1^x \frac{1}{t}\,dt$, $F'(e)$.

**Solution.** $1/e$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why the dummy variable $t$ must not be $x$.

**Solution.** The upper limit is the variable of $F$; the integrand’s name is bound.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
