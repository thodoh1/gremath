# Lesson M-982 — Basic least-squares approximation

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Interpolation & approximation** · Medium

## 1. Why this skill exists

## 3. Precise statement

Given overdetermined $(x_i,y_i)$, $i=1,\ldots,m$, the best polynomial $p$ of degree $\le n<m-1$ in the $\ell^2$ sense minimizes $\sum(y_i-p(x_i))^2$. For degree $1$ this is the LS line. In matrix form, $A\mathbf{c}\approx\mathbf{y}$ with Vandermonde-like $A$, solved by the normal equations $A^\top A\mathbf{c}=A^\top\mathbf{y}$ (or better, QR). This is approximation, not interpolation: residuals need not vanish.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Best constant (degree $0$) LS fit to $y_1,\ldots,y_m$.

**Solution.** The mean $\bar y$ (minimizer of $\sum(y_i-c)^2$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
