# Lesson M-172 — 2D coordinate arguments

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Coordinate geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **2D coordinate arguments** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **2D coordinate arguments**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coordinate geometry*

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra. Tangency is discriminant zero. 3D distance is the same Pythagorean idea with one more square.

### This skill, specifically

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A 2D coordinate argument proves a geometric claim by placing the figure conveniently: put a vertex at the origin, a side on an axis, use vectors or distances. Generality is preserved by the placement if it uses remaining symmetries.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove the midpoint of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is equidistant from all three vertices, by coordinates.

**Solution.** Place right angle at $(0,0)$, legs along axes to $(2a,0)$ and $(0,2b)$. Midpoint of hypotenuse $(a,b)$. Distances: to origin $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, to $(2a,0)$ $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, to $(0,2b)$ the same.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Centroid of a triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(0,2)$.

**Solution.** $\bigl(\frac23,\frac23\bigr)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Placing a general triangle with no remaining freedom and then claiming a special property that depended on the placement.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Show $AB=AC$ for $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,2)$, $C=(-1,2)$ by distance.

**Solution.** Both $\sqrt{5}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Centroid coordinates are the averages of vertices.

**Solution.** Yes.
