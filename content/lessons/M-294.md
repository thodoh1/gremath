# Lesson M-294 — Subsequences

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Sequences** · Critical

## 3. Precise statement

A subsequence $(a_{n_k})$ is $a_{n_1},a_{n_2},\ldots$ with $n_k$ strictly increasing. If $a_n\to L$ then every subsequence $\to L$. Conversely, if every subsequence has a further subsequence $\to L$, then $a_n\to L$. Subsequential limits are limits of convergent subsequences.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Subsequential limits of $a_n=(-1)^n$.

**Solution.** $\{-1,1\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Picking a subsequence that ignores the terms that break a hoped-for limit and claiming the original converges.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $a_n=\sin(n\pi/2)$ have a subsequence $\to 0$?

**Solution.** Yes: $n=2k$. Also subsequences $\to\pm 1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** The set of subsequential limits of a convergent sequence is

**Solution.** a singleton.
