# Lesson M-242 — Curve sketching

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Applications of derivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-E.4`.


This lesson isolates **Curve sketching** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Curve sketching**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Applications of derivatives*

Critical points, first and second derivative tests, concavity, sketching, optimization, related rates, parametric derivatives, linear approximation, MVT and Rolle: this is why derivatives exist on the GRE. The skill is translating a word or a graph into $f'=0$ plus endpoint checks.

## 3. Precise statement

Curve sketching combines: domain, intercepts, asymptotes (limits), first-derivative chart (inc/dec, local extrema), second-derivative chart (concavity, inflections), and a consistent picture. The GRE often asks only one of these layers.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Sketch features of $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x-1}$.

**Solution.** Vertical asymptote $x=1$, horizontal $y=1$, intercept $(0,0)$, $f'=-1/(x-1)^2<0$ always decreasing on each component.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** End behavior of a cubic with positive leading coefficient

**Solution.** $+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$, $-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Drawing a graph that crosses a vertical asymptote.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $e^{-x^2}$ have horizontal asymptotes?

**Solution.** $y=0$ both sides.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A rational function of equal degree has horizontal asymptote

**Solution.** the ratio of leading coefficients.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
