# Lesson M-1022 — Solve unfamiliar mixed-topic problems

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Solve unfamiliar mixed-topic problems** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Solve unfamiliar mixed-topic problems**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Unfamiliar mixed-topic items join two chapters. Typical GRE hybrids: (geometry $+$ calculus) max area; (linear algebra $+$ ODE) $y'=Ay$; (complex $+$ geometry) $|e^z|=1$; (probability $+$ combinatorics) already one language; (series $+$ DE) power-series solutions. Classify both halves before computing either. The computation is usually standard once the join is named.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For which real $z$ is $|e^z|=1$? Mixed complex $+$ modulus.

**Solution.** $|e^{x+iy}|=e^x=1$, so $x=0$: the imaginary axis. Geometry of a complex equation.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Max of $xy$ on $x^2/4+y^2/9=1$. Mixed calc $+$ conic: parametrize $x=2\cos t$, $y=3\sin t$, or Lagrange. $xy=$

**Solution.** $3\sin(2t)$, max $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Computing only one half (expanding $e^z$ in series) and never taking moduli.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $y'=Ay$ with $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $y(0)=(1,0)$. Solution?

**Solution.** $(\cos t,\sin t)$ (rotation ODE).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Expected area of a random triangle? If too vague, a well-posed mixed item is: expected number of fixed points of a random permutation is

**Solution.** $1$ by linearity of indicators — probability $+$ counting.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
