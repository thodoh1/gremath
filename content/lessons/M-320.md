# Lesson M-320 — Geometric-series expansion

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Power/Taylor series** · Critical

Prerequisites: `G.2-E.5`.


This lesson isolates **Geometric-series expansion** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Geometric-series expansion**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Power/Taylor series*

Power series have a radius of convergence. Inside the radius you may differentiate and integrate termwise. Taylor series of $e^x,\sin x,\cos x,1/(1-x)$ and the binomial series should be writable in your sleep.

### This skill, specifically

Power series have a radius of convergence.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Geometric expansion is the engine: $\frac{1}{1-u}=\sum u^n$ for $|u|<1$. Substitute $u=$ a polynomial or multiple of $x$ to expand rationals. Differentiate/integrate the geometric series to get $\frac{1}{(1-x)^2}$ and $-\ln(1-x)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Expand $\frac{1}{1+x}$ about $0$.

**Solution.** $\sum (-1)^n x^n$, $|x|<1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\frac{1}{1-2x}$

**Solution.** $\sum 2^n x^n$, $|x|<1/2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Substituting $u=x+x^2$ and ignoring that $|u|<1$ is a smaller $x$-interval.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Expand $x/(1-x)$.

**Solution.** $\sum_{n=1}^\infty x^n$ for $|x|<1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
