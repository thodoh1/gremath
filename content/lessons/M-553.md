# Lesson M-553 — Parity of permutations

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Permutation groups** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Parity of permutations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Parity of permutations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Permutation groups*

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters. Cycle decomposition, disjoint cycles commute, transpositions generate, sign homomorphism, $A_n=\ker(\mathrm{sign})$.

### This skill, specifically

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A permutation is even if it is a product of an even number of transpositions, odd otherwise. The sign $\mathrm{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm 1\}$ is a homomorphism, kernel $A_n$. A $k$-cycle is even iff $k$ is odd (because it is $k-1$ transpositions). Sign is well-defined: you cannot write an even permutation as an odd product of transpositions.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $(1\,2\,3)$ even? Is $(1\,2)(3\,4\,5)$ even?

**Solution.** $(1\,2\,3)$ is a $3$-cycle, even. $(1\,2)$ odd times a $3$-cycle even is odd.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sign of an $n$-cycle in $S_n$

**Solution.** $(-1)^{n-1}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating every cycle as odd, or using ‘number of cycles’ instead of ‘number of transpositions’.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Parity of $(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)(6\,7)$.

**Solution.** Even $5$-cycle times odd transposition: odd.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can a product of $3$ transpositions equal a $3$-cycle?

**Solution.** Yes in length, but a $3$-cycle is even, $3$ transpositions odd: not equal. (A $3$-cycle is $2$ transpositions.)

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
