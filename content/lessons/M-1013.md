## 3. Precise statement

Estimate to kill choices: a probability is in $[0,1]$; an area of a region inside the unit square is $\le 1$; $\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx$ is between $e^{-1}$ and $1$; $n!$ is vastly larger than $2^n$ for $n=100$ by Stirling or by pairing. If four choices are $0.02, 0.5, 2, 50$ and the quantity is a probability, only the first two survive; a second estimate finishes it.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which is larger, $\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx$ or $1/2$? Use $e^{-x^2}\ge 1-x^2$ on $[0,1]$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^1(1-x^2)\,dx=2/3>1/2$, so the original integral is $>1/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $P(\text{exactly }50\text{ heads in }100\text{ fair tosses})$ is nearest $0.08$, $0.5$, or $0$? Crude: about $1/\sqrt{2\pi n p(1-p)}\approx 1/\sqrt{50\pi}\approx 0.08$. Not $0.5$.

**Solution.** $0.08$ (local CLT / Stirling). The $0.5$ distractor is $P(\text{more heads than tails})$-adjacent confusion.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Comparing $\sqrt{50}$ to $7$: $7^2=49$, so $\sqrt{50}$ is slightly more than $7$, eliminating $8$ if that were a choice.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is $4\pi/3\approx 4$, not $2\pi^2$ (that is $S^3$ volume). Estimate $\pi\approx 3$ gives

**Solution.** $4$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\int_0^1 x^{10}\,dx$ closer to $0$, to $1/2$, or to $1$?

**Solution.** Closer to $0$: the value is $1/11\approx 0.09$.
