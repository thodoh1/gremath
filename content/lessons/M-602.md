# Lesson M-602 — Factorization concepts

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Polynomial rings & factorization** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1-N.2`.


This lesson isolates **Factorization concepts** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Factorization concepts**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Polynomial rings & factorization*

$F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and UFD. Irreducibility tests, rational root theorem, division. CRT for rings: $R/(I\cap J)\cong R/I\times R/J$ when $I+J=R$.

### This skill, specifically

$F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and UFD.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A UFD has factorization into irreducibles unique up to units and order. $F[x]$ is a UFD (in fact a Euclidean domain). Gauss: $R$ UFD $\Rightarrow R[x]$ UFD, so $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a UFD. Content and Gauss's lemma: a primitive polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Z}$ iff irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Eisenstein: if a prime $p$ divides every non-leading coefficient, $p\nmid a_n$, $p^2\nmid a_0$, then $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $x^3+3x+3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein, and factor $x^4-1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$.

**Solution.** $p=3$ divides $3$ and $3$, not the leading $1$, and $9\nmid 3$. Irreducible (degree $3$, alternatively no rational root). $x^4-1=(x^2-1)(x^2+1)=(x-1)(x+1)(x^2+1)$, irreducible factors over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Eisenstein $p=2$ on $x^4+2x+2$?

**Solution.** $2$ divides $2,2$, not $1$; $4\nmid 2$. Yes, irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying Eisenstein with $p$ dividing the leading coefficient, or concluding reducible from Eisenstein failing.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $x^2+x+2$ yield to a shift of Eisenstein?

**Solution.** No: $f(x+1)=x^2+3x+4$ still fails Eisenstein. It is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ anyway (discriminant $-7$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ a UFD?

**Solution.** Yes, by Gauss's lemma, though not a PID.
