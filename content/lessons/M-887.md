# Lesson M-887 — Fisher-information awareness

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.1-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Fisher-information awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Fisher-information awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Fisher information in one observation is $I(\theta)=E_\theta\bigl[(\partial_\theta\log f(X\mid\theta))^2\bigr]=-E_\theta[\partial_{\theta\theta}\log f]$ when differentiation under the integral is valid. For $n$ iid observations, information is $nI(\theta)$. Cramér–Rao: if $\hat\theta$ is unbiased and regularity holds, $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\ge 1/(nI(\theta))$. Large-sample MLE: $\sqrt{n}(\hat\theta-\theta)\approx N\bigl(0,1/I(\theta)\bigr)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Bernoulli($p$): score $\partial_p\log f=(X-p)/(p(1-p))$. Show $I(p)=1/(p(1-p))$.

**Solution.** $E[(X-p)^2]/(p(1-p))^2=\mathrm{Var}(X)/(p(1-p))^2=p(1-p)/(p(1-p))^2=1/(p(1-p))$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Cramér–Rao bound for unbiased estimators of Bernoulli $p$ from $n$ iid trials is

**Solution.** $p(1-p)/n$, achieved by $\bar X$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $I(\theta)$ as if it were a second derivative computed at $\hat\theta$ without the expectation, then treating the inequality as an equality always.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
