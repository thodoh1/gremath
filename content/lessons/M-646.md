# Lesson M-646 — Comparison principles

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Series & convergence** · High

Prerequisites: `G.2-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Comparison principles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Comparison principles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Comparison: if $0\le a_n\le b_n$ and $\sum b_n$ converges, so does $\sum a_n$; if $\sum a_n$ diverges, so does $\sum b_n$. Limit comparison: $a_n,b_n>0$ and $a_n/b_n\to L\in(0,\infty)$ imply $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ both converge or both diverge.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $\sum 1/(n^2+3n+1)$ converge?

**Solution.** Yes: $1/(n^2+3n+1)\le 1/n^2$, or limit-compare with $1/n^2$ (ratio $\to 1$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Limit-compare $\sum\frac{n}{n^3-1}$ with

**Solution.** $\sum 1/n^2$; ratio $\to 1$, converges.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Comparing with a smaller divergent series and claiming divergence, or with a larger convergent series and claiming nothing.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $\sum 1/(n\log n)$ ($n\ge 2$) converge?

**Solution.** No: integral test / Cauchy condensation, compare with harmonic after grouping.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Solution.** diverges.
