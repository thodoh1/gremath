# Lesson M-923 — Cyclic quadrilaterals

Part V. V. Geometry · **Circle & polygon geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `V.1-C.5`.


This lesson isolates **Cyclic quadrilaterals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cyclic quadrilaterals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A quadrilateral is cyclic iff a circle passes through all four vertices, iff opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$, iff an exterior angle equals the opposite interior, iff both pairs of opposite angles are supplementary. Ptolemy: $AC\cdot BD=AB\cdot CD+AD\cdot BC$ for a cyclic $ABCD$. Equal inscribed angles subtending the same arc give another cyclic test.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Cyclic $ABCD$ with $\angle A=70^\circ$, $\angle B=100^\circ$. Find $\angle C$ and $\angle D$.

**Solution.** $\angle C=110^\circ$, $\angle D=80^\circ$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A rectangle is cyclic because

**Solution.** each angle is $90^\circ$, so opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$ (its circumcenter is the diagonal intersection).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using opposite angles of a parallelogram that is not a rectangle (they need not be supplementary unless it is cyclic, hence a rectangle).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Isosceles trapezoid: cyclic?

**Solution.** Yes (base angles equal $\Rightarrow$ opposite angles supplementary).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Ptolemy on a rectangle of sides $a,b$: $d\cdot d=$

**Solution.** $a^2+b^2$, recovering Pythagoras.
