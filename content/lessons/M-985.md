# Lesson M-985 — Numerical quadrature error

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Numerical integration & ODEs** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Numerical quadrature error** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 3. Precise statement

A quadrature rule $Q(f)=\sum w_i f(x_i)$ has degree of precision $m$ if it is exact for all polynomials of degree $\le m$. Error expansions typically start at $h^{m+1}$ or better by symmetry (Simpson gains a degree). Composite error: local error times number of panels. Compare rules by precision vs number of evaluations, not by a single lucky integrand.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Midpoint rule on one panel is exact for degree $\le 1$ (same as trapezoid) by symmetry. Error is $O(h^3)$ locally.

**Solution.** Yes: the linear term integrates exactly by centering.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Gaussian $n$-node quadrature has precision

**Solution.** $2n-1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Declaring a rule ‘more accurate’ because it used more points on one smooth integral, without an error formula, or mixing global $h$ with local $h$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
