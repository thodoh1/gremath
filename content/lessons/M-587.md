# Lesson M-587 — Units

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Units** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Units**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A unit of a unital ring $R$ is an element $u$ with some $v$ satisfying $uv=vu=1$. The units form a group $R^\times$. $\mathbb{Z}^\times=\{\pm 1\}$, $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has order $\phi(n)$, $F[x]^\times=F^\times$ for a field $F$, $M_n(F)^\times=GL_n(F)$. In a field, $R^\times=R\setminus\{0\}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Units of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, and of $\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** Gaussian units: $\pm 1,\pm i$ (norm $1$). $\mathbb{Z}/8$: $\{1,3,5,7\}$, each of order dividing $2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(\mathbb{R}[x])^\times$ equals

**Solution.** the nonzero constant polynomials, $\mathbb{R}^\times$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling every nonzero element a unit in $\mathbb{Z}$ or in $\mathbb{Z}/n$ for composite $n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Units of $\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $1,2,4,5,7,8$ ($\phi(9)=6$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $1+x$ a unit in $\mathbb{R}[[x]]$ (power series)?

**Solution.** Yes: geometric series $1-x+x^2-\cdots$.
