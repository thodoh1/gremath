# Lesson M-295 — Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Sequences** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1; O.1`.


This lesson isolates **Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

## 3. Precise statement

Bolzano–Weierstrass: every bounded sequence in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has a convergent subsequence. Proof idea in $\mathbb{R}$: bisection of a bounding interval, or limsup. Equivalent to compactness of closed bounded intervals.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $a_n=(-1)^n n/(n+1)$ have a convergent subsequence?

**Solution.** Yes, bounded; e.g. even terms $\to 1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a_n=n$ has a convergent subsequence?

**Solution.** No: unbounded. BW needs boundedness.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming the whole sequence converges because a subsequence does.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Bounded sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ must have

**Solution.** at least one subsequential limit in $\mathbb{R}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is BW true in $\mathbb{Q}$?

**Solution.** No: bounded sequences of rationals need not have a rational subsequential limit.
