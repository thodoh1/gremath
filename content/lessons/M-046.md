# Lesson M-046 — Parameter-dependent numbers of solutions

Part A. A. Foundations & Algebraic Fluency · **Equations & inequalities** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.2-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Parameter-dependent numbers of solutions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Algebraic fluency is not a listed ETS bucket of its own, but it is the cost of every calculus and algebra item. Expect it to be invisible when you have it and fatal when you do not.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Parameter-dependent numbers of solutions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Equations & inequalities*

An equation asks where two expressions agree; an inequality asks where one dominates. Linear and quadratic cases are complete (you can finish them). Higher polynomial, rational, radical, and absolute-value cases need domains, sign charts, and checks for extraneous roots. Parameters turn “solve” into “for which $a$ are there $0,1,2,\ldots$ solutions?” — a GRE obsession.

### This skill, specifically

An equation asks where two expressions agree; an inequality asks where one dominates.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A parameter $a$ can change the number of solutions. Split on coefficients that might vanish, discriminants, and domain constraints. Report the solution set as a function of $a$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** How many real $x$ satisfy $x^2-2ax+a=0$ as $a$ varies?

**Solution.** $\Delta=4a^2-4a=4a(a-1)$. Two distinct real roots if $a<0$ or $a>1$; one if $a=0$ or $1$; none if $0<a<1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(a-1)x=2$ has infinitely many solutions when

**Solution.** Never: if $a=1$ it is $0=2$ empty; else unique $x$. (Compare $0\cdot x=0$.)

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the case $a=0$ that drops degree.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** For which $a$ does $|x-a|=a$ have two solutions?

**Solution.** $a>0$ (then $x=0$ or $x=2a$). If $a=0$, one solution; if $a<0$, empty.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Number of real solutions of $e^x=a$.

**Solution.** One if $a>0$, else zero.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
