# Lesson M-722 — Uniqueness proofs

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Uniqueness proofs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Uniqueness proofs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A uniqueness proof has two parts: existence, and if $a,b$ both satisfy the property then $a=b$. Typical tools: subtract equations, apply a cancellation law, or note that a strictly monotone function hits a value at most once. ``There exists a unique'' is $\exists!$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove the additive identity in a group is unique.

**Solution.** If $e$ and $e'$ are identities, $e=e\cdot e'=e'$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Uniqueness of $\sup S$ for nonempty $S\subset\mathbb{R}$ bounded above: why?

**Solution.** The least upper bound is unique: if $M,M'$ are both least, $M\le M'$ and $M'\le M$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Proving uniqueness without existence (or vice versa) when the problem asked for $\exists!$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** At most one root of $e^x=2$: reason.

**Solution.** $e^x$ is strictly increasing, hence injective.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Division algorithm: uniqueness of $q,r$ with $a=bq+r$, $0\le r<b$. Idea.

**Solution.** If two remainders, their difference is a multiple of $b$ smaller than $b$, hence $0$.
