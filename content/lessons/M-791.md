# Lesson M-791 — Connected components

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Connected components** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Connected components**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Connected means every pair of vertices is joined by a path. A connected component is a maximal connected subgraph. The relation `joined by a path' is an equivalence relation on $V$. A graph with $c$ components, $n$ vertices, and no cycles is a forest with $n-c$ edges.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A graph on $10$ vertices with three components, all trees. How many edges?

**Solution.** $10-3=7$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Adding one edge inside a component vs between components: effect on $c$.

**Solution.** Inside: $c$ unchanged (creates a cycle if it was a tree). Between: $c$ drops by $1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Counting isolated vertices as non-components, or assuming `connected' forbids degree $1$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $G$ has $8$ vertices, $6$ edges, and is a forest. Components?

**Solution.** $c=8-6=2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $K_1\cup K_1\cup K_3$ connected? Components?

**Solution.** No; three components (two isolated vertices and a triangle).
