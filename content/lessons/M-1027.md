# Lesson M-1027 — Practice without knowing the topic in advance

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Practice without knowing the topic in advance** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Practice without knowing the topic in advance**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Hard-problem training*

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

### This skill, specifically

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Practice items with the topic label hidden. The skill is classification under uncertainty: a stem with $\sum$, $\int$, and a matrix is still one of series, calculus, or linear algebra as the governor. Time a mixed set from several chapters without peeking at the section title. Afterward, write the one-line governing concept for each item — that log is the training, not the score.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Unlabeled: ‘Let $A$ be $n\times n$ real symmetric. Which is true of $A^2$?’ Possible governors: spectral theorem, not ‘ODE’. Answer: $A^2$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, eigenvalues $\lambda_i^2$.

**Solution.** Spectral theorem: $A=QDQ^\top$, $A^2=QD^2Q^\top$, eigenvalues $\lambda_i(A)^2\ge 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Unlabeled: $\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(1+\frac{z}{n}\bigr)^n$ for complex $z$. Governor:

**Solution.** the definition/limit for $e^z$, not L’Hôpital in $n$ treated as real without care — it is still $e^z$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reading the nearby textbook section title and then solving a different problem than the one asked.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Unlabeled: $P(X>2)$ for $X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$. Governor

**Solution.** a Poisson pmf / tail, $1-e^{-1}(1+1+1/2)=1-5/(2e)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Unlabeled: area inside $r=2\cos\theta$. Governor

**Solution.** polar area $\frac12\int r^2\,d\theta$, which is a disk of radius $1$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
