# Lesson M-536 — Continued fractions

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Additional elementary number theory** · Medium

Prerequisites: `L.2-L.3`.


This lesson isolates **Continued fractions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Continued fractions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Additional elementary number theory*

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation. Recognition plus one computation.

### This skill, specifically

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A (simple) continued fraction $[a_0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]$ with $a_i\in\mathbb{Z}$, $a_i>0$ for $i\ge 1$, equals $a_0+1/(a_1+1/(a_2+\cdots))$. Finite CFs are rational; infinite CFs are irrational. Quadratic irrationals have eventually periodic CFs. Convergents $p_k/q_k$ are best approximations: $|x-p/q|<1/(2q^2)$ implies $p/q$ is a convergent.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Expand $13/8$ as a continued fraction, and recall $\sqrt{2}$.

**Solution.** $13/8=1+5/8=1+1/(8/5)=1+1/(1+3/5)=1+1/(1+1/(5/3))=1+1/(1+1/(1+2/3))=[1;1,1,1,2]$. $\sqrt{2}=[1;\overline{2}]$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The first two convergents of $\sqrt{2}=[1;2,2,\ldots]$

**Solution.** $1/1$ and $3/2$ ($1+1/2$), then $7/5$, $17/12$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Allowing $a_i=0$ in the tail (that just shortens the CF) or thinking every irrational has a finite CF.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $22/7$ as a CF.

**Solution.** $22/7=3+1/7=[3;7]$, the famous $\pi$ convergent.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Convergent $p_1/q_1$ of $[a_0;a_1]$.

**Solution.** $(a_0 a_1+1)/a_1$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
