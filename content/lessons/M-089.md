# Lesson M-089 — Inverse-function reflection

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Function transformations** · Critical

Prerequisites: `B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Inverse-function reflection** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Inverse-function reflection**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Function transformations*

Replacing $x$ by $x-h$ or $x/a$, replacing $f$ by $f+k$ or $cf$, and reflecting through axes are rigid rules that people reverse under pressure. $y=f(x-h)$ shifts the graph of $f$ to the right by $h$. Inverse functions reflect through $y=x$. Combining transformations requires a consistent order.

### This skill, specifically

Replacing $x$ by $x-h$ or $x/a$, replacing $f$ by $f+k$ or $cf$, and reflecting through axes are rigid rules that people reverse under pressure.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The graph of $y=f^{-1}(x)$ is the reflection of $y=f(x)$ across $y=x$. Domain and range swap. Tangents: if $f'(a)=m\neq 0$, then $(f^{-1})'(f(a))=1/m$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Reflect $y=e^x$ across $y=x$.

**Solution.** $y=\ln x$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $f(3)=8$ and $f'(3)=4$, then $(f^{-1})'(8)=$

**Solution.** $1/4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reflecting across $y=0$ or $x=0$ instead of $y=x$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Solution.** $y=x^2$ restricted to $x\ge 0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why must $f'$ be nonzero to invert locally?

**Solution.** Horizontal tangent $\Rightarrow$ not locally injective.
