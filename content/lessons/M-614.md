# Lesson M-614 — Field definition

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Fields & extensions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.3-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Field definition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Field definition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Fields & extensions*

A field has every nonzero element invertible. Extensions $K/F$ have degree $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. Algebraic vs transcendental, minimal polynomials, splitting fields, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

### This skill, specifically

A field has every nonzero element invertible.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A field is a commutative unital ring in which every nonzero element is a unit (equivalently a commutative division ring). $1\neq 0$. Examples: $\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{F}_p=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, $F(x)$ rational functions, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. Finite fields have prime-power order. Characteristic is $0$ or prime $p$; the prime field is $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2+1)$ are fields?

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}/4$ no ($2$ not invertible). $\mathbb{Z}/5$ yes. $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{Q}(i)$, a field because $x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A finite integral domain is a

**Solution.** field.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $\mathbb{Z}$ a field, or $\mathbb{Z}/n$ a field for composite $n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Characteristic of $\mathbb{Q}$ and of $\mathbb{F}_9$.

**Solution.** $0$ and $3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $F[[x]]$ a field?

**Solution.** No: $x$ is not invertible in formal power series. The Laurent series $F((x))$ is a field.
