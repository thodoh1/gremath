# Lesson M-866 — Uniqueness intuition

Part T. T. Probability · **Generating/transform methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.4-T.6; H.4`.


This lesson isolates **Uniqueness intuition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Uniqueness intuition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Generating/transform methods*

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases. Transformations: CDF method, Jacobian for densities.

### This skill, specifically

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Uniqueness: if two random variables have MGFs that agree on an open interval about $0$, they have the same distribution. Practical use: identify a law by matching a computed MGF to a catalog (Poisson, normal, gamma, binomial). Existence of an MGF in a neighborhood is stronger than existence of all moments; some laws (lognormal) are not determined by their moments.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A r.v. has $M(t)=(0.7+0.3e^t)^{12}$. Name the law.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Bin}(12,0.3)$, because that is $\bigl(1-p+pe^t\bigr)^n$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $M(t)=\exp(5t+8t^2)$. Law?

**Solution.** $N(5,16)$ because $\exp(\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2)$ with $\sigma^2/2=8$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Matching $M(t)$ at a single $t\neq 0$ and declaring the law unique, or assuming every distribution with finite moments has an MGF in a neighborhood of $0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $M(t)=(2/(2-t))^3$ for $t<2$. Law?

**Solution.** Gamma(shape $3$, rate $2$), sum of three Exp($2$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Independent $X_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$. MGF of $\sum X_i$ shows the sum is

**Solution.** $N(\sum\mu_i,\sum\sigma_i^2)$.
