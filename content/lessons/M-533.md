# Lesson M-533 — Möbius function

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Additional elementary number theory** · Medium

Prerequisites: `L.2-L.3`.


This lesson isolates **Möbius function** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Möbius function**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Additional elementary number theory*

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation. Recognition plus one computation.

### This skill, specifically

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The Möbius function: $\mu(1)=1$; $\mu(n)=0$ if $p^2\mid n$ for some prime $p$; $\mu(n)=(-1)^k$ if $n$ is a product of $k$ distinct primes. $\mu$ is multiplicative. $\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=1$ if $n=1$ and $0$ otherwise (characteristic of $\{1\}$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Evaluate $\mu(1),\mu(6),\mu(12),\mu(30)$.

**Solution.** $\mu(1)=1$. $6=2\cdot 3$, $\mu=1$. $12=2^2\cdot 3$, $\mu=0$. $30=2\cdot 3\cdot 5$, $\mu=-1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mu(p^k)$ for $k\ge 2$

**Solution.** $0$. For $k=1$, $\mu(p)=-1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\mu(n)=(-1)^{\omega(n)}$ even when $n$ is not squarefree.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\mu(15)$.

**Solution.** $15=3\cdot 5$, two primes, $+1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\sum_{d\mid 6}\mu(d)$.

**Solution.** $\mu(1)+\mu(2)+\mu(3)+\mu(6)=1-1-1+1=0$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
