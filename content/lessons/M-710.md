# Lesson M-710 — Predicates

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Predicate logic** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Predicates** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Predicates**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Predicate logic*

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$). Necessary vs sufficient is $\to$ direction. This is how $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ is written.

### This skill, specifically

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$).

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A predicate $P(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is an open sentence with free variables; it becomes a proposition when the variables are instantiated or bound by quantifiers. The universe of discourse is the intended domain. Equality $x=y$ is a binary predicate.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For $P(x):$ ``$x^2=2$'' on universe $\mathbb{R}$ vs $\mathbb{Q}$, which instantiations are true?

**Solution.** On $\mathbb{R}$, $P(\sqrt{2})$ is true. On $\mathbb{Q}$, $P(q)$ is false for every rational $q$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $P(x)$ a proposition?

**Solution.** Not until $x$ is specified or quantified.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Changing the universe silently (``there exists $x$ with $x^2=-1$'' in $\mathbb{R}$ vs $\mathbb{C}$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Arity of ``$x$ divides $y$''.

**Solution.** Binary.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Predicate vs function: $f(x)=x^2$ returns a number; $P(x):x^2=2$ returns

**Solution.** a truth value.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
