# Lesson M-552 — Transpositions

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Permutation groups** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Transpositions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Transpositions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Permutation groups*

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters. Cycle decomposition, disjoint cycles commute, transpositions generate, sign homomorphism, $A_n=\ker(\mathrm{sign})$.

### This skill, specifically

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A transposition is a $2$-cycle. Adjacent transpositions $(i\,i+1)$ generate $S_n$. Any $k$-cycle is a product of $k-1$ transpositions: $(a_1\,\ldots\,a_k)=(a_1\,a_k)\cdots(a_1\,a_2)$. The factorization into transpositions is not unique, but the parity of the number of factors is.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Write $(1\,2\,3\,4)$ as transpositions, and factor $(1\,3)$ using adjacent transpositions.

**Solution.** $(1\,2\,3\,4)=(1\,4)(1\,3)(1\,2)$. And $(1\,3)=(1\,2)(2\,3)(1\,2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A $5$-cycle is a product of how many transpositions in the standard factorization?

**Solution.** $4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking the transposition factorization is unique (so $(1\,2)(1\,2)(1\,3)$ would ‘change’ $(1\,3)$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $(1\,2)(1\,2)$ equals

**Solution.** the identity (even number of transpositions).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Generate $S_3$ by $\{(1\,2),(1\,2\,3)\}$?

**Solution.** Yes: those two generate (standard presentation).
