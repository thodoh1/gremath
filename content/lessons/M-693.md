# Lesson M-693 — Sequential compactness in metric spaces

Part P. P. Topology · **Separation & compactness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **Sequential compactness in metric spaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Sequential compactness in metric spaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Separation & compactness*

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens. Compact: every open cover has a finite subcover. Continuous images of compact sets are compact. In $\mathbb{R}^n$, compact $\Leftrightarrow$ closed and bounded (Heine–Borel).

### This skill, specifically

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Sequential compactness: every sequence has a subsequence converging to a point of the space. In metric spaces, compact $\Leftrightarrow$ sequentially compact $\Leftrightarrow$ complete and totally bounded. Bolzano--Weierstrass is sequential compactness of closed bounded subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $(0,1)$ not sequentially compact?

**Solution.** $x_n=1/n$ has no subsequence converging in $(0,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** In metric spaces, compactness and sequential compactness are

**Solution.** equivalent.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using sequential compactness as the definition in a general topological space (there it is weaker than compactness).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is an infinite subset of a sequentially compact metric space guaranteed a limit point in the space?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the product topology is compact by Tychonoff; metrizable?

**Solution.** Yes (countable product of metric intervals), hence sequentially compact.
