# Lesson M-735 — Power set

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Power set** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Power set**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The power set $\mathcal{P}(A)=\{B:B\subset A\}$ has $2^{|A|}$ elements when $A$ is finite. Always $\emptyset,A\in\mathcal{P}(A)$. $A\subset B$ implies $\mathcal{P}(A)\subset\mathcal{P}(B)$. Cantor's theorem: there is no surjection $A\to\mathcal{P}(A)$, so $|A|<|\mathcal{P}(A)|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** List $\mathcal{P}(\{1,2\})$ and compute $|\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,3\})|$.

**Solution.** $\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\}$; $8$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|\mathcal{P}(\emptyset)|$ equals

**Solution.** $1$, because $\mathcal{P}(\emptyset)=\{\emptyset\}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Counting only nonempty subsets, or writing $n^2$ instead of $2^n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\{1\}\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2\})$? Is $\{1\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\{1,2\})$?

**Solution.** Yes; no. The subset claim would require $1\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2\})$, but $1$ is not a subset of $\{1,2\}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Cantor: $|\mathbb{N}|$ vs $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})|$.

**Solution.** Strictly smaller; $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ is uncountable.
