# Lesson M-381 — Green theorem

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Integral theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `I.2-I.3`.


This lesson isolates **Green theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Green theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Green's theorem: for a positively oriented, piecewise $C^1$ simple closed curve $C=\partial D$ and $C^1$ field $(P,Q)$ on a neighborhood of $D$, $\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA$. It converts circulation to area-integral of scalar curl.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\mathbf{F}=(-y,x)$ around the unit circle, counterclockwise. Compute $\oint\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ by Green.

**Solution.** $Q_x-P_y=2$, $\iint_D 2\,dA=2\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\oint_{\partial D} -y\,dx$ for area of $D$ is

**Solution.** $\iint_D 1\,dA=\mathrm{Area}(D)$ (here $Q=0$, $P=-y$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using clockwise orientation without flipping the sign, or applying Green to a region with a hole without accounting for the inner boundary.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Area of the ellipse $x=2\cos t$, $y=\sin t$ via Green.

**Solution.** $\pi\cdot 2\cdot 1=2\pi$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $P=x$, $Q=y$ on the unit disk: $\oint P\,dx+Q\,dy$ equals

**Solution.** $0$ because $Q_x-P_y=0$.
