This lesson isolates **Trails** and trains it until it is automatic.

## 3. Precise statement

A trail is a walk with no repeated edge (vertices may repeat). An Euler trail (see later) traverses every edge exactly once. Closed trails are circuits in some texts. Deleting used edges is the right mental model.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $K_{2,3}$ have a trail that uses all $6$ edges?

**Solution.** Yes: the three vertices of the size-$2$ part have degrees $3,3$; the other part degrees $2,2,2$. Exactly two odd degrees, so an Euler trail exists.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can a trail repeat a vertex?

**Solution.** Yes; it cannot repeat an edge.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling every trail a path, or allowing a trail to reuse an edge because the vertex is new.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** In a path of $4$ edges, the longest trail has length

**Solution.** $4$ (the path itself).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Start at a degree-$1$ vertex in a tree. Any trail can be extended until

**Solution.** you reach a dead-end; trees have no extra unused edges at interior vertices once you leave them, so a maximal trail from a leaf is a path to another leaf.
