# Lesson M-529 — Quadratic residues

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Additional elementary number theory** · Medium

Prerequisites: `L.2-L.3`.


This lesson isolates **Quadratic residues** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Quadratic residues**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Additional elementary number theory*

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation. Recognition plus one computation.

### This skill, specifically

Quadratic residues, Legendre symbol, reciprocity (awareness), primitive roots, Möbius inversion, multiplicative functions, continued fractions, $p$-adic valuation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An integer $a$ is a quadratic residue mod $n$ if $\gcd(a,n)=1$ and $x^2\equiv a\pmod{n}$ is solvable (sometimes $0$ is included as a trivial residue). Mod an odd prime $p$ there are $(p-1)/2$ nonzero residues and $(p-1)/2$ nonresidues. The residues are the distinct values of $1^2,2^2,\ldots,((p-1)/2)^2\bmod p$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Find the quadratic residues modulo $7$.

**Solution.** $1^2\equiv 1$, $2^2\equiv 4$, $3^2\equiv 2$, $4^2\equiv 2$, $5^2\equiv 4$, $6^2\equiv 1$. Nonzero residues: $1,2,4$. Nonresidues: $3,5,6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $3$ a quadratic residue mod $11$?

**Solution.** $1^2=1$, $2^2=4$, $3^2=9$, $4^2=5$, $5^2=3$. Yes, $5^2\equiv 3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Testing only $x=1,2$ and declaring a nonresidue, or including $0$ when the problem said coprime residues.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Squares modulo $8$ among odd units.

**Solution.** Odd squares are $1\bmod 8$ only.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Number of solutions of $x^2\equiv 1\pmod{8}$.

**Solution.** Four: $1,3,5,7$ all square to $1$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
