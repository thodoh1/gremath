# Lesson M-747 — Countable sets

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Functions & cardinality** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.4`.


This lesson isolates **Countable sets** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Countable sets**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Functions & cardinality*

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses. Countable vs uncountable, Cantor diagonal, $|\mathbb{N}|=|\mathbb{Z}|=|\mathbb{Q}|<|\mathbb{R}|$, axiom of choice as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A set is countable if it is finite or in bijection with a subset of $\mathbb{N}$ (equivalently: there is an injection into $\mathbb{N}$). Countable unions of countable sets are countable; finite products of countable sets are countable. $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$, and the algebraic numbers are countable.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $\mathbb{Z}$ countable? Why is $\mathbb{Q}$ countable?

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}$ lists as $0,1,-1,2,-2,\ldots$. $\mathbb{Q}$ lists by diagonals of $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}$ (lowest terms), skipping repeats.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A subset of a countable set is

**Solution.** countable.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming $\mathbb{Q}$ is uncountable because it is dense, or that ``infinite'' means countable.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is a countable union of finite sets countable?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** The set of finite binary strings is

**Solution.** countable (union over length).
