# Lesson M-274 — Average value of a function

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Average value of a function** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Average value of a function**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The average value of integrable $f$ on $[a,b]$ is $\displaystyle\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$. MVT for integrals: if $f$ is continuous, some $c$ has $f(c)$ equal to this average.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Average of $x^2$ on $[0,3]$.

**Solution.** $\frac13\int_0^3 x^2=3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Average of $\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$

**Solution.** $2/\pi$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting to divide by $b-a$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Average of a constant $k$.

**Solution.** $k$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does a discontinuous integrable $f$ still have an average?

**Solution.** Yes as a number; it may not be attained.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
