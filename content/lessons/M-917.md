# Lesson M-917 — Tangent properties

Part V. V. Geometry · **Circle & polygon geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `V.1-C.5`.


This lesson isolates **Tangent properties** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Tangent properties**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A tangent line meets a circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius at that point. Two tangents from an external point are equal in length and make equal angles with the line to the center. Alternate segment theorem: the angle between tangent and chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Radius $6$, external point with tangent length $8$. Distance from center to the point?

**Solution.** Right triangle: $\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Two tangents from $P$ touch at $A,B$. If $PA=7$, then $PB=$

**Solution.** $7$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Drawing a tangent that is not perpendicular to the radius, or using the secant-tangent power with a chord that is not from the external point.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
