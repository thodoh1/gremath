# Lesson M-686 — Continuous maps

Part P. P. Topology · **Topological spaces** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1`.


This lesson isolates **Continuous maps** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Continuous maps**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Topological spaces*

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections. Bases, subbases, subspace and product topologies, continuity as preimages of opens, homeomorphism as topological isomorphism.

### This skill, specifically

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$f:X\to Y$ is continuous iff $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$ whenever $V$ is open in $Y$. Equivalent: preimages of closed sets are closed; or $f(\overline{A})\subset\overline{f(A)}$. In metric spaces this matches $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ and sequential continuity. Continuity is local.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $f:\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{usual}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{discrete}}$, $f(x)=x$, not continuous?

**Solution.** $\{0\}$ is open in the discrete line, but $f^{-1}(\{0\})=\{0\}$ is not open in the usual topology.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Preimage of an open set under a continuous map is

**Solution.** open.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming continuous maps send opens to opens (those are open maps, a stronger property).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is a constant map continuous?

**Solution.** Yes: preimage of an open is either $\emptyset$ or $X$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Identity $\mathrm{id}:(X,\tau)\to(X,\sigma)$ is continuous iff

**Solution.** $\tau$ is finer than $\sigma$.
