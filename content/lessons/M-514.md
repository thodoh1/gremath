# Lesson M-514 — Chinese remainder theorem

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Congruences** · High

Prerequisites: `L.1`.


This lesson isolates **Chinese remainder theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Chinese remainder theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Congruences*

$a\equiv b\pmod n$ means $n\mid(a-b)$. Residue classes form $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. Inverses exist iff coprime to $n$. Linear congruences and CRT are the solving machine. Fast exponentiation is repeated squaring.

### This skill, specifically

$a\equiv b\pmod n$ means $n\mid(a-b)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Chinese remainder theorem: if $\gcd(m,n)=1$, then the system $x\equiv a\pmod{m}$, $x\equiv b\pmod{n}$ has a unique solution modulo $mn$. Construct $x=a n(n^{-1}\bmod m)+b m(m^{-1}\bmod n)$, or solve sequentially. The map $\mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a ring isomorphism.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $x\equiv 2\pmod{5}$, $x\equiv 3\pmod{7}$.

**Solution.** Modulus $35$. Write $x=7k+3$. Then $7k+3\equiv 2\pmod{5}$, $2k\equiv -1\equiv 4\pmod{5}$, $k\equiv 2\pmod{5}$, $k=5t+2$, $x=35t+17$. Check: $17\equiv 2\pmod{5}$, $17\equiv 3\pmod{7}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The system $x\equiv 1\pmod{4}$, $x\equiv 3\pmod{5}$ has unique solution mod

**Solution.** $20$. Indeed $x\equiv 13\pmod{20}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying CRT when moduli are not coprime (need the compatibility $a\equiv b\pmod{\gcd}$ instead).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $x\equiv 0\pmod{3}$, $x\equiv 1\pmod{4}$.

**Solution.** $x\equiv 9\pmod{12}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** How many solutions mod $15$ has $x\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, $x\equiv 2\pmod{5}$?

**Solution.** One: $x\equiv 2\pmod{15}$ (same remainder, coprime moduli).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
