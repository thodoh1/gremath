# Lesson M-965 — Evaluating real integrals via residues — awareness

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Residue methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.3`.


This lesson isolates **Evaluating real integrals via residues — awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Evaluating real integrals via residues — awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Residue methods*

Simple-pole residue $p/q'$ or $\lim(z-z_0)f$, residue theorem $2\pi i\sum\mathrm{Res}$, argument principle, winding numbers, real integrals via semicircles as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Simple-pole residue $p/q'$ or $\lim(z-z_0)f$, residue theorem $2\pi i\sum\mathrm{Res}$, argument principle, winding numbers, real integrals via semicircles as awareness.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Awareness: many real integrals equal $2\pi i$ times a sum of residues of a complex extension. Typical: $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \dfrac{dx}{1+x^2}=\pi$ via $1/(z^2+1)$ and the upper-half-plane pole at $i$ (Res $=1/(2i)$, so $2\pi i\cdot 1/(2i)=\pi$). For Fourier integrals, close in the half-plane where the exponential decays. Jordan’s lemma and indentation around real poles are the usual technical add-ons, not always needed on GRE.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \dfrac{dx}{x^2+1}$ by residues.

**Solution.** $f(z)=1/(z^2+1)$, pole $i$ in the upper half-plane, $\mathrm{Res}=1/(2i)$, integral $2\pi i\cdot 1/(2i)=\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For $\int \dfrac{\cos x}{1+x^2}\,dx$ on $\mathbb{R}$, one uses

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Re}\int e^{iz}/(z^2+1)\,dz$ closed upward.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Closing the contour in the half-plane where $e^{iz}$ blows up, or including both $i$ and $-i$ for a semicircle that only contains one.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Res of $1/(z^2+1)$ at $-i$.

**Solution.** $-1/(2i)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\int_{-\infty}^\infty dx/(x^2+4)$ by the same method equals

**Solution.** $\pi/2$ (scale, or Res at $2i$ is $1/(4i)$, times $2\pi i$ is $\pi/2$).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
