# Lesson M-539 — Group axioms

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Groups** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Group axioms** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Group axioms**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Groups*

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses. Abelian means commutative. Subgroups, cyclic groups, orders of elements and of the group: Lagrange is coming.

### This skill, specifically

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A group is a set $G$ with an associative binary operation, an identity $e$ with $eg=ge=g$, and inverses $g^{-1}$ with $gg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=e$. (Some texts list closure separately.) Cancellation holds: $ab=ac\Rightarrow b=c$. Examples: $(\mathbb{Z},+)$, $(\mathbb{R}^\times,\cdot)$, $GL_n(F)$, $S_n$. Nonexamples: $(\mathbb{N},+)$, $(\mathbb{R},\cdot)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $(\mathbb{Z},+)$, $(\mathbb{N},+)$, $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$, $(\mathbb{R},\cdot)$ are groups?

**Solution.** $(\mathbb{Z},+)$ yes. $(\mathbb{N},+)$ no inverses. $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$ yes. $(\mathbb{R},\cdot)$ no: $0$ has no inverse.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The identity in $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ is

**Solution.** the identity matrix $I_2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking inverses but not associativity (or vice versa); or requiring commutativity as an axiom.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\{1,-1\}$ under multiplication a group?

**Solution.** Yes: cyclic of order $2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does every group satisfy $(ab)^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}$?

**Solution.** No: $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
