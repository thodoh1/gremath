# Lesson M-639 — Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Bolzano--Weierstrass: every bounded sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{R}^d$) has a convergent subsequence. Proof: nested bisection of an interval containing all terms, or extract a monotone subsequence and apply MCT.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** The sequence $a_n=(-1)^n(1+1/n)$ is bounded. Name a convergent subsequence and its limit.

**Solution.** Evens: $a_{2k}=1+1/(2k)\to 1$. Odds $\to -1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does every sequence of reals have a convergent subsequence?

**Solution.** No: $a_n=n$ is unbounded, every subsequence $\to+\infty$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying BW in $\mathbb{Q}$: a bounded sequence of rationals has a subsequence convergent in $\mathbb{R}$, not necessarily in $\mathbb{Q}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is BW true in an incomplete space?

**Solution.** The subsequence may converge only in the completion.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A bounded sequence in $[0,1]$ has a subsequence converging to a point of

**Solution.** $[0,1]$ (closed).
