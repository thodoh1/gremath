# Lesson M-478 — Characteristic polynomial

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Eigenvalues & eigenvectors** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.6`.


This lesson isolates **Characteristic polynomial** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Characteristic polynomial**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Eigenvalues & eigenvectors*

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. Characteristic polynomial, algebraic vs geometric multiplicity, diagonalization iff enough independent eigenvectors. Trace is sum of eigenvalues; det is product. Cayley–Hamilton.

### This skill, specifically

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The characteristic polynomial is $p_A(\lambda)=\det(A-\lambda I)$ (some texts use $\det(\lambda I-A)$, which multiplies by $(-1)^n$). It is monic of degree $n$ up to that sign convention. Eigenvalues are its roots. $p_A$ is similarity-invariant. Cayley–Hamilton says $p_A(A)=0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$. Compute $p_A(\lambda)=\det(A-\lambda I)$ and the eigenvalues.

**Solution.** $(1-\lambda)^2-4=\lambda^2-2\lambda-3=0$, $\lambda=3,-1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Degree of $p_A$ for $4\times 4$ $A$ is

**Solution.** $4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Expanding $\det(A-\lambda I)$ as $\det A-\lambda\det I$ (false: det is not linear).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $p_A$ for $A=\mathrm{diag}(2,5)$.

**Solution.** $(2-\lambda)(5-\lambda)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Similar matrices have

**Solution.** the same characteristic polynomial.
