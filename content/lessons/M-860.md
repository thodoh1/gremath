# Lesson M-860 — Central limit theorem

Part T. T. Probability · **Limit theorems** · High

Prerequisites: `T.5-O.2`.


This lesson isolates **Central limit theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Central limit theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Limit theorems*

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation. CLT: standardized sums $\to$ normal. Continuity correction for lattice $\to$ continuous. Weak convergence is the topology behind the CLT.

### This skill, specifically

LLN: sample means $\to$ expectation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Central limit theorem: i.i.d. $X_i$ with mean $\mu$ and finite positive variance $\sigma^2$, then $\sqrt{n}\,(\bar X_n-\mu)/\sigma\to N(0,1)$ in distribution. Equivalently $S_n=\sum X_i$ is approximately $N(n\mu,n\sigma^2)$ for large $n$. The CLT is about fluctuations of order $1/\sqrt{n}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** I.i.d. with $\mu=2$, $\sigma=3$, $n=36$. Approximate $P(\bar X>2.5)$.

**Solution.** $\bar X\approx N(2,9/36)=N(2,1/4)$. $P(\bar X>2.5)=P\bigl(Z>(2.5-2)/(1/2)\bigr)=P(Z>1)=1-\Phi(1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sum of $100$ i.i.d. with mean $0$ variance $1$ is approximately

**Solution.** $N(0,100)$, so $\mathrm{SD}=10$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\sigma$ instead of $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ as the SD of $\bar X$, or applying the CLT to $n=2$ with a highly skewed law as if it were exact.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Standardize $S_{25}$ if $\mu=4$, $\sigma=2$.

**Solution.** $(S_{25}-100)/10$ is approx $N(0,1)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $\sigma=0$, does the CLT apply?

**Solution.** No: nondegenerate variance is required. The average is already exactly $\mu$.
