# Lesson M-865 — Using MGF for moments

Part T. T. Probability · **Generating/transform methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.4-T.6; H.4`.


This lesson isolates **Using MGF for moments** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Using MGF for moments**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Generating/transform methods*

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases. Transformations: CDF method, Jacobian for densities.

### This skill, specifically

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $M$ is finite in a neighborhood of $0$, then $\mathbb{E}[X^k]=M^{(k)}(0)$. In particular $\mathbb{E}[X]=M'(0)$ and $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=M''(0)$, so $\mathrm{Var}(X)=M''(0)-(M'(0))^2$. This recovers means and variances from a closed-form MGF.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $M(t)=\lambda/(\lambda-t)$ for $t<\lambda$ (exponential rate $\lambda$). Compute $M'(0)$ and $M''(0)$, hence mean and variance.

**Solution.** $M'(t)=\lambda/(\lambda-t)^2$, $M'(0)=1/\lambda$. $M''(t)=2\lambda/(\lambda-t)^3$, $M''(0)=2/\lambda^2$. $\mathrm{Var}=2/\lambda^2-1/\lambda^2=1/\lambda^2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $M(t)=1-p+pe^t$. $M'(0)$.

**Solution.** $p$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Evaluating $M'(t)$ at $t=1$ instead of $0$, or using $M''(0)$ as the variance without subtracting $(M'(0))^2$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $M(t)=\exp(\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2)$. $M'(0)$.

**Solution.** $\mu$ (product rule: $(\mu+\sigma^2 t)M(t)$, at $0$ is $\mu$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $X\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)$, $M(t)=\exp(\lambda(e^t-1))$. Show $M'(0)=\lambda$.

**Solution.** $M'(t)=\lambda e^t M(t)$, so $M'(0)=\lambda$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
