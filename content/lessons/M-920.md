# Lesson M-920 — Inscribed angles

Part V. V. Geometry · **Circle & polygon geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `V.1-C.5`.


This lesson isolates **Inscribed angles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Inscribed angles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

An inscribed angle has vertex on the circle and sides through two other points of the circle; its measure is half the intercepted arc (half the central angle subtending the same arc). Angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$ (Thales). Inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal. An angle inscribed in a major arc is acute if it intercepts a minor arc.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Central angle $80^\circ$ intercepting arc $AB$. Inscribed angle intercepting the same arc?

**Solution.** $40^\circ$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A triangle inscribed in a circle with one side a diameter is

**Solution.** right-angled at the third vertex.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the full central angle instead of half, or taking the intercepted arc to be the one not ‘seen’ by the angle.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Inscribed angle intercepting a $180^\circ$ arc.

**Solution.** $90^\circ$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Two inscribed angles intercepting arc $AB$ from the same side: they are

**Solution.** equal.
