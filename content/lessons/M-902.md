# Lesson M-902 — Simple linear regression

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Regression** · Medium

Prerequisites: `K.8-U.1`.


This lesson isolates **Simple linear regression** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Simple linear regression**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Simple linear regression assumes $E[Y\mid x]=\beta_0+\beta_1 x$. Fitted values $\hat y_i=\hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1 x_i$. Prediction at a new $x^*$ is $\hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1 x^*$, with extra uncertainty beyond $\mathrm{se}$ of the mean line (prediction interval vs confidence interval for the mean). Extrapolation beyond the $x$-range is dangerous.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Line $\hat y=3-2x$. Predict at $x=4$.

**Solution.** $-5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A confidence interval for $E[Y\mid x^*]$ is narrowest at

**Solution.** $x^*=\bar x$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting a prediction as exact, or using a mean-response CI when the question asks for a new observation.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If $\hat\beta_1=0$, the best LS prediction is

**Solution.** $\bar y$ for every $x$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why is prediction variance larger than mean-response variance?

**Solution.** A new $\varepsilon^*$ is still unknown.
