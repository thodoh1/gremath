# Lesson M-794 — Trees

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Trees** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trees**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A tree is a connected acyclic graph. Equivalent: connected with $n-1$ edges; unique path between any pair; minimally connected; maximally acyclic. Any two of `connected', `acyclic', `$|E|=n-1$' imply the third.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A connected graph on $15$ vertices with $14$ edges. Must it be a tree?

**Solution.** Yes: connected $+\,|E|=n-1$ $\Rightarrow$ acyclic.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A graph on $8$ vertices with $7$ edges. Is it a tree?

**Solution.** Not necessarily: it could be $C_3$ plus isolateds and a path, i.e. disconnected. Need connectedness.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $|E|=n-1$ alone to declare a tree.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** How many leaves (degree $1$) at least in a tree on $n\ge 2$ vertices?

**Solution.** At least $2$: if fewer than two leaves then $\sum\deg\ge 1+2(n-1)=2n-1>2n-2=2|E|$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Add one edge to a tree. What appears?

**Solution.** Exactly one cycle (the unique paths close).
