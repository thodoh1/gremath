# Lesson M-333 — Partial derivatives

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multivariable functions** · High

Prerequisites: `E.2; K.1`.


This lesson isolates **Partial derivatives** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Partial derivatives**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multivariable functions*

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ has domains, level sets, and limits that must hold along every path. Partials are ordinary derivatives with other variables frozen. Mixed partials agree when they are continuous.

### This skill, specifically

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ has domains, level sets, and limits that must hold along every path.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The partial $f_x$ at $\mathbf{a}$ is the ordinary derivative of $g(x)=f(x,a_2,\ldots)$ at $a_1$. Computationally, treat other variables as constant. Existence of all partials does not imply continuity, nor differentiability.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $f=x^2 y+y^3$, $f_x$ and $f_y$.

**Solution.** $f_x=2xy$, $f_y=x^2+3y^2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f=xy/(x^2+y^2)$ off origin, $f_x(0,0)$ if $f(0,0)=0$

**Solution.** Along the $x$-axis $f=0$, so $f_x(0,0)=0$. Similarly $f_y(0,0)=0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the one-variable quotient rule while differentiating in $x$ but letting $y$ also vary.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f=e^{xy}$, $f_x$.

**Solution.** $y e^{xy}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $z=\sin(x/y)$, $z_y$.

**Solution.** $\cos(x/y)\cdot(-x/y^2)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
