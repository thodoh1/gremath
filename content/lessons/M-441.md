# Lesson M-441 — Elementary row operations

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Systems & row reduction** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2`.


This lesson isolates **Elementary row operations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Elementary row operations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems & row reduction*

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm. REF/RREF, consistency, free variables, rank. Homogeneous systems always have at least the zero solution.

### This skill, specifically

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Elementary row operations: (E1) swap two rows; (E2) multiply a row by a nonzero scalar; (E3) add a multiple of one row to a different row. They are invertible and do not change the row space or the solution set of a linear system (for ops on the augmented matrix). Multiplying a row by $0$ is not allowed.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which operation turns $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&5\end{pmatrix}$ into $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}$?

**Solution.** $R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1$ (E3).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** May you replace $R_1$ by $0\cdot R_1$?

**Solution.** No: that is not elementary (not invertible).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $R_2\leftarrow 2 R_1$ and discarding the old $R_2$ (that replaces a row rather than adding, and generally changes the row space dimension).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Swap $R_1,R_2$ on $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\3&4\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}3&4\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Do elementary ops change $\mathrm{Nul}\,A$?

**Solution.** No: they realize an invertible left factor $E$, and $EA\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}\Leftrightarrow A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
