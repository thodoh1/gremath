# Lesson M-111 — Parametric equations

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Parametric & polar basics** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Parametric equations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Parametric equations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Parametric & polar basics*

Parametric equations describe a curve by $x(t),y(t)$. Polar coordinates describe a point by radius and angle. Eliminating $t$ recovers a Cartesian relation when you need it; keeping $t$ is better for motion. Polar area uses $\frac12\int r^2\,d\theta$.

### This skill, specifically

Parametric equations describe a curve by $x(t),y(t)$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Parametric equations $x=x(t)$, $y=y(t)$ describe a curve by a parameter. The graph is the set of points $(x(t),y(t))$. Different parametrizations can trace the same set at different speeds or directions.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. What curve?

**Solution.** The unit circle, traced counterclockwise once.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $x=t^2$, $y=t$ traces

**Solution.** a parabola $x=y^2$ (rightward, both ways as $t$ from $-\infty$ to $\infty$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Eliminating $t$ and forgetting that the parametrization may only cover part of the Cartesian curve.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $x=1+t$, $y=2-3t$ is the line through

**Solution.** $(1,2)$ with direction $(1,-3)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $x=t^2$, $y=t^2$ trace $y=x$?

**Solution.** Only the ray $x\ge 0$, $y=x$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
