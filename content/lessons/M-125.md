# Lesson M-125 — Reference angles

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Trigonometry foundations** · Critical

Prerequisites: `B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Reference angles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Reference angles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Trigonometry foundations*

Radians are the calculus unit. The unit circle defines $\sin,\cos$ as coordinates, and the other four functions as ratios. Pythagorean, even/odd, and reciprocal identities are rewriting tools. Exact values at $0,\pi/6,\pi/4,\pi/3,\pi/2$ and their cousins must be instant. Quadrant signs are ASTC (or whatever mnemonic you will not botch).

### This skill, specifically

Radians are the calculus unit.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The reference angle is the acute angle to the $x$-axis. The trig value equals $\pm$ the Q1 value of the reference angle, sign from the quadrant.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Reference angle of $5\pi/6$.

**Solution.** $\pi/6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sin(7\pi/6)$.

**Solution.** $-1/2$ (Q3, ref $\pi/6$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Measuring the reference angle to the $y$-axis.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Reference of $300^\circ$.

**Solution.** $60^\circ$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\cos(4\pi/3)$.

**Solution.** $-1/2$.
