# Lesson M-555 — Alternating group An

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Permutation groups** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Alternating group An** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Alternating group An**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Permutation groups*

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters. Cycle decomposition, disjoint cycles commute, transpositions generate, sign homomorphism, $A_n=\ker(\mathrm{sign})$.

### This skill, specifically

$S_n$ is the group of bijections on $n$ letters.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The alternating group $A_n$ is the kernel of $\mathrm{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm 1\}$, hence a normal subgroup of index $2$, order $n!/2$ ($n\ge 2$). It consists of the even permutations and is generated by $3$-cycles. $A_n$ is simple for $n\ge 5$. $A_4$ has a normal Klein four-group $V=\{e,(1\,2)(3\,4),(1\,3)(2\,4),(1\,4)(2\,3)\}$ and is not simple.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** List $A_3$ and decide whether $A_4$ has an element of order $6$.

**Solution.** $A_3=\{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. $A_4$ has no element of order $6$: a $6$-cycle is odd, and a product of disjoint $2$- and $3$-cycles is odd. Orders in $A_4$ divide $12$ but $6$ does not occur.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Including transpositions in $A_n$, or thinking $A_n$ abelian for $n\ge 4$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $(1\,2)(3\,4)$ in $A_n$?

**Solution.** Yes: product of two transpositions, even.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Index $[S_n:A_n]$.

**Solution.** $2$.
