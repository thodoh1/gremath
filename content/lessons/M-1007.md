## 3. Precise statement

Sign traps: multiplying an inequality by $-1$ reverses it; $|x|<a$ is $-a<x<a$ only if $a>0$; $|x|>a$ is $x<-a$ or $x>a$; $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$. For $x^2>4$, do not take ‘$x>2$’ alone. In $\mathbb{C}$, there is no compatible total order: never write $z>0$ for a nonreal. Log is increasing, so $\ln a<\ln b$ preserves inequality for $a,b>0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $1-2x>3$.

**Solution.** $-2x>2$, divide by $-2$: $x<-1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|2x-1|\ge 3$.

**Solution.** $2x-1\le -3$ or $2x-1\ge 3$, so $x\le -1$ or $x\ge 2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting to reverse the inequality, or writing $|x|>-1$ as $x>1$ or $x<-1$ (it is all $\mathbb{R}$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $x^2>4$.

**Solution.** $x<-2$ or $x>2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $i>0$?

**Solution.** No: $\mathbb{C}$ is not ordered as a field.
