# Lesson M-585 — Subrings

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Subrings** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subrings**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A subring $S\subseteq R$ is a subset that is a ring under the same operations. Tests: $S$ nonempty (or $0\in S$), closed under subtraction and multiplication. If rings are unital, require $1_R\in S$. Then $n\mathbb{Z}$ is a subring of $\mathbb{Z}$ in the non-unital convention, but not unital-sharing unless $n=\pm 1$. $\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}$. Intersection of subrings is a subring.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ a subring of $\mathbb{C}$? Is $\{0,2,4\}$ a subring of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** Yes: Gaussian integers closed under $+,-,\cdot$, contain $1$. Second: $2\cdot 2=4$, $2\cdot 4=2$, $4\cdot 4=4\bmod 6$, additive subgroup $\{0,2,4\}$. Unital? Does not contain $1$. So a rng-subring, not a unital subring.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{R}$ as a subring of $\mathbb{C}$ is

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Q}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking multiplicative closure but not additive inverses.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ a subring of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Smallest subring of $\mathbb{R}$ containing $1$.

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}$ (the prime ring of characteristic $0$).
