# Lesson M-192 — Magnitude/argument relationships under multiplication and division

Part D. D. Complex Numbers · **Complex polynomial problems** · High

Prerequisites: `D.1-D.2`.


This lesson isolates **Magnitude/argument relationships under multiplication and division** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Complex numbers appear in algebra, polynomials, eigenvalues, and as a bridge to Part W.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Magnitude/argument relationships under multiplication and division**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex polynomial problems*

Over $\mathbb{C}$, every nonconstant polynomial factors into linears. Real polynomials have conjugate-root symmetry. Roots of unity turn $z^n=1$ into geometry. Magnitude-argument arithmetic is how you track $|p(z)|$ without expanding.

### This skill, specifically

Over $\mathbb{C}$, every nonconstant polynomial factors into linears.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$|zw|=|z||w|$ and $\arg(zw)=\arg z+\arg w$ (mod $2\pi$). Division: $|z/w|=|z|/|w|$ and $\arg(z/w)=\arg z-\arg w$. In particular $|z^n|=|z|^n$ and $\arg(z^n)=n\arg z$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** If $|z|=2$ and $\arg z=\pi/6$, find $|z^3|$ and $\arg(z^3)$.

**Solution.** $8$ and $\pi/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|z/w|=1$ means

**Solution.** $|z|=|w|$ (if $w\neq 0$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Adding arguments without reducing mod $2\pi$ when a principal Arg is required: $\mathrm{Arg}((-1)(-1))=0\neq \pi+\pi$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $|i^{100}|$.

**Solution.** $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $\arg\bigl((1+i)/(1-i)\bigr)$.

**Solution.** $(1+i)/(1-i)=i$, so $\pi/2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
