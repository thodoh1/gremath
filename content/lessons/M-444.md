# Lesson M-444 — Particular/general solutions

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Systems & row reduction** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2`.


This lesson isolates **Particular/general solutions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Particular/general solutions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems & row reduction*

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm. REF/RREF, consistency, free variables, rank. Homogeneous systems always have at least the zero solution.

## 3. Precise statement

If $A\mathbf{x}_p=\mathbf{b}$, the general solution of $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_p+\mathbf{x}_h$ where $\mathbf{x}_h$ runs through $\mathrm{Nul}\,A$. All particular solutions differ by a null-space vector. If the null space is trivial, the particular solution is unique.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $x+y=3$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Give particular and general solutions.

**Solution.** Particular $(3,0)$. Homogeneous $t(-1,1)$. General $(3,0)+t(-1,1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $\mathrm{Nul}\,A=\{\mathbf{0}\}$ and $\mathbf{b}\in\mathrm{Col}\,A$, the number of solutions is

**Solution.** $1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Adding two particular solutions and calling that a particular solution (that solves $A\mathbf{x}=2\mathbf{b}$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\0\end{pmatrix}$. General solution?

**Solution.** $(2,0)+t(-1,1)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Solution.** $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$.
