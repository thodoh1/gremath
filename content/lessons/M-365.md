# Lesson M-365 — Directional derivative as gradient dot unit vector

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Directional derivative as gradient dot unit vector**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Vector fields*

A vector field assigns a vector to each point. Gradients are special fields. Velocity and acceleration are derivatives of vector-valued paths.

### This skill, specifically

A vector field assigns a vector to each point.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The directional derivative of $f$ at $\mathbf{a}$ in the direction of a unit vector $\mathbf{u}$ is $D_{\mathbf{u}}f(\mathbf{a})=\nabla f(\mathbf{a})\cdot\mathbf{u}$. It is the slope of $f$ along the line $\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{u}$. The maximum is $|\nabla f|$, attained when $\mathbf{u}$ is parallel to $\nabla f$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $f=x^2+4y$ at $(1,2)$, direction of $(3,4)$. Compute $D_{\mathbf{u}}f$.

**Solution.** $\nabla f=(2x,4)=(2,4)$, $\mathbf{u}=(3/5,4/5)$, so $D_{\mathbf{u}}f=6/5+16/5=22/5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dotting $\nabla f$ with a non-unit direction vector and calling that the directional derivative.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
