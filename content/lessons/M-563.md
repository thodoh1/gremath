# Lesson M-563 — Subgroups of cyclic groups

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Cosets & Lagrange** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-M.2`.


This lesson isolates **Subgroups of cyclic groups** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subgroups of cyclic groups**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Cosets & Lagrange*

Left/right cosets partition $G$. $|G|=|H|\,[G:H]$. Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Consequences: orders of elements divide $|G|$; groups of prime order are cyclic.

### This skill, specifically

Left/right cosets partition $G$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Subgroups of a cyclic group: if $G=\langle g\rangle$ has order $n$, then for each $d\mid n$ there is a unique subgroup of order $d$, namely $\langle g^{n/d}\rangle$. It is cyclic. Infinite cyclic $\mathbb{Z}$ has unique subgroup of each finite index $n$, namely $n\mathbb{Z}$, and they are all cyclic. Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ correspond to divisors of $n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$. Which has order $4$?

**Solution.** Divisors $1,2,3,4,6,12$. Unique of order $4$: $\langle 3\rangle=\{0,3,6,9\}$. Unique of order $6$: $\langle 2\rangle=\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Number of subgroups of a cyclic group of order $p^3$

**Solution.** $4$: orders $1,p,p^2,p^3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming uniqueness of a subgroup of order $d$ in a noncyclic group (Klein has three order-$2$ subgroups).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** The unique subgroup of order $5$ in $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $\langle 2\rangle=\{0,2,4,6,8\}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z},+)$.

**Solution.** $n\mathbb{Z}$ for $n\ge 0$.
