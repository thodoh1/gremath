# Lesson M-356 — Cylindrical coordinates

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multiple integration** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Cylindrical coordinates** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cylindrical coordinates**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multiple integration*

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map. $|\det D\Phi|$ is the volume distortion. Setup beats brute force.

### This skill, specifically

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Cylindrical: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, $z=z$, $dV=r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz$. Best for solids with circular symmetry about the $z$-axis (cylinders, paraboloids, cones).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Volume of the unit cylinder $x^2+y^2\le 1$, $0\le z\le 1$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^1 r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Ice cream: cone $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ under the hemisphere $z=\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}$

**Solution.** Cylindrical: $z$ from $r$ to $\sqrt{1-r^2}$, $r$ from $0$ to $1/\sqrt{2}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $dV=dr\,d\theta\,dz$ without $r$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Mass of $\rho=r$ on the unit cylinder $0\le z\le 1$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 r\cdot r\,dr=\int_0^{2\pi} 1/3=2\pi/3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Cylindrical $r$ is

**Solution.** distance to the $z$-axis.
