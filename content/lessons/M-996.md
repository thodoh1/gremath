# Lesson M-996 — Analyze convergence ranges

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Parameter problems** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.4-E.5-K.7-G.2`.


This lesson isolates **Analyze convergence ranges** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Analyze convergence ranges**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Parameter problems*

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter. Degenerate cases (zero leading coefficient, repeated roots) are often the point.

### This skill, specifically

Track how the number of roots, extrema, eigenvalues, or convergence depends on a parameter.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Convergence that depends on a parameter: ratio/root tests for series $\sum a_n(x)$, radius of power series $1/R=\limsup |c_n|^{1/n}$, $p$-integrals $\int_1^\infty x^{-p}$ converges iff $p>1$, geometric $|r|<1$. Always treat endpoints of an interval of convergence separately. Absolute vs conditional convergence can split at endpoints.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For which $p$ does $\int_0^1 x^{p}\,dx$ converge?

**Solution.** Need $p>-1$ (antiderivative $x^{p+1}/(p+1)$ at $0$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Radius of $\sum n^n x^n$: $R=$

**Solution.** $0$ (root test $|n x|\to\infty$ unless $x=0$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Including endpoints automatically with the open interval from the ratio test, or using $p>1$ for $\int_0^1 x^{-p}\,dx$ (that integral is about $0$, not $\infty$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{(x-2)^n}{n 3^n}$: interval of convergence.

**Solution.** $[-1,5)$ because $R=3$, endpoint $x=5$ is harmonic, $x=-1$ is $-$harmonic hence converges.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Geometric $\sum (2x)^n$ converges iff

**Solution.** $|2x|<1$, i.e. $|x|<1/2$ (not at endpoints).
