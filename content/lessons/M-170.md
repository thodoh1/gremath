# Lesson M-170 — Circle-line intersections

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Coordinate geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Circle-line intersections** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Circle-line intersections**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coordinate geometry*

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra. Tangency is discriminant zero. 3D distance is the same Pythagorean idea with one more square.

### This skill, specifically

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A line $y=mx+k$ meets $x^2+y^2=r^2$ by substitution: a quadratic in $x$. Discriminant $>0$ two points, $=0$ tangent, $<0$ miss.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Intersection of $y=x$ with $x^2+y^2=2$.

**Solution.** $2x^2=2$, $x=\pm 1$, points $(\pm 1,\pm 1)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** When is $y=1$ tangent to $x^2+y^2=1$?

**Solution.** $x^2+1=1$, $x=0$, one point $(0,1)$: tangent.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Solving and discarding a valid negative root.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** How many intersections of $x=2$ with $x^2+y^2=1$?

**Solution.** Zero.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $x^2+y^2=25$ and $y=3x$ points.

**Solution.** $x^2+9x^2=25$, $x=\pm\sqrt{2.5}=\pm\sqrt{5/2}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
