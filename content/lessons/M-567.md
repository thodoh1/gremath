# Lesson M-567 — Injective/surjective homomorphisms

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Homomorphisms & quotients** · High

Prerequisites: `M.3`.


This lesson isolates **Injective/surjective homomorphisms** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Injective/surjective homomorphisms**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Homomorphisms & quotients*

A homomorphism preserves the operation. Kernel is a normal subgroup. First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\phi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\phi$. Quotients exist precisely because of normality.

### This skill, specifically

A homomorphism preserves the operation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A homomorphism is injective iff $\ker=\{e\}$, iff it is a monomorphism, iff it embeds $G$ as a subgroup of the codomain. It is surjective iff $\mathrm{im}$ is the whole codomain (epimorphism). A bijective hom is an isomorphism; the inverse map is automatically a hom. Finite groups: injective $\Leftrightarrow$ surjective when $|G|=|K|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\phi:\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$, $\phi([x]_4)=[3x]_6$ well-defined and injective?

**Solution.** Well-defined: $x\equiv x'\pmod{4}$ $\Rightarrow$ $3x\equiv 3x'\pmod{12}$ hence $\pmod{6}$? $x=x'+4k$, $3x=3x'+12k\equiv 3x'\pmod{6}$. Kernel: $3x\equiv 0\pmod{6}$ iff $x$ even, so $\ker=\{0,2\}\neq 0$, not injective.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Inclusion $n\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ is

**Solution.** injective, not surjective for $n\neq\pm 1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking $\phi(a)=\phi(b)\Rightarrow a=b$ only on generators and skipping the kernel test.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Can an injective hom $\mathbb{Z}/2\to S_3$ exist?

**Solution.** Yes: send the generator to any transposition.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $|G|=|K|$ finite and $\phi:G\to K$ is a hom with trivial kernel, then

**Solution.** it is an isomorphism.
