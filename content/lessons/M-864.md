# Lesson M-864 — Moment-generating function definition

Part T. T. Probability · **Generating/transform methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.4-T.6; H.4`.


This lesson isolates **Moment-generating function definition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Moment-generating function definition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Generating/transform methods*

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases. Transformations: CDF method, Jacobian for densities.

### This skill, specifically

MGF $E[e^{tX}]$, derivatives at $0$ give moments, uniqueness in nice cases.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The moment-generating function of $X$ is $M_X(t)=\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]$, defined on a neighborhood of $0$ (or at least at $0$, where $M(0)=1$). Discrete: $\sum e^{tx}p(x)$. Continuous: $\int e^{tx}f(x)\,dx$. Independent sum: $M_{X+Y}=M_X M_Y$. Bernoulli: $1-p+pe^t$. Poisson($\lambda$): $\exp(\lambda(e^t-1))$. $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$: $\exp(\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2)$. Exp($\lambda$): $\lambda/(\lambda-t)$ for $t<\lambda$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Bern}(1/3)$. Write $M_X(t)$ and $M_X(0)$.

**Solution.** $M(t)=\frac23+\frac13 e^t$. $M(0)=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $X\sim\mathrm{Pois}(4)$. $M_X(t)$.

**Solution.** $\exp\bigl(4(e^t-1)\bigr)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $M(t)=\mathbb{E}[t^X]$ (that is a probability-generating function, with $t$ replaced by $e^t$ to get an MGF), or multiplying MGFs without independence.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** MGF of $N(0,1)$.

**Solution.** $e^{t^2/2}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Independent $X\sim\mathrm{Pois}(2)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Pois}(3)$. MGF of $X+Y$, hence the law.

**Solution.** $\exp(5(e^t-1))$, so $\mathrm{Pois}(5)$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
