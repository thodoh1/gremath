# Lesson M-443 — Homogeneous systems

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Systems & row reduction** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2`.


This lesson isolates **Homogeneous systems** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Homogeneous systems**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems & row reduction*

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm. REF/RREF, consistency, free variables, rank. Homogeneous systems always have at least the zero solution.

### This skill, specifically

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A homogeneous system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$ is always consistent ($\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$). Nontrivial solutions exist iff there is a free variable iff $\mathrm{Nul}\,A\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}$ iff $A$ is singular when $A$ is square. The solution set is a subspace (the null space).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$.

**Solution.** $x+2y=0$, so $\mathbf{x}=t\begin{pmatrix}-2\\1\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A $3\times 3$ invertible $A$ has homogeneous solutions

**Solution.** only $\mathbf{0}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing 'no solution' for $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$ because RREF has a zero row (zero rows are harmless when the right-hand side is $0$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Nontrivial homogeneous solutions exist for a $3\times 5$ matrix?

**Solution.** Always: at most $3$ pivots, at least $2$ free variables.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** The homogeneous solution set is closed under

**Solution.** addition and scalar multiplication.
