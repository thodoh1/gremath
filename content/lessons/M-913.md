# Lesson M-913 — Incenter

Part V. V. Geometry · **Euclidean geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Incenter** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Geometry is additional topics plus analytic geometry inside calculus/algebra items.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Incenter**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The incenter $I$ is the intersection of the interior angle bisectors and is the center of the unique incircle, which is tangent to all three sides. Inradius $r=A/s$ where $A$ is area and $s$ is the semiperimeter. Distances from $I$ to the sides equal $r$. The incenter is equidistant from the sides, not necessarily from the vertices.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Right triangle $3$-$4$-$5$: $r=A/s$.

**Solution.** $A=6$, $s=6$, $r=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Inradius of an equilateral triangle of side $2$.

**Solution.** Altitude $\sqrt{3}$, $A=\sqrt{3}$, $s=3$, $r=1/\sqrt{3}=\sqrt{3}/3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $r=A/s$ with perimeter instead of semiperimeter, or thinking $I$ is equidistant from the vertices (that is the circumcenter).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Tangents from a vertex to the points of tangency of the incircle are

**Solution.** equal in length.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $I$ of a right triangle lies

**Solution.** inside; $r=(a+b-c)/2$ for legs $a,b$ and hypotenuse $c$.
