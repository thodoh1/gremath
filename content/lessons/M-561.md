# Lesson M-561 — Lagrange theorem

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Cosets & Lagrange** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-M.2`.


This lesson isolates **Lagrange theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Lagrange theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Cosets & Lagrange*

Left/right cosets partition $G$. $|G|=|H|\,[G:H]$. Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Consequences: orders of elements divide $|G|$; groups of prime order are cyclic.

### This skill, specifically

Left/right cosets partition $G$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Lagrange's theorem: if $H\le G$ and $G$ is finite, then $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Proof: the left cosets partition $G$ into $[G:H]$ sets each of size $|H|$. Consequence: $|g|$ divides $|G|$ for every $g$. The converse is false: $A_4$ has order $12$ but no subgroup of order $6$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Possible orders of subgroups of a group of order $15$. Does $A_4$ have a subgroup of order $6$?

**Solution.** Divisors of $15$: $1,3,5,15$. (In fact groups of order $15$ are cyclic, so all occur.) $A_4$: no subgroup of order $6$, even though $6\mid 12$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
