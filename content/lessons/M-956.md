# Lesson M-956 — Annulus of convergence

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Annulus of convergence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Annulus of convergence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A Laurent series $\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty c_n (z-a)^n$ converges in an annulus $r<|z-a|<R$, where $R$ is governed by the nonnegative powers (like a Taylor radius) and $r$ by the negative powers (a Taylor radius in $1/(z-a)$). On the inner/outer circles, convergence is a separate 1D question. The maximal annulus is limited by singularities.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Where does $\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n + \sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-n}$ converge?

**Solution.** The first sum needs $|z|<1$, the second $|z|>1$, so nowhere. (Geometric inner and outer conflict.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty 2^{-|n|} z^n$ converges for

**Solution.** $1/2<|z|<2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting a disk of convergence for a series with infinitely many negative powers, or ignoring the inner radius.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

**Drill 1.** Laurent of $1/(z(z-2))$ about $0$, valid in $0<|z|<2$: inner/outer?

**Solution.** Inner $0$ (pole at $0$), outer $2$ (pole at $2$).
