# Lesson M-581 — Classification of finite abelian groups — statement

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Group actions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4`.


This lesson isolates **Classification of finite abelian groups — statement** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Classification of finite abelian groups — statement**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Group actions*

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Orbit-stabilizer: $|G|=|\mathrm{Orb}|\,|\mathrm{Stab}|$. Conjugacy is a special action. Finite abelian groups decompose into cyclic groups.

### This skill, specifically

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Classification of finite abelian groups: every finite abelian group is isomorphic to a unique product $\mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z}$ with $n_{i+1}\mid n_i$ (invariant factors), and also to a unique product of cyclic groups of prime-power order $\prod\mathbb{Z}/p_i^{e_i}\mathbb{Z}$ (elementary divisors), unique up to ordering. The two forms convert by grouping prime powers and using CRT when exponents allow.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** List the abelian groups of order $12$ up to isomorphism, in both forms.

**Solution.** Elementary divisors: $\mathbb{Z}/4\times\mathbb{Z}/3\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$ and $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/3\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/6$. Invariant factors: $\mathbb{Z}/12$ and $\mathbb{Z}/6\times\mathbb{Z}/2$. Two groups.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Number of abelian groups of order $p^3$

**Solution.** Three: $\mathbb{Z}/p^3$, $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\times\mathbb{Z}/p$, $\mathbb{Z}/p\times\mathbb{Z}/p\times\mathbb{Z}/p$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the divisibility condition and listing $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$ as a third invariant-factor form of order $8$ distinct from $\mathbb{Z}/4\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ (they are the same up to order).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Abelian groups of order $pq$, distinct primes.

**Solution.** Only $\mathbb{Z}/pq$ (CRT).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/3\cong\mathbb{Z}/6\times\mathbb{Z}/2$?

**Solution.** Yes.
