# Lesson M-855 — Uniform distribution

Part T. T. Probability · **Common distributions** · High

Prerequisites: `T.2-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Uniform distribution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Uniform distribution**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Common distributions*

### This skill, specifically

Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, negative binomial, hypergeometric, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal, gamma/beta at property level.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Uniform family: discrete $\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,n\}$ has PMF $1/n$, mean $(n+1)/2$, variance $(n^2-1)/12$. Continuous $\mathrm{Unif}(a,b)$ as in M-840. A uniform law is the maximum-entropy distribution on a bounded interval (continuous) / finite set (discrete) with no further constraints.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Discrete uniform on $\{1,2,\ldots,9\}$. Mean and $P(X\ge 7)$.

**Solution.** Mean $5$; $P=3/9=1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Variance of $\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,6\}$ (a fair die).

**Solution.** $(36-1)/12=35/12$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using continuous variance $(b-a)^2/12$ on a die, or mean $n/2$ for $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $X\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,1,2,3,4\}$. $\mathbb{E}[X]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X^2]$.

**Solution.** Mean $2$; $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=(0+1+4+9+16)/5=6$, $\mathrm{Var}=6-4=2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Continuous $\mathrm{Unif}(3,9)$ vs discrete $\mathrm{Unif}\{3,\ldots,9\}$. Means?

**Solution.** Both $6$; variances $3$ vs $(49-1)/12=4$.
