# Lesson M-430 — Matrix multiplication

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Matrices** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1`.


This lesson isolates **Matrix multiplication** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Matrix multiplication**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Matrices*

Matrices encode linear maps and systems. Multiplication is composition, not entrywise. Transpose, inverse, special shapes (diagonal, triangular, symmetric) are computational gold.

### This skill, specifically

Matrices encode linear maps and systems.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The product $AB$ is defined when $A$ is $m\times n$ and $B$ is $n\times p$; then $AB$ is $m\times p$ with $(AB)_{ij}=$ (row $i$ of $A$)$\cdot$(column $j$ of $B$). Matrix multiplication is associative and distributive, not commutative in general. $A\mathbf{x}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$ with weights from $\mathbf{x}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\4&3\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $(1,2,3)\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}$ (row times column) equals

**Solution.** $-2$, a $1\times 1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Multiplying entrywise (Hadamard product) when matrix product is required, or multiplying $2\times 3$ by $2\times 3$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $AB=BA$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$?

**Solution.** No: $AB=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $BA=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Size of a $2\times 5$ times a $5\times 3$ product.

**Solution.** $2\times 3$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
