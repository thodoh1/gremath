# Lesson M-712 — Existential quantifier

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Predicate logic** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Existential quantifier** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Existential quantifier**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Predicate logic*

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$). Necessary vs sufficient is $\to$ direction. This is how $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ is written.

### This skill, specifically

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$).

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The existential quantifier $\exists x\,P(x)$ means $P(a)$ holds for at least one $a$ in the universe. A witness is such an $a$. $\exists x\in A,\,P(x)$ means $\exists x\,(x\in A\wedge P(x))$. Uniqueness $\exists! x$ means existence plus uniqueness.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Give a witness for $\exists x\in\mathbb{Z},\, x^2=4$ and decide $\exists x\in\mathbb{Q},\, x^2=2$.

**Solution.** Witness $x=2$ (or $-2$). The second is false.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\exists x\in\emptyset,\, P(x)$ is

**Solution.** false.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing $\exists$ with $\forall$, or thinking a constructive witness is required in classical logic (nonconstructive existence is allowed).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $\exists n\in\mathbb{N},\, n^2=n$ hold?

**Solution.** Yes: $n=0$ or $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Translate ``some primes are even''.

**Solution.** $\exists p\,(P(p)\wedge E(p))$, witness $2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
