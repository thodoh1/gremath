# Lesson M-684 — Subspace topology

Part P. P. Topology · **Topological spaces** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1`.


This lesson isolates **Subspace topology** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subspace topology**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Topological spaces*

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections. Bases, subbases, subspace and product topologies, continuity as preimages of opens, homeomorphism as topological isomorphism.

### This skill, specifically

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $A\subset X$, the subspace topology is $\{U\cap A:U\text{ open in }X\}$. A set open in $A$ need not be open in $X$. If $d$ is a metric on $X$, the restricted metric on $A$ induces the subspace topology. $[0,1/2)$ is open in $[0,1]$, not in $\mathbb{R}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $[0,1)$ open in the subspace $[0,1]$? In $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes in $[0,1]$: $[0,1)=(-1,1)\cap[0,1]$. No in $\mathbb{R}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Closed sets in the subspace $A$ are

**Solution.** intersections of $A$ with closed sets of $X$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming every relatively open set is open in the ambient space.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\{0\}$ open in $\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}$ with the subspace topology?

**Solution.** Yes: $\{0\}=(-1/2,1/2)\cap\mathbb{Z}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Subspace topology on $\mathbb{Q}$: is $\{0\}$ open in $\mathbb{Q}$?

**Solution.** No: every ambient interval about $0$ contains other rationals.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
