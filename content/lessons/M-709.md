# Lesson M-709 — De Morgan laws

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Propositional logic** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **De Morgan laws** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **De Morgan laws**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Propositional logic*

Propositions, $\land,\lor,\neg,\to,\leftrightarrow$, truth tables, tautology, contradiction, converse vs contrapositive, De Morgan. Implication is not the arrow in your heart; $P\to Q$ is false only when $P$ is true and $Q$ false.

### This skill, specifically

Propositions, $\land,\lor,\neg,\to,\leftrightarrow$, truth tables, tautology, contradiction, converse vs contrapositive, De Morgan.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

De Morgan's laws: $\neg(P\wedge Q)\equiv\neg P\vee\neg Q$ and $\neg(P\vee Q)\equiv\neg P\wedge\neg Q$. Quantified forms: $\neg\forall x\,P\equiv\exists x\,\neg P$. Set forms: $(A\cap B)^c=A^c\cup B^c$. Use them to push negations inward to atoms.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Negate ``$x>0$ and $x<1$'' and ``$x\le 0$ or $x\ge 1$''.

**Solution.** ``$x\le 0$ or $x\ge 1$''; ``$0<x<1$'' (i.e. $x>0$ and $x<1$). They are De Morgan duals.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\neg(P\wedge\neg Q)$ simplifies to

**Solution.** $\neg P\vee Q$, which is $P\to Q$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\neg(P\wedge Q)$ as $\neg P\wedge\neg Q$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Set form of the first De Morgan law.

**Solution.** $(A\cap B)^c=A^c\cup B^c$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Negate ``it is raining and cold''.

**Solution.** It is not raining or it is not cold (or both).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
