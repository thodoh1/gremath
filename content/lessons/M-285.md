# Lesson M-285 — Improper integrals with infinite limits

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Improper integrals** · High

Prerequisites: `F.2`.


This lesson isolates **Improper integrals with infinite limits** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Improper integrals with infinite limits**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Improper integrals*

Replace an infinite limit or a singularity by a proper integral and take a limit. Comparison is the series test’s twin. Convergence is a yes/no that does not require evaluating the integral.

### This skill, specifically

Replace an infinite limit or a singularity by a proper integral and take a limit.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An improper integral with infinite limits: $\int_a^\infty f=\lim_{B\to\infty}\int_a^B f$, and $\int_{-\infty}^b=\lim_{A\to-\infty}\int_A^b$. Two-sided $\int_{-\infty}^\infty$ needs both one-sided limits separately (not a single $A\to\infty$ of $\int_{-A}^A$, which is the Cauchy principal value).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\lim_{B\to\infty}(1-1/B)=1$, converges.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int_1^\infty x^{-1}\,dx$

**Solution.** $\lim\ln B=\infty$, diverges.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $\int_{-\infty}^\infty x\,dx$ as $0$ by oddness without checking that both sides diverge.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx$.

**Solution.** $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{x}{1+x^2}\,dx$ converge?

**Solution.** No (harmonic-like logs); PV is $0$.
