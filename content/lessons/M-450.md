# Lesson M-450 — Linear independence

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Linear independence** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Linear independence**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Vectors $\mathbf{v}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{v}_k$ are linearly independent if $c_1\mathbf{v}_1+\cdots+c_k\mathbf{v}_k=\mathbf{0}$ implies every $c_i=0$. Equivalently, none is a combination of the others, equivalently the matrix with those columns has trivial null space. In $\mathbb{R}^n$, more than $n$ vectors are dependent.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Are $(1,0),(0,1)$ independent? Are $(1,2),(2,4)$?

**Solution.** Yes. No: $2(1,2)-(2,4)=\mathbf{0}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ are always

**Solution.** linearly dependent.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding independence because no vector is a scalar multiple of one other, while missing a dependence involving three or more.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Give a linearly independent subset of $\{(1,1,0),(0,1,1),(1,0,-1),(2,2,0)\}$.

**Solution.** $\{(1,1,0),(0,1,1)\}$ (the third vector equals the first minus the second; the fourth is $2$ times the first).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** The empty set / a single nonzero vector is

**Solution.** independent.
