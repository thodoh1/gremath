# Lesson M-683 — Subbasis

Part P. P. Topology · **Topological spaces** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1`.


This lesson isolates **Subbasis** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Subbasis**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Topological spaces*

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections. Bases, subbases, subspace and product topologies, continuity as preimages of opens, homeomorphism as topological isomorphism.

### This skill, specifically

A topology is a family of “open sets” closed under unions and finite intersections.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A subbasis $\mathcal{S}$ generates a topology by taking finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{S}$ as a basis, then arbitrary unions. On $\mathbb{R}$, $\{(-\infty,a)\}\cup\{(a,+\infty)\}$ is a subbasis for the usual topology. The product topology is the coarsest topology making all projections continuous, generated by the subbasis $\pi_i^{-1}(U_i)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Finite intersections of $(-\infty,a)$ and $(b,+\infty)$ produce what basis elements?

**Solution.** Open rays and open intervals $(b,a)$ (when $b<a$), which generate the usual topology.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The product topology's subbasis consists of

**Solution.** inverse images of opens under the coordinate projections.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing subbasis (finite intersections first) with basis (unions only).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does a subbasis have to cover $X$?

**Solution.** No: the empty finite intersection is $X$, so $X$ is open in the generated topology anyway.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Subbasis of Sorgenfrey line: half-open intervals $[a,b)$. The generated topology is

**Solution.** finer than the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$.

## 9. Lock it in
