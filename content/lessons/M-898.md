# Lesson M-898 — Residuals

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Regression** · Medium

Prerequisites: `K.8-U.1`.


This lesson isolates **Residuals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Residuals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Residual $e_i=y_i-\hat y_i$. Algebra: $\sum e_i=0$ when an intercept is present, and $\sum x_i e_i=0$ (normal equations). Residual plots vs $x$ or vs $\hat y$ diagnose curvature, heteroskedasticity, and outliers. A small residual is not the same as an influential point (leverage).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Points $(0,0),(1,1),(2,1)$. The LS line is $\hat y=\tfrac12 x+\tfrac16$. Residual at $x=1$.

**Solution.** $\hat y(1)=2/3$, $y=1$, so $e=1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Sum of residuals with intercept included is

**Solution.** $0$.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $y_i-\bar y$ a residual, or assuming $\sum e_i=0$ in a no-intercept model $\hat y=\hat\beta_1 x$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
