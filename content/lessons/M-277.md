# Lesson M-277 — Volumes by washers

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Applications of integration** · High

Prerequisites: `F.2`.


This lesson isolates **Volumes by washers** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Volumes by washers**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Applications of integration*

## 3. Precise statement

Washer method: a hole means $V=\int \pi\bigl(R_{\mathrm{outer}}^2-R_{\mathrm{inner}}^2\bigr)\,dx$ (or $dy$). Radii are distances to the axis of rotation, not necessarily the function values themselves if the axis is shifted.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Revolve the region between $y=x$ and $y=x^2$ on $[0,1]$ about the $x$-axis.

**Solution.** $\pi\int_0^1(x^2-x^4)\,dx=\pi(\frac13-\frac15)=\frac{2\pi}{15}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Same region about $y=1$

**Solution.** Washers in $x$: outer $1-x^2$, inner $1-x$, $V=\pi\int_0^1\bigl[(1-x^2)^2-(1-x)^2\bigr]dx$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Subtracting radii then squaring, instead of subtracting squares.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Revolve $y=1$ and $y=0$ on $[0,1]$ about $x$-axis: disk or washer?

**Solution.** Disk of radius $1$, volume $\pi$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Annulus area is

**Solution.** $\pi(R^2-r^2)$, the washer cross-section.
