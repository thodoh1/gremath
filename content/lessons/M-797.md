# Lesson M-797 — Spanning trees

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Spanning trees** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Spanning trees**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Special graph classes*

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton. Euler is degree-parity; Hamilton is hard.

### This skill, specifically

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A spanning tree of a connected graph $G$ is a subgraph that is a tree on all of $V(G)$. It has $n-1$ edges. Cayley's formula: $K_n$ has $n^{n-2}$ distinct spanning trees (labeled). Kruskal/Prim find minimum-weight spanning trees when edges are weighted.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Spanning trees of $K_4$ (labeled), by Cayley.

**Solution.** $4^{2}=16$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $C_5$ have a spanning tree? How many edges does it use?

**Solution.** Yes: delete any one edge, get $P_5$, which has $4$ edges.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- A spanning tree of a disconnected graph (it cannot exist), or counting spanning trees of $K_n$ as $n!$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A connected graph on $20$ vertices. Edges in any spanning tree?

**Solution.** $19$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** How many spanning trees has $K_{2,3}$?

**Solution.** $m^{n-1}n^{m-1}=2^{2}3^{1}=12$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
