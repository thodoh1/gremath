# Lesson M-1029 — Analyze every missed question by concept and trap

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Analyze every missed question by concept and trap** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Analyze every missed question by concept and trap**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Hard-problem training*

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

### This skill, specifically

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

For every missed item, log four fields: (1) governing concept (theorem/definition), (2) trap type (hypothesis, algebra, off-by-one, distractor, pace), (3) the one-line correct solution, (4) a sibling drill you then solve correctly. ‘I was careless’ is not a concept. A repeated concept in the log is a Level-1 hole, not a personality trait. Review the log before the next timed block.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Missed: used $s^2$ with divisor $n$ on a $5$-point sample asked for the unbiased variance, got $8/5$ instead of $2$. Log it.

**Solution.** Concept: unbiased sample variance $n-1$. Trap: population/MLE divisor $n$. Correct: $\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2/(n-1)$. Sibling: data $1,3,5$, $s^2=4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A miss on $\int \dfrac{dx}{x^2+1}$ written as $\ln(x^2+1)$ is concept

**Solution.** arctan vs log, trap ‘every $1/f$ is $\ln|f|$’.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Logging only the score ‘$4$ wrong’ with no concept names.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Sibling drill after missing Rolle by skipping $f(a)=f(b)$: apply Rolle to $f(x)=x^2-1$ on $[-1,1]$.

**Solution.** $f' =2x=0$ at $0$, and $f(-1)=f(1)=0$, hypotheses hold.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If the same trap (division by $x$) appears three times in a week, the next practice set must

**Solution.** include three explicit ‘do not divide by the unknown’ equations, then retest.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
