# Lesson M-884 — Consistency intuition

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.1-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Consistency intuition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Consistency intuition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Estimation*

Sample vs population, estimator as a random variable, bias $E[\hat\theta]-\theta$, unbiasedness, consistency, likelihood and MLE, Fisher information as awareness.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Consistency: $\hat\theta_n\to\theta$ in probability as $n\to\infty$ (for each fixed $\theta$). Sufficient typical conditions: bias$\to 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta_n)\to 0$ (then Chebyshev). WLLN says $\bar X$ is consistent for $\mu$ when iid with finite mean. Consistency is asymptotic; unbiasedness is finite-sample.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is $\bar X$ consistent for $\mu$ if $\mathrm{Var}(X_i)=\sigma^2<\infty$?

**Solution.** $E[\bar X]=\mu$, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)=\sigma^2/n\to 0$, so Chebyshev gives $P(|\bar X-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le \sigma^2/(n\varepsilon^2)\to 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is an unbiased estimator automatically consistent?

**Solution.** No: $X_1$ is unbiased for $\mu$ but does not improve with $n$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing consistency with unbiasedness, or thinking ‘consistent’ means ‘no arithmetic contradiction’.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** MLE $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ for normal $\sigma^2$: consistent?

**Solution.** Yes (bias vanishes, variance $\to 0$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $2\bar X$ consistently estimate $\mu$?

**Solution.** No: it converges to $2\mu$.

## 9. Lock it in
