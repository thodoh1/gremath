# Lesson M-273 — Accumulation functions

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Accumulation functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Accumulation functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An accumulation function $A(x)=\int_a^x f$ measures net accumulation of $f$ from $a$ to $x$. $A(a)=0$, $A'(x)=f(x)$, and $A$ is increasing where $f>0$. Units of $A$ are (units of $f$)$\times$(units of $x$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing accumulation with total distance (that would be $\int|f|$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
