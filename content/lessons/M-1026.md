# Lesson M-1026 — Solve questions where the shortest route differs from the standard textbook method

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Solve questions where the shortest route differs from the standard textbook method** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Solve questions where the shortest route differs from the standard textbook method**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The shortest GRE route is often not the textbook chapter method: row-dependence beats cofactor expansion; oddness beats finding an antiderivative; Vieta beats solving; a symmetry $f(x)+f(1-x)$ beats two substitutions; $\mathrm{tr}$ and $\det$ beat finding all eigenvectors if the question only needs the characteristic polynomial of a $2\times 2$. Ask: ‘what would make this a one-liner?’

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_0^{\pi/2}\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx$. Textbook: $t=\tan x$. Shortcut: $I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx$ by $x\mapsto \pi/2-x$, so $2I=\int_0^{\pi/2}1=\pi/2$, $I=\pi/4$.

**Solution.** $\pi/4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4\\ 0&5&6&7\\ 0&0&8&9\\ 0&0&0&10\end{pmatrix}=$

**Solution.** $1\cdot 5\cdot 8\cdot 10=400$ (triangular), not $24$ cofactors.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Computing a four-term partial-fraction decomposition when the definite integral of an odd function on a symmetric interval is $0$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $p(x)=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$. $p'(2)$ without expanding: logarithmic or product rule

**Solution.** $(2-1)(2-3)=-1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Average of $\sin$ over a full period is

**Solution.** $0$, no antiderivative needed if the question only asks the average.
