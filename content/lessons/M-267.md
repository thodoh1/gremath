# Lesson M-267 — Riemann sums

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Definite integrals** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-E.1`.


This lesson isolates **Riemann sums** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Riemann sums**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Definite integrals*

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area. FTC I says the derivative of $\int_a^x f$ is $f(x)$; FTC II says $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$. Accumulation and average value are FTC in costume.

### This skill, specifically

The definite integral is a limit of Riemann sums, equal (for nice $f$) to net signed area.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A Riemann sum for $f$ on $[a,b]$ partitioned into $n$ subintervals of width $\Delta x_i$ is $\sum f(x_i^*)\Delta x_i$, with $x_i^*$ a sample (left, right, midpoint). As mesh $\to 0$, this $\to\int_a^b f$ when $f$ is integrable.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Right Riemann sum for $x^2$ on $[0,1]$ with $n=4$.

**Solution.** $\Delta x=1/4$, heights $(1/4)^2,\ldots,1^2$, sum $\frac14(\frac1{16}+\frac4{16}+\frac9{16}+1)=\frac{15}{32}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Left vs right sums for an increasing $f$

**Solution.** Left underestimates, right overestimates.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $n$ sample points but $\Delta x=1$ instead of $(b-a)/n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Midpoint sum for $f=x$ on $[0,2]$ with $n=2$.

**Solution.** $\Delta x=1$, samples $0.5,1.5$, sum $2$. Exact integral is also $2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Write $\int_0^1 x\,dx$ as $\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^n (k/n)(1/n)$.

**Solution.** Right sums: $\lim \frac{n(n+1)}{2n^2}=1/2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
