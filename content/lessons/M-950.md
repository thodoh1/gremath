## 3. Precise statement

The contour integral $\int_\gamma f=\int_a^b f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\,dt$. It is linear in $f$ and additive under concatenation. $|\int_\gamma f|\le \mathrm{length}(\gamma)\cdot\sup_\gamma|f|$. If $f$ has an antiderivative $F$ on a domain containing $\gamma$, then $\int_\gamma f=F(\gamma(b))-F(\gamma(a))$, hence $0$ on closed curves.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\int_\gamma z\,dz$ along the unit circle $\gamma(t)=e^{it}$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$.

**Solution.** $\int_0^{2\pi} e^{it}\cdot ie^{it}\,dt=i\int_0^{2\pi} e^{2it}\,dt=0$. (Also $z^2/2$ is an antiderivative.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
