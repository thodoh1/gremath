# Lesson M-804 — Planar graphs

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Coloring & planarity** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.2`.


This lesson isolates **Planar graphs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Planar graphs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coloring & planarity*

Chromatic number, $\chi(G)\le \Delta+1$, bipartite iff $2$-colorable. Planar graphs, Euler’s formula, $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ as nonplanar signatures.

### This skill, specifically

Chromatic number, $\chi(G)\le \Delta+1$, bipartite iff $2$-colorable.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A graph is planar if it can be drawn in the plane with no edge crossings. $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ are the forbidden minors (Kuratowski/Wagner). Subdivisions of those are nonplanar. Planar graphs are $4$-colorable (four-color theorem); $K_4$ is planar, $K_5$ is not.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $K_4$ planar? $K_5$? $K_{2,6}$?

**Solution.** $K_4$ yes. $K_5$ no. $K_{2,6}$ is a star-like complete bipartite with a part of size $2$, planar (all edges can go without crossing).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Any tree is planar?

**Solution.** Yes.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Declaring a graph nonplanar because a particular drawing has crossings (re-draw it). $K_4$ often looks crossed.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $C_{20}$ planar?

**Solution.** Yes, as a convex $20$-gon.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $K_{3,3}$ minus one edge. Planar?

**Solution.** Yes: that graph is a subdivision of something planar; explicitly drawable.
