# Lesson M-336 — Directional derivatives

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Differentiability** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-K.4`.


This lesson isolates **Directional derivatives** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Directional derivatives**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Differentiability*

Existence of partials is not differentiability. The derivative is a linear map (Jacobian). Directional derivatives are $\nabla f\cdot u$. The chain rule in several variables is matrix multiplication of Jacobians.

### This skill, specifically

Existence of partials is not differentiability.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The directional derivative of $f$ at $\mathbf{a}$ in the direction of a unit vector $\mathbf{u}$ is $D_{\mathbf{u}}f(\mathbf{a})=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(\mathbf{a}+h\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{a})}{h}$. If $f$ is differentiable, $D_{\mathbf{u}}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{u}$. The maximum is $\|\nabla f\|$ in the direction of $\nabla f$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $f=x^2+y^2$ at $(1,0)$, $\mathbf{u}=(0,1)$. $D_{\mathbf{u}}f$.

**Solution.** $\nabla f=(2x,2y)=(2,0)$, dot $(0,1)=0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Steepest ascent of $f=xy$ at $(1,1)$

**Solution.** $\nabla f=(y,x)=(1,1)$, unit $\mathbf{u}=(1/\sqrt{2},1/\sqrt{2})$, rate $\sqrt{2}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using a non-unit $\mathbf{u}$ in $\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{u}$ and calling it the directional derivative (that is a scaled version).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f=x-2y$ at $(0,0)$ toward $(3,4)$. Unit?

**Solution.** $(3/5,4/5)$, $D_{\mathbf{u}}=3/5-8/5=-1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $\nabla f=0$, all directional derivatives

**Solution.** are $0$ (when differentiability holds).
