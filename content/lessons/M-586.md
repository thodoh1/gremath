# Lesson M-586 — Integral domains

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Integral domains** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Integral domains**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An integral domain is a commutative unital ring $R\neq 0$ with no zero divisors: $ab=0\Rightarrow a=0$ or $b=0$. Equivalent: cancellation $ab=ac$ and $a\neq 0\Rightarrow b=c$. $\mathbb{Z}$, $F[x]$ for a field $F$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are domains. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain iff $n$ is prime (then it is a field). A finite integral domain is a field.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Which of $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ are integral domains?

**Solution.** $\mathbb{Z}/7$ is a field, hence a domain. $\mathbb{Z}/8$: $2\cdot 4=0$, not a domain. $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a domain because $\mathbb{Z}$ is.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Finite domains are

**Solution.** fields.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ a domain because $4$ is a prime power.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\mathbb{Z}/pq\mathbb{Z}$ a domain for distinct primes $p,q$?

**Solution.** No: $\bar p\bar q=0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Cancellation in $\mathbb{Z}$: $2x=2y\Rightarrow x=y$. In $\mathbb{Z}/6$?

**Solution.** No: $2\cdot 2=2\cdot 5\bmod 6$ but $2\neq 5$.
