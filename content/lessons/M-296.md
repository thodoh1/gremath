# Lesson M-296 — Cauchy sequences

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Sequences** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1; O.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cauchy sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cauchy sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Sequences*

### This skill, specifically

Sequence convergence, monotonicity, boundedness, subsequences, Cauchy sequences, and completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ are analysis language used on calculus items.

## 3. Precise statement

$(a_n)$ is Cauchy if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N$ so that $m,n>N$ implies $|a_m-a_n|<\varepsilon$. In $\mathbb{R}$, Cauchy $\Leftrightarrow$ convergent. Cauchy is an intrinsic test: you do not name $L$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $a_n=1/n$ is Cauchy.

**Solution.** $|1/m-1/n|\le 1/N$ for $m,n>N$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a_n=H_n$ (harmonic) is Cauchy?

**Solution.** No: $H_{2n}-H_n\ge 1/2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Proving Cauchy by $|a_n-L|<\varepsilon$ without knowing you need completeness to get $L$ from Cauchy.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** In $\mathbb{R}$, Cauchy sequences converge: true or false?

**Solution.** True.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A sequence with $|a_{n+1}-a_n|\to 0$ is Cauchy?

**Solution.** Not necessarily ($H_n$).

## 9. Lock it in
