# Lesson M-785 — Simple graphs

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Simple graphs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Simple graphs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A simple graph has no loops and no multiple edges. Then $0\le |E|\le\binom{|V|}{2}$ and $0\le\deg(v)\le n-1$. Many GRE statements (Turán, planar bounds, $\chi\le\Delta+1$) assume simplicity unless they say otherwise.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Maximum edges in a simple graph on $7$ vertices.

**Solution.** $\binom{7}{2}=21$ (namely $K_7$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can a simple graph on $5$ vertices have a vertex of degree $5$?

**Solution.** No: max degree $4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying $\binom{n}{2}$ as an upper bound to multigraphs, where parallel edges can exceed it.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Simple graphs on $3$ labeled vertices: how many?

**Solution.** $2^{\binom{3}{2}}=8$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A looped vertex of degree $2$ and nothing else: simple?

**Solution.** No, a loop is forbidden in a simple graph.
