## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Inner-product spaces*

Inner products give geometry: orthogonality, norms, projections, Gram–Schmidt, least squares. Symmetric real matrices are orthogonally diagonalizable (spectral theorem). SVD is the rectangular cousin.

## 3. Precise statement

A real matrix $Q$ is orthogonal if $Q^T Q=I$, equivalently columns (and rows) form an ONB of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Then $Q^{-1}=Q^T$, $\det Q=\pm 1$, and $Q$ preserves dots and lengths: $|Q\mathbf{x}|=|\mathbf{x}|$. Rotations have $\det=1$; reflections through hyperplanes have $\det=-1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ orthogonal? Interpret.

**Solution.** $Q^T Q=I$, $\det=1$: rotation by $\pi/2$ counterclockwise.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $Q^{-1}$ for orthogonal $Q$ is

**Solution.** $Q^T$.
