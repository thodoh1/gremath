This lesson isolates **Finite-field basics F_{p^n}** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Finite-field basics F_{p^n}**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Fields & extensions*

A field has every nonzero element invertible. Extensions $K/F$ have degree $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. Algebraic vs transcendental, minimal polynomials, splitting fields, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

### This skill, specifically

A field has every nonzero element invertible.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

For each prime $p$ and $n\ge 1$ there is a unique (up to iso) finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ of order $p^n$, namely the splitting field of $x^{p^n}-x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. It is a degree-$n$ extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$. $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^\times$ is cyclic of order $p^n-1$. Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ are $\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ for $d\mid n$, unique for each $d$. Construction: $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\cong\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f)$ for any irreducible $f$ of degree $n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Construct $\mathbb{F}_4$ as $\mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)$ and list the subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{16}$.

**Solution.** $x^2+x+1$ is the unique irr quadratic over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Let $\omega=\bar x$, then $\omega^2=\omega+1$ (char $2$), $\mathbb{F}_4=\{0,1,\omega,\omega+1\}$ with $\omega^3=1$. Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{16}=\mathbb{F}_{2^4}$: $d\mid 4$ so $\mathbb{F}_2$, $\mathbb{F}_4$, $\mathbb{F}_{16}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $\mathbb{F}_8\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{16}$ (orders $8=2^3$, $16=2^4$, $3\nmid 4$, so no containment).

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $\mathbb{F}_{9}$ contain $\mathbb{F}_{3}$? $\mathbb{F}_{27}$?

**Solution.** $9=3^2$, so yes $\mathbb{F}_3\subset\mathbb{F}_9$. $27=3^3$, $2\nmid 3$, so $\mathbb{F}_9\not\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{27}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
