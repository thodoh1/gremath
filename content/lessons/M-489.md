# Lesson M-489 — Orthogonal complements

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Orthogonal complements**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Inner-product spaces*

Inner products give geometry: orthogonality, norms, projections, Gram–Schmidt, least squares. Symmetric real matrices are orthogonally diagonalizable (spectral theorem). SVD is the rectangular cousin.

## 3. Precise statement

The orthogonal complement of $W\subseteq V$ is $W^\perp=\{v\in V:\langle v,w\rangle=0\text{ for all }w\in W\}$, a subspace. In finite dimensions $V=W\oplus W^\perp$ and $\dim W+\dim W^\perp=\dim V$. $(W^\perp)^\perp=W$ if $W$ is a subspace of a finite-dimensional inner-product space. $\mathrm{Row}\,A$ and $\mathrm{Nul}\,A$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $W=\mathrm{span}\{(1,0,0)\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Describe $W^\perp$.

**Solution.** The $yz$-plane: $x=0$. Dimension $2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
