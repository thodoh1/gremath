# Lesson M-613 — Vector spaces as modules over fields

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Modules** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1`.


This lesson isolates **Vector spaces as modules over fields** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Vector spaces as modules over fields**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Modules*

Modules are vector spaces over rings. Submodules, quotients, homomorphisms, generators, free modules. When the ring is a field, you recover linear algebra.

### This skill, specifically

Modules are vector spaces over rings.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $F$ is a field, $F$-modules are precisely $F$-vector spaces. Submodules are subspaces, homs are linear maps, free modules are those with a basis (all vector spaces, assuming choice for the infinite case), rank is dimension. The structure theory of modules over a PID specializes to rational canonical form / Jordan form when $R=F[x]$ acting on a finite-dimensional $F$-space.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Reinterpret $T:V\to V$ linear as an $F[x]$-module. What is a cyclic submodule?

**Solution.** $x\cdot v=T(v)$, and $f(x)\cdot v=f(T)(v)$. A cyclic submodule $F[x]v$ is the $T$-invariant subspace spanned by $\{v,Tv,T^2v,\ldots\}$, the cyclic subspace generated by $v$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Dimension is the module-theoretic

**Solution.** rank (for free modules / vector spaces).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $\mathbb{Z}$-modules as vector spaces (no division by $n$ when $n\neq\pm 1$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Every finitely generated module over a field is

**Solution.** free of finite rank (choose a basis).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Jordan form exists when $F$ is

**Solution.** algebraically closed (or at least contains the eigenvalues).
