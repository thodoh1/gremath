# Lesson M-720 — Counterexample

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Counterexample** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Counterexample**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A single counterexample disproves a universal claim $\forall x\,P(x)$. It does not disprove an existential claim. To show a construction always works, examples are not enough. Check the exact quantifiers before hunting counterexamples.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Disprove: every injective $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ is surjective.

**Solution.** Counterexample $f(n)=n+1$: injective, misses $1$ (or $0$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $n\mapsto 2n$ on $\mathbb{N}$ disprove ``every injective self-map is surjective''?

**Solution.** Yes: injective, image the evens.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Offering a counterexample to an existential statement, or an example as a proof of $\forall$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Disprove ``if $f'>0$ everywhere then $f$ is unbounded''.

**Solution.** $\arctan$: $f'>0$ on $\mathbb{R}$ but range $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** One counterexample to $\forall n,\, 2^n>n^2$ over $\mathbb{N}$.

**Solution.** $n=2$: $4\not>4$. Also $n=3,4$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
