# Lesson M-843 — Joint PMF/PDF

Part T. T. Probability · **Joint distributions** · High

Prerequisites: `T.2-T.3`.


This lesson isolates **Joint PMF/PDF** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Joint PMF/PDF**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A discrete pair has joint PMF $p(x,y)=P(X=x,Y=y)$, with $\sum_{x,y}p=1$. A continuous pair has joint PDF $f(x,y)\ge 0$, $\iint f=1$, and $P((X,Y)\in A)=\iint_A f$. Independence of coordinates is a special joint, not the default.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Joint PMF $p(x,y)=c$ on $\{1,2,3\}\times\{1,2\}$. Find $c$ and $P(X=Y)$.

**Solution.** $6c=1$, $c=1/6$. $P(X=Y)=p(1,1)+p(2,2)=1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Joint PDF $f(x,y)=8xy$ on $0<x<y<1$? First check the region: actually take $f=2$ on the triangle $x>0,y>0,x+y<1$. Is that a PDF?

**Solution.** Area $1/2$, $\iint 2=1$, yes.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Factoring a joint as $f_X(x)f_Y(y)$ without justification, or integrating a joint PMF as if it were continuous.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $p(0,0)=0.1$, $p(0,1)=0.2$, $p(1,0)=0.3$, $p(1,1)=0.4$. Valid PMF? $P(X=1)$.

**Solution.** Sums to $1$, yes. $P(X=1)=0.7$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Support of a joint PDF is a disk. $P(Y>X)$ is generally

**Solution.** an integral over the half-disk $y>x$, not automatically $1/2$ unless the joint is symmetric.
