# Lesson M-165 — Point-slope form

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Coordinate geometry** · High

Prerequisites: `A.3; B.1`.


This lesson isolates **Point-slope form** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Point-slope form**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Coordinate geometry*

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra. Tangency is discriminant zero. 3D distance is the same Pythagorean idea with one more square.

### This skill, specifically

Distance, midpoint, slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular, point-line distance, and circle-line intersection are the plane as algebra.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Point-slope: $y-y_0=m(x-x_0)$. This is the fastest form when you have a point and a slope (or a tangent line in calculus).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Line through $(2,-1)$ with slope $3$.

**Solution.** $y+1=3(x-2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Tangent to $y=x^2$ at $x=1$ has slope $2$, so

**Solution.** $y-1=2(x-1)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $y=mx+b$ and solving for $b$ when point-slope is already done.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Line through $(0,0)$ slope $-1/2$.

**Solution.** $y=-x/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Rewrite $y-4=2(x+1)$ in slope-intercept.

**Solution.** $y=2x+6$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
