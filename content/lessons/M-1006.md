## 3. Precise statement

Illegal cancellation: from $ab=ac$ one may conclude $b=c$ only if $a\neq 0$. From $\dfrac{x(x-2)}{x}=x-2$ one must keep $x\neq 0$. Dividing a polynomial equation by $x-r$ drops the root $r$. In matrices, $AB=AC$ does not imply $B=C$ without invertibility of $A$. Never divide an inequality or equation by an unknown factor without a case split.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $x(x-1)=x$.

**Solution.** Bring to $x(x-1)-x=0$, $x(x-2)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x=2$. Dividing by $x$ at the start would lose $x=0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $A\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 0\end{pmatrix}=A\begin{pmatrix}0\\ 0\end{pmatrix}$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\ 0&0\end{pmatrix}$, may we cancel $A$?

**Solution.** No: $A$ is singular, and $(1,0)^\top\neq\mathbf{0}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dividing $x^2=x$ by $x$ and reporting only $x=1$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ as a function on its domain.

**Solution.** $x+2$ for $x\neq 2$; undefined at $2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
