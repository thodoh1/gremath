# Lesson M-846 — Independence of random variables

Part T. T. Probability · **Joint distributions** · High

Prerequisites: `T.2-T.3`.


This lesson isolates **Independence of random variables** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Independence of random variables**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Joint distributions*

### This skill, specifically

Joint PMF/PDF, marginals by summing/integrating, conditionals, independence as factorization, covariance matrices, $E[X|Y]$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Random variables $X,Y$ are independent if $p(x,y)=p_X(x)p_Y(y)$ for all $x,y$ (discrete) or $f(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)$ a.e. (continuous), equivalently $F_{X,Y}(x,y)=F_X(x)F_Y(y)$. Then $\mathbb{E}[g(X)h(Y)]=\mathbb{E}[g(X)]\mathbb{E}[h(Y)]$ for suitable $g,h$, and $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Table $p(0,0)=0.08$, $p(0,1)=0.12$, $p(1,0)=0.32$, $p(1,1)=0.48$. Independent?

**Solution.** $p_X(0)=0.2$, $p_Y(0)=0.4$, product $0.08$ matches; $0.2\cdot 0.6=0.12$; $0.8\cdot 0.4=0.32$; $0.8\cdot 0.6=0.48$. Yes.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Uniform on the triangle $x>0,y>0,x+y<1$. Independent?

**Solution.** No: the support is not a product set.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking independence by $\mathrm{Cov}=0$ only, or multiplying the original joint by a constant and calling it independent.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f(x,y)=6e^{-2x-3y}$ for $x>0,y>0$. Independent? Marginals?

**Solution.** Yes: $2e^{-2x}$ and $3e^{-3y}$ (the $6=2\cdot 3$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Independent $X,Y$. $P(X\le 1,Y\le 2)$.

**Solution.** $F_X(1)F_Y(2)$.
