## 3. Precise statement

Squaring both sides of $f=g$ produces $f^2=g^2$, i.e. $f=\pm g$, so extraneous roots appear. Clearing a denominator multiplies by an expression that may be $0$ or change a restriction. Always substitute candidates into the original equation. $\sqrt{\,\cdot\,}$ is $\ge 0$, so $\sqrt{x}=x-2$ forces $x-2\ge 0$ before squaring.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $\sqrt{x+2}=x$.

**Solution.** Square: $x+2=x^2$, $x^2-x-2=0$, $(x-2)(x+1)=0$. $x=2$ works; $x=-1$ fails ($\sqrt{1}\neq -1$). Only $x=2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Solve $\dfrac{x}{x-1}=2$.

**Solution.** $x=2(x-1)$, $x=2$. Check: $x\neq 1$, OK.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Keeping $x=-1$ in the square-root example because it satisfies the squared equation.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\sqrt{2x-1}=x-2$: after squaring, $x=1$ or $5$. Which survive?

**Solution.** Only $x=5$ ($x=1$ gives $1=-1$ and also $x-2<0$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Equation $\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-1}=0$. Solutions?

**Solution.** For $x\neq 1$, this is $x+1=0$, so $x=-1$, which is allowed. One solution.
