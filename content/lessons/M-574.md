# Lesson M-574 — Group action definition

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Group actions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4`.


This lesson isolates **Group action definition** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Group action definition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Group actions*

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Orbit-stabilizer: $|G|=|\mathrm{Orb}|\,|\mathrm{Stab}|$. Conjugacy is a special action. Finite abelian groups decompose into cyclic groups.

### This skill, specifically

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A (left) group action of $G$ on a set $X$ is a map $G\times X\to X$, $(g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x$, with $e\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$. Equivalent: a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Examples: $S_n$ on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$; $G$ on itself by left multiplication (Cayley) or by conjugation; $GL(V)$ on $V$; $G$ on left cosets $G/H$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** The map $GL_2(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$, $(A,v)\mapsto Av$ is an action. What is the associated hom $GL_2\to S_{\mathbb{R}^2}$?

**Solution.** Each invertible matrix is sent to the permutation of $\mathbb{R}^2$ given by left-multiplication by $A$. Kernel of $G\to S_X$ is the kernel of the action (elements acting as the identity map).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Left multiplication action of $G$ on $G$ is free: stabilizer of each point is

**Solution.** $\{e\}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using a right formula $(gh)\cdot x=h\cdot(g\cdot x)$ while claiming a left action.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $n\cdot x=x+n$ define an action of $(\mathbb{Z},+)$ on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Trivial action: $g\cdot x=x$ for all $g$. Associated hom?

**Solution.** The trivial hom $G\to S_X$.
