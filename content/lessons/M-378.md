# Lesson M-378 — Orientation of curves/surfaces

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Curl, divergence & flux** · High

Prerequisites: `I.1-H.2`.


This lesson isolates **Orientation of curves/surfaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Orientation of curves/surfaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Curl, divergence & flux*

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface. Orientation is a sign convention you must not treat as optional.

### This skill, specifically

Div is source density; curl is local rotation; flux is flow through a surface.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An orientation on a curve is a continuous unit tangent; reversing it negates $\int\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$. On a surface it is a continuous unit normal. Boundary orientation is the right-hand rule: fingers along $\partial S$, thumb in $\mathbf{n}$. For a plane region, positive $\partial D$ keeps $D$ on the left.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Unit circle, counterclockwise, bounds the unit disk in the $xy$-plane. What normal does the right-hand rule give?

**Solution.** $+\mathbf{k}$, upward.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $C$ is clockwise around a plane region $D$, Green's theorem acquires

**Solution.** a minus sign relative to the usual $+\mathbf{k}$ orientation.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Mixing 'outward' on a closed surface with 'upward' on a graph without checking the problem's convention.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Hemisphere $z=\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}$ with outward (from origin) normal: boundary equator orientation?

**Solution.** Counterclockwise viewed from above.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
