# Lesson M-363 — Vector fields

Part I. I. Vector Calculus · **Vector fields** · High

Prerequisites: `H.2`.


This lesson isolates **Vector fields** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Vector calculus (Green/Stokes/divergence) appears less often than single-variable calculus but is a high-leverage discriminator.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Vector fields**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Vector fields*

A vector field assigns a vector to each point. Gradients are special fields. Velocity and acceleration are derivatives of vector-valued paths.

### This skill, specifically

A vector field assigns a vector to each point.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A vector field on a region $U\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is a map $\mathbf{F}:U\to\mathbb{R}^n$. In $\mathbb{R}^2$, $\mathbf{F}=(P,Q)$; in $\mathbb{R}^3$, $\mathbf{F}=(P,Q,R)$. At each point it attaches an arrow. Plots of $\mathbf{F}(x,y)/|\mathbf{F}|$ show direction only.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Evaluate $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=(y,-x)$ at $(3,4)$ and at $(0,2)$.

**Solution.** $(4,-3)$ and $(-2,0)$. The field rotates clockwise about the origin.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=(x,y,-2z)$ at $(1,-1,3)$ is

**Solution.** $(1,-1,-6)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing the field $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$ with the tangent $\mathbf{r}'$ of a path (they live on different objects until you restrict $\mathbf{F}$ to a curve).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\mathbf{F}=(1,0)$ a radial field?

**Solution.** No: it is constant, everywhere pointing right.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Sketch $\mathbf{F}=(x,y)$ at $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$.

**Solution.** $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$: outward radial.

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
