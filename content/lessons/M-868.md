# Lesson M-868 — Jacobian method for continuous transformations

Part T. T. Probability · **Generating/transform methods** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.4-T.6; H.4`.


This lesson isolates **Jacobian method for continuous transformations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Probability is a solid additional-topics block: Bayes, expectation, named distributions, CLT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Jacobian method for continuous transformations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Generating/transform methods*

## 3. Precise statement

Jacobian method: if $\mathbf{Y}=g(\mathbf{X})$ is a $C^1$ bijection between open sets in $\mathbb{R}^d$, $f_{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbf{y})=f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\,\bigl|\det D g^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\bigr|=f_{\mathbf{X}}(x)/|\det Dg(x)|$ at $x=g^{-1}(y)$. For two variables, $|\det|=\bigl|x_u y_v-x_v y_u\bigr|$ in $u,v$-coordinates. Check the image of the support.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $X,Y$ i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. $U=X+Y$, $V=X-Y$. Jacobian and joint PDF of $(U,V)$.

**Solution.** $X=(U+V)/2$, $Y=(U-V)/2$, $\det\partial(x,y)/\partial(u,v)=1/2$. Joint of $(X,Y)$ is $(2\pi)^{-1}e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}$. Times $1/2$: $f_{U,V}(u,v)=(2\pi)^{-1}e^{-(u^2+v^2)/4}\cdot\frac12=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-(u^2+v^2)/4}$. So $U,V$ independent $N(0,2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $U=X$, $V=X+Y$ with $X,Y$ independent Exp($1$). Support of $(U,V)$?

**Solution.** $v>u>0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting $|\det J|$, or transforming the PDF as if it were a mass function. Also inverting only one coordinate in a two-dimensional map.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Polar: $X=R\cos\Theta$, $Y=R\sin\Theta$. Area Jacobian.

**Solution.** $|\det|=r$, so $dx\,dy=r\,dr\,d\theta$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $g$ is two-to-one (e.g. $Y=X^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$), the Jacobian formula on one branch must be

**Solution.** summed over preimages: $f_Y(y)=\sum_{x:g(x)=y}f_X(x)/|g'(x)|$.
