# Lesson M-738 — Equivalence relations

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Set theory** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1-Q.3`.


This lesson isolates **Equivalence relations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Equivalence relations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

An equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Standard examples: equality; $a\equiv b\pmod n$; similarity of matrices; ``has the same cardinality''. An equivalence relation is precisely the relation of belonging to the same block of a partition.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Show $a\sim b$ iff $a-b\in\mathbb{Z}$ is an equivalence relation on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Solution.** Reflexive: $0\in\mathbb{Z}$. Symmetric: $b-a=-(a-b)$. Transitive: integers form a group under addition.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is ``differ by a multiple of $2\pi$'' an equivalence on $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** Yes: it is congruence modulo $2\pi$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dropping reflexivity (``parallel lines'' if a line is not declared parallel to itself).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $x\sim y$ iff $xy>0$ an equivalence on $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$?

**Solution.** Yes: same (strict) sign. On $\mathbb{R}$ it fails reflexivity at $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Congruence modulo $n$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is an equivalence: the three properties come from

**Solution.** divisibility of differences.
