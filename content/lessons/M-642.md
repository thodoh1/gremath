# Lesson M-642 — limsup and liminf basics

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **limsup and liminf basics** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **limsup and liminf basics**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Sequences*

Sequence convergence, monotonicity, boundedness, subsequences, Cauchy sequences, and completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ are analysis language used on calculus items. $\limsup$ is awareness-level.

### This skill, specifically

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$\limsup a_n=\inf_n(\sup_{k\ge n}a_k)$ and $\liminf a_n=\sup_n(\inf_{k\ge n}a_k)$, possibly $\pm\infty$. They are the largest and smallest subsequential limits in the extended reals. $(a_n)$ converges to a finite $L$ iff $\limsup a_n=\liminf a_n=L$. Always $\liminf\le\limsup$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\limsup$ and $\liminf$ of $a_n=(-1)^n+1/n$.

**Solution.** Even terms $\to 1$, odd $\to -1$, so $\limsup=1$, $\liminf=-1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a_n=n+(-1)^n n$: $\limsup$ and $\liminf$

**Solution.** Evens $4k\to+\infty$, odds $0$, so $\limsup=+\infty$, $\liminf=0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking $\limsup$ is $\sup a_n$ rather than the limit of the tail-suprema.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If $a_n\to L$ finite, $\limsup a_n$.

**Solution.** $L$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Sequence with $\liminf=0$, $\limsup=1$: example.

**Solution.** $a_n=\frac{1+(-1)^n}{2}$.
