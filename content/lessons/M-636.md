## 3. Precise statement

Monotone convergence theorem: a monotone bounded real sequence converges. An increasing sequence bounded above converges to its supremum; a decreasing sequence bounded below converges to its infimum. Boundedness is essential: $a_n=n$ increases but diverges.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why does $a_n=(1+1/n)^n$ converge?

**Solution.** It is increasing and bounded above (e.g. by $3$), hence converges (to $e$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A decreasing sequence of positive terms

**Solution.** converges (bounded below by $0$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using MCT in $\mathbb{Q}$: a bounded monotone sequence of rationals need not have a rational limit.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does every increasing sequence converge in $\mathbb{R}$?

**Solution.** No: it must be bounded above.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.
