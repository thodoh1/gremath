# Lesson M-949 — Parametrized contours

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex integration** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1`.


This lesson isolates **Parametrized contours** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Parametrized contours**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A contour is a piecewise $C^1$ path $\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}$, $\gamma(t)=x(t)+iy(t)$. It is closed if $\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)$, simple if it does not cross itself (except possibly endpoints). The standard positive orientation of a simple closed curve is counterclockwise (region on the left). Opposite path $-\gamma$ reverses orientation. Smooth concatenations $\gamma_1+\gamma_2$ are allowed.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Parametrize the unit circle once counterclockwise.

**Solution.** $\gamma(t)=e^{it}=\cos t+i\sin t$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The segment from $0$ to $1+i$ is

**Solution.** $\gamma(t)=t(1+i)$, $t\in[0,1]$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using clockwise as the default positive orientation in Cauchy’s theorem, or parametrizing $|z|=2$ as $e^{it}$ without scaling the radius.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Parametrize $|z-1|=2$ counterclockwise.

**Solution.** $1+2e^{it}$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Length of $\gamma(t)=e^{it}$ on $[0,2\pi]$.

**Solution.** $2\pi$ (speed $|i e^{it}|=1$).
