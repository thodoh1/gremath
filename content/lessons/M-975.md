# Lesson M-975 — Secant method

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Root finding** · Medium

Prerequisites: `E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Secant method** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Secant method**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Secant method: $x_{n+1}=x_n-f(x_n)\dfrac{x_n-x_{n-1}}{f(x_n)-f(x_{n-1})}$, the root of the chord through two previous points. It needs two starts and no explicit $f'$. Convergence order is the golden ratio $\varphi\approx 1.618$ (superlinear) for simple roots, under standard hypotheses. It can break if $f(x_n)=f(x_{n-1})$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Secant step for $f(x)=x^2-2$ with $x_0=1$, $x_1=2$.

**Solution.** $f(1)=-1$, $f(2)=2$, $x_2=2-2\cdot(2-1)/(2-(-1))=2-2/3=4/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using three points as if it were Newton, or dividing by $f(x_n)-f(x_{n-1})$ when they are equal.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If $x_0=0$, $x_1=1$, $f(0)=1$, $f(1)=-1$, next secant iterate (root of the chord).

**Solution.** $1/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Order of convergence at a simple root (typical).

**Solution.** $\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$.
