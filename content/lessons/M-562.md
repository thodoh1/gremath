# Lesson M-562 — Consequences of Lagrange theorem

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Cosets & Lagrange** · High

Prerequisites: `M.1-M.2`.


This lesson isolates **Consequences of Lagrange theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Consequences of Lagrange theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Cosets & Lagrange*

Left/right cosets partition $G$. $|G|=|H|\,[G:H]$. Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Consequences: orders of elements divide $|G|$; groups of prime order are cyclic.

### This skill, specifically

Left/right cosets partition $G$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Lagrange consequences: $g^{|G|}=e$ (finite $G$); Fermat/Euler in unit groups; groups of prime order are cyclic; at most one subgroup of a given prime order if it is unique of that order it is normal. Cauchy: if $p\mid|G|$ then some element has order $p$ (not a corollary of Lagrange alone). Index $2$ $\Rightarrow$ normal. $A_4$ shows $6\mid 12$ need not give a subgroup.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is every group of order $17$ cyclic? Why is there no element of order $6$ in $A_4$?

**Solution.** Order $17$ prime: any $g\neq e$ has order $17$, so $\langle g\rangle=G$. $A_4$ has no element of order $6$, hence cannot have a cyclic subgroup of order $6$; in fact it has no subgroup of order $6$ at all.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a^6=e$ for all $a$ in a group of order $6$?

**Solution.** Yes, Lagrange. (In $S_3$ element orders are $1,2,3$, all dividing $6$.)

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $g^{|G|}=e$ in an infinite group, or claiming Cauchy from Lagrange without proof.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Does $g^{12}=e$ for all $g\in A_4$?

**Solution.** Yes by Lagrange; orders actually $1,2,3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Unique subgroup of index $2$ is always

**Solution.** normal (the left/right cosets are $H$ and $G\setminus H$).
