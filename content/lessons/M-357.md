# Lesson M-357 — Spherical coordinates

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multiple integration** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Spherical coordinates** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Spherical coordinates**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multiple integration*

## 3. Precise statement

Spherical: $x=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z=\rho\cos\phi$, $dV=\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$. $\rho\ge 0$, $\phi\in[0,\pi]$ from the positive $z$-axis, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$. Balls, cones from the origin, $x^2+y^2+z^2$ integrands.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Volume of the unit ball.

**Solution.** $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\int_0^1 \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta=\frac43\pi$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The factor $\sin\phi$ is $0$ at

**Solution.** the poles $\phi=0,\pi$ (coordinate singularity, like $r=0$ in polar).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $\phi$ as the polar angle in the $xy$-plane (that is $\theta$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Average of $\rho$ on the unit ball.

**Solution.** $\iiint\rho\,dV/\frac{4}{3}\pi=\frac{3}{4}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $dV$ in spherical is

**Solution.** $\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$.
