# Lesson M-442 — Consistency of systems

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Systems & row reduction** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.2`.


This lesson isolates **Consistency of systems** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Consistency of systems**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Systems & row reduction*

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm. REF/RREF, consistency, free variables, rank. Homogeneous systems always have at least the zero solution.

### This skill, specifically

Gaussian elimination is the GRE algorithm.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is consistent iff $\mathbf{b}$ lies in the column space of $A$, iff RREF of $[A|\mathbf{b}]$ has no row $[0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]$ with $c\neq 0$. Consistency does not require a unique solution; free variables yield infinitely many solutions.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Are $x+y=1$, $2x+2y=3$ consistent? Are $x+y=1$, $2x+2y=2$?

**Solution.** First: no ($0=1$ after elimination). Second: yes, infinitely many ($x+y=1$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A row $[0\ 0\ 0\mid 5]$ in RREF means

**Solution.** inconsistent.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Declaring inconsistency because there are fewer equations than unknowns (underdetermined consistent systems are common).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $x+2y+z=3$, $2x+4y+2z=6$. Consistent?

**Solution.** Yes: second is twice the first.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $A$ has a pivot in every row, $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is

**Solution.** consistent for every $\mathbf{b}$.

## 9. Lock it in
