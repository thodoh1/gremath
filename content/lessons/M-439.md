## 3. Precise statement

A matrix is in row echelon form (REF) if: (i) all zero rows are at the bottom; (ii) each leading entry of a row is strictly to the right of the leading entry above; (iii) all entries below a leading entry are $0$. Leading entries are pivots. REF is not unique.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&1&4\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ in REF? Is $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$?

**Solution.** Yes. No: the leading $1$ of row $2$ is not to the right of row $1$'s leading entry (and not even a staircase).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Pivots of $\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\0&0&5\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ sit in columns

**Solution.** $1$ and $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

**Drill 1.** Can two different REF matrices be row-equivalent?

**Solution.** Yes: scale a pivot row, or clear above or not.
