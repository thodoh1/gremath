# Lesson M-951 — Cauchy theorem

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex integration** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cauchy theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cauchy theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Cauchy’s theorem: if $f$ is holomorphic on a simply connected domain $U$ and $\gamma$ is a closed contour in $U$, then $\int_\gamma f=0$. More generally, if $f$ is holomorphic on and inside a simple closed positively oriented $\gamma$, the integral vanishes. Goursat’s version needs only complex differentiability on a convex set, no a-priori continuity of $f'$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int_{|z|=1} e^z\,dz$.

**Solution.** $0$ ($e^z$ entire).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int_{|z|=1} \dfrac{1}{z}\,dz$ is not $0$ because

**Solution.** $1/z$ is not holomorphic at $0$, which lies inside.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Applying Cauchy to $1/z$ on the unit circle, or requiring simple connectedness of the contour rather than of the domain.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
