# Lesson M-147 — Heron's formula

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Triangle trigonometry** · High

Prerequisites: `C.1`.


This lesson isolates **Heron's formula** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Heron's formula**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Triangle trigonometry*

Law of sines, law of cosines, area $\frac12 ab\sin C$, Heron, and the ambiguous SSA case are the computational triangle toolkit. SSA can yield $0,1,$ or $2$ triangles; that is a GRE favorite. Inscribed-angle facts connect this topic to circles.

## 3. Precise statement

Heron: $s=(a+b+c)/2$, area $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$. Use SSS.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Area of $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle.

**Solution.** $s=6$, $\sqrt{6\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1}=6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Equilateral side $a$: Heron gives

**Solution.** $s=3a/2$, $\sqrt{\frac{3a}{2}(\frac{a}{2})^3}=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using side lengths that violate the triangle inequality (sqrt of negative).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Area of $5$-$5$-$6$.

**Solution.** $s=8$, $\sqrt{8\cdot 3\cdot 3\cdot 2}=12$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why Heron equals $\frac12 ab\sin C$.

**Solution.** Both compute the same area; $\sin C$ from cosine law.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
