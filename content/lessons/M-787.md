# Lesson M-787 — Walks

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Walks** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Walks**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A walk is a sequence $v_0 e_1 v_1\cdots e_k v_k$ of incident vertices and edges (repetition of vertices and edges allowed). Length is $k$, the number of edges. The $(i,j)$ entry of $A^k$ counts walks of length $k$ from $i$ to $j$ in a simple graph with adjacency matrix $A$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** In $C_4$ labeled sequentially, how many walks of length $2$ from a vertex to itself?

**Solution.** $2$: out-and-back along either incident edge. ($A^2_{ii}=\deg(i)=2$.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does a walk of length $3$ exist from $u$ to $v$ if they are adjacent in a path of $2$ edges only?

**Solution.** Not without extra structure; on $P_3$ with ends $u,v$, walks of length $3$ from $u$ to $v$ do exist (there-and-back-and-there).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Requiring walks to have distinct vertices (that is a path, not a walk).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Walks of length $1$ from $v$ equal

**Solution.** $\deg(v)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** On $K_3$, walks of length $2$ from $v$ to a different vertex $w$.

**Solution.** $1$: the unique walk $v$-$u$-$w$ through the third vertex.
