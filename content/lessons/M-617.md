# Lesson M-617 — Extension degree

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Fields & extensions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.3-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Extension degree** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Extension degree**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Fields & extensions*

A field has every nonzero element invertible. Extensions $K/F$ have degree $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. Algebraic vs transcendental, minimal polynomials, splitting fields, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

### This skill, specifically

A field has every nonzero element invertible.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The degree $[K:F]$ is $\dim_F K$ (possibly $\infty$). Tower law: if $F\subseteq E\subseteq K$ then $[K:F]=[K:E][E:F]$ (cardinal product if infinite). $[F(\alpha):F]=\deg m_\alpha$ when $\alpha$ is algebraic. $[\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R}]=2$, $[\mathbb{R}:\mathbb{Q}]=\infty$, $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]=3$. If $[K:F]$ is prime, there is no proper intermediate field.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3}):\mathbb{Q}]$ by a tower.

**Solution.** $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}):\mathbb{Q}]=2$. $x^2-3$ remains irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (else $\sqrt{3}\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$), so $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3}):\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})]=2$, product $4$. Basis $\{1,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\sqrt{6}\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $[K:F]=1$ iff

**Solution.** $K=F$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Adding degrees in a tower instead of multiplying.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]$.

**Solution.** $3$, min poly $x^3-2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can a degree-$6$ extension have an intermediate field of degree $5$ over the base?

**Solution.** No: $5\nmid 6$.

## 9. Lock it in
