## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Taylor polynomial definition**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Taylor theorem & approximation*

Taylor polynomials are the unique polynomials that match $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at a point. Remainder estimates tell you the error. Big-O notation tracks the order. Limits that look like L’Hôpital often die faster with two terms of Taylor.

### This skill, specifically

Taylor polynomials are the unique polynomials that match $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at a point.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The Taylor polynomial of degree $n$ for $f$ at $a$ is $P_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n \dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k$. It is the unique degree $\le n$ polynomial matching $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at $a$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $P_2$ for $e^x$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $1+x+x^2/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $P_2$ for $\sin x$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $x$ (even part vanishes; $P_2=P_1$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $P_2$ for $\ln(1+x)$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $x-x^2/2$.
