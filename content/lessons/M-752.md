# Lesson M-752 — Axiom of choice awareness

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Functions & cardinality** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.4`.


This lesson isolates **Axiom of choice awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Axiom of choice awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Functions & cardinality*

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses. Countable vs uncountable, Cantor diagonal, $|\mathbb{N}|=|\mathbb{Z}|=|\mathbb{Q}|<|\mathbb{R}|$, axiom of choice as awareness.

### This skill, specifically

Injection, surjection, bijection, composition, inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The axiom of choice (AC): the product of a family of nonempty sets is nonempty (a choice function exists). Equivalent (over ZF): Zorn's lemma; every set can be well-ordered; every vector space has a Hamel basis. Countable choice is enough for many analysis arguments (selecting $x_n\in U_n$). AC yields nonmeasurable sets (Vitali).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Where is AC used in ``every vector space has a basis''?

**Solution.** Zorn: order proper linearly independent sets by inclusion; a maximal one spans. The maximal element is not constructed explicitly in general.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A choice function on $\{A_i\}_{i\in I}$ picks

**Solution.** one element from each $A_i$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Thinking AC is needed to choose one element from a single nonempty set, or from finitely many (ZF suffices).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Vitali set in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$: AC chooses

**Solution.** one representative from each coset of $\mathbb{Q}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is AC needed to well-order $\mathbb{N}$?

**Solution.** No: the usual order already well-orders $\mathbb{N}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
