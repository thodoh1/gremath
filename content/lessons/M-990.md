# Lesson M-990 — Check theorem hypotheses

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Theorem/definition recognition** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Q`.


This lesson isolates **Check theorem hypotheses** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Check theorem hypotheses**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Every named theorem has hypotheses. IVT needs continuity on a closed interval and opposite signs (or a value between). MVT needs continuity on $[a,b]$ and differentiability on $(a,b)$. Cauchy’s theorem needs holomorphicity on a domain containing the contour and its interior (or simple connectedness). Rank-nullity needs a linear map between finite-dimensional spaces. If a hypothesis fails, the conclusion may fail — exhibit a counterexample rather than ‘almost applying’ the theorem.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $f(x)=1/x$ on $(0,1]$ have a maximum? EVT requires a closed bounded domain. Here the domain is not closed in $\mathbb{R}$ (or not compact): $f$ is unbounded. Do not quote EVT.

**Solution.** No maximum. Hypothesis ‘closed bounded interval’ fails.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Can you apply MVT to $f(x)=|x|$ on $[-1,1]$?

**Solution.** No: not differentiable at $0$. (Indeed $\frac{f(1)-f(-1)}{2}=0$ but $f'$ is never $0$ where it exists.)

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Invoking Cauchy’s integral theorem for $1/z$ on the unit circle: holomorphicity inside fails.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** IVT for $f(x)=1/x$ on $[-1,1]\setminus\{0\}$: $f(-1)<0<f(1)$, is there a root?

**Solution.** No: not defined (hence not continuous) on the whole interval.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A $2\times 3$ matrix cannot be invertible as a square matrix; rank-nullity still says

**Solution.** $\mathrm{rank}+\mathrm{nullity}=3$.
