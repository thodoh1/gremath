# Lesson M-978 — Polynomial interpolation

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Interpolation & approximation** · Medium

## 1. Why this skill exists

## 3. Precise statement

There is a unique polynomial of degree at most $n$ interpolating $n+1$ distinct nodes $(x_i,y_i)$. Existence: Lagrange or Newton form; uniqueness: the difference would have $n+1$ zeros. Interpolating a degree-$\le n$ polynomial recovers it exactly. High-degree interpolation on equally spaced nodes can oscillate (Runge). Piecewise interpolation (splines) is the usual remedy.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Unique line through $(0,1)$ and $(2,5)$.

**Solution.** $y=2x+1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Two different cubics cannot agree at

**Solution.** $4$ distinct $x$-values unless they are identical (difference degree $\le 3$ with $4$ zeros).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Claiming infinitely many interpolants of degree $\le n$ at $n+1$ points, or interpolating the same $x$ twice with conflicting $y$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Degree of the interpolant of $4$ points in general position.

**Solution.** At most $3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does the interpolant of $1,x,x^2$ at $0,1,2$ equal $x^2$ restricted to those nodes? 

**Solution.** The values $0,1,4$ are interpolated by $x^2$ itself, the unique degree $\le 2$ interpolant.
