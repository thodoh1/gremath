# Lesson M-225 — Derivative of inverse functions

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Derivative definition & rules** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.1-E.2`.


This lesson isolates **Derivative of inverse functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Derivative of inverse functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Derivative definition & rules*

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists. Geometrically it is slope of the tangent; physically it is instantaneous rate. Power, product, quotient, chain, implicit, logarithmic, inverse, and higher derivatives are the machine that computes $f'$ without returning to the limit every time.

### This skill, specifically

$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ when the limit exists.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f$ is differentiable and $f'(a)\neq 0$ with $f$ locally invertible, then $(f^{-1})'(b)=1/f'(a)$ where $b=f(a)$. In Leibniz form $\dfrac{dx}{dy}=1\big/\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Derivative of $\arcsin x$ from this rule.

**Solution.** $y=\arcsin x$, $x=\sin y$, $dx/dy=\cos y=\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (principal range), so $dy/dx=1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $f(2)=5$ and $f'(2)=4$, $(f^{-1})'(5)$

**Solution.** $1/4$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $1/f'(b)$ instead of $1/f'(a)$ where $b=f(a)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Derivative of $\ln x$ as inverse of $e^x$.

**Solution.** $1/x$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $f'(a)=0$, can $f^{-1}$ be differentiable at $f(a)$?

**Solution.** No finite derivative: horizontal tangent becomes vertical.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
