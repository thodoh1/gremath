# Lesson M-798 — Euler paths

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Euler paths** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Euler paths**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Special graph classes*

### This skill, specifically

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

An Euler path (Euler trail) traverses every edge exactly once. Existence (connected, ignoring isolated vertices): exactly zero or two odd-degree vertices. If two odds, the path must start at one odd vertex and end at the other.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Degrees $4,4,3,3,2$. Euler path? Euler circuit?

**Solution.** Two odds: Euler path yes, circuit no.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $K_5$ Euler path?

**Solution.** All degrees $4$, even: it has an Euler circuit, hence a path.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking vertices of degree $0$ as odd, or starting an Euler path at an even vertex when two odds exist (then some edge is stranded).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $K_{4,3}$ has all degrees? Euler path?

**Solution.** Degrees $3$ (four vertices of the $4$-side) and $4$ (three vertices). Four odds: no Euler path.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** House-shaped pentagon plus two diagonals from the roof peak to the base corners: classic two odds?

**Solution.** The square-plus-roof drawing of two rooms: typically two odd vertices; Euler path exists, circuit does not.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
