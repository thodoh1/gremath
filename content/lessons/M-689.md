# Lesson M-689 — T1 spaces

Part P. P. Topology · **Separation & compactness** · Medium

Prerequisites: `P.1-P.2`.


This lesson isolates **T1 spaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Topology appears as compactness, connectedness, open/closed, Heine–Borel.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **T1 spaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Separation & compactness*

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens. Compact: every open cover has a finite subcover. Continuous images of compact sets are compact. In $\mathbb{R}^n$, compact $\Leftrightarrow$ closed and bounded (Heine–Borel).

### This skill, specifically

Hausdorff: points can be separated by opens.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$T_1$: for $x\neq y$ there is an open $U\ni x$ with $y\notin U$ (and vice versa). Equivalent: singletons are closed. $T_2\Rightarrow T_1\Rightarrow T_0$. Infinite cofinite topology is $T_1$ not $T_2$. Discrete is $T_1$; indiscrete on two points is not.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Why is the cofinite topology on $\mathbb{Z}$ $T_1$ but not Hausdorff?

**Solution.** Singletons are finite, hence closed, so $T_1$. Any two nonempty opens have finite complements, hence infinite intersection, so not disjoint.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Singletons closed iff the space is

**Solution.** $T_1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Equating $T_1$ with Hausdorff.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology $T_1$?

**Solution.** Yes (it is even metrizable).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Finite complement topology on a finite set is

**Solution.** discrete, hence Hausdorff.
