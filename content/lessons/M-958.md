# Lesson M-958 — Poles

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Poles** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Poles**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

$a$ is a pole of order $m\ge 1$ if $c_{-m}\neq 0$ and $c_n=0$ for all $n<-m$ in the Laurent series, equivalently $(z-a)^m f(z)$ has a holomorphic nonzero value at $a$. Then $|f(z)|\to\infty$ as $z\to a$. Simple pole: $m=1$. Rational functions have poles at denominator zeros remaining after cancelling common factors.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Order of the pole of $1/\sin z$ at $0$.

**Solution.** Simple: $\sin z\sim z$, so $z/\sin z\to 1\neq 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $1/z^3$ at $0$ has pole order

**Solution.** $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting the order as the vanishing order of the numerator, or calling every denominator zero a pole without cancelling.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Pole order of $(z+1)/(z-2)^2$ at $z=2$.

**Solution.** $2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $e^z/z^2$ at $0$: order?

**Solution.** $2$ ($e^0=1\neq 0$).
