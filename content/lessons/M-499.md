## 3. Precise statement

Division algorithm: for $a\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $d>0$ there exist unique $q,r\in\mathbb{Z}$ with $a=dq+r$ and $0\le r<d$. Here $q=\lfloor a/d\rfloor$ and $r=a-dq$ is the remainder. The remainder is $0$ iff $d\mid a$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Divide $47$ by $9$, and divide $-17$ by $5$.

**Solution.** $47=5\cdot 9+2$. For $-17$: $-17=(-4)\cdot 5+3$, since $-20+3=-17$ and $0\le 3<5$ (not remainder $-2$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $a=dq+r$ with $0\le r<d$ is unique. If $100=7q+r$, then $r$ equals

**Solution.** $2$, because $98=14\cdot 7$ and $100-98=2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Allowing a negative remainder, or using $r<|a|$ instead of $r<d$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Remainder of $1001$ on division by $13$.

**Solution.** $13\cdot 77=1001$, remainder $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** What is $\lfloor -17/5\rfloor$?

**Solution.** $-4$, matching $q$ above.
