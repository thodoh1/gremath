# Lesson M-418 — Vectors in R^n

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vectors & geometry** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Vectors in R^n** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Vectors in R^n**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Vectors & geometry*

Vectors add, scale, dot (angles, projections), cross in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (area, orthogonality), triple product (volume). Geometry and algebra are the same object.

### This skill, specifically

Vectors add, scale, dot (angles, projections), cross in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (area, orthogonality), triple product (volume).

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is an $n$-tuple $\mathbf{v}=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$, equivalently $v_1\mathbf{e}_1+\cdots+v_n\mathbf{e}_n$ in the standard basis. Two vectors are equal iff corresponding components are equal. Geometric vectors (arrows) are identified with their components once a basis is fixed.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Write $\mathbf{v}=(2,-1,4)$ in the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Solution.** $2\mathbf{e}_1-\mathbf{e}_2+4\mathbf{e}_3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** The zero vector in $\mathbb{R}^4$ is

**Solution.** $(0,0,0,0)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $(2,-1)$ as equal to $(2,-1,0)$ without embedding $\mathbb{R}^2\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^3$ explicitly.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Third component of $(7,0,-5,1)$.

**Solution.** $-5$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Standard basis vector $\mathbf{e}_2$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Solution.** $(0,1,0)$.
