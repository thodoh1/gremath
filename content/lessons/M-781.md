# Lesson M-781 — Graph terminology

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Graph terminology** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Graph terminology**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A graph $G=(V,E)$ consists of a vertex set $V$ and an edge set $E$ of pairs of vertices. Informal language: vertices are nodes, edges are links. Loops join a vertex to itself; multiple edges join the same pair. A drawing is not the graph: only the incidence data matter.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A graph has vertices $\{a,b,c,d\}$ and edges $\{ab,bc,cd,da,ac\}$. How many vertices and edges? Is $bd$ an edge?

**Solution.** $|V|=4$, $|E|=5$. No $bd$ edge: it is a square with one diagonal.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Two drawings of four vertices in a square, one with crossing diagonals and one without. Same graph?

**Solution.** If both have all six pairs edged, both are $K_4$; drawings may hide crossings.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Counting a crossing in a drawing as an extra vertex, or treating labels as optional when the problem's vertices are labeled.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** The null graph on $5$ vertices has how many edges?

**Solution.** $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is a single loop at one vertex a graph on two vertices?

**Solution.** No: one vertex, one loop (a looped unicycle), unless an isolated second vertex is declared.
