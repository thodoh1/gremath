# Lesson M-479 — Eigenspaces

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Eigenvalues & eigenvectors** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.6`.


This lesson isolates **Eigenspaces** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Eigenspaces**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Eigenvalues & eigenvectors*

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. Characteristic polynomial, algebraic vs geometric multiplicity, diagonalization iff enough independent eigenvectors. Trace is sum of eigenvalues; det is product. Cayley–Hamilton.

### This skill, specifically

$Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The eigenspace $E_\lambda=\ker(A-\lambda I)$ is the subspace of all eigenvectors for $\lambda$ together with $\mathbf{0}$. Its dimension is the geometric multiplicity. Compute it by row-reducing $A-\lambda I$. $V$ is a direct sum of eigenspaces iff $A$ is diagonalizable.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $E_1$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.** $A-I=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, row-reduces to $x=y$, so $E_1=\mathrm{span}\{(1,1)\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $E_\lambda$ always contains

**Solution.** $\mathbf{0}$, and has dimension at least $1$ if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Omitting $\mathbf{0}$ and calling $E_\lambda$ 'the set of eigenvectors' as if it were a subspace without $\mathbf{0}$ (eigenvectors themselves exclude $\mathbf{0}$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $E_2$ for $2I_3$.

**Solution.** all of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $E_\lambda=V$, then $A$ is

**Solution.** $\lambda I$.
