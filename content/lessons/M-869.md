# Lesson M-869 — Mean

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Descriptive statistics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.2`.


This lesson isolates **Mean** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Mean**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The arithmetic mean of numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ is $\bar x=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$. For a discrete random variable it is $E[X]=\sum x\,p(x)$; for a density, $E[X]=\int x f(x)\,dx$. The mean is the balance point: $\sum(x_i-\bar x)=0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Mean of $2,5,5,8,10$.

**Solution.** $(2+5+5+8+10)/5=6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $3,5,x$ have mean $6$, then $x=$

**Solution.** $10$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Averaging already-averaged group means without weighting by group size.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Weighted mean of $10$ (weight $3$) and $20$ (weight $1$).

**Solution.** $12.5$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Mean of $1,2,\ldots,n$.

**Solution.** $(n+1)/2$.
