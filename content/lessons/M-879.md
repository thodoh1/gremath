# Lesson M-879 — Skewness basics

## 3. Precise statement

Skewness (third standardized central moment) is $\gamma_1=\mu_3/\sigma^3$. Right-skewed ($\gamma_1>0$) typically has a long right tail and mean $>$ median; left-skewed reverses. Symmetric distributions about the mean have $\gamma_1=0$ when the third moment exists. Skewness $0$ does not imply symmetry.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$, mean $1/\lambda$, median $(\ln 2)/\lambda$. Which is larger?

**Solution.** Mean, so the exponential is right-skewed.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Skewness of $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ is

**Solution.** $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming mean $>$ median iff the density ‘leans left’; the tail, not the peak, names the skew.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Binomial $n=2$, $p=0.1$: left or right skewed?

**Solution.** Right (mass piled at $0$, tail toward $2$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $Y=-X$ and $X$ has skewness $\gamma$, skewness of $Y$ is

**Solution.** $-\gamma$.
