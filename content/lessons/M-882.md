# Lesson M-882 — Bias

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

Prerequisites: `T.1-T.4`.


This lesson isolates **Bias** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Bias**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Bias of $\hat\theta$ is $E_\theta[\hat\theta]-\theta$. An estimator can be biased but useful (MLE variance of a normal). Mean squared error $\mathrm{MSE}=E[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2]=\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)+(\mathrm{bias})^2$. Unbiasedness is about the sampling distribution’s mean, not about one lucky sample.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** For iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, the MLE $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ has expectation

**Solution.** $\dfrac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$, hence bias $-\sigma^2/n$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** MSE of an unbiased estimator equals

**Solution.** its variance.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Saying ‘biased’ to mean ‘wrong on this sample’, or thinking unbiased implies small MSE.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Bias of $\bar X$ for $\mu$ in iid data with finite mean.

**Solution.** $0$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $\hat\theta$ is unbiased for $\theta$, is $\hat\theta^2$ unbiased for $\theta^2$?

**Solution.** No in general: $E[\hat\theta^2]=\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)+\theta^2$.
