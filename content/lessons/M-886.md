# Lesson M-886 — Likelihood function

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Estimation** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Likelihood function** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Likelihood function**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The likelihood $L(\theta;\mathbf{x})=\prod f(x_i\mid\theta)$ is a function of $\theta$ with data held fixed. It is not a pdf in $\theta$ (unless one puts a prior and normalizes — that’s Bayes). Two likelihoods that differ by a $\theta$-free factor yield the same MLE. For discrete laws, $L(\theta)=P_\theta(\text{observed sample})$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** One observation $x=3$ from Poisson($\lambda$): write $L(\lambda)$.

**Solution.** $L(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^3/3!$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For iid $N(\mu,1)$ data, $\ell(\mu)$ equals (up to a constant)

**Solution.** $-\frac n2(\bar x-\mu)^2$ plus a $\mu$-free term, equivalently $-\frac12\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Integrating $L(\theta)\,d\theta$ and calling the result $1$, or treating $L$ as $P(\theta\mid\text{data})$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

**Solution.** $p^2(1-p)$.
