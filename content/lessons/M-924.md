# Lesson M-924 — Polygon angle sums

Part V. V. Geometry · **Circle & polygon geometry** · High

## 3. Precise statement

The sum of interior angles of a simple $n$-gon is $(n-2)\pi$ radians or $(n-2)180^\circ$. One exterior angle at each vertex, taken consistently (e.g. all left turns), sums to $360^\circ$. A convex $n$-gon has each exterior between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Diagonals from one vertex: $n-3$; total diagonals: $n(n-3)/2$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Interior-angle sum of a hexagon.

**Solution.** $720^\circ$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $(n-2)180^\circ$ as one interior angle rather than the sum, or counting $n$ diagonals from one vertex.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Number of diagonals in a convex octagon.

**Solution.** $8\cdot 5/2=20$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Interior sum of a pentagon in radians.

**Solution.** $3\pi$.
