# Lesson M-897 — Least-squares line

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Regression** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Least-squares line** and trains it until it is automatic.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Least-squares line**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The least-squares line $\hat y=\hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1 x$ for pairs $(x_i,y_i)$ minimizes $\sum(y_i-\beta_0-\beta_1 x_i)^2$. Explicitly $\hat\beta_1=\dfrac{\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2}$ and $\hat\beta_0=\bar y-\hat\beta_1\bar x$. The fitted line always passes through $(\bar x,\bar y)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Points $(0,1),(2,3)$. Find the LS line.

**Solution.** $\bar x=1$, $\bar y=2$, $\hat\beta_1=( (0-1)(1-2)+(2-1)(3-2) ) / (1+1)=1$, $\hat\beta_0=1$, so $\hat y=1+x$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Three collinear points on $y=2x$: LS slope is

**Solution.** $2$.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Minimizing $\sum|y_i-\beta_0-\beta_1 x_i|$ and calling it least squares, or swapping $x$ and $y$ (regression of $x$ on $y$ is a different line unless $|\rho|=1$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
