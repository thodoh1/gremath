# Lesson M-955 — Laurent series

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex series & singularities** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Laurent series** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Laurent series**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

If $f$ is holomorphic in an annulus $r<|z-a|<R$, then $f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty c_n (z-a)^n$ there, with $c_n=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z-a|=\rho} \dfrac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz$ for any $r<\rho<R$. The principal part is the sum of negative powers. Laurent series in a given annulus is unique.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Laurent of $1/z$ at $0$ in $0<|z|<\infty$.

**Solution.** Just $z^{-1}$ (already Laurent).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^{1/z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \dfrac{1}{n!}\,z^{-n}$ on

**Solution.** $0<|z|<\infty$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Dropping negative powers because ‘Taylor is enough’, or using one Laurent expansion in two different annuli where they actually differ (e.g. $1/(z(z-1))$ for $|z|<1$ vs $|z|>1$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Laurent of $1/(z-1)$ at $0$ for $|z|>1$.

**Solution.** $1/(z-1)=z^{-1}(1-z^{-1})^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-n}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Principal part of $z^{-2}+3+z$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $z^{-2}$.

## 9. Lock it in
