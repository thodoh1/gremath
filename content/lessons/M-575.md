# Lesson M-575 — Orbit

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Group actions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4`.


This lesson isolates **Orbit** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Orbit**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Group actions*

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Orbit-stabilizer: $|G|=|\mathrm{Orb}|\,|\mathrm{Stab}|$. Conjugacy is a special action. Finite abelian groups decompose into cyclic groups.

### This skill, specifically

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The orbit of $x\in X$ is $G\cdot x=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}$. Orbits partition $X$. The action is transitive if there is one orbit. Examples: $S_n$ is transitive on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. $SO(2)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ has orbits $\{0\}$ and the circles of each radius $r>0$ (rotations preserve length).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Orbits of $\{\pm 1\}$ acting on $\mathbb{R}$ by multiplication.

**Solution.** Orbit of $0$ is $\{0\}$. Orbit of $x\neq 0$ is $\{x,-x\}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Number of orbits of $S_4$ on $\{1,2,3,4\}$

**Solution.** $1$ (transitive).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing orbit with stabilizer, or thinking orbits can overlap nontrivially.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Orbits of $\mathbb{Z}$ acting on $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ by addition? Addition of $\mathbb{Z}$ on itself?

**Solution.** On $\mathbb{Z}/6$ by $n\cdot[x]=[x+n]$: one orbit (transitive).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Rotation group $C_4$ of the square acting on $4$ vertices: orbit of a vertex?

**Solution.** All $4$ vertices.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
