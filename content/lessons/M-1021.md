# Lesson M-1021 — Avoid over-solving

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Speed & multiple-choice technique** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Avoid over-solving** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Avoid over-solving**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Speed & multiple-choice technique*

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

### This skill, specifically

Backsolve, special values, estimation, degree/dimension checks, pictures, parity, geometric shortcuts, knowing when not to finish a computation, pacing 170 minutes, flagging, not over-solving.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Stop at the asked quantity. If the question wants $xy$ given $x+y$ and $x^2+y^2$, use $(x+y)^2-2xy=x^2+y^2$, do not solve the quadratic for $x$ and $y$. If it wants $\det(e^A)$, use $e^{\mathrm{tr} A}$, do not exponentiate the matrix. If it wants $f'(0)$ from a series, read the linear coefficient. Over-solving burns the minutes that flag-and-return needs.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $x+y=5$, $x^2+y^2=13$. Find $xy$.

**Solution.** $25-2xy=13$, $xy=6$. No need for $x,y=2,3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\det e^A$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\pi\\ 2\pi&0\end{pmatrix}$. Trace $0$, so

**Solution.** $\det e^A=e^0=1$ (rotation by $2\pi$ is $I$ anyway).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Finding both roots of a quadratic when the question asked for the product (Vieta).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $p(x)=x^3-3x+1$. Product of roots?

**Solution.** $-1$ (Vieta, $(-1)^3$ times constant term). Do not solve the cubic.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Series $f(z)=1+2z+3z^2+\cdots$ for $|z|<1$. $f'(0)=$

**Solution.** $2$, the coefficient of $z$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
