# Lesson M-662 — Differentiability implications

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Differentiability & integration** · High

Prerequisites: `E.3-O.4`.


This lesson isolates **Differentiability implications** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Differentiability implications**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Differentiability & integration*

### This skill, specifically

Mean value theorems, Taylor with remainder, Darboux (derivatives have IVT), Riemann integrability criteria, FTC as a theorem not a slogan.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Differentiability at $a$ implies continuity at $a$. The converse is false ($|x|$ at $0$). Differentiable functions need not be $C^1$: $f'$ may fail to be continuous. $C^1$ implies locally Lipschitz. Strict increase does not require $f'>0$ everywhere ($x^3$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Give a function continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ but not differentiable at $0$, and one differentiable with $f'$ discontinuous at $0$.

**Solution.** $|x|$; $x^2\sin(1/x)$ for $x\neq 0$ and $0$ at $0$ (here $f'(0)=0$ but $f'$ has no limit at $0$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Does $f'(a)=0$ imply a local extremum?

**Solution.** No: $f(x)=x^3$ at $0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Concluding $f'$ is continuous from the mere existence of $f'$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $x^{1/3}$ differentiable at $0$?

**Solution.** No as a real derivative: difference quotients $\to+\infty$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Differentiable $\Rightarrow$ continuous is used how in MVT proofs?

**Solution.** To apply EVT to $f$ or to an auxiliary function on $[a,b]$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
