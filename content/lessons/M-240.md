## 3. Precise statement

$f$ is concave up on $I$ if $f'$ is increasing on $I$ (graph lies above tangents, for twice differentiable functions). If $f''>0$, concave up; $f''<0$, concave down. Concavity is about the bending, not about $f>0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Concavity of $f(x)=x^3$.

**Solution.** $f''=6x$: down on $(-\infty,0)$, up on $(0,\infty)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^x$ is

**Solution.** concave up everywhere.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $x^2$ concave down because it has a minimum.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Where is $\sin x$ concave down on $[0,2\pi]$?

**Solution.** Where $-\sin x<0$, i.e. $\sin x>0$: $(0,\pi)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $f''=0$ at a point imply a concavity change?

**Solution.** No: $x^4$ has $f''(0)=0$ and stays concave up.
