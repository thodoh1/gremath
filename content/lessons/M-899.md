# Lesson M-899 — Normal equations

Part U. U. Mathematical Statistics · **Regression** · Medium

Prerequisites: `K.8-U.1`.


This lesson isolates **Normal equations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Statistics is lighter than probability but bias, MLE, and tests do appear.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Normal equations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

In matrix form $Y=X\beta+\varepsilon$ with $X$ an $n\times p$ full-rank design matrix, the normal equations are $X^\top X\hat\beta=X^\top Y$, so $\hat\beta=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top Y$. For simple regression, $X$ has a column of ones and a column of $x_i$. The equations say $X^\top e=0$: residuals are orthogonal to the column space of $X$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** No-intercept model $y_i=\beta x_i+\varepsilon_i$. Write the $1\times 1$ normal equation.

**Solution.** $(\sum x_i^2)\hat\beta=\sum x_i y_i$, so $\hat\beta=(\sum x_i y_i)/(\sum x_i^2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.
