# Lesson M-276 — Volumes by disks

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Applications of integration** · High

Prerequisites: `F.2`.


This lesson isolates **Volumes by disks** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Volumes by disks**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Applications of integration*

## 3. Precise statement

Disk method: revolving $y=f(x)\ge 0$ about the $x$-axis, $V=\int_a^b \pi [f(x)]^2\,dx$. About the $y$-axis, use $x=g(y)$. Each cross-section is a disk of radius the distance to the axis.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Volume of a ball by revolving $y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}$ on $[-r,r]$.

**Solution.** $\int_{-r}^r \pi(r^2-x^2)\,dx=\frac43\pi r^3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Solid from $y=x$ on $[0,2]$ about the $x$-axis

**Solution.** $\int_0^2\pi x^2\,dx=\frac{8\pi}{3}$ (cone).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting $\pi$, or using $f$ instead of $f^2$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Revolve $y=\sqrt{x}$ on $[0,1]$ about $x$-axis.

**Solution.** $\pi\int_0^1 x\,dx=\pi/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A cone of height $h$ radius $r$: $y=(r/h)x$ on $[0,h]$, volume

**Solution.** $\pi\int_0^h (r/h)^2 x^2\,dx=\frac13\pi r^2 h$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
