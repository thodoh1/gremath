# Lesson M-345 — Hessian matrix

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Optimization** · High

Prerequisites: `H.2-K.7`.


This lesson isolates **Hessian matrix** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Hessian matrix**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Optimization*

### This skill, specifically

Critical points of $f(x,y)$ via $\nabla f=0$, Hessian test, extrema on closed bounded sets (check boundary), Lagrange multipliers $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The Hessian $H$ of $f$ is the symmetric matrix of second partials $H_{ij}=f_{x_i x_j}$ (when Clairaut applies). At a critical point, the quadratic form $\mathbf{h}^T H\mathbf{h}$ governs the local shape.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Hessian of $f=x^2+xy+2y^2$.

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&4\end{pmatrix}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f=xy$, Hessian

**Solution.** $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing the Hessian with first partials on the diagonal.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f=x^3+y^3$, $H$ at $(0,0)$.

**Solution.** Zero matrix.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Hessian of a quadratic form $\frac12\mathbf{x}^T A\mathbf{x}$ (A symmetric) is

**Solution.** $A$.
