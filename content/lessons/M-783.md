# Lesson M-783 — Degree

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Graph basics** · Medium

Prerequisites: `Q.4-R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Degree** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Degree**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The degree $\deg(v)$ is the number of incident edge ends at $v$ (a loop contributes $2$). The degree sequence is the list of degrees, usually nonincreasing. The neighborhood $N(v)$ has size $\deg(v)$ in a simple graph. Minimum/maximum degree: $\delta(G)$, $\Delta(G)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** In $C_5$ plus one chord, degrees of the two chord endpoints and of a far vertex?

**Solution.** Chord endpoints degree $3$; the vertex opposite a non-endpoint on $C_5$ may be degree $2$. Concretely $C_5$ on $(1,2,3,4,5)$ with chord $1$-$3$: degrees $3,2,3,2,2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Regular of degree $3$ on $10$ vertices is called

**Solution.** cubic; every degree is $3$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Counting a loop as degree $1$, or mixing up $\delta$ and $\Delta$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Degree of each vertex in $K_8$.

**Solution.** $7$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** A simple graph on $6$ vertices with $\Delta=5$. What must be true of that vertex?

**Solution.** It is adjacent to every other vertex.
