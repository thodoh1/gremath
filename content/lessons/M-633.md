# Lesson M-633 — Formal convergence of sequences

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Formal convergence of sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Formal convergence of sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

## 3. Precise statement

A sequence $(a_n)$ in $\mathbb{R}$ converges to $L$ if the terms become and remain arbitrarily close to $L$: the tail lies in every neighborhood of $L$. The limit is unique when it exists. Convergence implies boundedness; the converse is false.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $a_n=(-1)^n$ converge? Does $a_n=1/n$?

**Solution.** No: terms oscillate between $-1$ and $1$. Yes: $1/n\to 0$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $a_n\to L$ and $a_n\to M$, then

**Solution.** $L=M$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling a sequence convergent because it is bounded, or because $a_{n+1}-a_n\to 0$ (harmonic counterexample).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is every convergent sequence bounded?

**Solution.** Yes.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Give a bounded divergent sequence.

**Solution.** $(-1)^n$.
