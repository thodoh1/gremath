# Lesson M-312 — Rearrangement effects

Part G. G. Infinite Series · **Infinite series** · Critical

Prerequisites: `F.1-G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Rearrangement effects** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Series tests and Taylor series are standard GRE calculus. Do not treat this part as optional analysis.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Rearrangement effects**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Infinite series*

A series converges when its partial sums converge. Terms $\to 0$ is necessary, not sufficient. Geometric, telescoping, $p$, comparison, limit comparison, integral, alternating, ratio, root, absolute vs conditional: pick the test that fits in one glance.

### This skill, specifically

A series converges when its partial sums converge.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Rearrangement: if $\sum a_n$ is absolutely convergent, every rearrangement has the same sum. If conditionally convergent, rearrangements can produce any sum in $[-\infty,\infty]$ (Riemann). On the GRE: do not rearrange conditionally convergent series casually.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Can you rearrange $\sum (-1)^{n+1}/n=\ln 2$ and change the sum?

**Solution.** Yes if you permute order (Riemann). Pairing consecutive terms does not change the sum of a convergent series, but a general rearrangement can produce any real.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A geometric series may be rearranged freely because

**Solution.** it is absolutely convergent for $|r|<1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating Grandi’s $1-1+1-1+\cdots$ as a rearrangement example — it does not converge at all.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** True or false: if $\sum |a_n|<\infty$, rearrangements preserve the sum.

**Solution.** True.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Regrouping $(1-1)+(1-1)+\cdots$ vs $1+(-1+1)+\cdots$.

**Solution.** Both are invalid as sums of a divergent series.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
