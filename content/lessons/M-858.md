## 3. Precise statement

Gamma($(\alpha,\lambda)$) (shape-rate): PDF $\dfrac{\lambda^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\lambda x}$ for $x>0$. Mean $\alpha/\lambda$, variance $\alpha/\lambda^2$. Gamma($1,\lambda$)=Exp($\lambda$). Beta($\alpha,\beta$): PDF $\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}$ on $(0,1)$, mean $\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)$. Beta($1,1$)=Unif($0,1$).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Gamma shape $4$, rate $2$. Mean and variance.

**Solution.** Mean $2$, variance $1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Beta($2,5$). Mean.

**Solution.** $2/7$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing shape-rate with shape-scale (scale $\theta=1/\lambda$ makes mean $\alpha\theta$). Using Beta on $(0,\infty)$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
