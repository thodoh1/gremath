# Lesson M-261 — u-substitution

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Antiderivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **u-substitution** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **u-substitution**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Antiderivatives*

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$. The toolkit: power rule, substitution, parts, partial fractions, trig integrals, trig sub, reduction. Choosing the tool is the skill; carrying out the algebra is the tax.

### This skill, specifically

An antiderivative of $f$ is $F$ with $F'=f$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$u$-substitution: if $u=g(x)$ is $C^1$, $\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du$. For definite integrals, change the limits: $\int_a^b f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\,du$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\int 2x\cos(x^2)\,dx$.

**Solution.** $u=x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$, $\sin(x^2)+C$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\int_0^1 x(x^2+1)^3\,dx$

**Solution.** $u=x^2+1$, $u:1\to 2$, $\frac12\int_1^2 u^3\,du=\frac{15}{8}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Substituting $u$ but keeping $dx$ limits in $x$, or forgetting $du=g'\,dx$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\,dx$.

**Solution.** $\sqrt{1+x^2}+C$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why rewrite $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos x\,dx$ with $u=\sin x$?

**Solution.** $u:0\to 1$, $\int_0^1 u\,du=1/2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
