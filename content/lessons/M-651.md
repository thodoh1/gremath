# Lesson M-651 — Power-series convergence

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Series & convergence** · High

## 3. Precise statement

A power series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n(x-a)^n$ has a radius of convergence $R\in[0,\infty]$ with $1/R=\limsup |c_n|^{1/n}$ (or ratio $|c_n/c_{n+1}|$ when that limit exists). The series converges (absolutely) for $|x-a|<R$ and diverges for $|x-a|>R$. Endpoints $x=a\pm R$ require separate tests.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Find $R$ for $\sum n! x^n$ and for $\sum x^n/n!$.

**Solution.** Ratio: first $|(n+1)x|\to\infty$ unless $x=0$, so $R=0$. Second $|x|/(n+1)\to 0$, so $R=\infty$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** For $\sum x^n/n$, the radius is $1$. At $x=1$ and $x=-1$?

**Solution.** $x=1$ harmonic diverges; $x=-1$ alternating harmonic converges (conditionally).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Declaring convergence at the endpoints from the value of $R$ alone.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Radius of $\sum (x-2)^n/2^n$.

**Solution.** $R=2$ (geometric $|x-2|/2<1$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Inside the open disk of convergence, the series converges

**Solution.** absolutely.
