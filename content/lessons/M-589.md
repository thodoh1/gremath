# Lesson M-589 — Ring homomorphisms

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4-L.2`.


This lesson isolates **Ring homomorphisms** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Ring homomorphisms**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Rings*

Two operations, distributive. Commutative rings, identity, subrings, domains, units, zero-divisors, homomorphisms. $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the GRE’s favorite ring.

### This skill, specifically

Two operations, distributive.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A ring homomorphism $\phi:R\to S$ satisfies $\phi(a+b)=\phi(a)+\phi(b)$ and $\phi(ab)=\phi(a)\phi(b)$. Unital homs also have $\phi(1_R)=1_S$. Then $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(-a)=-\phi(a)$. Examples: reduction $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$; evaluation $F[x]\to F$, $f\mapsto f(a)$; inclusion of a subring; $R\to R\times S$, $r\mapsto(r,0)$ is a non-unital hom.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** The evaluation $\mathrm{ev}_2:\mathbb{Z}[x]\to\mathbb{Z}$, $f\mapsto f(2)$. Kernel?

**Solution.** A unital ring hom. Remainder theorem: $f=(x-2)q+f(2)$ with $q\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ because $x-2$ is monic, so $\ker=(x-2)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Unique unital hom $\mathbb{Z}\to R$ for a unital ring $R$

**Solution.** $n\mapsto n\cdot 1_R$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting multiplicativity (only checking additivity: that is a hom of abelian groups).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Reduction $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is surjective. Kernel?

**Solution.** $n\mathbb{Z}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $x\mapsto 2x$ a ring hom $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** No: $2(ab)\neq (2a)(2b)$ in general.
