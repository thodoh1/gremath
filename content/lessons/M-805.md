# Lesson M-805 — Euler formula for planar graphs

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Coloring & planarity** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.2`.


This lesson isolates **Euler formula for planar graphs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Euler formula for planar graphs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Euler's formula for a connected plane graph: $v-e+f=2$, where $f$ counts faces including the infinite outer face. For a plane graph with $c$ components, $v-e+f=1+c$. A forest has $e=v-c$ and $f=1$, which matches: $v-(v-c)+1=c+1$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A connected plane graph with $v=8$, $e=12$. How many faces?

**Solution.** $f=2-v+e=2-8+12=6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Tree on $10$ vertices drawn in the plane. Faces?

**Solution.** $f=2-10+9=1$, only the outer face.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting the outer face, so $v-e+f=1$ by mistake, or using $v-e+f=2$ on a disconnected graph.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Cube skeleton: $v=8$, $e=12$, $f=?$

**Solution.** $6$, and $8-12+6=2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Connected plane graph, all faces degree $3$ (including outer), $v=6$. Find $e,f$.

**Solution.** $2e=3f$ and $v-e+f=2\Rightarrow 6-e+2e/3=2\Rightarrow 4=e/3\Rightarrow e=12$, $f=8$ (octahedron).

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
