# Lesson M-282 — Center of mass

Part F. F. Calculus II — Integration · **Applications of integration** · High

Prerequisites: `F.2`.


This lesson isolates **Center of mass** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Integrals, FTC, and applications are first-class GRE citizens. Improper integrals feed series.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Center of mass**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Center of mass of a lamina with density $\rho$ in the plane: $\bar x=M_y/m$, $\bar y=M_x/m$, with $m=\iint\rho\,dA$, $M_y=\iint x\rho\,dA$, $M_x=\iint y\rho\,dA$. For uniform $\rho$ along a region under $f$ on $[a,b]$: $\bar x=\frac{1}{A}\int x f\,dx$, $\bar y=\frac{1}{A}\int \frac12 f^2\,dx$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Centroid of the triangle under $y=1-x$ on $[0,1]$.

**Solution.** $A=1/2$, $\bar x=\frac{1}{A}\int_0^1 x(1-x)\,dx=1/3$, $\bar y=\frac{1}{A}\int_0^1\frac12(1-x)^2\,dx=1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Centroid of a semicircular disk of radius $r$

**Solution.** On the axis of symmetry, $\bar y=4r/(3\pi)$ from the diameter.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Averaging the endpoint heights and calling that $\bar y$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Uniform rod on $[0,L]$, $\bar x$.

**Solution.** $L/2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If density is higher on the right, $\bar x$ moves

**Solution.** right of the geometric centroid.
