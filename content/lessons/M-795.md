# Lesson M-795 — Rooted trees

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Rooted trees** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Rooted trees**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

$K_n$, bipartite (including trees), rooted trees, leaves, spanning trees, Euler vs Hamilton.

## 3. Precise statement

A rooted tree distinguishes a root $r$. Every other vertex has a unique parent (the neighbor toward $r$); children are the other neighbors. Depth of $v$ is $d(r,v)$. The rooting does not change the underlying undirected tree, only the orientation of edges away from $r$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A rooted tree on $7$ vertices. How many parent pointers besides the root?

**Solution.** $6$, one for each non-root; equivalently $6$ edges.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Root a path $P_5$ at an endpoint vs at the middle vertex. Heights?

**Solution.** Height $4$ vs height $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming every rooted tree is binary, or counting the root as having a parent.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** In a rooted tree, sum over non-roots of $1$ equals

**Solution.** $n-1$, also equal to the number of children-slots filled.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Can two vertices have the same parent?

**Solution.** Yes, they are siblings.
