# Lesson M-775 — Derangements

Part R. R. Combinatorics · **Advanced counting tools** · Medium

This lesson isolates **Derangements** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Combinatorics is additional topics and also probability setup.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Derangements**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A derangement is a permutation with no fixed points. The number is $!n=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^k/k!$, equivalently $!n=(n-1)\bigl[!(n-1)+!(n-2)\bigr]$ with $!0=1$, $!1=0$. Also $!n$ is the nearest integer to $n!/e$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $!4$ and $!5$.

**Solution.** $!4=9$, $!5=44$. Check: $24(1-1+1/2-1/6+1/24)=24\cdot 9/24=9$; $120\cdot(1-1+1/2-1/6+1/24-1/120)=44$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $!6$ equals

**Solution.** $265$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $n!-1$ (that only removes the identity) or $n!/e$ without rounding, or counting permutations that fix at least one point as $n$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Probability that a random permutation of $8$ letters is a derangement, to two decimals.

**Solution.** $!8/8!\approx 1/e\approx 0.37$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Hats returned at random to $3$ people. $P(\text{no one gets own hat})$.

**Solution.** $!3/3!=2/6=1/3$.
