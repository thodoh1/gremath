# Lesson M-505 — Prime factorization

Part L. L. Number Theory · **Divisibility & primes** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1-A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Prime factorization** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Number theory is additional-topics / algebra: congruences, Euler, CRT.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Prime factorization**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Divisibility & primes*

$a\mid b$, division algorithm, gcd, lcm, Euclid, Bézout, unique factorization, $\tau$ and $\sigma$ from prime factors. This is the arithmetic of $\mathbb{Z}$ as a Euclidean domain.

### This skill, specifically

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Fundamental theorem of arithmetic: every integer $n>1$ factors uniquely as $n=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}$ with primes $p_1<\cdots<p_k$ and $e_i\ge 1$. Uniqueness is up to order. From this, $a\mid b$ iff every exponent in $a$ is $\le$ the corresponding exponent in $b$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Factor $360$ completely.

**Solution.** $360=36\cdot 10=2^2 3^2\cdot 2\cdot 5=2^3\cdot 3^2\cdot 5$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $84$ factors as

**Solution.** $2^2\cdot 3\cdot 7$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $1$ as a prime in the factorization, or forgetting a repeated factor ($12=2\cdot 6$ is incomplete).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Prime factorization of $1001$.

**Solution.** $7\cdot 11\cdot 13$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Does $2^2\cdot 3$ divide $2^3\cdot 3^2\cdot 5$?

**Solution.** Yes: exponents $2\le 3$ and $1\le 2$.
