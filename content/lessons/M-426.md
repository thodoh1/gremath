# Lesson M-426 — Cross product in R^3

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vectors & geometry** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cross product in R^3** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cross product in R^3**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

The cross product on $\mathbb{R}^3$: $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}$ is perpendicular to both, $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{u}|\,|\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta$ (parallelogram area), and $(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})$ is right-handed. In coordinates, $\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{k}$ and cyclic, and $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\ u_1&u_2&u_3\\ v_1&v_2&v_3\end{pmatrix}$. Anticommutative; $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $(2,0,0)\times(0,3,0)$ and $(1,2,3)\times(4,5,6)$.

**Solution.** $(0,0,6)$ and $(12-15,12-6,5-8)=(-3,6,-3)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{i}$ equals

**Solution.** $-\mathbf{k}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating $\times$ as defined in $\mathbb{R}^2$ as a vector (the $2$D analogue is the scalar $u_1 v_2-u_2 v_1$), or using $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{u}$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $|(1,0,0)\times(1,1,0)|$.

**Solution.** $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Area of the parallelogram spanned by $(1,2,0)$ and $(0,3,0)$.

**Solution.** $3$.
