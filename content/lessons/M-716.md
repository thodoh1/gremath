# Lesson M-716 — Direct proof

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Direct proof** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Direct proof**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A direct proof of $P\to Q$ assumes $P$ and, using definitions and known theorems, deduces $Q$. Algebraic identities, chasing elements in sets, and unfolding definitions are typical. Keep the hypothesis in play until the conclusion appears.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove directly: the product of two even integers is even.

**Solution.** Let $m=2k$, $n=2\ell$. Then $mn=4k\ell=2(2k\ell)$, even.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Direct proof of ``odd times odd is odd'': write

**Solution.** $(2k+1)(2\ell+1)=2(\cdots)+1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Inserting an unjustified step that secretly uses the conclusion (circularity).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Prove: if $a\mid b$ and $b\mid c$ then $a\mid c$.

**Solution.** $b=aq$, $c=br=aqr$, so $a\mid c$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** To prove $A\subset B$ directly, take $x\in A$ and show

**Solution.** $x\in B$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
