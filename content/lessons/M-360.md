# Lesson M-360 — Area/volume/mass applications

Part H. H. Multivariable Calculus · **Multiple integration** · High

Prerequisites: `H.1-E.3`.


This lesson isolates **Area/volume/mass applications** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Multivariable calculus is explicitly in the calculus portion: partials, gradients, multiple integrals, Jacobians.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Area/volume/mass applications**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Multiple integration*

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map. $|\det D\Phi|$ is the volume distortion. Setup beats brute force.

### This skill, specifically

Iterate, change order, Jacobian in polar/cylindrical/spherical or a general map.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Applications: area $\iint 1\,dA$, volume $\iiint 1\,dV$, mass $\iiint\rho\,dV$, average $\frac{1}{\mathrm{meas}}\iiint f$, centroid $\bar x=\iiint x\rho\,dV/m$. Choose coordinates that match the region and the integrand.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Mass of the unit disk with $\rho=1+x^2+y^2$.

**Solution.** Polar: $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1(1+r^2)r\,dr=\pi(1+1/2)=3\pi/2$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Centroid of a uniform semicircular disk of radius $a$

**Solution.** $\bar y=4a/(3\pi)$ on the axis of symmetry.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Computing mass with $dA$ but forgetting $\rho$, or using the wrong Jacobian.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Volume of the tetrahedron $x,y,z\ge 0$, $x+y+z\le 1$.

**Solution.** $1/6$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Solution.** centroid (geometric center).
