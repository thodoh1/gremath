# Lesson M-780 — Combinatorial probability setups

Part R. R. Combinatorics · **Advanced counting tools** · Medium

Prerequisites: `R.2-G.2`.


This lesson isolates **Combinatorial probability setups** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Combinatorics is additional topics and also probability setup.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Combinatorial probability setups**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Combinatorial probability: if every outcome in a finite sample space $S$ is equally likely, $P(A)=|A|/|S|$. Compute $|S|$ and $|A|$ by the counting tools above (permutations, combinations, complement, PIE). Dependence of draws (with/without replacement) changes $|S|$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** An urn has $12$ balls, $5$ red. Draw $3$ without replacement. $P(\text{exactly }2\text{ red})$.

**Solution.** $\dfrac{\binom{5}{2}\binom{7}{1}}{\binom{12}{3}}=\dfrac{10\cdot 7}{220}=\dfrac{7}{22}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A fair $6$-sided die is rolled twice. $P(\text{sum}=9)$.

**Solution.** $4$ outcomes $(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3)$ out of $36$, so $1/9$. (Not $(4.5,4.5)$.)

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $n^k$ when sampling is without replacement, or $\binom{n}{k}$ when order is built into the sample space of sequences.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $P(\text{both aces})$ when drawing $2$ cards from $52$.

**Solution.** $\binom{4}{2}/\binom{52}{2}=6/1326=1/221$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Five distinct books shelved at random. $P(\text{two particular books are adjacent})$.

**Solution.** Treat the pair as a block: $2\cdot 4!/5!=2/5$.
