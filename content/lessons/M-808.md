## 3. Precise statement

An incidence matrix $B$ (oriented) is $n\times m$ with rows vertices and columns edges; each column has a $+1$ and a $-1$ at the endpoints (orientation chosen). Then $B B^T$ is a Laplacian $D-A$. Unoriented $(0,1)$-incidence has two $1$s per column. Rank of the Laplacian is $n-c$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A path of two edges (vertices $1,2,3$). Give a $3\times 2$ oriented incidence matrix.

**Solution.** Columns for $1\to 2$ and $2\to 3$: $\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ (signs only need to be opposite on each column).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Confusing incidence with adjacency (square vs $n\times m$), or putting degree on the diagonal of $B$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Unoriented incidence: number of $1$s in the whole matrix for a simple graph.

**Solution.** $2|E|$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Kernel dimension of $B^T$ for a connected graph.

**Solution.** $1$: $B^T\mathbf{1}=0$, and connectedness makes $\ker B^T$ exactly the constants.
