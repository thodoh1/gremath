# Lesson M-711 — Universal quantifier

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Predicate logic** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Universal quantifier** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Universal quantifier**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Predicate logic*

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$). Necessary vs sufficient is $\to$ direction. This is how $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ is written.

### This skill, specifically

Predicates, $\forall,\exists$, nested quantifiers, negation rules ($\neg\forall=\exists\neg$).

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The universal quantifier $\forall x\,P(x)$ means $P(a)$ holds for every $a$ in the universe. Vacuous truth: $\forall x\in\emptyset,\,P(x)$ is true. Restricted form $\forall x\in A,\,P(x)$ means $\forall x\,(x\in A\to P(x))$. To prove $\forall$, take an arbitrary element and argue.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Truth of $\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\, n\ge 0$ and of $\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\, n\ge 2$.

**Solution.** True; false (counterexample $n=0$ or $1$, depending on whether $0\in\mathbb{N}$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\forall x\in\emptyset,\, x=x$ is

**Solution.** true (vacuous).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Proving $\forall$ by checking a few examples, or using a specific $x$ that was not arbitrary.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Negation of $\forall x\,P(x)$.

**Solution.** $\exists x\,\neg P(x)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** To disprove $\forall n,\, n^2>n$ over $\mathbb{N}$, give

**Solution.** a counterexample, e.g. $n=1$ if $1\in\mathbb{N}$, or $n=0$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
