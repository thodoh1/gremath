# Lesson M-071 — Even and odd functions

Part B. B. Precalculus & Functions · **Functions** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A.3`.


This lesson isolates **Even and odd functions** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Functions and precalculus appear inside calculus items constantly: domains of compositions, inverses, piecewise definitions, sequences.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Even and odd functions**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Functions*

A function $f:A\to B$ assigns to each $x\in A$ exactly one $f(x)\in B$. Domain, codomain, image, injectivity, surjectivity, composition, and inverses are set-theoretic ideas that later become linear maps, homomorphisms, and random variables. Graphs are pictures of functions, not the functions themselves.

### This skill, specifically

A function $f:A\to B$ assigns to each $x\in A$ exactly one $f(x)\in B$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

$f$ is even if $f(-x)=f(x)$ (reflection symmetry through the $y$-axis). Odd if $f(-x)=-f(x)$ (rotational symmetry $180^\circ$). Even$\cdot$even is even; odd$\cdot$odd is even; even$\cdot$odd is odd.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is $x^3-x$ even, odd, or neither?

**Solution.** Odd.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^x+e^{-x}$ is

**Solution.** even (a multiple of $\cosh$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking only $f(-1)=f(1)$ and declaring even.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is $\sin x+\cos x$ odd?

**Solution.** Neither.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Product of two odd functions.

**Solution.** Even.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
