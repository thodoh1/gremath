# Lesson M-541 — Identity and inverses

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Groups** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Identity and inverses** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Identity and inverses**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Groups*

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses. Abelian means commutative. Subgroups, cyclic groups, orders of elements and of the group: Lagrange is coming.

### This skill, specifically

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

The identity is unique: if $e,e'$ both act as identities then $e=ee'=e'$. The inverse of $g$ is unique, and $(g^{-1})^{-1}=g$, $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$. Left identity plus left inverses already imply a group (a common shortcut). Solving $ax=b$ gives $x=a^{-1}b$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove the identity is unique, and compute $(2\,3)^{-1}$ in $S_3$.

**Solution.** If $eg=g=e'g$ then $e=e(e')=(ee')=e'$. A transposition is its own inverse: $(2\,3)^{-1}=(2\,3)$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Canceling ‘the identity’ of two different operations (e.g. $0$ vs $1$).
