# Lesson M-538 — Binary operations

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Groups** · High

Prerequisites: `A.1-Q.1`.


This lesson isolates **Binary operations** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Binary operations**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Groups*

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses. Abelian means commutative. Subgroups, cyclic groups, orders of elements and of the group: Lagrange is coming.

### This skill, specifically

A group is a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A binary operation on a set $S$ is a map $S\times S\to S$ (closure). Examples: $+,\cdot$ on $\mathbb{Z}$; composition of functions; matrix multiplication on $M_n$. It may or may not be associative ($(ab)c=a(bc)$) or commutative ($ab=ba$). Subtraction on $\mathbb{Z}$ is binary but not associative; division is not even a binary operation on $\mathbb{Z}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is subtraction on $\mathbb{Z}$ associative? Is matrix multiplication on $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ commutative?

**Solution.** $(1-2)-3=-4$ but $1-(2-3)=2$, not associative. Matrix multiplication is associative but not commutative: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\neq$ the swap.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $a*b=(a+b)/2$ a binary operation on $\mathbb{Z}$?

**Solution.** No: not closed ($1*2=1.5\notin\mathbb{Z}$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting closure (max on $\mathbb{Q}$ is fine; geometric mean on $\mathbb{R}$ fails negatives).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Is union a binary operation on the power set of $\{1,2,3\}$?

**Solution.** Yes, associative and commutative.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Exponentiation $a*b=a^b$ on $\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$: associative?

**Solution.** No: $(2^3)^2=64$, $2^{(3^2)}=512$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
