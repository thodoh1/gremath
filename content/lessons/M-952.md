# Lesson M-952 — Cauchy integral formula

Part W. W. Complex Analysis · **Complex integration** · Medium

Prerequisites: `W.1`.


This lesson isolates **Cauchy integral formula** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Complex analysis is additional topics: Cauchy–Riemann, residues at a basic level.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Cauchy integral formula**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Complex integration*

### This skill, specifically

Contours, $\int_\gamma f\,dz$, Cauchy’s theorem (vanishing on closed curves in simply connected holomorphic regions), Cauchy integral formula, deformation.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Cauchy integral formula: if $f$ is holomorphic on and inside a simple closed positively oriented $\gamma$, and $a$ is inside $\gamma$, then $f(a)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \dfrac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz$. Differentiating under the integral: $f^{(n)}(a)=\dfrac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \dfrac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz$. In particular, $f$ is infinitely differentiable.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=1}\dfrac{e^z}{z}\,dz$.

**Solution.** $e^0=1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f''(0)$ for $f$ entire equals

**Solution.** $\dfrac{2}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=1}\dfrac{f(z)}{z^3}\,dz$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using CIF when $a$ is outside $\gamma$ (then the integral is $0$ if $f$ is holomorphic inside), or dropping $2\pi i$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\int_{|z|=2}\dfrac{\sin z}{z-\pi/2}\,dz$.

**Solution.** $2\pi i\sin(\pi/2)=2\pi i$ ($\pi/2$ is inside $|z|=2$).

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** CIF with $n=1$: $f'(a)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int\dfrac{f(z)}{(z-a)^2}\,dz$. True or false?

**Solution.** True.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
