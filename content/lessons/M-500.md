## 3. Precise statement

The greatest common divisor $\gcd(a,b)$ is the largest positive integer dividing both (set $\gcd(0,0)$ undefined or $0$ by convention). $\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(|a|,|b|)=\gcd(b,a)=\gcd(a,b-aq)$. Integers are coprime when $\gcd=1$. $\gcd(a,0)=|a|$ for $a\neq 0$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Compute $\gcd(54,24)$ by listing, then by subtracting.

**Solution.** Positive common divisors of $54,24$ are $1,2,3,6$, so $\gcd=6$. Also $\gcd(54,24)=\gcd(24,6)=\gcd(6,0)=6$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\gcd(0,12)$ equals

**Solution.** $12$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Reporting a negative gcd, or taking $\gcd(8,12)=8$ because $8<12$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $\gcd(35,10)$.

**Solution.** $5$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Is $\gcd(9,-6)$ equal to $\gcd(9,6)$?

**Solution.** Yes, both $3$.
