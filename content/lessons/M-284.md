## 3. Precise statement

Average value $\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f$ is also the geometric probability that a random vertical line hits below $y=f$ if you recast areas as probabilities (uniform on a rectangle). Geometric probability: $P=\mathrm{area}(\mathrm{favorable})/\mathrm{area}(\mathrm{sample})$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** A point uniform in the unit square. $P(y<x^2)$.

**Solution.** Area under $x^2$ is $1/3$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Average of $f=2x$ on $[0,1]$ equals

**Solution.** $1$, which is also $P(U\le 2X)$ type pictures in unit square after scaling.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using net signed area as a probability (probabilities cannot be negative).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Two random numbers in $[0,1]$: $P(|X-Y|<1/2)$.

**Solution.** Area of the band about $y=x$ of width $1/2$ in the square: $1-(1/2)^2=3/4$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Buffon’s needle is geometric probability using

**Solution.** an integral (or area) in an angle-position square.
