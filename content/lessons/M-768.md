# Lesson M-768 — Vandermonde-type identity awareness

Part R. R. Combinatorics · **Combinatorial identities** · High

Prerequisites: `R.1`.


This lesson isolates **Vandermonde-type identity awareness** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Combinatorics is additional topics and also probability setup.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Vandermonde-type identity awareness**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Vandermonde: $\sum_{j}\binom{m}{j}\binom{n}{r-j}=\binom{m+n}{r}$. Combinatorially: choose $r$ people from $m$ men and $n$ women. Algebraically it is the coefficient of $x^r$ in $(1+x)^m(1+x)^n=(1+x)^{m+n}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Choose $3$ from $4$ men and $6$ women. Expand by how many men are chosen and total.

**Solution.** $\binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{3}+\binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{2}+\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{1}+\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{0}=20+60+36+4=120=\binom{10}{3}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $\sum_{j}\binom{8}{j}\binom{5}{3-j}$ equals

**Solution.** $\binom{13}{3}=286$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Summing $\binom{m}{j}\binom{n}{j}$ and calling it $\binom{m+n}{j}$ (that is a different, unsigned, identity only after extra factors).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Committee of $4$ from $7$ juniors and $5$ seniors. Number of committees with exactly $2$ juniors.

**Solution.** $\binom{7}{2}\binom{5}{2}=21\cdot 10=210$, one Vandermonde term.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Check $j=0$ to $2$ for $\sum\binom{3}{j}\binom{2}{2-j}$.

**Solution.** $\binom{3}{0}\binom{2}{2}+\binom{3}{1}\binom{2}{1}+\binom{3}{2}\binom{2}{0}=1+6+3=10=\binom{5}{2}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
