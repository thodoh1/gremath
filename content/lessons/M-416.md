# Lesson M-416 — Separation of variables for basic PDEs

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Transforms & expansions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `J.1-G.3`.


This lesson isolates **Separation of variables for basic PDEs** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Separation of variables for basic PDEs**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Transforms & expansions*

Laplace transforms turn ODEs into algebra. Fourier series expand periodic functions in sines and cosines. Separation of variables is the PDE cameo.

## 3. Precise statement

Separation of variables for a linear PDE: seek $u=X(x)T(t)$ (or $X(x)Y(y)$). The PDE splits into ODEs linked by a constant $-\lambda$, chosen so that boundary conditions make an eigenvalue problem. Superpose the product solutions (Fourier series).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Heat equation $u_t=k u_{xx}$, $u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0$. Find product solutions.

**Solution.** $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=X(\pi)=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $X=\sin nx$, $\lambda=n^2$, $T=e^{-n^2 k t}$. So $u_n=\sin(nx)e^{-n^2 k t}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Laplace $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ with $u=X(x)Y(y)$ yields

**Solution.** $X''/X=-Y''/Y=\pm\lambda$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Forgetting boundary conditions when selecting $\lambda$ (taking $\lambda<0$ for Dirichlet on a finite interval, which typically fails).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Wave $u_{tt}=c^2 u_{xx}$, same Dirichlet, time factor?

**Solution.** $T=\cos(n c t)$ or $\sin(n c t)$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why superpose?

**Solution.** Linearity: $\sum b_n \sin(nx)e^{-n^2 k t}$ matches an initial sine series.
