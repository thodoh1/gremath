# Lesson M-1028 — Practice timed blocks

Part Y. Y. Mixed GRE Problem-Solving · **Hard-problem training** · Critical

Prerequisites: `A-Y`.


This lesson isolates **Practice timed blocks** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is not content weight; it is how every weighted item is actually scored under a clock.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Practice timed blocks**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Hard-problem training*

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

### This skill, specifically

Unfamiliar mixes, proof-flavored MCQ, counterexamples, existence/uniqueness, nonstandard routes, topic-blind practice, timed blocks, post-mortem of every miss.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Timed-block criterion: a $10$-item mixed block in $25$ minutes at $\ge 8/10$ correct, with at most one item left blank-then-guessed without classification. That is the exam’s average pace. Use an actual clock, not a vibes clock. After each block, log the two slowest items by concept, not by ‘I was tired’. Repeat until three consecutive blocks meet the bar.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Diagnostic block (do under $25$ min): (1) $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\ 3&4\end{pmatrix}$ (2) $\int_0^1 2x\,dx$ (3) radius of $\sum n x^n$ (4) $P(\text{two heads in two tosses})$ (5) centroid of $(0,0),(6,0),(0,6)$ (6) Res$(e^z/z,0)$ (7) Newton step for $x^2-2$ at $1$ (8) eigenvalues of a $2\times 2$ diagonal (9) $|i^{10}|$ (10) number of real roots of $x^2+1=0$.

**Solution.** Answers: $-2$; $1$; $1$; $1/4$; $(2,2)$; $1$; $3/2$; the diagonal entries; $1$; $0$. Gate: $\ge 8/10$ in $25$ min.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** A passing timed block still requires

**Solution.** written classification plus the numeric answer, not a lucky guess on $8$ items.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Untimed grinding of $50$ similar derivatives, then calling it timed practice.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** If a $10$-item block takes $40$ minutes at $9/10$, the failure mode is

**Solution.** pace, not accuracy; flag-and-return was not used.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Three consecutive passing blocks is the repeatable standard because

**Solution.** one lucky block does not show a stable pace.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
