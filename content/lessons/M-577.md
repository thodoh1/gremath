# Lesson M-577 — Orbit-stabilizer theorem

Part M. M. Group Theory · **Group actions** · Medium

Prerequisites: `M.4`.


This lesson isolates **Orbit-stabilizer theorem** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Group theory is the main abstract-algebra GRE diet: orders, Lagrange, homomorphisms.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Orbit-stabilizer theorem**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Group actions*

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$. Orbit-stabilizer: $|G|=|\mathrm{Orb}|\,|\mathrm{Stab}|$. Conjugacy is a special action. Finite abelian groups decompose into cyclic groups.

### This skill, specifically

An action is a homomorphism $G\to S_X$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Orbit-stabilizer: $|G\cdot x|=[G:G_x]$. If $G$ is finite, $|G|=|G\cdot x|\,|G_x|$. This computes orbit sizes and $|G|$ (e.g. $|S_n|=n\cdot(n-1)!$ by stab of $1$ isomorphic to $S_{n-1}$). Counting formula: $|X|=\sum |G|/|G_{x_i}|$ summed over orbit representatives.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Verify orbit-stabilizer for $S_3$ acting on $\{1,2,3\}$ at the point $1$.

**Solution.** Orbit of $1$ has size $3$, $|G_1|=2$, product $6=|S_3|$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $|GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)|$ by choosing images of a basis: first column

**Solution.** $p^2-1$ nonzero vectors, second $p^2-p$ not a multiple of the first: $(p^2-1)(p^2-p)$. Orbit-stab on the set of bases.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $|G|=|G\cdot x|+|G_x|$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Size of the orbit of a vertex under $D_4$ (order $8$).

**Solution.** Stabilizer of a vertex is $\{e,$ reflection through that vertex$\}$, order $2$, orbit $4$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** If $|G|=12$ and $|G_x|=4$, the orbit has size

**Solution.** $3$.
