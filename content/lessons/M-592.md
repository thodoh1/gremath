# Lesson M-592 — Principal ideals

Part N. N. Rings, Modules & Fields · **Ideals & quotient rings** · Medium

Prerequisites: `N.1`.


This lesson isolates **Principal ideals** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Rings and fields appear as recognition and finite examples, especially $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Principal ideals**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Ideals & quotient rings*

Ideals are kernels of ring maps. Principal, prime, maximal. $R/I$ is a field iff $I$ is maximal (in commutative unital rings). Polynomial ideals in $F[x]$ are principal.

### This skill, specifically

Ideals are kernels of ring maps.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

A principal ideal is generated by one element: $(a)=\{ra:r\in R\}$ in a commutative unital ring (or $RaR$ two-sided in general). $\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$ are PIDs: every ideal is principal. $(2,x)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not principal. $(a)\subseteq(b)$ iff $b\mid a$ in a domain. $(a)=(b)$ iff $a$ and $b$ are associates.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove $(2,x)$ is not principal in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

**Solution.** If $(2,x)=(f)$, then $f\mid 2$ so $f=\pm 1$ or $\pm 2$ (units of $\mathbb{Z}$). $f=\pm 1$ gives $(1)=\mathbb{Z}[x]\neq(2,x)$. $f=\pm 2$: then $x\in(2)$, so $x=2g$, impossible by degrees/content.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$: all principal, generated by

**Solution.** the nonnegative generator $n$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $(2,x)=(2)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ because $x$ ‘doesn’t matter’.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $(6)\cap(9)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $(18)=\mathrm{lcm}(6,9)\mathbb{Z}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $(6)+(9)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.** $(3)=\gcd(6,9)\mathbb{Z}$.
