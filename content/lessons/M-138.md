# Lesson M-138 — Trig equations on restricted domains

Part C. C. Trigonometry & Conics · **Trigonometric equations & inequalities** · Critical

Prerequisites: `C.1-C.2`.


This lesson isolates **Trig equations on restricted domains** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Trigonometry appears in calculus, geometry, and as standalone identity/equation items. Exact values and identities are speed.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Trig equations on restricted domains**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Trigonometric equations & inequalities*

Because of periodicity, trig equations have infinitely many solutions unless the domain is restricted. General solutions add $2\pi k$ or $\pi k$ according to the function. Inverse trig functions return principal values; they are not full inverse relations. Always state the range convention.

### This skill, specifically

Because of periodicity, trig equations have infinitely many solutions unless the domain is restricted.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

On a restricted domain, take the general solution and keep those $k$ that land in the interval. Draw the unit circle or one period of the graph.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Solve $\cos x=-1/2$ for $x\in[0,\pi]$.

**Solution.** $x=2\pi/3$ only (the other standard angle $4\pi/3$ is outside).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Number of solutions of $\sin x=1/3$ in $[0,4\pi)$.

**Solution.** Four.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Including endpoints incorrectly when the function is undefined there ($\tan$ at $\pi/2$).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Solve $\tan x=\sqrt{3}$ on $(-\pi,\pi)$.

**Solution.** $x=\pi/3, -2\pi/3$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** How many $x\in[0,2\pi)$ with $\sin 2x=0$?

**Solution.** Four: $0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
