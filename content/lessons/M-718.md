# Lesson M-718 — Proof by contradiction

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Proof by contradiction** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Proof by contradiction**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Proof by contradiction: to prove $P$, assume $\neg P$ and derive a falsehood (e.g. $1=0$, or a previously known theorem's negation). Classic: $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. Unlike contrapositive, there is no implication being rewritten; the whole claim is at stake.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

**Solution.** If $\sqrt{2}=p/q$ in lowest terms, then $p^2=2q^2$, so $p$ even, $p=2k$, then $q^2=2k^2$, $q$ even, contradicting lowest terms.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Assume there are finitely many primes, Euclid-style, and derive

**Solution.** a new prime $N=p_1\cdots p_k+1$ (more carefully: a prime factor of $N$ not in the list).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Assuming extra structure not in $\neg P$, or stopping at a surprising but possible statement.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Prove there is no largest integer by contradiction.

**Solution.** If $N$ is largest, $N+1>N$ is an integer.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Contradiction vs contrapositive for $P\to Q$.

**Solution.** Contradiction assumes $P$ and $\neg Q$; contrapositive assumes only $\neg Q$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
