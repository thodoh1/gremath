# Lesson M-799 — Euler circuits

Part S. S. Graph Theory & Algorithms · **Special graph classes** · Medium

Prerequisites: `S.1`.


This lesson isolates **Euler circuits** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Graph theory is additional topics: trees, Euler, handshaking, planarity.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Euler circuits**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

An Euler circuit (Euler tour) is a closed Euler trail: every edge exactly once, return to start. Existence: the graph is connected (up to isolated vertices) and every degree is even. Then $\sum\deg=2|E|$ is consistent automatically.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Does $C_7$ have an Euler circuit? $K_4$?

**Solution.** $C_7$ yes (it is $2$-regular). $K_4$ is $3$-regular, four odd vertices: no.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $K_{2,2}$ is $C_4$: Euler circuit?

**Solution.** Yes, all degrees $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Requiring an Euler circuit to visit every vertex exactly once (that is Hamiltonian).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** A connected $4$-regular graph on $11$ vertices. Euler circuit?

**Solution.** Yes: connected and all even degrees. $|E|=22$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Add one extra copy of a single edge of $C_6$. Euler circuit?

**Solution.** Those two endpoints become degree $3$; two odds: circuit no, path yes.
