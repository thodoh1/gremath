## 3. Precise statement

The exponential generating function (EGF) of $(a_n)$ is $\sum a_n x^n/n!$. Products of EGFs correspond to partitioning a labeled set into labeled blocks (the exponential formula). $e^x$ is the EGF for $a_n=1$; $1/(1-x)$ is the EGF for $a_n=n!$; $\exp(e^x-1)$ enumerates set partitions (Bell numbers).

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** What sequence has EGF $e^{2x}$? Evaluate $a_4$.

**Solution.** $a_n=2^n$, so $a_4=16$. (Functions from an $n$-set to a $2$-set, or subsets of a labeled $n$-set with $2$ colors.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** EGF for derangements is

**Solution.** $\sum !n\, x^n/n! = e^{-x}/(1-x)$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating an EGF like an OGF and reading $a_n$ as the raw coefficient instead of $n![x^n]$.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $n!\,[x^n]e^{x}$ equals

**Solution.** $1$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** EGF for $a_n=n!$ is $1/(1-x)$. What does $a_n$ count?

**Solution.** Permutations of an $n$-element labeled set.
