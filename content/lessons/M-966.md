# Lesson M-966 — Absolute error

Part X. X. Numerical Analysis · **Numerical error** · Medium

Prerequisites: `A.1`.


This lesson isolates **Absolute error** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Medium skill: it appears less often, but when it appears it is often a discriminator. Numerical methods are additional topics: Newton, error, quadrature recognition.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Absolute error**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

Absolute error of an approximation $\hat x$ to a true value $x$ is $|x-\hat x|$. It has the same units as $x$. A stopping criterion $|x_{n+1}-x_n|<\varepsilon$ bounds the increment, not necessarily the true absolute error, unless one has a contraction estimate converting the two. Rounding a number to $d$ decimal places produces absolute error at most $\frac12\times 10^{-d}$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Approximate $\pi$ by $22/7$. Absolute error?

**Solution.** $|22/7-\pi|\approx 0.00126$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $x=2$ and $\hat x=2.1$, absolute error is

**Solution.** $0.1$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Calling $|x-\hat x|/|x|$ the absolute error, or thinking a small last increment proves a small true error for a slowly converging sequence.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Round $1.26$ to one decimal: max absolute error of the rounding rule.

**Solution.** $0.05$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Solution.** $2\times 10^{-4}$ (since $\sqrt{2}\approx 1.41421$).
