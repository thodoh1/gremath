# Lesson M-254 — Local approximation

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Taylor theorem & approximation** · High

Prerequisites: `E.4`.


This lesson isolates **Local approximation** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Local approximation**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Taylor theorem & approximation*

Taylor polynomials are the unique polynomials that match $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at a point. Remainder estimates tell you the error. Big-O notation tracks the order. Limits that look like L’Hôpital often die faster with two terms of Taylor.

### This skill, specifically

Taylor polynomials are the unique polynomials that match $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at a point.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Local approximation: near $a$, $f(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)+\frac12 f''(a)(x-a)^2+o((x-a)^2)$ if $f''$ is continuous. The quadratic Taylor polynomial is the best parabolic fit at $a$. Used to read local min/max from the second-degree term.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Local shape of $\cos x$ near $0$.

**Solution.** $1-x^2/2+\cdots$: a downward parabola, local max.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $e^x$ near $0$ vs $1+x$

**Solution.** The next term $x^2/2>0$ so $e^x$ lies above its tangent.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using a global identity when the question only needs local behavior.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Local approximation of $\sqrt{1+h}$ for small $h$.

**Solution.** $1+h/2-h^2/8+\cdots$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why does $f''(a)>0$ give a local min?

**Solution.** The quadratic term $\frac12 f''(a)(x-a)^2$ dominates and is positive.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
