## 3. Precise statement

The winding number of a closed curve $\gamma$ about $a\notin\gamma$ is $n(\gamma,a)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \dfrac{dz}{z-a}=\dfrac{\Delta\arg(\gamma(t)-a)}{2\pi}$. It is an integer, constant on connected components of the complement of $\gamma$, and $0$ on the unbounded component for a compact $\gamma$. Residue theorem generalizes to $\int_\gamma f=2\pi i\sum n(\gamma,a_k)\mathrm{Res}(f,a_k)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Winding number of the unit circle about $0$, positively oriented.

**Solution.** $1$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $n(\gamma,0)$ if $\gamma$ is $|z|=1$ traversed twice counterclockwise.

**Solution.** $2$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Treating winding number as always $1$ for every closed curve around a point, including a figure-eight or clockwise circle.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
