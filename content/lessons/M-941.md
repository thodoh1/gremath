## 3. Precise statement

A real quadratic form $ax^2+bxy+cy^2=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b/2\\ b/2&c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\ y\end{pmatrix}$. The conic $ax^2+bxy+cy^2+dx+ey+f=0$ is classified by the discriminant $b^2-4ac$ (ellipse/circle $<0$, parabola $=0$, hyperbola $>0$) after nondegeneracy checks, equivalently by the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix after translating away linear terms. Principal axes are the eigenvectors: rotate to eliminate $xy$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Classify $x^2+xy+y^2=1$.

**Solution.** $b^2-4ac=1-4=-3<0$, ellipse (positive definite form: eigenvalues $1\pm 1/2>0$).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using $b^2-4ac$ on the full six-coefficient conic without completing the square/translation, then missing degenerate cases ($x^2-y^2=0$ is a pair of lines).
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.
