# Lesson M-637 — Bounded monotone sequences

Part O. O. Real Analysis · **Sequences** · High

Prerequisites: `G.1`.


This lesson isolates **Bounded monotone sequences** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Real analysis items are often ‘which statement is true?’ definition checks inside additional topics.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Bounded monotone sequences**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A bounded monotone sequence is the standard way to manufacture a limit without naming it: show $a_{n+1}\ge a_n$ and $a_n\le M$, then $a_n\to\sup a_n$. Recurrences $a_{n+1}=f(a_n)$ are handled by checking monotonicity and an invariant bound.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $a_1=\sqrt{2}$, $a_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+a_n}$. Show $(a_n)$ converges and find the limit.

**Solution.** Induct: $a_n<a_{n+1}<2$. MCT gives a limit $L=\sqrt{2+L}$, so $L^2-L-2=0$, $L=2$ (positive).

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** If $0\le a_{n+1}\le a_n$, then $(a_n)$

**Solution.** converges to some $L\ge 0$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Passing to the limit in $a_{n+1}=f(a_n)$ without first knowing $(a_n)$ converges.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $a_{n+1}=(a_n+2/a_n)/2$, $a_1=1$. Bound?

**Solution.** By AM-GM $a_n\ge\sqrt{2}$; decreasing after $a_2$, limit $\sqrt{2}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Why identify the limit by solving $L=f(L)$?

**Solution.** Continuity of $f$ and $a_{n+1}\to L$ too.
