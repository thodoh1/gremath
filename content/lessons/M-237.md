# Lesson M-237 — First derivative test

Part E. E. Calculus I — Limits & Differentiation · **Applications of derivatives** · Critical

Prerequisites: `E.3-E.4`.


This lesson isolates **First derivative test** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. This is the core of the calculus half of the exam. Limits, derivatives, and applications are everywhere.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **First derivative test**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Applications of derivatives*

Critical points, first and second derivative tests, concavity, sketching, optimization, related rates, parametric derivatives, linear approximation, MVT and Rolle: this is why derivatives exist on the GRE. The skill is translating a word or a graph into $f'=0$ plus endpoint checks.

## 3. Precise statement

First derivative test: if $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$ at $c$, local max; $-$ to $+$, local min; no sign change, neither. Requires $f$ continuous at $c$ and differentiable on a punctured neighborhood.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Classify $x=0$ for $f(x)=x^4$.

**Solution.** $f'=4x^3$: $-$ to $+$ , local min.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $f(x)=x^3$ at $0$

**Solution.** $f'=3x^2\ge 0$ both sides: not an extremum.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Using the test when $f'$ does not exist on one side and you did not check the definition of local extremum.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $f(x)=|x|$, first derivative test at $0$.

**Solution.** Left $f'=-1$, right $+1$: local min.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $f(x)=-x^4$ at $0$.

**Solution.** $+$ to $-$ wait: $f'=-4x^3$, left positive, right negative: local max.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
