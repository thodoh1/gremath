# Lesson M-401 — Distinct real roots

Part J. J. Differential Equations · **Second-order linear ODEs** · High

Prerequisites: `J.1`.


This lesson isolates **Distinct real roots** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. ODEs appear as additional or calculus-adjacent items: separable, linear first order, characteristic equations.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Distinct real roots**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Second-order linear ODEs*

For constant coefficients, the characteristic polynomial’s roots dictate $e^{rt}$, $te^{rt}$, or $e^{\alpha t}\cos/\sin$. Undetermined coefficients and variation of parameters handle the nonhomogeneous term.

### This skill, specifically

For constant coefficients, the characteristic polynomial’s roots dictate $e^{rt}$, $te^{rt}$, or $e^{\alpha t}\cos/\sin$.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Distinct real roots $r_1\neq r_2$: general solution $y=c_1 e^{r_1 x}+c_2 e^{r_2 x}$. IVP: solve the $2\times 2$ system from $y(x_0)$ and $y'(x_0)$.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** $y''-5y'+6y=0$, $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=5$. Solve.

**Solution.** $y=c_1 e^{2x}+c_2 e^{3x}$. $c_1+c_2=1$, $2c_1+3c_2=5$ $\Rightarrow$ $c_1=-2$, $c_2=3$. So $y=-2e^{2x}+3e^{3x}$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** $y''-y=0$ general solution

**Solution.** $c_1 e^{x}+c_2 e^{-x}$ (or $\cosh,\sinh$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Writing $e^{2+3x}$ instead of a linear combination of two exponentials.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** $y''-3y'+2y=0$. General solution?

**Solution.** $c_1 e^{x}+c_2 e^{2x}$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** $y''-y'-6y=0$, $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=5$.

**Solution.** $r=3,-2$; $y=e^{3x}-e^{-2x}$.

## 9. Lock it in

You own this skill when you can do Drill 1 and Drill 2 tomorrow, mixed among unrelated problems, in under five minutes each, with the traps named in Section 7 avoided on purpose.
