# Lesson M-719 — Proof by cases

Part Q. Q. Logic, Sets & Proof · **Proof methods** · High

Prerequisites: `Q.2`.


This lesson isolates **Proof by cases** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a High skill: it is standard GRE material. You should be able to do it without warming up. Logic and proof are how other items are worded. Counting and analysis both depend on quantifiers.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Proof by cases**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 2. From zero

### Landscape of *Proof methods*

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering. Pick the method from the logical shape of the claim.

### This skill, specifically

Direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexample, existence, uniqueness, induction, strong induction, well-ordering.

You are not learning the whole topic again. You are learning the exact move named in the title. Neighboring lessons exist; use them if a word here is still foreign, then come back.

## 3. Precise statement

Proof by cases splits on a partition of the universe (even/odd, sign of $x$, $n\bmod 3$, $x\in A$ vs not). Each case must be exhaustive and mutually exclusive enough to cover all possibilities. The conclusion must be the same in every case, or pieced together carefully.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

## 4. How a working mathematician uses it

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

On the exam, this should feel like a reflex: see the pattern, apply the move, check hypotheses, box the quantity asked for.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Prove $n^2-n$ is even for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ by cases.

**Solution.** If $n=2k$, then $n(n-1)=2k(2k-1)$ even. If $n=2k+1$, then $n-1=2k$, product even. (Consecutive integers.)

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** To prove $|x|\ge x$ for all real $x$, cases are

**Solution.** $x\ge 0$ ($|x|=x$) and $x<0$ ($|x|=-x>x$).

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Missing a case (zero, or overlapping moduli) or using overlapping cases with incompatible substitutions.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Prove $x^2\ge 0$ by cases on the sign of $x$.

**Solution.** Nonnegative: square of square root or $x\cdot x$. Negative: $(-x)^2$ with $-x>0$. Or $(x-0)^2$.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Cases $n\equiv 0,1,2\pmod{3}$ exhaust

**Solution.** all integers.
