# Lesson M-447 — Vector-space axioms

Part K. K. Linear Algebra · **Vector spaces** · Critical

Prerequisites: `K.1-K.3`.


This lesson isolates **Vector-space axioms** and trains it until it is automatic.

This is a Critical skill: it is used constantly, and a hole here leaks points all over the paper. Linear algebra is a large share of the algebra portion. Rank, kernels, eigenvalues, and determinants are exam staples.

## 1. Why this skill exists

If you cannot do **Vector-space axioms**, every later item that secretly uses it becomes a two-minute panic. The GRE will not name the skill. It will drop you into a computation or a “which statement is true?” and wait.

## 3. Precise statement

A vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) is a set $V$ with addition and scalar multiplication satisfying the usual axioms: associativity, commutativity of $+$, zero and inverses, $1\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}$, and two distributivities. Examples: $\mathbb{R}^n$, $M_{m\times n}$, $P_n$, $C[a,b]$. The axioms are checked, not 'visualized as arrows'.

Copy that statement onto paper and replace each symbol with a tiny example. A definition you cannot instantiate is a definition you do not have.

Write the hypotheses, apply the move, check the conclusion against the question asked.

## 5. Worked example

**Problem.** Is the set of $2\times 2$ real matrices a vector space under the usual $+$, scalar $\cdot$?

**Solution.** Yes: $M_{2\times 2}\cong\mathbb{R}^4$.

Now cover the solution and redo it. If you cannot, you are still in Section 2.

## 6. GRE-style problem

**Problem.** Is $\{(x,y):x\ge 0\}$ a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$?

**Solution.** No: not closed under $\mathbf{v}\mapsto -\mathbf{v}$.

Notice what was *not* done: no extra theory, no calculator, no essay. The governing idea, then the computation.

## 7. Traps that cost points

- Checking only $\mathbf{0}\in V$ and skipping closure, or claiming functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ fail to form a space because they are 'not vectors'.
- Skip checking a candidate in the original equation.

## 8. Drills

Work these closed-book. Then check.

**Drill 1.** Zero vector of $P_2$.

**Solution.** the zero polynomial.

Cover that solution, redo the drill on paper, then continue.

**Drill 2.** Must scalar multiplication use the same field as the coordinate field?

**Solution.** Yes: a real vector space is not automatically a complex one.
